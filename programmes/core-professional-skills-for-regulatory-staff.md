## Aim and Purpose

To equip regulatory staff with the everyday professional skills that turn technical knowledge into effective supervisory work. Participants leave able to write clearly, analyse evidence, run and record firm meetings, challenge management constructively, present to committees and manage their caseload and data with discipline.

**Coverage:** Regulatory writing, analysis and use of evidence, planning and running firm meetings, challenging management, presenting to committees and panels, time and case management, and working with data and spreadsheets.

## Intended Audience

- New joiners and developing staff in supervisory, authorisation, policy and enforcement roles
- Staff recently promoted into case-handling roles who want to strengthen core skills
- Line managers seeking a common standard of professional practice for their teams

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Write clear, concise and evidence-based regulatory documents for firms and internal audiences
- Analyse information critically and distinguish evidence from assertion
- Plan, run and record effective meetings with firms
- Challenge management confidently, constructively and professionally
- Present findings and recommendations persuasively to committees and decision-makers
- Manage time, caseload and data with discipline and an auditable record

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Writing for Regulatory Purposes

The principles of clear regulatory writing, from structuring an argument to choosing the right tone for letters, file notes and internal papers.

**Objectives**

- Structure documents so the key message comes first
- Write in plain, precise and defensible language
- Adapt style and tone for firms, colleagues and decision-makers

**Syllabus**

- Top-down structure: conclusion first, then supporting evidence
- Plain language, precision and avoiding ambiguity
- Letters to firms, file notes, emails and briefing notes
- Exercise: rewriting a weak supervisory letter

### Module 2: Analysis, Evidence and Critical Thinking

How to analyse information from firms, test it against other sources and reach conclusions that are supported by evidence rather than assertion.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish facts, evidence, opinion and assertion
- Test information for reliability, consistency and completeness
- Draw proportionate conclusions and record the reasoning behind them

**Syllabus**

- Sources of evidence: documents, data, interviews and observation
- Corroboration, triangulation and spotting inconsistencies
- Common reasoning errors and cognitive bias
- Recording analysis so another reviewer can follow it

### Module 3: Planning and Running Firm Meetings

How to prepare for, conduct and follow up meetings with firms so that each meeting has a clear purpose and produces usable evidence.

**Objectives**

- Set objectives and an agenda for a firm meeting
- Use questioning and listening techniques to obtain useful information
- Record outcomes accurately and agree follow-up actions

**Syllabus**

- Preparation: objectives, information request and roles
- Opening, questioning, listening and controlling the meeting
- Taking notes and producing an accurate meeting record
- Follow-up letters, actions and deadlines

### Module 4: Challenging Management with Confidence

How to challenge senior people in firms constructively, handle pushback and hold the authority's position without becoming adversarial.

**Objectives**

- Recognise when and how to challenge management's account
- Use evidence-based questioning to probe weak answers
- Handle resistance, deflection and seniority with professionalism

**Syllabus**

- The supervisor's role: curious, sceptical and respectful
- Probing techniques: follow-up, clarification and silence
- Handling pushback, emotion and appeals to seniority
- Role-play: challenging a chief executive

### Module 5: Presenting to Committees and Panels

How to prepare and deliver concise, persuasive presentations to internal committees, panels and senior decision-makers, and how to handle questions.

**Objectives**

- Prepare a clear, evidence-based paper and presentation
- Present recommendations persuasively and concisely
- Respond to challenging questions from senior colleagues

**Syllabus**

- Knowing the audience and the decision required
- Structuring papers and slides for decision-makers
- Delivery, timing and confident body language
- Exercise: presenting a case to a mock committee

### Module 6: Time, Workload and Case Management

Practical techniques for prioritising competing demands, managing a portfolio of cases and keeping work moving against deadlines.

**Objectives**

- Prioritise work by risk, urgency and impact
- Plan and track multiple cases to deadline
- Maintain an accurate, auditable case record

**Syllabus**

- Prioritisation frameworks and managing competing demands
- Case planning, milestones and escalation
- Record-keeping, document management and audit trails
- Managing workload, wellbeing and knowing when to ask for help

### Module 7: Working with Data

An introduction to using regulatory returns, firm data and spreadsheets to support supervisory work, including basic analysis, visualisation and data quality checks.

**Objectives**

- Use regulatory data and firm information to identify trends and outliers
- Apply basic spreadsheet analysis and visualisation techniques
- Assess data quality and recognise its limitations

**Syllabus**

- Sources of supervisory data: returns, firm data and market information
- Spreadsheet essentials: sorting, filtering, pivot tables and charts
- Peer comparison, trend analysis and spotting outliers
- Data quality, confidentiality and secure handling

### Module 8: Putting Skills into Practice: Integrated Workshop

Brings the programme together in a simulated supervisory exercise in which participants analyse information, meet a firm and present their findings.

**Objectives**

- Combine analysis, meeting, challenge and writing skills in a realistic scenario
- Produce a clear written output from the exercise
- Reflect on strengths and set personal development priorities

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing and analysis of firm information
- Mock firm meeting with challenge and note-taking
- Drafting a follow-up letter and short committee paper
- Workshop: presentations, peer feedback and personal development plans

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module is highly practical, using exercises, role-play and peer feedback, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's templates and processes.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior experience is required. The programme is suitable for staff in their first years at the authority as well as those wishing to refresh core skills.
