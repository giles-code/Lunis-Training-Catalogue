## Aim and Purpose

To give compliance, risk and financial crime staff the practical judgement and professional skills that technical knowledge of the rules does not provide. Participants leave able to weigh evidence and reach defensible conclusions, advise and challenge the business, handle difficult conversations, escalate at the right time and write in a way that boards and supervisors trust.

## What It Covers

Professional judgement in the control functions, evidence and professional scepticism, cognitive bias, proportionality and prioritisation, advising the business, investigative questioning and fact-finding, difficult conversations, writing for the board and the regulator, escalation and breach decisions, and ethics and independence.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance, risk and financial crime analysts, officers and advisers in their first years in the role
- Experienced control function staff moving into advisory, investigation or reporting roles who want to sharpen their judgement and writing
- Business-side risk and control staff, such as first-line risk teams, who advise colleagues and escalate concerns

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why control function work depends on judgement and what makes a judgement defensible
- Assess evidence with professional scepticism and recognise the biases that distort conclusions
- Advise the business clearly and proportionately, and challenge it constructively
- Apply investigative questioning and fact-finding techniques to breaches, complaints and internal reviews
- Write clear, accurate and defensible papers, reports and correspondence for the board and the regulator
- Decide when and how to escalate, and uphold ethical standards and independence under pressure

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, drafting exercises, role-play and peer feedback, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and control function structure.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of working in or alongside a compliance, risk or financial crime function is helpful. No legal qualification is required.

## Core Reading

- Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011)
- Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony and Cass R. Sunstein, Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment (2021)
- Rebecca Milne and Ray Bull, Investigative Interviewing: Psychology and Practice (1999)
- IOSCO, Compliance Function at Market Intermediaries (2006)
