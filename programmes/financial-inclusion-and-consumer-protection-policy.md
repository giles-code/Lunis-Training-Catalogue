## Aim and Purpose

To give policymakers a practical understanding of how to expand access to useful, affordable financial services and how to protect consumers once they use them. Participants leave able to design a national financial inclusion strategy, strengthen the consumer protection framework, set up effective complaints and redress arrangements, and respond to the growing threat of fraud and scams.

## What It Covers

Measuring financial access and exclusion, national financial inclusion strategies, digital financial services and mobile money, digital identity and proportionate customer due diligence, financial literacy and capability strategies, consumer protection frameworks and institutional models, complaints handling and ombudsman schemes, fraud and scams, and evaluating policy impact.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance ministries, treasuries and central banks responsible for financial inclusion and consumer policy
- Staff of consumer protection agencies, ombudsman schemes and financial education bodies
- Legislative drafters, legal advisers and parliamentary committee staff working on consumer and financial inclusion legislation

## Learning Objectives

- Measure financial access, usage and exclusion and identify the groups most affected
- Design a national financial inclusion strategy with clear targets and governance
- Assess the opportunities and risks of digital financial services for inclusion
- Develop a national financial literacy and capability strategy
- Evaluate consumer protection frameworks, institutional models and redress arrangements
- Design a coordinated policy response to fraud and scams

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with country case studies and policy exercises, ending in a strategy workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the government's priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with financial services, social or consumer policy is helpful. No technical background in financial regulation is required.

## Core Reading

- G20/OECD (2022), High-Level Principles on Financial Consumer Protection
- World Bank (2017), Good Practices for Financial Consumer Protection
- OECD (2020), Recommendation of the Council on Financial Literacy
- CPMI and World Bank (2016), Payment Aspects of Financial Inclusion
- World Bank (2022), The Global Findex Database 2021: Financial Inclusion, Digital Payments, and Resilience in the Age of COVID-19
