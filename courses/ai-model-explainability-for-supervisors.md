## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' practical skills in explainability techniques such as LIME and SHAP, which supervisors can use to assess whether firms' AI models are sufficiently auditable and transparent. Structure informed by EU-SDFA's published course on supervising and regulating AI in the financial sector.

## Intended Audience

Mid-level supervisory staff building capability in AI-related supervision and internal AI use.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why explainability matters for supervised AI models
- Describe the LIME and SHAP explainability techniques
- Assess whether a firm's approach to explainability is proportionate to the model's stakes
- Apply an explainability framework to a sample AI model output
- Evaluate the limitations of current explainability techniques

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Why explainability matters (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain the importance of explainability*
- The 'black box' problem in complex AI models
- Why explainability matters for supervisory challenge and accountability
- Explainability versus interpretability: important distinctions
- Regulatory expectations around explainable AI
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: black-box model concerns participants have encountered

**Session 2: LIME and SHAP techniques (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Describe and apply LIME and SHAP*
- How LIME approximates model behaviour locally
- How SHAP allocates feature contribution using game-theoretic principles
- Practical demonstration of both techniques on a sample model output
- Choosing between explainability techniques for different model types
Activities:
- Exercise: apply LIME or SHAP-style analysis to a sample AI model output

**Session 3: Proportionality and limitations (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Assess proportionality and evaluate limitations*
- Matching explainability rigour to the stakes of the model's decisions
- Limitations: explainability techniques can themselves be imperfect approximations
- Case study: a firm's explainability approach under supervisory review
Activities:
- Case study: assess whether a firm's explainability approach is proportionate and adequate

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: why explainability matters, LIME/SHAP, proportionality
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Why explainability matters (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: LIME and SHAP techniques (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Proportionality and limitations (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Explainability techniques like LIME and SHAP give supervisors practical tools to probe otherwise opaque models
- The right level of explainability rigour depends on how much is at stake in the model's decisions
- Even good explainability techniques are approximations and should be interpreted with appropriate caution
