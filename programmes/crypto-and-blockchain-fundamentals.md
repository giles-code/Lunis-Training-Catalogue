## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a comprehensive, practical grounding in how blockchain technology works and how crypto-asset markets, products and service providers operate. Participants leave able to analyse business models and their economic function, identify the key prudential, conduct and financial crime risks, and apply risk-based judgement to digital finance scenarios.

**Coverage:** Blockchain technology, crypto-asset typology and market structure, wallets and custody, virtual asset service providers, tokenisation, stablecoins and unbacked crypto-assets, DeFi and emerging technologies, supervisory risks, financial crime and the travel rule, and digital finance strategy and policy.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and authorisation staff responsible for, or moving into, oversight of virtual asset service providers
- Risk, policy, legal and financial crime specialists working on digital finance
- Senior managers shaping the authority's approach to crypto-assets and digital finance

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how blockchain and distributed ledger technology operate within digital finance
- Analyse crypto-asset types and business models to determine their economic function and risk profile
- Evaluate the supervisory risks of custody, tokenisation and stablecoins
- Assess the activities, governance and controls of virtual asset service providers
- Apply AML/CFT requirements, including the travel rule, to crypto-asset activity
- Evaluate emerging trends and policy choices that balance innovation with effective risk management

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Foundations of Blockchain Technology

An introduction to how blockchains record and validate transactions, and the advantages, trade-offs and limitations of decentralised ledgers compared with centralised databases.

**Objectives**

- Explain how blockchain technology operates in practice
- Analyse consensus mechanisms and their implications
- Evaluate technological limitations and scalability challenges

**Syllabus**

- Distributed ledgers: how data is recorded and validated across networks
- Blocks, hashing and immutability: how cryptography protects integrity
- Proof of work, proof of stake and other consensus mechanisms
- Public, private and permissioned networks: use cases and limits

### Module 2: Crypto-Assets and Market Structure

The typology of crypto-assets and the structure of crypto markets, following the money to understand how value is created, transferred and traded across centralised and decentralised platforms.

**Objectives**

- Classify the main types of crypto-asset
- Analyse market structures, incentives and trading dynamics
- Evaluate risks associated with different market participants

**Syllabus**

- Cryptocurrencies, utility, governance and security tokens
- Exchanges, brokers, market makers and institutional participants
- Centralised exchanges versus decentralised trading platforms
- Liquidity, price formation and market fragmentation risks

### Module 3: Wallets, Custody and Key Management

How control of crypto-assets rests on cryptographic keys, the custody models firms use to safeguard client assets, and the operational and governance risks supervisors should test.

**Objectives**

- Explain how private keys determine control of assets
- Evaluate custody models and their risks
- Assess operational and cyber risks in custody arrangements

**Syllabus**

- Public and private keys: how asset control works
- Hot, cold, hardware, multi-signature and custodial wallets
- Segregation, reconciliation and safeguarding of client assets
- Key loss, hacks and system failure: lessons from custody incidents

### Module 4: Virtual Asset Service Providers

The roles, activities and business models of virtual asset service providers, and the challenges of defining and policing the regulatory perimeter around them.

**Objectives**

- Identify core service provider activities and services
- Analyse business models and revenue sources in crypto markets
- Assess perimeter, licensing and registration considerations

**Syllabus**

- Exchanges, brokers and trading platforms: operating structures
- Custodians, wallet providers, issuers and token platforms
- Infrastructure providers supporting crypto-asset markets
- Hybrid and decentralised models: perimeter implications

### Module 5: Tokenisation

How traditional financial assets are represented on distributed ledgers, the legal, operational and technological considerations involved, and the supervisory challenges as market structure evolves.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main tokenisation models
- Analyse tokenised asset and fund structures
- Evaluate the legal and operational risks of tokenisation

**Syllabus**

- Tokenising real-world assets: equities, bonds and funds
- Smart contracts governing asset behaviour and transfer restrictions
- Claimed benefits: efficiency, transparency and liquidity
- Legal enforceability, settlement finality and custody risks

### Module 6: Stablecoins and Unbacked Crypto-Assets

The design, uses and risks of stablecoins compared with unbacked crypto-assets, with a focus on reserves, redemption and the conditions under which a peg can fail.

**Objectives**

- Classify stablecoin types and distinguish them from unbacked crypto-assets
- Evaluate reserve, liquidity and redemption risks
- Assess governance, disclosure and oversight requirements

**Syllabus**

- Fiat-backed, crypto-collateralised and algorithmic stablecoins
- Use cases in payments, trading and settlement
- Reserve composition, redemption mechanics and stress scenarios
- De-pegging, governance failures and transparency red flags

### Module 7: DeFi and Emerging Financial Technologies

The technologies reshaping digital finance, including smart contracts, decentralised finance, interoperability and digital identity, and their implications for supervision and policy.

**Objectives**

- Explain smart contracts and decentralised finance
- Analyse interoperability and integration challenges
- Evaluate the risks and opportunities of new technologies

**Syllabus**

- Smart contracts and automated transaction execution
- DeFi protocols: lending, exchange and yield structures
- Bridges, interoperability and cross-chain risk
- Digital identity, on-chain credentials and AI in digital finance

### Module 8: Supervisory Risks in Crypto-Assets and Digital Finance

A structured framework for identifying and assessing risks across digital finance, showing how market, operational, cyber and governance risks interact within firms and markets.

**Objectives**

- Identify the key risk categories in digital finance
- Analyse operational, cyber and governance risks in crypto firms
- Evaluate market volatility and market abuse risks

**Syllabus**

- Market volatility, leverage and price dynamics
- Operational failures, cyber threats and attack vectors
- Governance and control failures: lessons from firm collapses
- Market abuse and manipulation in crypto markets

### Module 9: Financial Crime and AML/CFT Risks

Financial crime risks in crypto-asset markets and the application of AML/CFT frameworks, including blockchain analytics, privacy-enhancing technologies and the FATF travel rule.

**Objectives**

- Explain AML/CFT obligations for virtual asset service providers
- Apply travel rule requirements and recognise implementation challenges
- Evaluate transaction monitoring and blockchain analytics tools

**Syllabus**

- Illicit finance typologies in crypto-asset ecosystems
- Blockchain tracing and analytics: capabilities and limits
- Mixers, privacy coins and privacy-enhancing technologies
- FATF Recommendation 15 and the travel rule in practice

### Module 10: Digital Finance Strategy and Policy

Brings the programme together to examine how tokenisation, stablecoins and DeFi affect a jurisdiction's digital finance strategy, and how authorities can balance innovation with effective risk management.

**Objectives**

- Explain the trends shaping the future of digital finance
- Compare regulatory approaches and international standards across jurisdictions
- Evaluate policy options, supervisory capability and resourcing needs

**Syllabus**

- Comprehensive crypto-asset regimes, convergence and divergence across jurisdictions
- International standards: FSB, IOSCO, FATF and the Basel Committee
- Institutional adoption, stablecoins in payments and tokenised infrastructure
- Workshop: balancing growth, market integrity and supervisory capability

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about four hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines technical explanation with case studies, red flags and supervisory question exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of financial supervision, authorisation or financial crime work is helpful. No technical background in blockchain or crypto-assets is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Crypto-Asset Activities and Markets (2023)
- IOSCO, Policy Recommendations for Crypto and Digital Asset Markets (2023)
- FATF, Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers (2021)
- Basel Committee, Prudential Treatment of Cryptoasset Exposures (2022)
