## Aim and Purpose

To give firms a clear, practical understanding of how authorities select, investigate and resolve enforcement cases, taught from the investigator's side so the firm can anticipate each step. Participants leave able to run a disciplined response from the first information request to settlement or decision, handle privilege, interviews and individual liability, and turn the outcome into lasting remediation.

**Coverage:** How enforcement cases begin, enforcement readiness, self-reporting and early engagement, information requests and formal powers, legal privilege and document preservation, compelled interviews of staff, cooperation and remediation, warning notices and settlement, individual liability and parallel proceedings, and communications and lessons learned.

## Intended Audience

- Chief executives, senior managers and directors who may be accountable when the firm is investigated
- Heads of compliance, risk, internal audit and financial crime, and their deputies
- General counsel, in-house lawyers and company secretaries who coordinate the firm's response

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how authorities identify, select and investigate enforcement cases, and what drives their decisions
- Design an enforcement readiness framework so the firm can respond quickly and consistently to an investigation
- Manage information requests, document preservation and legal privilege without creating new regulatory risk
- Prepare and support staff for compelled interviews while managing conflicts between the firm and individuals
- Evaluate cooperation, remediation, settlement and contest options and their effect on the outcome
- Plan communications, board reporting and post-case remediation that restore the authority's confidence

## Syllabus

### Module 1: How Enforcement Cases Begin

How authorities use enforcement within the supervisory toolkit, where cases come from and how triage decides which matters become formal investigations, so the firm can recognise its own exposure early.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of enforcement and the principle of credible deterrence
- Identify the routes by which issues at the firm reach the enforcement function
- Recognise the early warning signs that a supervisory matter may escalate

**Syllabus**

- Credible deterrence and where supervision ends and enforcement begins
- Referral sources: supervision, whistleblowers, complaints, surveillance and other authorities
- Triage criteria: seriousness, harm, evidential strength and public interest
- Warning signs: escalating requests, thematic outliers and repeat findings

### Module 2: Enforcement Readiness: Before the Investigation

The arrangements a firm should have in place before an investigation starts: a response protocol, a named team, records that can be produced, insurance and a clear route to the board.

**Objectives**

- Design an investigation response protocol with clear roles and escalation
- Assess whether records, communications and data can be produced reliably
- Identify insurance, indemnity and external adviser arrangements in advance

**Syllabus**

- The response playbook: team, decision rights, external counsel and board escalation
- Record-keeping, communications capture and retention as enforcement evidence
- Directors' and officers' insurance notifications and indemnity policies
- Exercise: stress-testing the firm's readiness against a first notice

### Module 3: Self-Reporting, Notification and Early Engagement

When and how the firm should tell the authority about a potential breach, how candour affects the outcome, and how to shape scope and timetable through early engagement.

**Objectives**

- Apply notification duties and judge when a self-report is required or advisable
- Explain how openness and timely self-reporting influence enforcement outcomes
- Plan early engagement with the authority on scope, timing and the firm's own review

**Syllabus**

- Notification duties, materiality and the expectation of openness
- Self-reporting: timing, content and what the authority will do with it
- Running an internal review alongside or ahead of the authority's investigation
- Scoping meetings, case teams and agreeing a realistic timetable

### Module 4: Information Requests and Formal Powers

How authorities use their powers to require information, documents and attendance, and how the firm responds completely, on time and without inadvertently widening the case.

**Objectives**

- Explain the information-gathering powers authorities typically hold
- Manage formal requirements, deadlines and data production as a controlled project
- Negotiate scope and format while preserving credibility with the case team

**Syllabus**

- Requirements for information, documents, attendance and on-site inspections
- Reading a notice: scope, deadlines, format and consequences of non-compliance
- Production governance: search, review, quality control and sign-off
- Requests to third parties, service providers and group companies

### Module 5: Legal Privilege, Confidentiality and Evidence Preservation

How to preserve evidence from the outset, protect legal privilege and confidentiality, and decide what to share with the authority, including internal investigation reports.

**Objectives**

- Implement legal holds and preserve documents, devices and communications
- Apply legal privilege and confidentiality, and recognise how they are lost
- Evaluate the risks and benefits of sharing internal reports with the authority

**Syllabus**

- Legal holds, forensic copies and chain of custody
- Electronic disclosure, search terms and review workflows
- Legal privilege, its limits and the risk of waiver
- Sharing interview notes and reports: cooperation versus protection

### Module 6: Compelled Interviews: Preparing and Supporting Staff

How authorities plan and conduct compelled and voluntary interviews, and how the firm prepares staff fairly, manages legal representation and conflicts, and supports wellbeing.

**Objectives**

- Explain interviewees' rights and how answers given under compulsion may be used
- Prepare staff for interview without coaching or compromising the evidence
- Manage representation where the interests of the firm and individuals diverge

**Syllabus**

- Compelled versus voluntary interviews: rights, cautions and use of answers
- How investigators plan interviews and use documents and bundles
- Firm and individual representation, conflicts and separate counsel
- Role-play: preparing a senior manager for a compelled interview

### Module 7: Cooperation, Remediation and Credit

What genuine cooperation looks like to an authority, how remediation and customer redress affect the outcome, and how to evidence that root causes have been fixed.

**Objectives**

- Explain how authorities assess cooperation, aggravating and mitigating factors
- Design remediation and redress that address root causes, not symptoms
- Evidence progress through independent reviews and attestations

**Syllabus**

- Cooperation in practice: openness, timeliness and no surprises
- Aggravating and mitigating factors: concealment, self-reporting and remediation
- Root cause analysis, redress programmes and independent expert reviews
- Case study: an outcome transformed by early remediation

### Module 8: Warning Notices, Representations and Settlement

How the authority moves from findings to decision, how the firm makes effective representations, and how to weigh settlement against contesting the case.

**Objectives**

- Analyse proposed findings against the evidence and the standard of proof
- Prepare representations that are focused, evidenced and persuasive
- Evaluate settlement terms, discounts and the case for contesting

**Syllabus**

- Investigation reports, warning notices and access to material
- Effective representations: structure, evidence and tone
- Settlement: timing, discounts, agreed facts and admissions
- Penalty frameworks, contested decisions, tribunals and appeals

### Module 9: Individual Liability and Parallel Proceedings

How investigations reach senior individuals, how the firm manages conflicts between corporate and personal interests, and how to handle criminal, civil and cross-border proceedings running in parallel.

**Objectives**

- Explain how authorities pursue individuals and what reasonable steps they expect
- Manage conflicts, indemnities and separate representation for individuals
- Coordinate the response across criminal, civil and multiple-authority proceedings

**Syllabus**

- Senior manager accountability, reasonable steps and prohibition orders
- Conflicts of interest, indemnities and employment actions during investigations
- Criminal referral, sequencing and the national FIU and other agencies
- Cross-border cooperation between authorities and follow-on civil claims

### Module 10: Communications, Lessons Learned and Simulation Workshop

Brings the programme together: managing publicity and stakeholder communications, reporting to the board, embedding lessons, and a simulation that takes a case from first notice to outcome.

**Objectives**

- Plan communications with staff, customers, investors and counterparties
- Report the case and its lessons to the board with candour
- Apply the programme in a full enforcement simulation

**Syllabus**

- Final notices, publicity and reputational management
- Board reporting, disclosure obligations and investor communications
- Post-case reviews, culture change and rebuilding the supervisory relationship
- Workshop: simulation from first notice to settlement decision

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing from the investigator's side with case studies, exercises and role-play, ending in a full enforcement simulation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business and risk profile.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of compliance, legal or senior management work in a regulated firm is helpful. No prior experience of enforcement proceedings is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Credible Deterrence in the Enforcement of Securities Regulation (2015)
- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
- FSB, Strengthening Governance Frameworks to Mitigate Misconduct Risk: A Toolkit for Firms and Supervisors (2018)
- IOSCO, Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding (MMoU) and Enhanced MMoU (2016)
