## Aim and Purpose

To give directors a practical understanding of their responsibilities for preventing money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions breaches, fraud, bribery and the facilitation of tax evasion. Participants leave able to set the board's expectations, read financial crime MI critically, oversee remediation and see their firm the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** The board's AML/CFT, sanctions, fraud, bribery and tax evasion responsibilities, financial crime risk appetite, the role and reporting of the MLRO, reading financial crime MI, culture and conduct, supervisory findings and remediation, and directors' personal liability.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, non-executive and independent directors and board committee members, especially on risk and audit committees
- Executive directors and senior managers accountable for financial crime, compliance and operations
- Company secretaries and heads of governance supporting board oversight

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the board's responsibilities across AML/CFT, sanctions, fraud, bribery and tax evasion
- Set a financial crime risk appetite with measurable limits and escalation triggers
- Evaluate the role, independence and reporting of the MLRO
- Interpret financial crime MI and identify what is missing
- Oversee responses to supervisory findings and remediation programmes
- Assess directors' personal exposure and evidence effective oversight

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Financial Crime and the Board

Plain-language grounding in the financial crime risks a firm faces, the international standards behind them and why supervisors hold the board accountable for the firm's defences.

**Objectives**

- Explain money laundering, terrorist and proliferation financing, sanctions evasion, fraud, bribery and tax evasion
- Identify the international standards that shape national requirements
- Define the board's responsibilities and distinguish them from management's

**Syllabus**

- The financial crime landscape and how criminals exploit financial firms
- FATF Recommendations, mutual evaluations and the risk-based approach
- Board accountability and senior executive responsibility for financial crime
- Lessons from major enforcement cases

### Module 2: Risk Assessment and Risk Appetite

How the firm assesses its financial crime exposure across customers, products, channels and geographies, and how the board sets a risk appetite that management can apply.

**Objectives**

- Assess the quality of the business-wide financial crime risk assessment
- Set a risk appetite with in-scope and prohibited business and measurable limits
- Challenge strategic decisions that change the firm's financial crime exposure

**Syllabus**

- Business-wide risk assessment: inherent risk, controls and residual risk
- Drafting a financial crime risk appetite statement
- High-risk customers, jurisdictions, products and new business lines
- Growth, de-risking and the board's strategic choices

### Module 3: Sanctions, Fraud, Bribery and Tax Evasion

The board's oversight of the financial crime risks beyond money laundering, where liability is often strict and failures can be sudden and severe.

**Objectives**

- Explain the board's obligations under sanctions, anti-bribery and tax evasion regimes
- Evaluate whether the firm has adequate procedures to prevent these offences
- Assess the firm's fraud exposure, including payment scams and internal fraud

**Syllabus**

- Sanctions screening, ownership and control, and evasion typologies
- Fraud risk: customers, staff, third parties and emerging scams
- Bribery and corruption: gifts, intermediaries and adequate procedures
- Failure to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion

### Module 4: The MLRO and the Financial Crime Function

How the board ensures the MLRO and financial crime function have the standing, independence and resources to do their job, and how it uses their reporting.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate the independence, seniority and resourcing of the MLRO
- Assess the MLRO's annual report and the board's response to it
- Identify warning signs of an under-powered financial crime function

**Syllabus**

- Role, access to the board and the MLRO's relationship with the business
- Resourcing, systems and the use of outsourced or group functions
- The MLRO annual report: what good looks like
- Suspicious activity reporting and the national FIU

### Module 5: Reading Financial Crime MI

The information boards need to oversee financial crime, how to read MI and dashboards critically, and how to obtain independent assurance rather than relying on assertion.

**Objectives**

- Specify the management information and indicators the board needs
- Interpret financial crime MI and recognise false comfort
- Obtain independent assurance from compliance monitoring and internal audit

**Syllabus**

- The core board information set: risk profile, alerts, backlogs and reporting
- Key risk indicators: periodic review, screening, transaction monitoring and training
- Recognising comfort without evidence and green dashboards
- Compliance monitoring, internal audit and external reviews as assurance

### Module 6: Culture, Conduct and Speaking Up

How tone from the top, incentives and the board's own behaviour shape whether staff report concerns and whether commercial pressure overrides financial crime controls.

**Objectives**

- Assess the firm's financial crime culture and the signals the board sends
- Evaluate whether incentives and commercial pressure undermine controls
- Identify how the board oversees whistleblowing and speaking up

**Syllabus**

- Tone from the top, the middle and the front line
- Remuneration, sales incentives and accountability for breaches
- Whistleblowing arrangements and the board's champion
- Training and awareness that changes behaviour

### Module 7: Supervisory Findings and Remediation

How the board responds when the authority identifies serious failings, oversees remediation and look-back exercises, and avoids repeat findings.

**Objectives**

- Explain how supervisors assess financial crime controls and escalate concerns
- Evaluate remediation plans, independent reviews and look-backs
- Challenge management on root causes, timelines and sustainable fixes

**Syllabus**

- Supervisory inspections, findings letters and risk mitigation programmes
- Remediation governance: ownership, milestones and board reporting
- Look-backs, independent reviewers and validation of closure
- Repeat findings as a sign of a firm that is not learning

### Module 8: Personal Liability and the Board's Action Plan

Brings the programme together into a board action plan, examining directors' personal exposure and how the board evidences effective financial crime oversight.

**Objectives**

- Assess directors' personal regulatory, civil and criminal exposure
- Prepare the board to evidence its oversight to supervisors
- Produce a board action plan for financial crime oversight

**Syllabus**

- Personal accountability, fitness and probity and enforcement against individuals
- Reasonable steps: what the minutes and papers need to show
- Board skills, training and external advice on financial crime
- Workshop: the board's twelve-month financial crime oversight plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the board's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board or senior management experience in financial services is helpful. No specialist background in financial crime compliance is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- FATF, Guidance on Risk-Based Supervision (2021)
- Basel Committee, Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015)
