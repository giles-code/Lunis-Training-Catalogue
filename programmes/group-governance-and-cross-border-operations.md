## Aim and Purpose

To give boards and senior managers of firms that operate across borders a practical understanding of how group structures, intragroup arrangements and cross-border access shape governance, risk and regulatory exposure. Participants leave able to balance group direction with the independence of local boards, oversee intragroup services and booking models, manage conflicts between group and entity, and engage confidently with home and host supervisors.

**Coverage:** Group structures and why they matter to supervisors, subsidiaries versus branches, local board independence and group direction, intragroup outsourcing and service agreements, booking models and substance, passporting and third-country access, dealing with multiple supervisors and colleges, conflicts between group and local entity, and group stress and recovery.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, executive, non-executive and independent directors of regulated subsidiaries, and of parent and holding companies
- Country heads, branch managers and senior managers responsible for a local entity within a wider group
- Group and local heads of compliance, risk, legal, governance and regulatory affairs

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how group structures, legal entities and branches determine regulatory responsibility and supervisory scrutiny
- Evaluate the balance between group direction and the independence and accountability of local boards
- Oversee intragroup outsourcing, service agreements and booking models so that local substance and control are demonstrable
- Assess cross-border access options, including passporting, branches and third-country routes, and their governance consequences
- Manage relationships with home and host supervisors and prepare for college and joint engagement
- Identify and resolve conflicts between group and local entity interests, including in stress, and agree an action plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Group Structures and Why They Matter

How regulated firms organise across borders through holding companies, subsidiaries and branches, and why supervisors look at the group as well as the licensed entity.

**Objectives**

- Explain common cross-border group structures in investment, funds, payments and crypto-asset businesses
- Identify where regulated and unregulated entities create gaps or risks
- Explain how consolidated and group-wide supervision affects the local firm

**Syllabus**

- Holding companies, operating subsidiaries, branches and service companies
- Why groups grow complex: tax, licences, acquisitions and legacy
- Consolidated and group-wide supervision from the firm's side
- Mapping the group: legal entities, licences, people and money flows

### Module 2: Subsidiary or Branch: Structuring for Access and Control

The governance, capital, regulatory and operational trade-offs between serving a market through a subsidiary, a branch or cross-border services, and how the choice shapes supervisory expectations.

**Objectives**

- Compare subsidiaries, branches and cross-border services as routes to market
- Assess the governance and resourcing consequences of each structure
- Anticipate when a host authority may require a subsidiary or added safeguards

**Syllabus**

- Branch versus subsidiary: capital, governance, liability and resolvability
- Cross-border services without a local presence
- Host expectations of systemically or retail-significant branches
- Case study: restructuring a branch network into a regulated subsidiary

### Module 3: Local Board Independence and Group Direction

How the board of a regulated subsidiary discharges its own duties while operating within group strategy, policies and reporting lines, and what supervisors mean by a local board that is not a rubber stamp.

**Objectives**

- Explain the duties of subsidiary directors to the entity, its customers and its regulators
- Evaluate the balance between group policies, matrix reporting and local decision-making
- Apply practical tests of whether the local board genuinely governs

**Syllabus**

- Directors' duties in a subsidiary: whose interests come first
- Group policies, local adoption and documented deviations
- Matrix management, dual-hatted executives and independent directors
- Board question set: is our board governing or ratifying

### Module 4: Intragroup Outsourcing and Service Agreements

How local entities rely on group functions, technology and staff, and how to document, oversee and evidence those arrangements to the same standard as an external outsourcing.

**Objectives**

- Identify intragroup dependencies on people, systems, data and functions
- Assess intragroup service agreements, service levels and exit arrangements
- Oversee group-provided services so that responsibility is demonstrably retained locally

**Syllabus**

- Why intragroup does not mean low risk: the supervisor's view
- Service agreements: scope, standards, data, audit rights and termination
- Oversight, management information and escalation for group services
- Operational resilience and continuity when the group provider fails

### Module 5: Booking Models, Substance and Local Control

How groups decide where business is originated, executed, booked and risk-managed, and how to demonstrate that the licensed entity has real substance and control over the risks on its books.

**Objectives**

- Explain common booking and remote-booking models and the risks they create
- Assess whether the local entity has adequate people, decision-making and risk management on the ground
- Prepare the evidence of substance that host supervisors expect

**Syllabus**

- Origination, execution, booking and risk management across entities
- Back-to-back and remote booking: transfer of risk and its limits
- Substance tests: senior people, decision-making, controls and records
- Red flags supervisors look for in empty or letterbox entities

### Module 6: Passporting and Third-Country Access

How regional passporting, reliance and third-country regimes allow firms to serve clients across borders, the conditions and limits attached to them, and the governance needed to stay within them.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main models of cross-border market access in generic terms
- Assess the regulatory perimeter risks of serving clients from outside their jurisdiction
- Design governance and controls that keep cross-border activity within its permissions

**Syllabus**

- Passporting, equivalence, reliance and third-country regimes
- Reverse solicitation, delegation and other routes and their limits
- Losing access: planning for a change in regime or recognition
- Controls over cross-border marketing, onboarding and servicing

### Module 7: Dealing with Multiple Supervisors

How home and host supervisors divide responsibilities, share information and coordinate through colleges, and how the group and its local entities manage engagement, consistency and conflicting demands.

**Objectives**

- Explain home and host responsibilities and how supervisors share information
- Coordinate consistent, candid engagement across the group's supervisors
- Handle conflicting supervisory demands and joint reviews or college engagement

**Syllabus**

- Home and host roles, information-sharing and memoranda of understanding
- Supervisory colleges and joint risk assessment from the firm's side
- One message, many regulators: coordinating notifications and responses
- Exercise: responding to conflicting requests from two supervisors

### Module 8: Conflicts Between the Group and the Local Entity

Where the interests of the group and a regulated subsidiary diverge, from capital and liquidity transfers to pricing, client allocation and strategy, and how directors identify, manage and record those conflicts.

**Objectives**

- Identify common conflicts between group and local entity interests
- Apply a structured approach to managing and recording group conflicts
- Challenge group decisions that disadvantage the entity, its customers or its regulators

**Syllabus**

- Intragroup funding, dividends, guarantees and transfer pricing
- Client allocation, product shelf and revenue sharing across entities
- Local directors' conflicts when they also hold group roles
- Case study: a group restructuring the local board does not support

### Module 9: Group Stress, Recovery and Wind-Down: Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together in a cross-border stress scenario, covering contagion, recovery and wind-down planning, ring-fencing pressures and communication with supervisors, followed by an action plan.

**Objectives**

- Identify how stress spreads between entities and jurisdictions in a group
- Assess the local entity's recovery and wind-down arrangements in a group context
- Produce an action plan for strengthening group and local governance

**Syllabus**

- Contagion channels: funding, services, reputation and shared clients
- Recovery and wind-down planning for a group entity
- Simulation: a parent in difficulty and two supervisors seeking reassurance
- Workshop: the group and local governance action plan

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions for a group or subsidiary board. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies, scenarios and a director question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's group structure and markets.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board, senior management or control function experience in a firm that is part of a group or operates across borders is helpful. No legal background is required.
