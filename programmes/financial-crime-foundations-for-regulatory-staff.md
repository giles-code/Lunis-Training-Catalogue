## Aim and Purpose

To give regulatory staff a practical understanding of the main financial crime risks, the international framework set by the FATF and the controls firms are expected to operate. Participants leave able to recognise red flags in their own work, ask the right questions of firms and know when and how to escalate concerns, including to the national FIU.

## What It Covers

Money laundering and terrorist financing basics, the FATF framework and mutual evaluations, sanctions, fraud, how firms' controls work, red flags in supervisory work, working with the national FIU and law enforcement, and a red flags case workshop.

## Intended Audience

- New joiners and developing staff across supervisory, authorisation, policy and enforcement functions
- Supervisors and inspectors without a specialist financial crime background
- Staff joining a financial crime or AML/CFT supervision team

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the stages and methods of money laundering and terrorist financing
- Describe the FATF standards and how they shape national frameworks and the authority's role
- Explain how sanctions and fraud risks arise in financial firms
- Describe the controls firms are expected to operate and how supervisors test them
- Identify financial crime red flags in authorisation, supervisory and enforcement work
- Apply appropriate escalation, including cooperation with the national FIU and law enforcement

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with typologies, case studies and exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's mandate and sectors.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior financial crime experience is required. Some familiarity with financial services or the authority's work is helpful.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012, as updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- FATF (2021), Guidance on Risk-Based Supervision
- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2017, revised 2020), Sound Management of Risks Related to Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism
- FATF (2023), Guidance on Beneficial Ownership of Legal Persons
