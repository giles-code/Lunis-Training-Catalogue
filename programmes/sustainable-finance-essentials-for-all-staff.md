## Aim and Purpose

To give staff at every level a practical, balanced grounding in sustainable finance and why it matters to the firm, its clients and its supervisors. Participants leave able to discuss sustainability confidently, recognise climate and ESG risks, and make sustainability claims that are accurate and fair.

**Coverage:** ESG basics and terminology, why sustainability matters to financial firms, climate-related physical and transition risks, sustainable products and client preferences, greenwashing and fair communication, and sustainability in everyday roles.

## Intended Audience

- All staff in regulated firms, including new joiners and colleagues who are new to sustainability topics
- Client-facing, product, marketing, investment, operations and risk staff whose work touches ESG products or claims
- Line managers who need their teams to discuss sustainability accurately with clients

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain ESG and sustainable finance terminology in plain language
- Describe why sustainability matters to financial firms, their clients and supervisors
- Recognise physical and transition climate risks and how they affect the firm and its clients
- Explain how sustainability features shape products, client preferences and advice
- Identify greenwashing and make sustainability claims that are fair, clear and evidenced
- Apply sustainability to their own role and escalate concerns when needed

## Syllabus

### Module 1: ESG and Sustainable Finance: The Basics

Plain-language grounding in ESG, sustainable finance and the key terms staff will hear from clients, colleagues and the media.

**Objectives**

- Explain environmental, social and governance factors with practical examples
- Distinguish ESG integration, sustainable investment and impact investment
- Use core sustainability terminology accurately

**Syllabus**

- What ESG means and how it developed
- ESG integration, exclusions, best-in-class, thematic and impact approaches
- Net zero, transition, nature and social factors explained
- ESG ratings and data: uses and limitations

### Module 2: Why Sustainability Matters to Financial Firms

How sustainability has moved from a niche concern to a mainstream business, risk and regulatory issue, and what that means for the firm and every role within it.

**Objectives**

- Explain the commercial, risk and regulatory drivers of sustainable finance
- Describe the role of international standard-setters and disclosure frameworks
- Identify how sustainability affects their own role and team

**Syllabus**

- Client demand, investor expectations and reputational risk
- International initiatives: ISSB standards, the TCFD legacy, NGFS and IOSCO
- Taxonomies, disclosure regimes and supervisory expectations in outline
- Sustainability across the firm: products, risk, operations and people

### Module 3: Climate Risk in Plain Terms

How climate change creates physical and transition risks for the firm, its clients and the wider financial system, and how firms identify, measure and manage them.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish physical and transition climate risks with examples
- Explain how climate risk translates into credit, market, insurance and operational risk
- Describe scenario analysis and climate targets in plain language

**Syllabus**

- Physical risks: acute events and chronic change
- Transition risks: policy, technology, market sentiment and litigation
- How climate risk flows through to clients and the firm's balance sheet
- Scenario analysis, financed emissions and transition plans explained

### Module 4: Sustainability in Products and Client Conversations

How sustainability features are built into products and services, how client sustainability preferences are captured, and how to discuss ESG with clients accurately and fairly.

**Objectives**

- Explain how sustainability characteristics are reflected in product design and labels
- Capture and respond to client sustainability preferences appropriately
- Answer common client questions on ESG accurately and within their role

**Syllabus**

- Sustainable funds, green bonds, sustainability-linked loans and insurance products
- Product labels and categories: what they do and do not mean
- Client sustainability preferences and suitability
- Handling client questions: balance, evidence and knowing when to refer

### Module 5: Greenwashing and Fair Communication

What greenwashing is, how it arises in marketing, documents, client conversations and social media, and how to make sustainability claims that are fair, clear and substantiated.

**Objectives**

- Recognise common forms of greenwashing and greenhushing
- Apply fair, clear and not misleading standards to sustainability claims
- Check that claims are specific, evidenced and consistent across channels

**Syllabus**

- Forms of greenwashing: vague claims, cherry-picking, omissions and imagery
- Greenwashing at firm, product and individual communication level
- Substantiating claims: data, evidence and consistency
- Supervisory and enforcement focus on greenwashing

### Module 6: Sustainability in Your Role: Putting It into Practice

Brings the programme together by showing how sustainability applies across different roles, when to raise concerns, and practical scenarios on climate risk, client conversations and sustainability claims.

**Objectives**

- Identify the sustainability responsibilities attached to their own role
- Escalate sustainability and greenwashing concerns through the right channels
- Apply the programme to realistic workplace scenarios

**Syllabus**

- Sustainability in front-office, operations, technology, marketing and support roles
- The firm's own footprint: emissions, suppliers and people
- Speaking up on misleading claims and sustainability risks
- Workshop: what would you do? Sustainable finance scenarios

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about ninety minutes each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a one- to two-day programme or as a series of short live sessions, and well suited to conversion into e-learning. Modules combine short briefings with real-world examples and scenario questions, work well as annual refresher training, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's policies and business.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior knowledge of sustainability or ESG is required. The programme is suitable for new joiners and as annual refresher training.

## Core Reading

- ISSB (2023), IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures
- NGFS (2019), A Call for Action: Climate Change as a Source of Financial Risk
- IOSCO (2023), Supervisory Practices to Address Greenwashing
