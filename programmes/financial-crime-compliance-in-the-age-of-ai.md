## Aim and Purpose

To equip financial crime professionals with a practical command of the risk-based approach and of how AI is changing both the threat and the tools used to counter it. Participants leave able to strengthen risk assessment, due diligence, monitoring, screening and reporting, govern AI-driven controls, and show supervisors that the firm's framework works in practice, not just on paper.

## What It Covers

The risk-based approach and FATF standards, business-wide risk assessment, customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence, transaction monitoring and machine learning, sanctions and adverse media screening, fraud and authorised push payment scams, deepfakes and synthetic identity, generative-AI-enabled crime, model governance for AML tools, suspicious activity reporting, and evidencing effectiveness to supervisors.

## Intended Audience

- MLROs, deputy MLROs and heads of financial crime compliance
- Financial crime, AML, sanctions and fraud analysts, investigators and team leaders
- Compliance, risk, data and technology staff who design, validate or run financial crime systems

## Learning Objectives

- Apply the risk-based approach and FATF standards to the firm's business-wide risk assessment and controls
- Design proportionate customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence, including digital identity verification
- Evaluate machine learning in transaction monitoring, sanctions screening and fraud detection
- Identify AI-enabled threats, including deepfakes, synthetic identities and generative-AI-enabled scams
- Govern AI-driven financial crime tools through model risk management, explainability and fairness testing
- Evidence the effectiveness of the financial crime framework to the board and supervisors

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine briefings with typology case studies, alert and report review exercises and group workshops, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and risk profile.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience of AML, sanctions or fraud compliance in a regulated firm is helpful. No data science background is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- FATF, Opportunities and Challenges of New Technologies for AML/CFT (2021)
- FATF, Guidance on Digital Identity (2020)
