## Aim and Purpose

To equip firms to produce recovery and wind-down plans that are credible, usable in a crisis and proportionate to the business, rather than documents written once for a supervisor. Participants leave able to design scenarios and triggers, judge which recovery options would really work, plan an orderly solvent wind-down that returns client money and assets, and see their plan the way a supervisor does.

## What It Covers

Why plans matter, scenario and trigger design, recovery options and their credibility, liquidity and capital in stress, solvent wind-down mechanics, return of client money and assets, operational continuity and critical services, and governance, testing and supervisory review.

## Intended Audience

- Chief financial officers, chief risk officers and finance and treasury teams at investment firms, payment and e-money institutions, crypto-asset service providers and fund managers
- Compliance officers, heads of operations and plan owners responsible for drafting and maintaining recovery and wind-down plans
- Executive and non-executive directors who approve the plan and would lead the firm through a crisis

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why supervisors require recovery and wind-down plans and what makes them credible
- Design severe but plausible scenarios, early warning indicators and recovery triggers
- Evaluate recovery options for feasibility, timing and value under stress
- Plan an orderly solvent wind-down, including the return of client money and assets
- Identify the people, systems, suppliers and critical services needed to keep operating through a wind-down
- Embed the plan in governance, test it through fire drills and simulations, and evidence it to supervisors

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a briefing with worked examples and templates, the final module runs a crisis simulation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and plan.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of finance, risk or compliance in a regulated firm is helpful. No prior experience of recovery or wind-down planning is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions (2014)
- Basel Committee, Stress Testing Principles (2018)
- Basel Committee, Principles for Sound Liquidity Risk Management and Supervision (2008)
- ISO 22301:2019, Security and Resilience – Business Continuity Management Systems
