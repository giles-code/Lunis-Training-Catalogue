## Aim and Purpose

To give policymakers a clear understanding of the payments ecosystem, the new forms of digital money competing within it and the public policy questions they raise. Participants leave able to frame payments and e-money legislation, weigh the case for a central bank digital currency, set expectations of the central bank and regulators, and engage with international work on faster, cheaper cross-border payments.

## What It Covers

How payment systems work, the roles of central banks, banks and non-bank providers, payments regulation and licensing, fast payments and open banking, competition and access, stablecoins and tokenised deposits, central bank digital currencies, cross-border payments and remittances, operational resilience and financial crime, and a national payments strategy.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance ministries and treasuries responsible for payments, fintech and financial inclusion policy
- Legislative drafters, legal advisers and parliamentary committee staff working on payments or digital money legislation
- Staff of competition authorities, digital ministries and other public bodies with an interest in payments

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how retail, wholesale and cross-border payment systems work
- Describe the regulatory framework for payment and e-money providers and its policy choices
- Assess competition, access and inclusion issues in the payments market
- Evaluate the policy implications of stablecoins and tokenised money
- Weigh the case for and design choices of a central bank digital currency
- Design the elements of a national payments strategy

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a plain-language briefing with case studies and policy options analysis, ending in a strategy workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the government's priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with financial services or economic policy is helpful. No technical background in payments or monetary economics is required.

## Core Reading

- CPMI and IOSCO (2012), Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures
- BIS and a group of central banks (2020), Central Bank Digital Currencies: Foundational Principles and Core Features
- CPMI (2020), Enhancing Cross-Border Payments: Building Blocks of a Global Roadmap
- FSB (2023), High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Global Stablecoin Arrangements
- CPMI and World Bank (2016), Payment Aspects of Financial Inclusion
