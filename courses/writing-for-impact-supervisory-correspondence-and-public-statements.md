## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' skills in writing clear, authoritative supervisory correspondence and public statements that are accurate, defensible, and appropriately calibrated for their audience, from firm-level letters to public communications.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level supervisory staff developing core professional judgement skills.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the principles of clear, authoritative regulatory writing
- Apply structure and tone appropriate to different types of correspondence
- Draft a firm-level supervisory letter that is clear and defensible
- Draft a public statement calibrated for a general audience
- Evaluate a piece of regulatory writing for clarity and risk

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Principles of regulatory writing (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain principles of clear, authoritative writing*
- What makes regulatory writing clear and authoritative
- Calibrating tone: firm-level letters versus public statements
- Common weaknesses in regulatory correspondence
- Legal and reputational risk in written communication
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: examples of strong and weak regulatory writing

**Session 2: Drafting firm-level correspondence (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Apply structure and draft a supervisory letter*
- Structuring a supervisory letter: findings, expectations, next steps
- Balancing firmness with fairness in tone
- Ensuring the letter is defensible if challenged
- Common drafting pitfalls in firm-level correspondence
Activities:
- Exercise: draft a supervisory letter for a sample finding

**Session 3: Public statements and evaluation (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Draft a public statement and evaluate writing for risk*
- Calibrating a public statement for a general audience
- Balancing transparency with legal and market-sensitivity considerations
- Peer review techniques for evaluating regulatory writing
Activities:
- Exercise: draft a public statement for a sample scenario and peer-review a colleague's draft

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: principles, firm-level correspondence, public statements
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Principles of regulatory writing (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Drafting firm-level correspondence (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Public statements and evaluation (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Regulatory writing must serve multiple audiences at once — the firm, the public, and potentially a future court
- Structure and tone should be calibrated deliberately to the type of correspondence, not applied uniformly
- Peer review is one of the most effective ways to catch clarity and risk issues before a document is sent
