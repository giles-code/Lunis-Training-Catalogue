## Aim and Purpose

To give payment and e-money firms a practical understanding of the rules and supervisory expectations that apply to their business, from authorisation and safeguarding to fraud and wind-down. Participants leave able to strengthen their controls, evidence compliance and see their payments business the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** Authorisation and the regulatory perimeter, safeguarding, governance and capital, operational and security risk, fraud and authorised push payment scams, AML/CFT and sanctions, agents and distributors, open banking, wind-down planning, and stablecoins and new payment rails.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance officers, MLROs and risk managers at payment institutions and e-money issuers
- Finance, operations, safeguarding and fraud teams responsible for day-to-day controls
- Directors, senior managers and founders of payment and fintech firms

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the regulatory framework and authorisation requirements for payment and e-money firms
- Design and evidence robust safeguarding arrangements
- Assess governance, capital, operational and security risk controls
- Implement fraud, AML/CFT and sanctions controls suited to payment business models
- Oversee agents, distributors and open banking arrangements effectively
- Prepare a credible wind-down plan and assess the impact of new payment rails

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Authorisation and the Regulatory Perimeter

The main categories of payment and e-money business, how firms determine which permissions they need, and what the authority looks for in a strong application.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish payment institution, e-money and other payment business models
- Map the firm's activities to the regulatory perimeter and exclusions
- Prepare an authorisation application that anticipates supervisory concerns

**Syllabus**

- Payment services, e-money issuance and their regulatory treatment
- Perimeter questions: exclusions, commercial agents and limited networks
- Capital, business plans and the programme of operations
- Common weaknesses in payment and e-money applications

### Module 2: Safeguarding Customer Funds

Why safeguarding is the single most important customer protection in payments, the methods available and how firms design, reconcile and audit their arrangements.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose and methods of safeguarding
- Design safeguarding reconciliations and controls
- Prepare for safeguarding audits and supervisory reviews

**Syllabus**

- Segregation, insurance and guarantee methods
- Relevant funds, timing and designated safeguarding accounts
- Daily reconciliations, breaks and escalation
- Safeguarding audits, attestations and failures in insolvency

### Module 3: Governance, Capital and Fitness

The governance, risk management and capital arrangements a payment or e-money firm needs, and how boards and senior managers evidence sound oversight.

**Objectives**

- Assess governance arrangements against supervisory expectations
- Calculate and monitor own funds and capital requirements
- Apply fitness and propriety standards to directors and key function holders

**Syllabus**

- Board composition, control functions and three lines of defence
- Own funds calculations, capital planning and liquidity
- Fitness and propriety of directors and qualifying holders
- Management information and regulatory reporting

### Module 4: Operational, Security and Third-Party Risk

How payment firms manage operational and security risk, including incident reporting, strong customer authentication, outsourcing and reliance on technology providers.

**Objectives**

- Design an operational and security risk management framework
- Implement strong customer authentication and incident reporting
- Manage outsourcing and third-party risk, including cloud and banking partners

**Syllabus**

- Operational and security risk assessments and controls
- Strong customer authentication and its exemptions
- Major incident management and reporting to the authority
- Outsourcing, banking partners, cloud and concentration risk

### Module 5: Fraud and Authorised Push Payment Scams

How payment fraud and authorised push payment scams work, the controls that prevent them, and emerging approaches to reimbursement and liability sharing.

**Objectives**

- Recognise payment fraud and scam typologies
- Design fraud prevention, detection and customer warning controls
- Apply reimbursement, liability and data-sharing arrangements

**Syllabus**

- Fraud typologies: account takeover, impersonation and investment scams
- Transaction monitoring, confirmation of payee and effective warnings
- Reimbursement, liability sharing and vulnerable customers
- Fraud data, intelligence sharing and AI-enabled detection

### Module 6: AML/CFT and Sanctions in Payments

The financial crime risks specific to payment and e-money firms and how to build a risk-based AML/CFT and sanctions framework aligned with FATF standards.

**Objectives**

- Assess money laundering and terrorist financing risks in payments
- Design customer due diligence, monitoring and travel rule controls
- Implement sanctions screening for payments and customers

**Syllabus**

- Business-wide risk assessment for payment and e-money models
- Customer due diligence, simplified due diligence and e-money thresholds
- Transaction monitoring, payment transparency and suspicious activity reporting
- Sanctions screening, payment filtering and money mule risk

### Module 7: Agents, Distributors and Partners

How firms oversee agents, distributors and programme partners who act on their behalf, and remain responsible for their compliance and conduct.

**Objectives**

- Explain the firm's responsibility for agents and distributors
- Design onboarding, registration and monitoring of agents and distributors
- Assess risks in embedded finance and partner programmes

**Syllabus**

- Agents versus distributors: roles and regulatory treatment
- Due diligence, registration and fitness of agents
- Ongoing monitoring, audits and termination
- Embedded finance, programme managers and white-label models

### Module 8: Open Banking and Customer Outcomes

How account information and payment initiation services work, the obligations of providers and account holders, and how to deliver fair outcomes for payment customers.

**Objectives**

- Explain account information and payment initiation services
- Assess API access, consent and data protection obligations
- Evaluate customer outcomes, complaints and disclosure in payments

**Syllabus**

- Account information and payment initiation services
- Dedicated interfaces, consent and data sharing
- Fees, transparency and information requirements
- Complaints handling and redress

### Module 9: Wind-Down Planning and Resolution

How payment and e-money firms prepare for an orderly wind-down that returns customer funds promptly, and what supervisors expect to see in a credible plan.

**Objectives**

- Design a credible wind-down plan and trigger framework
- Identify the resources and dependencies needed for an orderly exit
- Test the plan and integrate it with safeguarding and recovery arrangements

**Syllabus**

- Wind-down triggers, indicators and governance
- Returning customer funds and insolvency considerations
- Critical staff, systems, partners and costs of exit
- Testing, review and board approval

### Module 10: Stablecoins and New Payment Rails

Brings the programme together with the developments reshaping payments: instant payments, stablecoins, tokenised deposits and agentic payments, closing with a compliance action plan.

**Objectives**

- Explain how instant payments, stablecoins and tokenised deposits work
- Assess the regulatory and risk implications of new payment rails
- Produce a payments compliance action plan

**Syllabus**

- Instant payments and settlement risk
- Stablecoins, e-money tokens and tokenised deposits under regional crypto-asset regimes
- Agentic payments and programmable money
- Workshop: seeing the payments business the way a supervisor does

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, group exercises and practical templates, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of payments, e-money or compliance is helpful. No prior knowledge of specific payment services rules is required.

## Core Reading

- CPMI, Fast Payments: Enhancing the Speed and Availability of Retail Payments (2016)
- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- CPMI and World Bank, Payment Aspects of Financial Inclusion (2016)
