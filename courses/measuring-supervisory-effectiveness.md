## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' skills in designing and using metrics that meaningfully measure supervisory effectiveness and outcomes, drawing on approaches used in international assessments such as IMF/World Bank Financial Sector Assessment Programs (FSAPs).

## Intended Audience

Mid-to-senior supervisory staff involved in setting or reviewing their authority's strategy, governance, or resourcing decisions.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why measuring supervisory effectiveness is difficult
- Describe approaches used in international assessments such as FSAPs
- Distinguish output metrics from outcome metrics
- Design a balanced set of effectiveness metrics for a supervisory function
- Evaluate the risks of poorly designed metrics

## Syllabus

**Session 1: The challenge of measuring effectiveness (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain why effectiveness measurement is difficult*
- Why supervisory effectiveness resists simple measurement
- Output metrics versus outcome metrics
- The risk of measuring what's easy rather than what matters
- International approaches: FSAP assessment methodology
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: metrics currently used in participants' own authorities

**Session 2: Designing balanced metrics (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Distinguish metric types and design a balanced set*
- Distinguishing output, process and outcome metrics
- Balancing quantitative and qualitative indicators
- Benchmarking against international peers
- Avoiding perverse incentives from poorly designed metrics
Activities:
- Group exercise: design a balanced metrics scorecard for a supervisory function

**Session 3: Risks and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Evaluate metric design risks and apply to a case study*
- Common metric design failures
- Gaming and unintended consequences of poor metrics
- Case study: a metrics framework and its unintended effects
Activities:
- Case study: critique a sample metrics framework and propose improvements

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: measurement challenges, metric design, risks
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: The challenge of measuring effectiveness (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Designing balanced metrics (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Risks and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Effectiveness metrics should measure outcomes, not just activity levels
- International assessment methodologies (like FSAPs) offer a useful external benchmark
- Poorly designed metrics can create incentives that work against genuine effectiveness
