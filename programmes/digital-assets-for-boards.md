## Aim and Purpose

To give directors a practical, jargon-free grounding in crypto-assets, stablecoins and tokenisation, the business models built on them and the risks they bring. Participants leave able to challenge management's digital asset strategy, set clear risk appetite and evidence effective oversight to supervisors and auditors.

**Coverage:** Crypto-assets, stablecoins and tokenisation, strategic opportunity and business model risk, custody and safeguarding, financial crime, market integrity, technology and cyber risk, international regulatory approaches, risk appetite, board oversight questions and a board action plan.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, non-executive and independent directors of firms offering, or planning to offer, digital asset products and services
- Executive directors and senior managers accountable for digital asset strategy, risk, compliance and operations
- Board committee members and company secretaries supporting oversight of new business lines

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain in plain language how crypto-assets, stablecoins and tokenised assets work and where the firm is exposed to them
- Challenge management's digital asset strategy, business case and business model assumptions
- Assess custody, safeguarding, financial crime and market integrity risks using structured board-level questions
- Evaluate technology, cyber and third-party risk in digital asset operations
- Compare international regulatory approaches and what supervisors expect of boards
- Set a digital asset risk appetite and agree a board action plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Digital Assets for Directors

Plain-language grounding in distributed ledgers, crypto-assets, stablecoins and tokenised assets, how they differ from traditional instruments and why they change the firm's risk profile.

**Objectives**

- Explain how blockchains, tokens and digital wallets work
- Distinguish crypto-assets, stablecoins, tokenised securities and central bank digital currencies
- Identify where the firm is already exposed to digital assets, directly or through clients and partners

**Syllabus**

- Distributed ledgers, consensus and smart contracts in plain language
- Types of digital asset and how each is used
- Stablecoins: reserve models, redemption and run risk
- The vocabulary directors need: keys, wallets, staking, DeFi, on-chain and off-chain

### Module 2: Strategy, Opportunity and Business Model Risk

How boards test whether digital assets fit the firm's strategy, challenge management's business case and recognise the business model risks that have caused failures in the sector.

**Objectives**

- Challenge the strategic rationale and claimed benefits of a digital asset proposition
- Assess revenue concentration, volatility and dependency in digital asset business models
- Recognise the business model weaknesses behind high-profile sector failures

**Syllabus**

- Business models: trading, custody, issuance, payments, tokenisation and infrastructure
- Questions to ask of a business case: demand, cost, capability, dependency and exit
- Conflicts of interest in vertically integrated platforms
- Case studies of sector failures and the governance lessons

### Module 3: Custody, Safeguarding and Client Assets

Why custody is the critical risk in digital assets, how client assets are held and protected, and what assurance the board needs that client holdings are safe and reconciled.

**Objectives**

- Explain how private keys, wallets and custody arrangements protect or expose client assets
- Evaluate segregation, reconciliation and safeguarding controls
- Judge the adequacy of independent assurance over client holdings

**Syllabus**

- Hot, warm and cold storage, multi-signature and key management
- Segregation of client assets and treatment on insolvency
- Reconciliation, proof of reserves and their limits
- Sub-custody and third-party custodian due diligence

### Module 4: Financial Crime and Sanctions Risk

The money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions and fraud risks specific to digital assets, and the controls and management information the board should expect.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main financial crime typologies involving digital assets
- Assess the firm's financial crime risk assessment, controls and use of analytics
- Oversee sanctions exposure and suspicious activity reporting

**Syllabus**

- Typologies: mixers, privacy coins, ransomware, scams and nested exchanges
- FATF standards for virtual assets and the travel rule
- Blockchain analytics, transaction monitoring and their limits
- Board-level financial crime MI and escalation

### Module 5: Market Integrity and Client Outcomes

How manipulation, conflicts and poor disclosure arise in digital asset markets, and how the board gains assurance that clients are treated fairly and markets operate with integrity.

**Objectives**

- Recognise market abuse and manipulation patterns in digital asset markets
- Assess conflicts of interest, listing decisions and client disclosures
- Evaluate whether products are appropriate for the clients they reach

**Syllabus**

- Wash trading, pump-and-dump schemes and insider dealing
- Token listing and delisting governance
- Financial promotions, marketing and retail client appropriateness
- Complaints, client outcomes and redress

### Module 6: Technology, Cyber and Third-Party Risk

How digital asset operations fail and are attacked, how dependency on protocols and providers concentrates risk, and whether the firm can withstand and recover from a serious incident.

**Objectives**

- Recognise the main technology and cyber threats to digital asset operations
- Assess dependency on blockchains, protocols and critical third parties
- Evaluate incident response, resilience testing and escalation to the board

**Syllabus**

- Hacks, key compromise, smart contract bugs and bridge exploits
- Protocol, network and oracle dependency risk
- Outsourcing and critical third-party oversight
- Incident response and recovery: what the board should require

### Module 7: International Regulatory Approaches and Supervisory Expectations

How jurisdictions are regulating crypto-assets and stablecoins, how this overlays existing financial services rules, and what supervisors expect to see from the board.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main regulatory approaches to crypto-assets, stablecoins and tokenisation
- Identify the licensing, conduct and prudential duties likely to apply to the firm
- Prepare the board to evidence oversight to supervisors

**Syllabus**

- International standards: FSB, IOSCO, FATF, Basel Committee and CPMI
- Regional crypto-asset regimes, stablecoin rules and tokenisation pilots
- Prudential treatment of crypto-asset exposures
- Operating across jurisdictions: licensing, passporting limits and divergence

### Module 8: Risk Appetite for Digital Assets

How boards decide which digital asset activities the firm will and will not undertake, and set a risk appetite with measurable limits, triggers and escalation.

**Objectives**

- Set a digital asset risk appetite with clear in-scope and prohibited activities
- Define limits and key risk indicators for exposure, counterparties and products
- Link risk appetite to capital, liquidity and wind-down planning

**Syllabus**

- Drafting a risk appetite statement for digital assets
- Limits on exposures, counterparties, assets and client types
- Stress scenarios: price collapse, stablecoin de-peg and platform failure
- Orderly exit and wind-down planning

### Module 9: Board Oversight, Information and Assurance

The information boards need to oversee digital asset activities, how to read reports critically and how to obtain credible independent assurance rather than relying on assertion.

**Objectives**

- Specify the management information and indicators the board needs
- Interpret digital asset reports and identify what is missing
- Obtain assurance from the control functions, internal audit and external reviewers

**Syllabus**

- The core board information set: exposures, client assets, incidents and financial crime
- Committee mandates and the three lines of defence for digital assets
- Recognising comfort without evidence
- A board question set for approving, expanding or exiting an activity

### Module 10: Leading Digital Asset Oversight: Board Action Plan

Brings the programme together into a board action plan covering the board's own capability, emerging developments such as tokenised markets and decentralised finance, and keeping governance current.

**Objectives**

- Assess the board's digital asset literacy and plan to close gaps
- Anticipate governance issues from tokenisation, decentralised finance and new market infrastructure
- Produce a board action plan for digital asset oversight

**Syllabus**

- Board skills, external advice and how much expertise is enough
- Tokenised deposits, securities and funds: what comes next
- Decentralised finance and accountability questions
- Workshop: the board's twelve-month digital asset oversight plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two- to three-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the board's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board or senior management experience in financial services is helpful. No technical background in blockchain or digital assets is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Crypto-Asset Activities and Markets (2023)
- IOSCO, Policy Recommendations for Crypto and Digital Asset Markets (2023)
- Basel Committee, Prudential Treatment of Cryptoasset Exposures (2022)
