## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical understanding of the business models of banks, investment firms, funds, payment firms and crypto-asset firms, and of the financial information that reveals their health. Participants leave able to read a firm's accounts with a supervisory eye, understand capital and liquidity basics and frame the right questions for management.

**Coverage:** How different financial firms make money, reading the balance sheet, income statement and cash flow, capital and liquidity basics, group structures and intra-group dependencies, sector-specific risks across banking, investment, funds, payments and crypto-assets, and a business model case workshop.

## Intended Audience

- New joiners and developing staff in supervisory, authorisation and policy functions
- Supervisors and inspectors moving into a new sector or wishing to strengthen their financial analysis
- Staff from legal, enforcement or support functions who need to understand firms' business and finances

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how banks, investment firms, funds, payment firms and crypto-asset firms generate revenue and incur risk
- Interpret the main financial statements and identify warning signs of financial weakness
- Describe the basic principles of capital and liquidity and why they matter to the authority
- Analyse group structures, intra-group dependencies and where risks can be hidden
- Identify the key risks for each sector and the supervisory questions they raise
- Apply business model analysis to form an initial view of a firm's viability and sustainability

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Business Models: How Financial Firms Make Money

An introduction to business model analysis, the main ways financial firms earn revenue and why supervisors need to understand where profits and risks come from.

**Objectives**

- Explain why business model analysis is central to supervision
- Identify the main revenue drivers of different types of financial firm
- Recognise business models that are unsustainable or depend on excessive risk

**Syllabus**

- Interest margin, fees, commissions, spreads and principal risk-taking
- Value chains, distribution and dependence on intermediaries
- Viability and sustainability: the supervisor's two questions
- Signs of a stretched model: rapid growth, outlier returns and concentration

### Module 2: Reading Financial Statements with a Supervisory Eye

How to read the balance sheet, income statement and cash flow statement, and how to spot what they reveal and what they conceal.

**Objectives**

- Interpret the structure and main lines of the financial statements
- Calculate and use basic ratios to assess performance and position
- Identify accounting judgements and disclosures that warrant challenge

**Syllabus**

- Balance sheet, income statement, cash flow and notes to the accounts
- Key ratios: profitability, leverage, cost efficiency and asset quality
- Audit opinions, going concern and related party disclosures
- Red flags: aggressive accounting, unexplained movements and late filing

### Module 3: Capital and Liquidity Basics

The purpose of capital and liquidity requirements, how they are measured and how supervisors judge whether a firm holds enough to absorb losses and meet obligations.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of capital and liquidity in protecting customers and markets
- Describe how capital requirements and liquidity measures are calculated in outline
- Assess whether a firm's capital and liquidity position supports its business plan

**Syllabus**

- Loss absorption, quality of capital and own funds
- Risk-weighted assets, leverage and fixed overheads requirements in outline
- Liquidity risk, funding profiles, buffers and stress
- Reading regulatory returns and capital planning

### Module 4: Banks and Deposit-Takers

How banks and deposit-takers create credit, manage maturity transformation and earn margin, and the credit, liquidity and concentration risks that most often cause failure.

**Objectives**

- Explain maturity transformation, credit creation and net interest margin
- Identify the main credit, liquidity and interest rate risks of banks
- Frame supervisory questions on lending, funding and asset quality

**Syllabus**

- Deposit-taking, lending and the net interest margin
- Credit risk, provisioning and non-performing exposures
- Funding concentration, deposit runs and interest rate risk
- Lessons from recent bank failures for supervisors

### Module 5: Investment Firms and Investment Funds

How brokers, asset managers, advisers and funds make money, the role of service providers and depositaries, and the conduct, liquidity and operational risks they carry.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish the business models of brokers, managers, advisers and funds
- Explain the roles of fund managers, depositaries, administrators and distributors
- Identify key risks including client asset, liquidity mismatch and conflict risks

**Syllabus**

- Brokerage, execution, asset management and advice revenue models
- Fund structures, fees and the fund service provider chain
- Client money and assets, liquidity mismatch and valuation risk
- Conflicts of interest and the supervisory question set

### Module 6: Payment Firms and Crypto-Asset Firms

The business models of payment, e-money and crypto-asset firms, how they earn revenue and why safeguarding, operational and financial crime risks dominate.

**Objectives**

- Explain how payment, e-money and crypto-asset firms generate revenue
- Assess safeguarding, custody and operational dependency risks
- Identify financial crime and consumer harm red flags specific to these sectors

**Syllabus**

- Payment flows, interchange, float income and e-money issuance
- Crypto-asset exchanges, custody, staking, lending and token issuance
- Safeguarding, segregation and the risk of customer shortfalls
- Rapid growth, thin capital and dependence on banking partners

### Module 7: Group Structures and Intra-Group Risks

How financial groups are organised, why firms sit within wider groups and how intra-group transactions, shared services and parent support affect the regulated entity.

**Objectives**

- Map a group structure and identify the regulated entity's position within it
- Assess intra-group dependencies, transactions and contagion risk
- Frame supervisory questions on group governance and ownership

**Syllabus**

- Holding companies, branches, subsidiaries and ownership chains
- Intra-group funding, guarantees, outsourcing and shared services
- Opaque and complex structures: a supervisory red flag
- Group-wide supervision and cooperation with other authorities

### Module 8: Business Model Analysis: Case Workshop

Brings the programme together in a facilitated case in which participants analyse a firm's business model and financial statements and present their supervisory concerns.

**Objectives**

- Analyse a firm's business model, financial position and key risks
- Prioritise concerns and draft supervisory questions for management
- Present and defend an initial view of the firm's viability

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing: firm profile, accounts and regulatory returns
- Ratio analysis, peer comparison and identifying outliers
- Drafting questions for a meeting with the finance director
- Workshop: presenting the business model assessment

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with worked examples and exercises using realistic financial statements, and modules can be selected or tailored to the sectors the authority supervises.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No accounting qualification is required. Basic numeracy and some familiarity with financial services are helpful.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2024), Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision
- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2008), Principles for Sound Liquidity Risk Management and Supervision
- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- FSB (2023), High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Crypto-asset Activities and Markets
