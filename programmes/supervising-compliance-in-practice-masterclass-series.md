## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical understanding of how firms build and run their compliance arrangements, and how to test them on the supervisor's side of the table. Participants leave able to judge whether arrangements are effective, spot the red flags of paper compliance and frame proportionate supervisory responses.

**Coverage:** The compliance function and monitoring programmes, AML/CFT compliance, conduct of business and suitability, complaints handling, outsourcing, governance and fitness and propriety, regulatory reporting, conflicts of interest, data protection and record-keeping, and compliance culture.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and inspectors responsible for assessing firms' compliance and control arrangements
- AML/CFT, conduct and governance specialists who review firms' policies, procedures and testing
- Newer supervisors and staff moving into supervision from compliance roles in industry

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain what effective compliance arrangements look like across the main areas of firm activity
- Assess the independence, resourcing and effectiveness of the compliance function
- Evaluate firms' policies, monitoring and testing against how they operate in practice
- Identify red flags of paper compliance, weak controls and poor customer outcomes
- Challenge senior management and control functions using targeted supervisory questions
- Formulate proportionate findings and supervisory responses for compliance weaknesses

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Compliance Function and Monitoring Programmes

How to assess the mandate, independence, resourcing and reporting lines of a firm's compliance function, and whether its risk-based monitoring programme provides real assurance.

**Objectives**

- Assess the independence, authority and resourcing of the compliance function
- Evaluate the design and coverage of a compliance monitoring programme
- Judge whether compliance reporting gives the board an accurate picture

**Syllabus**

- Compliance mandate, reporting lines and the head of compliance role
- Compliance risk assessment and the risk-based monitoring plan
- Testing methodology, findings tracking and reporting to the board
- Red flags: under-resourcing, outsourced compliance and box-ticking

### Module 2: Supervising AML/CFT Compliance Arrangements

How to assess a firm's business-wide risk assessment, customer due diligence, monitoring and reporting arrangements against the FATF Recommendations and the risk-based approach.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate a firm's business-wide and customer risk assessments
- Assess customer due diligence, transaction monitoring and suspicious activity reporting
- Identify the red flags of an ineffective AML/CFT framework

**Syllabus**

- The risk-based approach and the business-wide risk assessment
- Customer due diligence, enhanced due diligence and ongoing monitoring
- Suspicious activity reporting to the national FIU and the MLRO role
- Sample testing of customer files and alert handling

### Module 3: Conduct of Business and Suitability

How to supervise firms' conduct of business arrangements, including client categorisation, disclosure, suitability and appropriateness assessments, and fair treatment of customers.

**Objectives**

- Explain the core conduct of business obligations owed to clients
- Assess suitability and appropriateness processes and file quality
- Identify conduct risks arising from advice and distribution models

**Syllabus**

- Client categorisation, disclosure and fair, clear and not misleading communications
- Suitability and appropriateness: process, evidence and file reviews
- Advice and distribution models and their conduct risks
- Sample testing of client files: what good and poor look like

### Module 4: Complaints Handling and Root-Cause Analysis

How to assess whether a firm handles complaints fairly and promptly, analyses root causes, and uses complaints data to improve products, services and customer outcomes.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate the fairness and timeliness of a firm's complaints process
- Assess the quality of root-cause analysis and management information
- Use complaints data as an indicator of wider conduct risk

**Syllabus**

- Complaints policies, timelines, redress and escalation to dispute resolution schemes
- Complaints classification, recording and management information
- Root-cause analysis and feedback into products and processes
- Complaints as a proxy for culture and conduct risk

### Module 5: Outsourcing and Third-Party Arrangements

How to supervise firms' outsourcing and third-party arrangements, from due diligence and contracts to oversight, concentration risk and exit planning, where responsibility stays with the firm.

**Objectives**

- Explain why regulatory responsibility cannot be outsourced
- Assess due diligence, contractual safeguards and ongoing oversight
- Evaluate concentration risk, sub-outsourcing and exit strategies

**Syllabus**

- Material and critical outsourcing: identification and notification
- Due diligence, contracts, audit and access rights
- Ongoing oversight, performance monitoring and intra-group arrangements
- Concentration, sub-outsourcing and exit planning

### Module 6: Governance and Fitness and Propriety

How to assess board composition, challenge and accountability, and whether the firm's arrangements for assessing the fitness and propriety of key individuals are robust and ongoing.

**Objectives**

- Assess board composition, committee structure and quality of challenge
- Evaluate the firm's own fitness and propriety assessments of key individuals
- Identify governance weaknesses that undermine compliance

**Syllabus**

- Board structure, independence, committees and senior manager accountability
- Board papers, minutes and evidence of effective challenge
- Fitness and propriety: competence, integrity, financial soundness and time commitment
- Red flags: dominant individuals, weak challenge and rubber-stamping

### Module 7: Regulatory Reporting and Data Quality

How to assess the governance, controls and data lineage behind firms' regulatory returns, and why late, inaccurate or inconsistent reporting signals deeper control weaknesses.

**Objectives**

- Explain the supervisory reliance placed on regulatory returns
- Assess governance, reconciliation and sign-off controls over reporting
- Identify data quality red flags and frame a supervisory response

**Syllabus**

- Regulatory returns and their role in risk-based supervision
- Reporting governance: ownership, review and sign-off
- Data lineage, reconciliations and end-user computing risk
- Resubmissions, errors and breaches: supervisory follow-up

### Module 8: Conflicts of Interest

How to assess whether firms identify, prevent and manage conflicts of interest across their business, including inducements, personal account dealing and group relationships.

**Objectives**

- Identify the main sources of conflicts of interest in regulated firms
- Evaluate conflicts policies, registers and management arrangements
- Assess when disclosure is an inadequate response to a conflict

**Syllabus**

- Sources of conflict: remuneration, inducements, group and personal interests
- Conflicts policies, registers and information barriers
- Personal account dealing, gifts and hospitality
- Disclosure as a last resort: when it is not enough

### Module 9: Data Protection and Record-Keeping

How to assess firms' record-keeping arrangements and their handling of customer data, so that records are complete, retrievable and protected, and evidence the firm's compliance.

**Objectives**

- Explain record-keeping obligations and their importance to supervision
- Assess data protection governance and the handling of customer data
- Evaluate whether records are complete, retrievable and secure

**Syllabus**

- Record-keeping requirements: transactions, communications and decisions
- Data protection principles and governance in regulated firms
- Data breaches, incident response and notification
- Testing record retrieval during inspections

### Module 10: Compliance Culture: Masterclass and Capstone

Brings the series together by examining how compliance culture shows itself in behaviour, incentives and tone from the top, and applies the full series to an integrated firm case study.

**Objectives**

- Assess indicators of a firm's compliance culture
- Integrate findings across compliance areas into an overall view
- Formulate proportionate supervisory responses and follow-up

**Syllabus**

- Tone from the top and the middle: culture in practice
- Incentives, speaking up and breach reporting as cultural indicators
- From individual weaknesses to an overall compliance risk assessment
- Workshop: integrated firm case study and supervisory action plan

## Structure and Format

Ten half-day masterclasses of about three and a half hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, as a complete series or as standalone sessions. Each masterclass combines a short briefing with case studies, sample file exercises and a supervisory question set, and masterclasses can be selected or tailored to the authority's priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervision or of compliance in a regulated firm is helpful. Each masterclass is self-contained, so no prior attendance is required.
