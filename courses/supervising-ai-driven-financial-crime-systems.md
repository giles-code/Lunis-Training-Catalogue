## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' skills in assessing the governance, validation, and explainability of AI systems deployed by firms for AML/CFT purposes, applying supervisory judgement to model risk and human oversight controls.

## Intended Audience

Mid-level supervisory and financial crime specialist staff assessing firms' financial crime controls.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain model risk as it applies to AI-driven financial crime systems
- Describe expectations for model validation and ongoing monitoring
- Assess the explainability of an AI-driven financial crime system
- Evaluate the governance framework surrounding an AI model's deployment
- Formulate a supervisory assessment of an AI-driven financial crime system

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Model risk in financial crime AI (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain model risk concepts*
- What model risk means for AI-driven financial crime systems
- Model risk lifecycle: development, validation, deployment, monitoring
- Why financial crime AI models pose distinct governance challenges
- Regulatory expectations for model risk management
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: model risk incidents participants have encountered

**Session 2: Validation and explainability (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Describe validation expectations and assess explainability*
- Model validation standards and independent review
- Ongoing monitoring for model drift and degradation
- Explainability techniques for complex models
- Balancing model sophistication against explainability needs
Activities:
- Group exercise: assess a sample model validation report for adequacy

**Session 3: Governance and supervisory application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Evaluate governance and formulate an assessment*
- Governance framework expectations: ownership, sign-off, escalation
- Case study: a firm's AI model governance under review
Activities:
- Case study: formulate a supervisory assessment of a firm's AI financial crime system

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: model risk, validation, explainability, governance
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Model risk in financial crime AI (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Validation and explainability (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Governance and supervisory application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- AI models used for financial crime detection carry the same model risk discipline requirements as any other high-stakes model
- Explainability should be proportionate to the stakes of the decisions the model informs
- Strong governance — clear ownership, validation, and escalation — is as important as the model's technical performance
