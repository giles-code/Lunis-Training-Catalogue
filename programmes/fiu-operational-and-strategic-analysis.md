## Aim and Purpose

To give FIU analysts and their managers a practical grounding in the full intelligence cycle, from receiving and prioritising reports through operational and strategic analysis to dissemination and feedback. Participants leave able to produce intelligence that investigators can act on, identify trends and typologies for policy-makers and reporting entities, and use domestic and international cooperation channels with confidence.

**Coverage:** The FIU's mandate and models, the FATF standards for financial intelligence, receipt and triage of suspicious transaction reports, information sources and access, operational analysis techniques, strategic analysis and typologies, dissemination and use of intelligence, feedback and outreach to reporting entities, international cooperation through the Egmont Group, and data, analytics and measuring effectiveness.

## Intended Audience

- Operational and strategic analysts in financial intelligence units
- FIU heads of analysis, team leaders and staff managing intake, dissemination and international cooperation
- Investigators and liaison officers who request, receive or rely on FIU intelligence

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the FIU's core functions, models and obligations under the FATF standards
- Design risk-based processes to receive, triage and prioritise suspicious transaction reports
- Apply operational analysis techniques to identify targets, networks and money flows
- Produce strategic analysis that identifies trends, typologies and vulnerabilities
- Disseminate intelligence effectively and give useful feedback to reporting entities
- Use Egmont Group channels, data tools and performance measures to strengthen the FIU's impact

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The FIU: Mandate, Models and International Standards

The FIU's place in the national AML/CFT system: its core functions, the administrative, law-enforcement, judicial and hybrid models, and what the FATF standards and effectiveness assessments expect.

**Objectives**

- Explain the receipt, analysis and dissemination functions of an FIU
- Compare FIU models and their implications for powers and access to information
- Summarise what FATF Recommendation 29 and Immediate Outcome 6 require

**Syllabus**

- Core functions: receipt, analysis and dissemination
- Administrative, law-enforcement, judicial and hybrid models
- Operational independence, autonomy and protection of information
- FATF Recommendation 29, Immediate Outcome 6 and common evaluation findings

### Module 2: Receiving and Prioritising Suspicious Transaction Reports

How reports and other disclosures arrive, how their quality is checked, and how a risk-based triage process directs scarce analytical resource to the cases that matter most.

**Objectives**

- Describe the main report types and disclosures received by the FIU
- Assess report quality and recognise common reporting weaknesses
- Design a risk-based triage and prioritisation model

**Syllabus**

- Suspicious transaction reports, threshold reports and cross-border declarations
- Reporting quality: defensive reporting, gaps and data standards
- Triage criteria, risk scoring and case selection
- Managing backlogs, urgent requests and time-critical cases

### Module 3: Information Sources and Access

The information an FIU can draw on, from its own holdings to law-enforcement, administrative and open sources, and the legal and practical limits on obtaining and using it.

**Objectives**

- Identify the domestic information sources available to analysts
- Request further information from reporting entities effectively
- Use open-source and commercial data lawfully and with appropriate verification

**Syllabus**

- FIU holdings and law-enforcement, tax, customs and registry data
- Requests for further information from reporting entities
- Open-source intelligence: value, verification and safeguards
- Data protection, confidentiality and handling restrictions

### Module 4: Operational Analysis: Techniques and Tradecraft

Core methods for turning reports and data into actionable intelligence on specific targets, including link and network analysis, flow-of-funds analysis and the development and testing of hypotheses.

**Objectives**

- Apply link, network and flow-of-funds analysis to case data
- Develop and test hypotheses about suspected criminal activity
- Evaluate source reliability and grade intelligence consistently

**Syllabus**

- The analytical process: collation, evaluation, analysis and inference
- Link charts, network analysis and timelines
- Flow-of-funds and transaction pattern analysis
- Hypothesis testing, cognitive bias and source grading

### Module 5: Operational Analysis in Practice: Common Case Types

How operational analysis is applied to the cases FIUs see most often, from fraud and corruption to drug trafficking, tax crime and terrorist financing, and the financial indicators each tends to show.

**Objectives**

- Recognise the financial footprint of the main predicate offences
- Apply case-specific indicators to prioritise and develop analysis
- Identify when a case needs specialist or multi-agency input

**Syllabus**

- Fraud, corruption and politically exposed persons
- Drug trafficking, organised crime and cash-intensive laundering
- Tax crime, trade-based laundering and professional enablers
- Terrorist financing and sanctions-related reports

### Module 6: Strategic Analysis, Typologies and Risk Assessment

How FIUs use aggregated data to identify trends, typologies and vulnerabilities, and how strategic products inform national risk assessments, policy-making and supervisory priorities.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish strategic from operational analysis and their audiences
- Design a strategic analysis project from question to finished product
- Contribute FIU findings to national and sectoral risk assessments

**Syllabus**

- Purposes and audiences of strategic analysis
- Methods: trend analysis, aggregated data and environmental scanning
- Writing typologies and red-flag indicators
- Informing national risk assessment, policy and supervisory priorities

### Module 7: Dissemination and Use of Financial Intelligence

How intelligence is packaged and disseminated spontaneously and on request, how its use is tracked, and how FIUs balance timeliness, source protection and the needs of recipients.

**Objectives**

- Prepare intelligence products that recipients can act on
- Apply dissemination rules, handling conditions and source protection
- Track the use and outcomes of disseminated intelligence

**Syllabus**

- Spontaneous and on-request dissemination
- Writing for investigators, prosecutors, tax and supervisory recipients
- Handling codes, onward use and protecting reporters
- Tracking use, outcomes and case feedback

### Module 8: Feedback, Outreach and Reporting Entities

How FIUs improve reporting quality through case-specific and general feedback, guidance and outreach, and work with supervisors to address persistent weaknesses in reporting sectors.

**Objectives**

- Design effective general and case-specific feedback to reporting entities
- Identify under-reporting and poor-quality reporting by sector
- Coordinate with supervisors on reporting compliance

**Syllabus**

- Forms of feedback: case-specific, sectoral and typology-based
- Guidance, red-flag indicators and outreach programmes
- Measuring report quality and sector reporting patterns
- Working with supervisors on reporting failures

### Module 9: International Cooperation and the Egmont Group

How FIUs exchange information with foreign counterparts through Egmont Group channels and bilateral arrangements, the principles governing exchange, and how to make and answer requests well.

**Objectives**

- Explain the Egmont Group principles for information exchange
- Prepare clear, well-founded requests and timely responses
- Manage consent, onward dissemination and cooperation with non-counterparts

**Syllabus**

- The Egmont Group, its secure channel and membership expectations
- Principles of exchange: reciprocity, prior consent and confidentiality
- Drafting requests and spontaneous disclosures
- Diagonal cooperation and cross-border case coordination

### Module 10: Data, Analytics and FIU Effectiveness: Workshop

Brings the programme together by examining data and analytics tools and performance measurement, ending with a case workshop that runs the full intelligence cycle.

**Objectives**

- Assess how data platforms and analytics can strengthen FIU work
- Define meaningful measures of FIU performance and impact
- Apply the full intelligence cycle to a realistic case

**Syllabus**

- Data platforms, data matching, network analytics and machine learning
- Responsible and explainable use of advanced analytics
- Performance measures: outputs, outcomes and effectiveness
- Workshop: from report intake to dissemination

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case exercises and analytical worked examples, ending in an analysis workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the FIU's model and priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of financial intelligence, investigation or AML/CFT work is helpful. No specialist analytical or data science background is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012, as updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- Egmont Group (2013), Principles for Information Exchange Between Financial Intelligence Units
- Egmont Group (2013, as updated), Operational Guidance for FIU Activities and the Exchange of Information
- FATF (2013, as updated), Methodology for Assessing Technical Compliance with the FATF Recommendations and the Effectiveness of AML/CFT Systems
