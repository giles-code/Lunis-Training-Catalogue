## Aim and Purpose

To equip firms to understand how AI agents are moving from giving information to taking action, and the new risks this creates for authority, identity, payments, liability and financial crime. Participants leave able to design controls and governance for agentic AI and to see their firm's agent strategy the way a supervisor does.

## What It Covers

AI agents and agentic finance, delegated authority and mandates, identity and know your agent, payments by agents, controls and kill-switches, liability and customer outcomes, financial crime, third-party models, and governance and board oversight.

## Intended Audience

- Product, digital, technology and innovation leads designing or deploying AI agents
- Compliance, risk, financial crime and legal professionals overseeing agentic AI
- Senior managers and directors accountable for AI strategy and its outcomes

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how AI agents act and transact and where they are used in financial services
- Design delegated authority, mandates and identity controls for agents
- Assess payment, liability and customer outcome risks from agent actions
- Implement controls, monitoring and kill-switches for agentic systems
- Identify financial crime and third-party model risks and adapt controls
- Establish governance and board oversight and agree a readiness plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, control design exercises and group discussion, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's agentic use cases.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with AI, payments or digital products in a regulated firm is helpful. No technical background in AI is required.

## Core Reading

- OECD, Recommendation of the Council on Artificial Intelligence (2019, revised 2024)
- NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) (2023)
- FSB, The Financial Stability Implications of Artificial Intelligence (2024)
- FATF, Guidance on Digital Identity (2020)
