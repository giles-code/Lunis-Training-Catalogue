## Aim and Purpose

To equip staff with the distinct skills enforcement demands over and above ongoing supervision: evidence-gathering to a legal standard, procedural fairness and sanctioning judgement. Participants leave able to take a case from referral to outcome in a way that is proportionate, consistent and able to withstand challenge.

**Coverage:** Enforcement powers and purpose, the decision to investigate and case triage, investigation planning, evidential standards and disclosure, chain of custody and digital evidence, investigative interviews and witness statements, procedural fairness, proportionate sanctions, settlement, criminal referral and cooperation, and tribunal and court proceedings.

## Intended Audience

- Enforcement investigators, case managers and lawyers
- Supervisors who refer cases to enforcement or work alongside enforcement teams
- Members of decision-making committees and senior staff responsible for sanctioning and settlement decisions

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the enforcement function, its powers and how it differs from supervisory intervention
- Apply criteria and a triage framework to decide which cases to investigate
- Gather, preserve and present evidence to a standard that will withstand legal challenge
- Design and apply decision-making processes that meet procedural fairness standards
- Determine proportionate, consistent sanctions and judge when settlement serves the public interest
- Coordinate effectively with prosecutors and domestic and foreign authorities

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Enforcement Function and Its Powers

The purpose of enforcement within the supervisory toolkit, the powers an authority typically holds, and why enforcement requires different skills and decision standards from ongoing supervision.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purposes of enforcement: deterrence, remediation and market confidence
- Describe investigative, sanctioning and remedial powers
- Distinguish enforcement from ongoing supervisory intervention

**Syllabus**

- Credible deterrence and the role of enforcement in the authority's strategy
- Types of power: investigative, sanctioning, remedial and restitution
- IOSCO principles for enforcement and international practice
- Where supervision ends and enforcement begins

### Module 2: Deciding to Investigate: Referral and Case Triage

How cases reach enforcement, the criteria for opening an investigation, and how triage directs limited resource to the cases with the greatest impact.

**Objectives**

- Describe referral routes from supervision and other sources into enforcement
- Apply criteria for deciding whether to open an investigation
- Prioritise a caseload using severity, evidential strength and public interest

**Syllabus**

- Referral sources: supervision, whistleblowers, complaints and other authorities
- Statutory thresholds and the decision to investigate as a judgement
- Triage: severity, harm, evidential strength and public interest
- Exercise: triaging a set of sample referrals

### Module 3: Planning and Managing an Investigation

How to scope and plan an investigation, resource it with the right skills, and manage a caseload so that cases progress without drift or backlog.

**Objectives**

- Scope an investigation with clear hypotheses and lines of enquiry
- Plan resources, timelines and use of external support
- Manage a growing or backlogged caseload

**Syllabus**

- Investigation plans: scope, hypotheses, lines of enquiry and milestones
- Skills, time and external support: forensic accountants and experts
- Case governance, progress review and decisions to discontinue
- Managing caseload pressure and resource constraints

### Module 4: Evidential Standards and Disclosure

The higher evidential bar that enforcement action requires, the types of evidence available, and the disclosure and privilege obligations that run throughout an investigation.

**Objectives**

- Explain the evidential standards for enforcement compared with supervisory action
- Describe disclosure obligations to the subject of an investigation
- Identify the limits that legal privilege places on evidence

**Syllabus**

- Standards of proof and why supervisory evidence is often insufficient
- Documentary, testimonial, digital and expert evidence
- Disclosure obligations: managed throughout, not at the end
- Legal privilege and its limits

### Module 5: Gathering and Preserving Evidence

Practical techniques for obtaining evidence using formal powers, preserving it without contamination and maintaining a chain of custody that will survive challenge.

**Objectives**

- Apply formal information-gathering powers appropriately
- Maintain a defensible chain of custody and record of provenance
- Identify evidence-handling errors that undermine cases

**Syllabus**

- Information requirements, document production and on-site visits
- Preserving digital evidence: devices, communications and data
- Chain of custody and documenting provenance
- Exercise: finding the gaps in a sample evidence file

### Module 6: Investigative Interviews and Witness Statements

How to conduct compelled and voluntary interviews to an enforcement standard and take witness statements that are accurate, fair and evidentially sound.

**Objectives**

- Plan and conduct interviews to an enforcement standard
- Draft an evidentially sound witness statement
- Avoid leading or suggestive questioning that weakens evidence

**Syllabus**

- Compelled versus voluntary interviews and the rights of interviewees
- Interview planning, disclosure before interview and legal representation
- Structuring a witness statement and avoiding suggestion
- Case study: a case undermined by weak interview evidence

### Module 7: Procedural Fairness in Enforcement Decisions

The principles of natural justice as applied to enforcement, how the right to make representations is given effect, and how to design a process that is fair from the outset.

**Objectives**

- Explain the right to be heard and freedom from bias in enforcement
- Identify and manage conflicts of interest in the decision-making chain
- Design a procedurally fair process for an enforcement case

**Syllabus**

- Natural justice and the consequences of unfair process
- Warning notices, representations, timelines and format
- Separating investigation from decision-making
- Case study: a decision challenged on fairness grounds

### Module 8: Determining Proportionate Sanctions

How to apply a sanctioning framework consistently, weighing seriousness, harm and culpability with aggravating and mitigating factors, and using precedent as a guide rather than a formula.

**Objectives**

- Apply a structured sanctioning framework to the facts of a case
- Weigh aggravating and mitigating factors systematically
- Assess consistency with precedent and formulate a proportionate sanction

**Syllabus**

- Types of sanction: financial penalties, restrictions, prohibitions and public censure
- Seriousness, harm and culpability; penalty calculation methods
- Aggravating and mitigating factors: concealment, self-reporting, remediation
- Exercise: setting a sanction with reference to comparable cases

### Module 9: Settlement and Negotiated Outcomes

The benefits and risks of settlement, when it is and is not appropriate, negotiation technique specific to enforcement, and the public interest test every settlement must meet.

**Objectives**

- Explain the rationale for settlement and common discount mechanisms
- Assess when settlement should be pursued or resisted
- Evaluate a proposed settlement against deterrence and the public interest

**Syllabus**

- Speed, certainty and resource efficiency versus perceived leniency
- Settlement discounts, timing and transparency of settled cases
- Negotiation technique and power dynamics between authority and firm
- Role-play: negotiating a settlement

### Module 10: Cooperation, Litigation and Seeing a Case Through

Brings the programme together: criminal referral and parallel proceedings, cooperation with domestic and foreign authorities, giving evidence before tribunals and courts, and feeding outcomes back into supervision.

**Objectives**

- Decide when criminal referral is appropriate and manage parallel proceedings
- Use information-sharing gateways with prosecutors and domestic and foreign authorities
- Prepare to give evidence and defend a decision before a tribunal or court

**Syllabus**

- Criminal referral, sequencing and the risk of prejudicing proceedings
- Cooperation with police, tax authorities, the national FIU and foreign regulators
- Giving evidence, appeals and publication of outcomes
- Workshop: taking a case from referral to outcome

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short input with case studies, evidence exercises and negotiation role-play, ending in an end-to-end case workshop, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's own powers and procedures.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervision, investigations or regulatory legal work is helpful. No formal legal qualification is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
- IOSCO, Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding (MMoU) and Enhanced MMoU (2016)
