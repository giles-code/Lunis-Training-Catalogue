## Aim and Purpose

To equip risk managers and senior staff with a practical understanding of how an effective risk management framework is built, operated and evidenced in a regulated firm. Participants leave able to set and monitor risk appetite, assess capital and liquidity needs, run stress tests and report risk in a way that drives board decisions.

**Coverage:** The three lines model, risk appetite frameworks, risk identification and taxonomy, operational risk and risk and control self-assessment, capital and liquidity self-assessment, stress testing, emerging risks including AI, cyber and climate, risk management information and reporting, and the role of the CRO and the board.

## Intended Audience

- Chief risk officers, heads of risk and risk managers in regulated firms
- Risk analysts and operational risk staff developing their framework and reporting skills
- Finance, compliance, internal audit and business managers who own or oversee risks

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how the three lines model allocates responsibility for risk across the firm
- Design a risk appetite framework with measurable limits and triggers linked to strategy
- Apply risk identification, taxonomy and risk and control self-assessment techniques consistently
- Assess the firm's capital and liquidity needs through an internal self-assessment and stress testing
- Evaluate emerging risks, including AI, cyber and climate, and build them into the framework
- Produce risk reporting that supports board challenge and agree an action plan for the risk function

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Risk Governance and the Three Lines Model

How responsibility for risk is allocated between the business, the risk and compliance functions and internal audit, and why supervisors look closely at how this works in practice.

**Objectives**

- Explain the roles of the first, second and third lines
- Assess the independence, authority and resourcing of the risk function
- Identify common failures in how the three lines operate in practice

**Syllabus**

- Risk ownership in the business and the role of the second line
- Internal audit and independent assurance
- Risk committees, terms of reference and escalation routes
- Where the three lines blur: lessons from supervisory findings

### Module 2: Risk Appetite Frameworks

Translating strategy into a risk appetite statement with measurable metrics, limits and triggers that the board approves and management actually uses.

**Objectives**

- Design a risk appetite statement linked to the firm's strategy and business plan
- Apply quantitative metrics, limits and early warning triggers
- Embed risk appetite into decisions, planning and remuneration

**Syllabus**

- Risk capacity, appetite, tolerance and limits
- Qualitative statements and quantitative metrics
- Cascading appetite to business lines and legal entities
- Monitoring, breaches and escalation to the board

### Module 3: Risk Identification and Taxonomy

Building a complete, consistent picture of the firm's risks through a clear taxonomy, structured identification and a risk register that stays current.

**Objectives**

- Design a risk taxonomy suited to the firm's business model
- Apply structured techniques to identify current and emerging risks
- Maintain a risk register that links risks, controls and owners

**Syllabus**

- Principal risk categories and taxonomy design
- Top-down and bottom-up risk identification
- Risk registers, owners and control mapping
- Business model analysis: seeing risk as a supervisor does

### Module 4: Operational Risk and Risk and Control Self-Assessment

Managing operational risk through risk and control self-assessment, loss event data, key risk indicators and scenario analysis that produce reliable insight.

**Objectives**

- Explain the sources of operational risk and how they crystallise
- Design and run a risk and control self-assessment that challenges the business
- Apply loss data, key risk indicators and scenario analysis

**Syllabus**

- Operational risk events, causes and impacts
- Running risk and control self-assessments with credible challenge
- Loss event data, near misses and key risk indicators
- Control testing and the link to internal audit

### Module 5: Capital Adequacy Self-Assessment

How the firm identifies the risks capital must cover, assesses how much capital it needs over and above regulatory minimums, and documents the assessment for the board and supervisor.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of an internal capital adequacy self-assessment
- Assess capital needed for risks not fully captured by regulatory minimums
- Document the assessment in a way the board owns and supervisors can review

**Syllabus**

- Regulatory minimums and the case for additional internal capital
- Quantifying credit, market, operational and business risk
- Capital planning, buffers and the business plan
- Common weaknesses supervisors find in self-assessments

### Module 6: Liquidity Risk and Wind-Down Planning

Assessing the firm's liquidity needs under normal and stressed conditions, maintaining a contingency funding plan and preparing for an orderly wind-down if needed.

**Objectives**

- Explain the sources of liquidity risk in the firm's business model
- Assess liquidity adequacy under business-as-usual and stressed conditions
- Design a contingency funding plan and an orderly wind-down plan

**Syllabus**

- Liquidity risk drivers, metrics and limits
- Internal liquidity adequacy self-assessment
- Contingency funding plans and early warning indicators
- Wind-down planning: triggers, costs and timelines

### Module 7: Stress Testing and Reverse Stress Testing

Designing severe but plausible stress scenarios, assessing their impact on capital, liquidity and operations, and using reverse stress testing to find what would break the business model.

**Objectives**

- Design stress scenarios proportionate to the firm's risks and size
- Assess the impact of stress on capital, liquidity and profitability
- Apply reverse stress testing to identify business model vulnerabilities

**Syllabus**

- Scenario design: severity, plausibility and relevance
- Linking stress results to capital, liquidity and management actions
- Reverse stress testing and recovery options
- Presenting stress test results to the board

### Module 8: Emerging Risks: AI, Cyber and Climate

How the risk function identifies, assesses and monitors emerging risks from artificial intelligence, cyber threats and climate change, and integrates them into the existing framework.

**Objectives**

- Identify emerging risks relevant to the firm and its customers
- Assess AI, cyber and climate risks using the firm's existing risk tools
- Integrate emerging risks into appetite, scenarios and reporting

**Syllabus**

- Emerging risk radars and horizon scanning
- AI and model risk, cyber threats and third-party dependency
- Climate-related physical and transition risks
- Expectations of international standard-setters on emerging risks

### Module 9: Risk Management Information and Reporting

Building risk reporting that is accurate, timely and decision-useful, from data aggregation to dashboards that tell the board what it needs to know.

**Objectives**

- Design risk management information aligned to appetite and principal risks
- Apply principles of risk data aggregation and reporting quality
- Produce risk reports that support decisions rather than reassure

**Syllabus**

- Risk data aggregation, quality and lineage
- Dashboards, key risk indicators and trend analysis
- Writing the CRO report: insight, judgement and recommendations
- Case study: reporting that failed to warn the board

### Module 10: The CRO, the Board and Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together around the CRO's relationship with the board and risk committee, and a workshop in which participants assess and plan improvements to their own framework.

**Objectives**

- Explain the CRO's role, independence and relationship with the board
- Evaluate the effectiveness of the firm's risk framework from a supervisor's perspective
- Produce a prioritised action plan for the firm's risk management

**Syllabus**

- The CRO's mandate, independence and access to the board
- The risk committee: challenge, oversight and effectiveness
- Seeing the firm the way a supervisor does: a framework self-assessment
- Workshop: the risk function's twelve-month action plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines practical frameworks with case studies, group exercises and worked examples, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of risk management, finance or control work in a regulated firm is helpful. No quantitative modelling background is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Principles for an Effective Risk Appetite Framework (2013)
- Basel Committee, Principles for Effective Risk Data Aggregation and Risk Reporting (BCBS 239) (2013)
- Basel Committee, Stress Testing Principles (2018)
- Basel Committee, Revisions to the Principles for the Sound Management of Operational Risk (2021)
