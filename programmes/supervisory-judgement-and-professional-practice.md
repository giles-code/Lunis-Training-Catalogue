## Aim and Purpose

To equip supervisors with the professional disciplines that turn information into well-reasoned, proportionate and defensible supervisory judgements. Participants leave able to weigh evidence with scepticism, recognise bias, communicate findings clearly in writing and in person, and escalate and act with integrity and independence.

**Coverage:** The nature of supervisory judgement, evidence and professional scepticism, cognitive bias, proportionality and prioritisation, meetings with boards and senior management, difficult conversations, report and letter writing, findings and risk mitigation programmes, escalation, and ethics and independence.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and senior supervisors responsible for risk assessments, firm engagement and supervisory findings
- Team leaders and managers who review, challenge and sign off supervisory judgements and correspondence
- Staff in enforcement, authorisation and policy functions who rely on, or contribute to, supervisory conclusions

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain what supervisory judgement is and why it must be exercised under uncertainty
- Evaluate evidence with professional scepticism and recognise the biases that distort decisions
- Apply proportionality to prioritise risks and select the right supervisory response
- Communicate findings clearly and firmly in meetings, reports and supervisory letters
- Design findings and risk mitigation programmes that are specific, measurable and followed up
- Act with integrity and independence, and escalate and defend judgements under challenge

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Nature of Supervisory Judgement

Why supervision depends on judgement rather than rule-checking alone, where uncertainty comes from, and what accountability for a judgement requires of the individual supervisor and the authority.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of judgement in risk-based, forward-looking supervision
- Identify the main sources of uncertainty in supervisory decisions
- Distinguish judgement from discretion and describe what accountability requires

**Syllabus**

- From compliance checking to judgement-led supervision
- Sources of uncertainty: incomplete information, evolving risk and ambiguous rules
- The cost of over- and under-cautious judgements
- Judgement, discretion and the supervisor's accountability

### Module 2: Evidence, Professional Scepticism and Challenge

How supervisors gather and weigh evidence, test management's assertions rather than accept them, and decide when the evidence is sufficient to support a conclusion.

**Objectives**

- Assess the reliability, relevance and sufficiency of supervisory evidence
- Apply professional scepticism to firms' assertions and management information
- Challenge firms constructively while keeping an open mind

**Syllabus**

- Types of evidence: documents, data, interviews and observation
- Corroboration, triangulation and testing assertions
- Red flags: comfort without evidence and over-polished narratives
- When is enough evidence enough to conclude

### Module 3: Cognitive Bias and Structured Decision-Making

The biases that affect experienced and new supervisors alike, how they distort risk assessment, and the structured frameworks and safeguards that make judgements more consistent.

**Objectives**

- Identify common cognitive biases in supervisory decisions
- Apply structured judgement frameworks to reduce inconsistency
- Evaluate the safeguards that counter individual and group bias

**Syllabus**

- Confirmation bias, anchoring, groupthink, optimism and hindsight bias
- How bias distorts risk ratings and relationship supervision
- Structured frameworks, pre-mortems and devil's advocate techniques
- Panels, peer review and calibration as structural safeguards

### Module 4: Proportionality and Risk-Based Prioritisation

How to match supervisory effort and response to the size of the risk, prioritise issues across a portfolio, and select the right tool from the supervisory toolkit.

**Objectives**

- Apply proportionality to the scale, nature and impact of a firm's risks
- Prioritise issues using impact, probability and supervisory objectives
- Select a supervisory tool or intervention that fits the risk

**Syllabus**

- Proportionality in principle and in practice
- Impact and probability: prioritising across firms and issues
- The supervisory toolkit: from dialogue to formal powers
- Avoiding both regulatory overreach and supervisory forbearance

### Module 5: Meetings with Boards and Senior Management

How to plan and run effective supervisory meetings with chairs, directors and senior managers, set the agenda, test understanding and leave with clear outcomes.

**Objectives**

- Plan a supervisory meeting with clear objectives and lines of enquiry
- Engage senior individuals with authority, courtesy and challenge
- Record outcomes and agree next steps that can be followed up

**Syllabus**

- Purpose of meetings: risk assessment, findings and relationship building
- Preparation: briefing packs, agendas and question sets
- Chairing and steering the meeting while testing understanding
- Meeting notes, actions and confirming outcomes in writing

### Module 6: Difficult Conversations and Managing Pushback

How to deliver adverse findings and unwelcome decisions clearly and constructively, handle defensive or aggressive reactions, and stay firm without becoming inflexible.

**Objectives**

- Explain why firms react defensively to adverse findings
- Apply techniques for delivering difficult messages clearly
- Manage pushback while protecting credibility for future engagement

**Syllabus**

- Types of difficult supervisory conversation and how to prepare
- Structuring the delivery of an adverse finding
- Handling challenge, lobbying and appeals to seniority
- Role-play: delivering a finding to a senior manager

### Module 7: Writing Supervisory Reports and Letters

How to write clear, authoritative and defensible reports, internal papers and supervisory letters that set out findings, expectations and next steps for the right audience.

**Objectives**

- Apply the principles of clear, authoritative regulatory writing
- Structure internal papers and supervisory letters for decision and action
- Evaluate drafts for clarity, tone and legal and reputational risk

**Syllabus**

- Writing for the reader: decision-makers, boards and firms
- Structuring a supervisory letter: findings, expectations and deadlines
- Calibrating tone: firmness, fairness and avoiding ambiguity
- Drafting exercise: a supervisory letter following a review

### Module 8: Findings and Risk Mitigation Programmes

How to turn analysis into well-evidenced findings and a risk mitigation programme with specific, measurable actions, owners and deadlines, and how to track remediation to closure.

**Objectives**

- Formulate findings that link evidence, root cause and risk
- Design a risk mitigation programme with measurable actions and deadlines
- Assess whether remediation is complete, effective and sustained

**Syllabus**

- Anatomy of a good finding: issue, evidence, root cause and impact
- Designing actions, owners, deadlines and evidence of completion
- Tracking remediation and validating closure
- Red flags: repeat findings, slippage and cosmetic remediation

### Module 9: Escalation, Ethics and Independence

When and how to escalate concerns within the authority, the ethical standards supervisors must uphold, and how to protect independence against capture, conflicts and external pressure.

**Objectives**

- Identify the triggers and routes for escalating supervisory concerns
- Recognise conflicts of interest, capture and other threats to independence
- Apply ethical standards to grey-area situations in supervisory work

**Syllabus**

- Escalation triggers, routes and the escalation paper
- Regulatory capture, familiarity threats and conflicts of interest
- Gifts, hospitality, confidentiality and post-employment considerations
- Ethical dilemmas: case discussions from supervisory practice

### Module 10: Forming and Defending Judgements: Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together in a case study in which participants form a judgement on an ambiguous scenario, document it, present it to a review panel and defend it under challenge.

**Objectives**

- Formulate a reasoned judgement on an ambiguous supervisory scenario
- Document the reasoning so that it is defensible under later scrutiny
- Defend a judgement before a panel and respond to challenge

**Syllabus**

- Recording the rationale: options considered and reasons for the decision
- Hindsight, internal review and external scrutiny of supervisory decisions
- Presenting to a review or risk panel
- Workshop: form, document and defend a supervisory judgement

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, drafting exercises and role-play, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's supervisory approach.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervising regulated firms is helpful. The programme is suitable for both newer and experienced supervisors.
