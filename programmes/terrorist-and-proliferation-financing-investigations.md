## Aim and Purpose

To give specialist analysts, investigators and prosecutors an advanced understanding of how terrorist and proliferation networks raise, move and use funds, and of the tools available to detect and disrupt them. Participants leave able to develop financial intelligence on these threats, apply targeted financial sanctions, protect the non-profit sector proportionately and build cases with domestic and international partners.

**Coverage:** The terrorist and proliferation financing threat and the FATF standards, sources of terrorist funds, movement and storage of funds, risk-based protection of the non-profit sector, proliferation financing networks, targeted financial sanctions, financial intelligence and information sharing, and prosecution and disruption.

## Intended Audience

- FIU analysts and counter-terrorism financial investigators
- Prosecutors, intelligence officers and sanctions implementation officials
- Customs, export control and non-profit oversight officials working on terrorist or proliferation financing

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the terrorist and proliferation financing threat and the FATF standards that address it
- Analyse how terrorist and proliferation networks raise, move and store funds
- Assess non-profit sector risk proportionately, without disrupting legitimate activity
- Apply targeted financial sanctions, including identifying and freezing assets
- Develop financial intelligence and investigative strategies for these cases
- Share intelligence and coordinate disruption with domestic and international partners

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Threat and the International Framework

The evolving terrorist and proliferation financing threat, how it differs from money laundering, and the FATF standards and United Nations sanctions regimes that frame the national response.

**Objectives**

- Explain how terrorist and proliferation financing differ from money laundering
- Summarise FATF Recommendations 5 to 8 and the related Immediate Outcomes
- Assess risk assessment findings on terrorist and proliferation financing

**Syllabus**

- Small sums, legitimate sources and the purpose-based offence
- FATF Recommendations 5 to 8 and Immediate Outcomes 9 to 11
- United Nations Security Council targeted sanctions regimes
- Terrorist and proliferation financing risk assessment

### Module 2: Sources of Terrorist Funds

How terrorist organisations, small cells and lone actors raise funds, from legitimate income and abuse of donations to criminal activity, extortion and state sponsorship.

**Objectives**

- Identify the principal sources of terrorist funding
- Distinguish the financing needs of organisations, cells and individuals
- Recognise the links between terrorist financing and organised crime

**Syllabus**

- Legitimate income, self-funding and personal credit
- Criminal activity, extortion and control of territory
- Donations, crowdfunding and abuse of social media
- State sponsorship and the crime–terror nexus

### Module 3: Movement and Storage of Funds

The channels used to move and store terrorist funds, including banks, money service businesses, informal value transfer, cash couriers and crypto-assets, and the financial indicators they may leave.

**Objectives**

- Describe the channels used to move and store terrorist funds
- Identify relevant indicators in financial and payment data
- Assess the use of new payment methods and crypto-assets

**Syllabus**

- Banks, money service businesses and informal value transfer
- Cash couriers and cross-border cash movements
- Prepaid cards, online payments and crypto-assets
- Financial indicators and their limitations

### Module 4: The Non-Profit Sector: Risk-Based Protection

How non-profit organisations can be abused for terrorist financing, how to identify the subset at risk, and how to act proportionately without disrupting legitimate humanitarian and charitable work.

**Objectives**

- Explain FATF Recommendation 8 and the risk-based approach to non-profits
- Identify the forms of abuse and the organisations most at risk
- Avoid unintended consequences, including de-risking and financial exclusion

**Syllabus**

- Forms of abuse: diversion, affiliation, false representation and sham organisations
- Identifying the subset of non-profits at risk
- Outreach, oversight and investigation of abuse
- Unintended consequences and humanitarian safeguards

### Module 5: Proliferation Financing Networks

How networks finance the procurement of weapons of mass destruction and dual-use goods, conceal their activity through front companies and shipping, and how investigators identify them.

**Objectives**

- Explain how proliferation networks procure goods and move funds
- Recognise indicators of proliferation financing and sanctions evasion
- Link financial, trade and export control information

**Syllabus**

- Procurement networks, dual-use goods and front companies
- Sanctions evasion through trade, shipping and corporate structures
- Proliferation financing risk indicators
- Working with export control and customs authorities

### Module 6: Targeted Financial Sanctions in Practice

How targeted financial sanctions work from designation to freezing and delisting, how authorities identify and freeze assets without delay, and how breaches are investigated.

**Objectives**

- Explain designation, freezing obligations and exemptions
- Identify assets of designated persons, including those held indirectly
- Investigate and respond to sanctions breaches and circumvention

**Syllabus**

- Designation, proposal and listing processes
- Freezing without delay and ownership and control tests
- Licences, exemptions and delisting
- Investigating breaches and circumvention

### Module 7: Financial Intelligence and Information Sharing

How financial intelligence supports counter-terrorism and counter-proliferation work, from urgent reporting and rapid analysis to sharing with intelligence services, foreign FIUs and the private sector.

**Objectives**

- Apply financial intelligence techniques to terrorist and proliferation cases
- Manage urgent requests and time-critical financial information
- Share intelligence securely across agencies, borders and with the private sector

**Syllabus**

- Financial profiling of subjects and networks
- Urgent reporting, rapid analysis and monitoring
- Sharing with intelligence services, foreign FIUs and through the Egmont Group
- Public-private partnerships on terrorist and proliferation financing

### Module 8: Prosecution, Disruption and Multi-Agency Workshop

Brings the programme together by examining prosecution and disruption options, ending with a workshop in which teams develop a coordinated response to a terrorist or proliferation financing case.

**Objectives**

- Compare prosecution, sanctions and administrative disruption options
- Build the evidence needed for terrorist financing charges
- Agree a coordinated multi-agency response to a realistic case

**Syllabus**

- Terrorist financing offences and evidential challenges
- Using intelligence as evidence and protecting sources
- Disruption: freezing, designation and administrative measures
- Workshop: a coordinated multi-agency response

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines an expert briefing with case studies and analytical exercises, ending in a multi-agency workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the national threat picture.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Substantial experience of financial intelligence, counter-terrorism or financial crime investigation is helpful. Familiarity with the FATF standards is assumed.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2015), Emerging Terrorist Financing Risks
- FATF (2019), Terrorist Financing Risk Assessment Guidance
- FATF (2023), Best Practices on Combating the Abuse of Non-Profit Organisations
- FATF (2021), Guidance on Proliferation Financing Risk Assessment and Mitigation
- FATF (2013), International Best Practices: Targeted Financial Sanctions Related to Terrorism and Terrorist Financing (Recommendation 6)
