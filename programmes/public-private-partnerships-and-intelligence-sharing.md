## Aim and Purpose

To give FIU and law-enforcement managers a practical understanding of how financial information-sharing partnerships work and what makes them effective. Participants leave able to choose an appropriate partnership model, put lawful gateways and safeguards in place, run tactical and strategic sharing, and measure and demonstrate results.

**Coverage:** The case for public-private partnerships, partnership models and governance, legal gateways for sharing, data protection and confidentiality safeguards, tactical and strategic information sharing, private-to-private sharing and collaborative analytics, and measuring results.

## Intended Audience

- FIU heads, partnership managers and senior analysts
- Law-enforcement leads for economic crime, fraud and terrorist financing
- Policy, legal and data protection officers advising on information-sharing arrangements

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the purposes and benefits of public-private financial information sharing
- Compare partnership models and choose one suited to the national framework
- Identify the legal gateways that permit sharing and the limits they impose
- Apply data protection, confidentiality and tipping-off safeguards
- Run tactical and strategic sharing that produces actionable results
- Measure and demonstrate the partnership's outcomes and value

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Case for Public-Private Partnerships

Why one-way reporting alone is not enough, what public-private partnerships add to financial intelligence, and what FATF work and international experience say about their effectiveness.

**Objectives**

- Explain the limits of one-way suspicious transaction reporting
- Describe the benefits and risks of public-private partnerships
- Summarise FATF work on private sector information sharing

**Syllabus**

- From one-way reporting to two-way sharing
- Benefits: better targeting, faster response and improved reporting
- Risks: legal exposure, de-risking and unequal participation
- FATF guidance and international experience

### Module 2: Partnership Models and Governance

The main models of public-private partnership, from strategic forums to tactical taskforces, and the governance, membership and trust arrangements that make them sustainable.

**Objectives**

- Compare strategic, tactical and hybrid partnership models
- Design governance, membership and terms of reference
- Build trust and manage expectations between partners

**Syllabus**

- Strategic forums, tactical taskforces and hybrid models
- Membership: banks, payment firms, crypto-asset providers and others
- Terms of reference, chairing and decision-making
- Vetting, security and building trust

### Module 3: Legal Gateways and Data Protection

The legal bases that permit sharing between authorities and firms and between firms, and how to embed data protection, confidentiality and tipping-off safeguards from the outset.

**Objectives**

- Identify the legal gateways for public-to-private and private-to-private sharing
- Apply data protection principles, including necessity and proportionality
- Manage confidentiality, tipping-off and liability risks

**Syllabus**

- Statutory gateways, safe harbours and information-sharing provisions
- Data protection principles and impact assessments
- Confidentiality, tipping-off and protection from liability
- Privacy-enhancing technologies and collaborative analytics

### Module 4: Tactical and Strategic Sharing in Practice

How partnerships share information on specific cases and subjects and on threats and typologies, and how outputs feed investigations, reporting and firms' controls.

**Objectives**

- Run tactical sharing on live cases securely and lawfully
- Produce strategic threat and typology products with partners
- Turn shared intelligence into investigative and preventive action

**Syllabus**

- Tactical sharing: requests, responses and targeted reporting
- Strategic sharing: threat assessments, typologies and alerts
- Handling, dissemination and onward use of shared information
- Feeding results into investigations and firms' monitoring

### Module 5: Measuring Results and Demonstrating Value

How to measure the outputs, outcomes and wider impact of a partnership, avoid vanity metrics, and report results credibly to government, partners and assessors.

**Objectives**

- Define meaningful measures of partnership performance
- Collect and attribute results across agencies and firms
- Report outcomes credibly, including to mutual evaluation assessors

**Syllabus**

- Outputs, outcomes and impact: what to measure
- Attributing arrests, freezes and reporting improvements
- Qualitative evidence and case studies
- Reporting to government, partners and assessors

### Module 6: Designing a Partnership: Workshop

Brings the programme together in a workshop in which teams design or improve a partnership for their own context, covering model, legal basis, safeguards, priorities and measures.

**Objectives**

- Apply the programme to design or reform a partnership
- Identify legal and practical obstacles and how to address them
- Produce a partnership plan with priorities and measures

**Syllabus**

- Assessing the current information-sharing landscape
- Choosing the model, members and priority threats
- Legal, data protection and governance checklist
- Workshop: the partnership design and twelve-month plan

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with case studies of partnership models and design exercises, ending in a partnership planning workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the national legal framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of financial intelligence, investigation or AML/CFT policy is helpful. No legal or data protection expertise is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2017), Guidance on Private Sector Information Sharing
- FATF (2021), Stocktake on Data Pooling, Collaborative Analytics and Data Protection
- FATF (2022), Partnering in the Fight Against Financial Crime: Data Protection, Technology and Private Sector Information Sharing
- Egmont Group (2013), Principles for Information Exchange Between Financial Intelligence Units
