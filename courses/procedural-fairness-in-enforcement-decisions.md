## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of the principles of procedural fairness that must be applied in enforcement decision-making, including the right to make representations and managing conflicts of interest.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level enforcement investigators and case officers.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the principles of natural justice as applied to enforcement
- Describe the right to make representations and how it is given effect
- Identify and manage conflicts of interest in enforcement decision-making
- Assess whether a decision-making process meets procedural fairness standards
- Formulate a fair process design for a sample enforcement case

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Principles of natural justice (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain natural justice principles*
- The two pillars of natural justice: right to be heard, freedom from bias
- How natural justice applies specifically to enforcement decisions
- Consequences of procedural unfairness for enforcement outcomes
- International and domestic case law touchpoints
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: procedural fairness challenges participants have encountered

**Session 2: Representations and conflicts of interest (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Describe representation rights and manage conflicts of interest*
- Giving effect to the right to make representations
- Timelines and format for representations
- Identifying conflicts of interest in the decision-making chain
- Structural separation between investigation and decision functions
Activities:
- Group exercise: identify conflicts of interest in a sample decision-making structure

**Session 3: Assessing and designing fair process (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Assess fairness and design a fair process*
- Criteria for assessing procedural fairness of a process
- Designing a fair process from the outset rather than retrofitting
- Case study: a decision challenged on procedural fairness grounds
Activities:
- Case study: design a procedurally fair decision-making process for a sample case

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: natural justice, representations, conflicts of interest
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Principles of natural justice (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Representations and conflicts of interest (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Assessing and designing fair process (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Procedural fairness is not a technicality — unfair process can invalidate an otherwise justified enforcement outcome
- Structural separation between investigation and decision-making is one of the strongest safeguards against bias
- Building fairness into a process design from the outset is far more effective than correcting it after challenge
