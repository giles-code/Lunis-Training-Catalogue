## Aim and Purpose

What good board information looks like, why more information can mean worse oversight, and how to reshape board packs so that directors can see the issues that matter.

## Learning Objectives

- Define the qualities of good board information
- Evaluate board papers for a clear ask, real options and a balanced view of risk
- Specify improvements to board packs, presentations and dashboards

## Syllabus

- Good information: accurate, timely, focused, balanced and comparable
- Information overload, attention and reliance on mental shortcuts
- A good paper: the decision required, options, risks and prior challenge
- Presentations, executive summaries and conducting an information audit
