## Aim and Purpose

To give participants a structured, end-to-end understanding of investment funds, from first principles through organisation, legal form, regulation, governance, fund types, distribution, documentation, operations, investment, legal matters and the fund lifecycle. Participants leave able to follow how each party contributes to a fund and to speak confidently with managers, service providers, investors and supervisors.

## What It Covers

What funds are and why investors use them, the parties to a fund, legal forms and structures, regulation of funds and managers, governance and delegation, traditional and alternative fund types, marketing and distribution, fund documentation, dealing, pricing and liquidity, portfolio investment, legal considerations and the lifecycle from launch to wind-down.

## Intended Audience

- New joiners and graduates at fund managers, administrators, depositaries, transfer agents, law firms and distributors
- Staff of supervisory authorities joining fund supervision or authorisation teams
- Professionals moving into the funds industry from banking, audit or other sectors who need a complete foundation

## Learning Objectives

- Explain what an investment fund is, how it works and why different investors use funds
- Describe the roles of the management company, investment manager, depositary, administrator and other parties
- Compare the main legal forms and structures a fund can take and the reasons for choosing each
- Outline how funds, managers and their distribution are regulated and how fund governance and delegation work
- Distinguish traditional, money market, exchange traded and alternative funds and the documentation each produces
- Follow a fund's operations, investments, legal arrangements and risks across its lifecycle from launch to termination

## Structure and Format

Fourteen modules of around three hours each, deliverable as a two-week foundation course, a series of half-day sessions or a blended online programme. Each module combines short teaching inputs, worked examples using real fund documents and knowledge checks, and modules can be selected or tailored to a firm's or authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior knowledge of investment funds is required. A general awareness of financial markets is helpful.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- IOSCO (2013), Principles for the Valuation of Collective Investment Schemes
- IOSCO (2018), Recommendations for Liquidity Risk Management for Collective Investment Schemes
- FSB (2017), Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Asset Management Activities
