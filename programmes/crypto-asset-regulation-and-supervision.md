## Aim and Purpose

To equip supervisors with the practical tools to apply a comprehensive crypto-asset regime across the full supervisory lifecycle, from authorisation and risk-based supervision to enforcement and crisis management. Participants leave able to test business models, governance, prudential resources, custody and financial crime controls, and to look beneath firms' documentation to how they actually operate.

**Coverage:** The crypto-asset ecosystem, international standards and comprehensive crypto-asset regimes, service provider business models, governance and internal controls, prudential resources, authorisation, risk-based supervision, custody and operational resilience, market abuse, AML/CFT and sanctions, stablecoins and systemic risk, and cross-border cooperation, enforcement and crisis management.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and authorisation staff responsible for crypto-asset service providers and stablecoin issuers
- Risk, prudential, enforcement and financial crime specialists covering digital finance
- Senior managers responsible for the authority's crypto-asset supervisory strategy

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the international standards for crypto-assets and how comprehensive regimes apply them to issuers and service providers
- Assess business models, governance, internal controls and prudential resources of crypto-asset firms
- Evaluate authorisation applications and operate a risk-based approach to ongoing supervision and inspection
- Assess custody, safeguarding and operational resilience arrangements for crypto-asset operations
- Evaluate market abuse, money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions risks and the controls firms use to manage them
- Apply supervisory, enforcement and crisis management tools, including cross-border cooperation and stablecoin oversight

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Crypto-Asset Ecosystem for Supervisors

A supervisor's map of the technology, assets and market structure of the crypto ecosystem, focusing on where control really sits, how value and risk flow, and where failures typically originate.

**Objectives**

- Explain how distributed ledgers, tokens and crypto-asset markets operate in practice
- Identify the true control points, incentives and accountability gaps in the ecosystem
- Analyse the technical and operational failure modes that drive consumer and prudential harm

**Syllabus**

- Public, permissioned and hybrid ledgers: design choices and supervisory leverage
- Issuers, validators, venues, custodians, market makers and end users
- Control points: upgrades, forks, admin keys, oracles and listings
- Failure modes: smart contract flaws, oracle manipulation and off-chain dependencies

### Module 2: International Standards and Comprehensive Crypto-Asset Regimes

The international standards for crypto-assets and how comprehensive regimes translate them into perimeter, authorisation, conduct, prudential and disclosure requirements, including where gaps and ambiguities create supervisory risk.

**Objectives**

- Explain the objectives and content of the main international crypto-asset standards
- Analyse how comprehensive regimes classify crypto-assets, issuers and service providers
- Evaluate gaps between formal compliance documentation and actual firm behaviour

**Syllabus**

- FSB high-level recommendations and global stablecoin recommendations
- IOSCO crypto and DeFi policy recommendations; FATF Recommendation 15
- Basel Committee standard on banks' crypto-asset exposures
- Regime architecture: primary law, technical standards, guidance and white papers

### Module 3: Business Models of Crypto-Asset Service Providers

The business models of issuers, exchanges, brokers, custodians and infrastructure providers across the centralised-to-decentralised spectrum, and how supervisors use business model analysis to locate risk.

**Objectives**

- Differentiate centralised and decentralised models and where regulatory obligations attach
- Map common business models to regulated services and their prudential and conduct risks
- Apply business model analysis to test viability, conflicts and substance

**Syllabus**

- The centralised-to-decentralised spectrum and perimeter questions
- Conflicts of interest: exchanges as market makers, lenders and custodians
- Red flags: opaque fees, proprietary tokens and fragmented group structures
- Stress-testing projections, organisation charts and outsourcing registers

### Module 4: Governance, Risk Management and Internal Controls

How to look beneath a crypto firm's governance documents to judge whether its management body, risk, compliance and audit functions are genuinely effective, and how to intervene proportionately.

**Objectives**

- Assess the composition and functioning of the management body and its oversight functions
- Evaluate the independence, resourcing and influence of risk, compliance and internal audit
- Apply proportionate supervisory interventions to governance and control weaknesses

**Syllabus**

- Board composition, fitness and propriety and founder-dominated governance
- Three lines of defence and risk appetite in a crypto business
- Red flags: governance capture, nominal independence and marginalised control functions
- Intervention tools: management requirements and independent expert reviews

### Module 5: Prudential Risk, Financial Resources and Sustainability

How capital, liquidity and wind-down requirements apply to crypto-asset service providers and issuers, and how supervisors identify financially fragile firms before problems become critical.

**Objectives**

- Apply capital and liquidity requirements to different service provider and issuer types
- Evaluate liquidity mismatches, contingency funding and recovery planning
- Identify prudential red flags and select appropriate supervisory responses

**Syllabus**

- Own funds, liquidity and wind-down planning for service providers
- Revenue volatility, market cycles and reliance on affiliate funding
- Red flags: thin capital, related-party exposures and untested projections
- Prudential returns analysis, stress testing and early warning indicators

### Module 6: Authorising Crypto-Asset Service Providers

A proportionate, risk-based framework for assessing applications from crypto-asset service providers, testing substance, governance, group dependencies and operations before authorisation is granted.

**Objectives**

- Operate an authorisation gateway with clear quality thresholds and early challenge
- Assess application dossiers systematically, from programme of operations to ICT security
- Evaluate substance, group dependencies and conflicts to prevent letter-box authorisations

**Syllabus**

- Completeness, quality thresholds and risk-based prioritisation of applications
- Programme of operations, business plans, management and shareholder suitability
- Group structures, intragroup outsourcing and local substance
- Regulatory history, customer journeys and exposure to unregulated or DeFi services

### Module 7: Risk-Based Supervision in Practice

How to design and run a risk-based supervisory approach to crypto firms: prioritising resources, monitoring between engagements, conducting inspections and using graduated powers to drive remediation.

**Objectives**

- Design a risk-based framework that allocates resources by impact and probability
- Conduct ongoing monitoring using returns, market intelligence and early warning indicators
- Plan, execute and follow up inspections of crypto-asset firms

**Syllabus**

- Firm risk assessment, supervisory planning and resource allocation
- Red flags triggering enhanced engagement: rapid growth, turnover and incidents
- Inspection methodology: document requests, interviews and walkthroughs
- Graduated supervisory powers and tracking remediation

### Module 8: Custody, Safeguarding and Operational Resilience

How crypto custody and the wider ICT estate work in practice, the operational, cyber and third-party risks they create, and how supervisors verify that client assets and critical services are protected.

**Objectives**

- Explain custody architectures, including cold storage, hardware security modules, multi-signature and MPC
- Assess safeguarding, segregation and reconciliation of client assets
- Evaluate ICT risk management, incident reporting and third-party dependencies

**Syllabus**

- Proprietary custody, third-party custodians and sub-custody chains
- Red flags: commingling, weak key controls and missing independent reconciliation
- Cloud concentration, API dependencies and on-chain/off-chain integration risk
- Incident reporting, resilience testing and exit planning for critical providers

### Module 9: Preventing Crypto Market Abuse

How insider dealing and manipulation occur in crypto markets, from wash trading to maximal extractable value, and how supervisors assess surveillance and use suspicious order reports as intelligence.

**Objectives**

- Identify insider dealing and unlawful disclosure risks in token issuance and trading
- Detect traditional and crypto-native manipulation schemes
- Assess firms' trade surveillance and suspicious transaction and order reporting

**Syllabus**

- Market abuse prohibitions adapted to crypto-asset markets
- Wash trading, spoofing, pump-and-dump schemes and MEV exploitation
- Surveillance systems, alert calibration and investigation workflows
- Investigative techniques: order book analysis and on-chain tracing

### Module 10: AML/CFT, the Travel Rule and Sanctions

The money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion typologies seen in crypto markets, and how supervisors test firms' controls and coordinate findings with the national FIU.

**Objectives**

- Identify illicit finance and sanctions evasion typologies specific to crypto-assets
- Evaluate customer due diligence, transaction monitoring, travel rule and screening controls
- Apply AML/CFT supervisory techniques and coordinate with the national FIU

**Syllabus**

- Typologies: mixers, privacy coins, cross-chain hopping and high-risk exchanges
- Blockchain analytics: capabilities, limits and use as a verification tool
- FATF travel rule in practice: unhosted wallets and the sunrise issue
- Red flags, wallet screening and the role of the money laundering reporting officer

### Module 11: Stablecoins and Systemic Risk

How stablecoins are structured and supervised, the reserve, redemption and governance risks that determine whether a peg holds, and how stress can transmit to the wider financial system.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish stablecoin structures and how design drives risk and regulatory classification
- Evaluate reserve quality, liquidity and redemption resilience under stress
- Assess systemic risk, bank exposures and supervisory tools for significant stablecoins

**Syllabus**

- Fiat-backed, asset-referenced, crypto-collateralised and algorithmic designs
- Reserve composition, custody, attestation and redemption rights
- Red flags: affiliated reserves, redemption frictions and unchecked policy changes
- Runs, contagion and enhanced oversight of systemically significant stablecoins

### Module 12: Cross-Border Cooperation, Enforcement and Crisis Management

Brings the programme together on the hardest cases: firms operating across borders, escalation to enforcement, and the failure of a crypto-asset firm, alongside the emerging risks supervisors must prepare for.

**Objectives**

- Operate cross-border cooperation and information exchange with other authorities
- Apply the graduated enforcement toolkit proportionately with sound documentation
- Manage a crypto firm in distress, drawing on lessons from major market failures

**Syllabus**

- Home-host responsibilities, supervisory colleges and regulatory arbitrage
- From supervisory findings to enforcement: case files and rights of defence
- Crisis management: early warning, client asset recovery and wind-down
- Workshop: a cross-border crypto firm failure simulation

## Structure and Format

Twelve modules of about four hours each, grouped into five pathways and delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, as a multi-week programme or as a series of half-day sessions with pre-reading. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red-flag exercises and practical supervisory tools, and pathways or individual modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's regime.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Completion of Crypto and Blockchain Fundamentals, or equivalent experience of crypto-asset markets, is helpful. Some experience of authorisation, supervision or enforcement work is recommended but not mandatory.

- FSB, High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Global Stablecoin Arrangements (2023)
- IOSCO, Policy Recommendations for Crypto and Digital Asset Markets (2023)
- FATF, Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers (2021)
