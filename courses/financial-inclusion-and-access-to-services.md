## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to introduce participants to financial inclusion as a policy and supervisory concern, exploring the barriers that prevent access to financial services and the regulatory tools available to promote inclusive access without compromising other regulatory objectives such as financial crime prevention.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level supervisors assessing consumer protection outcomes.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the concept and measurement of financial inclusion
- Identify common barriers to accessing financial services
- Discuss the tension between financial inclusion and financial crime controls
- Evaluate inclusive product design practices
- Assess the role of regulation in promoting or hindering inclusion
- Identify policy options to improve financial inclusion outcomes

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Understanding financial inclusion (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain financial inclusion and its barriers*
- Defining and measuring financial inclusion
- Barriers: cost, access, documentation, trust, digital literacy
- Financial inclusion as a supervisory and policy concern
- Global perspectives on inclusion initiatives
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: barriers to access in participants' own markets

**Session 2: Inclusion versus other regulatory objectives (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Discuss the tension between inclusion and financial crime controls*
- The 'de-risking' problem: how AML/CFT controls can restrict access
- Balancing proportionate financial crime controls with inclusion
- Inclusive product design: simplified accounts, tiered KYC
- International examples of proportionate approaches
Activities:
- Group discussion: designing a proportionate KYC regime for a basic account product

**Session 3: Policy options and supervisory role (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Assess regulatory levers and identify policy options*
- The supervisor's role in promoting inclusion
- Policy levers: proportionality, guidance, innovation hubs
- Measuring progress on financial inclusion
- Case study: an inclusion initiative and its outcomes
Activities:
- Case study exercise: assess an inclusion initiative and propose improvements

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: barriers, tensions, policy options
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Understanding financial inclusion (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Inclusion versus other regulatory objectives (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Policy options and supervisory role (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Financial inclusion and financial crime prevention can pull in different directions and need active balancing
- Proportionality is the key regulatory tool for enabling inclusive access without excessive risk
- Supervisors have a genuine role to play in promoting, not just permitting, inclusion
