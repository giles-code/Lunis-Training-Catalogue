## Aim and Purpose

To equip the people who design products, serve customers and oversee conduct with the frameworks and tools to deliver and evidence good outcomes for retail customers. Participants leave able to test products, value, communications and support against outcomes, act on what the data shows and report credibly to the board and supervisors.

**Coverage:** Product governance, fair value, communications and disclosure, vulnerable customers, sales and advice, complaints and root cause analysis, outcomes testing and management information, conduct risk frameworks, culture and incentives, and board reporting.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance, conduct risk and consumer outcome specialists
- Product, marketing, pricing and customer service managers
- Senior managers and business-line owners accountable for retail customer outcomes

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how conduct risk arises and why supervisors focus on outcomes rather than process
- Design product governance and fair value assessments that withstand scrutiny
- Apply outcome testing to communications, sales, advice and customer support
- Identify and support customers in vulnerable circumstances
- Evaluate complaints, root causes and management information to find and fix customer harm
- Produce conduct risk reporting that supports board challenge and agree an action plan for the firm

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Conduct Risk and the Outcomes-Based Approach

Why supervisors have moved from rules-based compliance to testing real outcomes for customers, and what that means for the firm's governance and control functions.

**Objectives**

- Explain the drivers of conduct risk and customer harm
- Distinguish outcome-based supervision from process compliance
- Identify where the firm's business model creates conduct risk

**Syllabus**

- Conduct risk: definition, drivers and harm
- From disclosure to outcomes: how supervisory expectations have shifted
- Business model and strategy as sources of conduct risk
- Lessons from mis-selling and customer redress cases

### Module 2: Product Governance

Designing, approving and reviewing products so that they meet the needs of a defined target market and are distributed to the right customers.

**Objectives**

- Design a product approval and review process with clear accountability
- Define target markets and distribution strategies
- Assess existing products against target market needs

**Syllabus**

- Product approval committees and governance
- Target market definition and testing
- Manufacturer and distributor responsibilities
- Product reviews, withdrawal and closed-book customers

### Module 3: Fair Value and Pricing

Assessing whether the price customers pay is reasonable relative to the benefits they receive, and identifying pricing practices that cause harm.

**Objectives**

- Design a fair value assessment methodology
- Assess pricing practices, charges and differential treatment of customer groups
- Act on fair value findings through product or pricing change

**Syllabus**

- Fair value frameworks: price, benefits, costs and quality
- Loyalty penalties, price walking and hidden charges
- Distribution costs and remuneration in the value chain
- Documenting and evidencing fair value assessments

### Module 4: Communications and Disclosure

Producing communications that customers actually understand and that support good decisions, and testing their effectiveness.

**Objectives**

- Apply clear, fair and not misleading standards to customer communications
- Design communication testing to measure customer understanding
- Identify behavioural pitfalls such as sludge and choice architecture

**Syllabus**

- Financial promotions and disclosure standards
- Behavioural insights and choice architecture
- Communication testing methods and evidence
- Digital journeys, online sales and social media

### Module 5: Vulnerable Customers

Identifying customers in vulnerable circumstances, adapting products, services and support, and monitoring whether they achieve outcomes as good as other customers.

**Objectives**

- Explain the drivers of vulnerability and how they affect customer decisions
- Design processes to identify and support vulnerable customers
- Monitor outcomes for vulnerable customers compared with others

**Syllabus**

- Drivers of vulnerability: health, life events, resilience and capability
- Staff training, recording and data protection
- Adjusting products, journeys and support
- Outcome monitoring for vulnerable customer groups

### Module 6: Sales, Advice and Customer Support

Controlling sales and advice processes so that recommendations are suitable, and providing support that does not create unreasonable barriers for customers.

**Objectives**

- Assess sales and advice processes for suitability and pressure selling
- Design file reviews and quality assurance for advice
- Evaluate customer support journeys for barriers and friction

**Syllabus**

- Suitability, appropriateness and execution-only sales
- Advice file reviews and quality assurance
- Customer support: switching, cancellation and claims
- Case study: a sales process under supervisory review

### Module 7: Complaints and Root Cause Analysis

Handling complaints fairly and using complaint data to identify and fix the root causes of customer harm across the firm.

**Objectives**

- Design a complaints process that delivers fair and timely outcomes
- Apply root cause analysis to complaints and other customer data
- Identify when wider remediation or redress is required

**Syllabus**

- Complaint handling standards and timelines
- Root cause analysis techniques
- From individual complaints to systemic issues
- Customer remediation and redress programmes

### Module 8: Outcomes Testing and Management Information

Building outcome testing and management information that show whether customers are being well served, and acting on what the data reveals.

**Objectives**

- Design outcome metrics across products, value, understanding and support
- Apply outcome testing and data analysis to identify harm
- Use management information to drive action rather than reassurance

**Syllabus**

- Outcome metrics and indicators of customer harm
- Testing methods: data analysis, mystery shopping and file reviews
- Data quality and segmentation by customer group
- Data exercise: analysing a firm's outcomes data

### Module 9: Conduct Risk Frameworks, Culture and Incentives

Embedding conduct risk into the firm's risk framework and aligning culture, remuneration and performance management with good customer outcomes.

**Objectives**

- Design a conduct risk framework integrated with the firm's risk management
- Assess how culture, incentives and remuneration affect customer outcomes
- Apply the three lines model to conduct risk

**Syllabus**

- Conduct risk appetite, taxonomy and assessment
- Remuneration, sales incentives and performance management
- Culture indicators, speak-up and tone from the top
- The roles of the business, compliance and internal audit

### Module 10: Board Reporting and Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together into board reporting that gives directors a clear view of customer outcomes, followed by a workshop in which participants assess their own firm.

**Objectives**

- Design board reporting that evidences customer outcomes
- Explain how the board should challenge outcome reporting
- Produce a prioritised action plan for the firm's conduct risk management

**Syllabus**

- The annual board outcomes report
- Board challenge and the use of outcome management information
- Seeing the firm's customers the way a supervisor does
- Workshop: the firm's twelve-month customer outcomes action plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines practical tools with case studies, data exercises and group discussion, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of compliance, product or customer-facing work in a retail financial services firm is helpful. No specialist conduct background is required.

## Core Reading

- G20/OECD High-Level Principles on Financial Consumer Protection (2022)
- World Bank, Good Practices for Financial Consumer Protection (2017)
- FSB, Strengthening Governance Frameworks to Mitigate Misconduct Risk: A Toolkit for Firms and Supervisors (2018)
