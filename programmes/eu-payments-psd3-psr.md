## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and practitioners a clear map of EU payments regulation as it stands under PSD2 and the Second E-Money Directive, and of the changes brought by the PSD3 and Payment Services Regulation reform package. Participants leave able to assess authorisation, safeguarding, fraud and access arrangements against both regimes and to plan a structured transition.

**Coverage:** The PSD2 and EMD2 framework and its lessons, the PSD3 and PSR reform package, authorisation and the integration of e-money into payment services, safeguarding and wind-down, strong customer authentication, fraud and liability including impersonation fraud and payee verification, open banking and access to data, access to payment systems and bank accounts, instant payments and e-money tokens, and supervision and enforcement.

## Intended Audience

- Authorisation, supervision and policy staff at national competent authorities covering payment and e-money institutions
- Compliance, risk, legal, fraud and product staff at payment institutions, e-money institutions and banks
- Senior managers and directors of payment firms responsible for regulatory change and transition planning

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the current PSD2 and EMD2 framework and the main elements of the PSD3 and PSR reform package
- Apply authorisation, capital and safeguarding requirements, including the integration of e-money into the payments regime
- Assess strong customer authentication, fraud prevention and liability arrangements, including payee verification
- Evaluate open banking interfaces and the rules on access to data, payment systems and bank accounts
- Explain the interplay with instant payments, MiCA e-money tokens and the AML/CFT framework
- Design a supervisory approach or firm transition plan for the reformed regime

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The EU Payments Framework and the Reform Package

How PSD2 and EMD2 shaped EU payments, what the review found, and how the reform splits the framework between PSD3 for licensing and supervision and a directly applicable Payment Services Regulation for conduct rules.

**Objectives**

- Explain the scope, structure and achievements of PSD2 and EMD2
- Identify the weaknesses the review found in fraud, open banking, enforcement and the level playing field
- Map the PSD3 and PSR reform package and its legislative and transition stages

**Syllabus**

- Payment services, e-money and exclusions: the current perimeter
- Lessons from PSD2: fraud, interface quality, divergent application and arbitrage
- PSD3 for licensing and supervision, the PSR for conduct and operational rules
- EBA technical standards, guidelines and Q&As under both regimes

### Module 2: Authorisation, Capital and the Integration of E-Money

Authorising payment and e-money institutions today, how the reform folds e-money institutions into a single payment institution category, and the reauthorisation, capital and governance implications for existing firms.

**Objectives**

- Assess applications against authorisation, capital and fitness requirements
- Explain how e-money issuance becomes a payment service under the reform
- Plan for reauthorisation and transitional arrangements for existing licence holders

**Syllabus**

- Programme of operations, business plan, governance and own funds methods
- A single payment institution category with e-money services
- Reauthorisation, transitional periods and grandfathering questions
- Agents, distributors, passporting and the narrowing of exclusions

### Module 3: Safeguarding, Wind-Down and Prudential Resilience

How safeguarding protects user funds under PSD2 and EMD2, the changes proposed in the reform, and how supervisors and firms test safeguarding, reconciliation and wind-down readiness.

**Objectives**

- Apply segregation, insurance and guarantee safeguarding methods correctly
- Assess reconciliation, concentration risk and safeguarding audits
- Evaluate wind-down plans and the return of funds on failure

**Syllabus**

- Relevant funds, timing, safeguarding accounts and secure low-risk assets
- Reform changes: concentration risk and safeguarding with central banks
- Daily reconciliation, shortfalls and safeguarding audits
- Case study: safeguarding failures revealed on insolvency

### Module 4: Strong Customer Authentication and Operational Risk

The SCA regime and its exemptions, how the reform clarifies scope, accessibility and responsibilities, and the operational and security risk requirements that now sit alongside DORA.

**Objectives**

- Apply SCA requirements, exemptions and transaction risk analysis
- Explain reform changes to SCA scope, accessibility and outsourcing of authentication
- Assess the interaction between payment security rules and DORA

**Syllabus**

- SCA elements, dynamic linking and the main exemptions
- Merchant-initiated and mail and telephone order transactions
- Accessibility for persons with disabilities and customers without smartphones
- Payment security rules, DORA incident reporting and technical service providers

### Module 5: Fraud, Liability and Impersonation Scams

The shift from unauthorised fraud to authorised push payment scams, the reform's liability rules including impersonation of payment service provider staff, payee verification and fraud data sharing, and how fraud controls are assessed.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish unauthorised transactions, authorised push payment fraud and impersonation fraud
- Apply liability, refund and gross negligence rules under the current and reformed regimes
- Assess transaction monitoring, payee verification and fraud data sharing

**Syllabus**

- Unauthorised transactions, gross negligence and refund timelines
- IBAN and name verification under the Instant Payments Regulation and the reform
- Impersonation fraud refunds and cooperation with electronic communications providers
- Fraud monitoring, data sharing, spending limits and customer education

### Module 6: Open Banking and Access to Data

Account information and payment initiation services under PSD2, the reform's improvements to dedicated interfaces, permission dashboards and prohibited obstacles, and the wider move towards open finance.

**Objectives**

- Explain the rights and obligations of account servicing providers and third-party providers
- Assess dedicated interface performance, fallback arrangements and prohibited obstacles
- Evaluate customer permissions, data use and liability between providers

**Syllabus**

- Account information and payment initiation services: rights and conditions
- Dedicated interfaces, performance requirements and fallback mechanisms
- Permission dashboards, consent and interaction with data protection rules
- From open banking to open finance: emerging proposals

### Module 7: Access to Payment Systems, Accounts and New Payment Rails

Non-bank access to payment systems and bank accounts, instant payments, and the interplay between payments rules and MiCA e-money tokens, including dual-licensing questions for crypto-asset service providers.

**Objectives**

- Explain non-bank access to payment systems and objective, non-discriminatory account access
- Assess the implications of instant payments for liquidity, fraud and sanctions screening
- Analyse the interplay between e-money tokens, crypto-asset services and payment services

**Syllabus**

- Payment and e-money institution access to settlement finality systems
- Bank account access, refusals, reasons and de-risking
- Instant payments: availability, pricing, sanctions screening and payee verification
- E-money tokens under MiCA and the payment services boundary

### Module 8: Supervising Payments and Preparing for Transition

Risk-based supervision and enforcement of payment firms under the current and reformed regimes, including stronger powers, data and EBA convergence work, ending with a transition planning workshop for supervisors or firms.

**Objectives**

- Design risk-based supervision of payment and e-money institutions
- Apply supervisory, enforcement and cross-border cooperation tools, including for agents and passporting firms
- Plan the transition to PSD3 and the PSR

**Syllabus**

- Risk indicators: growth, complaints, fraud data, safeguarding and partner withdrawal
- Home–host cooperation, passporting and EBA convergence work
- Enforcement powers, penalties and the reform's more harmonised sanctions
- Workshop: a PSD3 and PSR transition roadmap

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red-flag exercises and change-mapping work, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisors, payment institutions, e-money institutions or banks.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of payment services, e-money or their supervision is helpful. No legal background or prior knowledge of EU law is required.

## Core Reading

- Directive (EU) 2015/2366 on payment services in the internal market (PSD2)
- Directive 2009/110/EC on the taking up, pursuit and prudential supervision of the business of electronic money institutions (Second E-Money Directive)
- Regulation (EU) 2024/886 amending Regulations (EU) No 260/2012 and (EU) 2021/1230 and Directives 98/26/EC and (EU) 2015/2366 as regards instant credit transfers in euro (Instant Payments Regulation)
- CPMI, Fast Payments: Enhancing the Speed and Availability of Retail Payments (2016)
