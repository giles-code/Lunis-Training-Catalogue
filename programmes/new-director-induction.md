## Aim and Purpose

To give new directors a practical, jargon-free grounding in their legal and regulatory responsibilities and in how a regulated firm is supervised, run and controlled. Participants leave able to see the firm the way a supervisor does, ask the right questions from their first board meeting and protect themselves through sound oversight.

## What It Covers

Directors' duties, the regulatory system and supervisory expectations of boards, fitness and propriety, senior manager accountability, business model and risk profile, capital and liquidity, conduct and consumer outcomes, financial crime, governance and committees, board information and challenge, and personal liability and protection.

## Intended Audience

- Newly appointed non-executive, independent and executive directors of regulated firms
- Directors moving into financial services from other sectors, or into a new type of regulated firm
- Senior managers preparing for board appointment or approval by the authority

## Learning Objectives

- Explain directors' legal duties and the regulatory responsibilities that sit on top of them
- Describe how the regulatory system works and what supervisors expect of boards
- Assess the firm's business model, risk profile and financial resilience
- Oversee conduct, consumer outcomes and financial crime using structured board-level questions
- Evaluate governance, committee arrangements and the quality of board information
- Identify personal liability risks and the protections available, and agree a personal induction plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two- to three-day programme or as a series of induction sessions for individual directors or the whole board. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and the director's experience.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Senior management or board experience is helpful. No prior experience of financial services regulation is required.

## Core Reading

- G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (2023)
- Basel Committee, Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015)
- FSB, Principles for an Effective Risk Appetite Framework (2013)
- FSB, Strengthening Governance Frameworks to Mitigate Misconduct Risk: A Toolkit for Firms and Supervisors (2018)
