## Aim and Purpose

To equip risk managers and senior staff with a practical understanding of how an effective risk management framework is built, operated and evidenced in a regulated firm. Participants leave able to set and monitor risk appetite, assess capital and liquidity needs, run stress tests and report risk in a way that drives board decisions.

## What It Covers

The three lines model, risk appetite frameworks, risk identification and taxonomy, operational risk and risk and control self-assessment, capital and liquidity self-assessment, stress testing, emerging risks including AI, cyber and climate, risk management information and reporting, and the role of the CRO and the board.

## Intended Audience

- Chief risk officers, heads of risk and risk managers in regulated firms
- Risk analysts and operational risk staff developing their framework and reporting skills
- Finance, compliance, internal audit and business managers who own or oversee risks

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how the three lines model allocates responsibility for risk across the firm
- Design a risk appetite framework with measurable limits and triggers linked to strategy
- Apply risk identification, taxonomy and risk and control self-assessment techniques consistently
- Assess the firm's capital and liquidity needs through an internal self-assessment and stress testing
- Evaluate emerging risks, including AI, cyber and climate, and build them into the framework
- Produce risk reporting that supports board challenge and agree an action plan for the risk function

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines practical frameworks with case studies, group exercises and worked examples, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of risk management, finance or control work in a regulated firm is helpful. No quantitative modelling background is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Principles for an Effective Risk Appetite Framework (2013)
- Basel Committee, Principles for Effective Risk Data Aggregation and Risk Reporting (BCBS 239) (2013)
- Basel Committee, Stress Testing Principles (2018)
- Basel Committee, Revisions to the Principles for the Sound Management of Operational Risk (2021)
