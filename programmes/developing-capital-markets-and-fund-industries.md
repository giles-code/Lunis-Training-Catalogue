## Aim and Purpose

To give the officials responsible for capital market development a practical framework for diagnosing market gaps and designing a credible reform programme. Participants leave able to identify the legal, regulatory and infrastructure prerequisites, grow the domestic issuer and investor base, develop pension and fund industries, and sequence reforms realistically.

**Coverage:** The role of capital markets in development, diagnosing market depth and gaps, government bond markets and the yield curve, market infrastructure, equity and corporate bond markets, the institutional investor base, pension and fund industry development, legal and regulatory prerequisites, foreign investor access, and sequencing a reform programme.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance ministries, treasuries and debt management offices responsible for capital market development
- Central bank and public agency staff working on market development, pensions and savings policy
- Staff of investment promotion agencies and financial centre bodies seeking to attract issuers, investors and fund managers

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how capital markets support growth and the conditions for them to develop
- Diagnose the depth, liquidity and gaps of national capital markets
- Assess the market infrastructure and legal and regulatory prerequisites for development
- Evaluate policies to broaden the issuer base and the domestic institutional investor base
- Design measures to develop pension and investment fund industries
- Sequence a realistic capital market reform programme

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Capital Markets and Economic Development

Why capital markets matter for growth, how they complement bank finance, and the economic, institutional and macroeconomic conditions that allow them to develop.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of capital markets in financing growth
- Describe how bank-based and market-based systems differ
- Identify the macroeconomic and institutional preconditions for development

**Syllabus**

- Capital markets, savings mobilisation and long-term finance
- Bank-based and market-based financial systems
- Macroeconomic stability, rule of law and investor confidence
- Case studies of capital market development paths

### Module 2: Diagnosing Market Gaps and Setting Strategy

How to assess the depth, liquidity and structure of national markets, identify binding constraints and build a capital market development strategy with stakeholders.

**Objectives**

- Apply diagnostic indicators of market depth and liquidity
- Identify binding constraints on supply, demand and infrastructure
- Build governance for a capital market development strategy

**Syllabus**

- Indicators: market capitalisation, turnover, issuance and investor mix
- Supply, demand and infrastructure constraints
- International assessments and technical assistance
- Strategy governance: ministries, regulators, exchanges and industry

### Module 3: Government Bond Markets and the Yield Curve

Why a well-functioning government bond market is the foundation for wider capital markets, and how debt management, primary dealers and repo markets support it.

**Objectives**

- Explain the benchmark role of government securities
- Assess issuance strategy, primary dealer and auction arrangements
- Evaluate secondary market liquidity, repo and money markets

**Syllabus**

- Debt management strategy and benchmark issuance
- Auctions, primary dealers and market makers
- Repo, money markets and secondary market liquidity
- Building a reliable yield curve

### Module 4: Market Infrastructure: Trading, Clearing and Settlement

The exchanges, trading platforms, central counterparties, securities depositories and settlement systems a market needs, and the choices in ownership, governance and regulation.

**Objectives**

- Describe the main market infrastructure components and their functions
- Apply the PFMI to assess infrastructure safety and efficiency
- Evaluate ownership, governance and regional integration options

**Syllabus**

- Exchanges and trading venues: models and demutualisation
- Central counterparties, securities depositories and settlement
- The PFMI and infrastructure oversight
- Regional linkages and digital market infrastructure

### Module 5: Broadening the Issuer Base: Equity and Corporate Bonds

How to encourage companies, including mid-sized and state-owned enterprises, to raise finance through equity and corporate bond markets, and the disclosure and governance standards investors expect.

**Objectives**

- Identify the barriers that deter companies from listing or issuing bonds
- Assess listing regimes, growth markets and private placement routes
- Explain the corporate governance and disclosure standards investors require

**Syllabus**

- Listing requirements, costs and growth or SME markets
- Corporate bonds, securitisation and private placements
- State-owned enterprise listings and privatisation
- Corporate governance, disclosure and minority shareholder protection

### Module 6: Building the Investor Base: Pensions and Institutional Investors

How pension reform, insurance and other institutional investors create long-term domestic demand for capital market instruments, and the policy choices in building that investor base.

**Objectives**

- Explain how institutional investors deepen capital markets
- Evaluate pension system design and investment rules
- Assess the risks of directed investment and home bias requirements

**Syllabus**

- Funded pensions, mandatory and voluntary schemes
- Pension governance, investment rules and the prudent person principle
- Insurance, sovereign wealth and other long-term investors
- Retail investors, savings incentives and financial education

### Module 7: Developing an Investment Fund Industry

How a domestic fund industry channels savings into capital markets, the legal and regulatory foundations it needs, and the options for building local management and servicing capacity.

**Objectives**

- Describe the fund vehicles and regulatory framework a domestic industry needs
- Evaluate policies to grow local fund management and servicing
- Assess the role of foreign managers, cross-border funds and private markets

**Syllabus**

- Fund legislation, vehicles and fund manager and fund product rules
- Depositaries, administrators and local servicing capacity
- Money market funds, exchange-traded funds and private capital funds
- Tax neutrality, distribution and cross-border access

### Module 8: Legal Prerequisites, Foreign Access and Sequencing Reform

Brings the programme together by examining the legal and regulatory prerequisites for market development, the opening of markets to foreign investors and the realistic sequencing of reforms.

**Objectives**

- Assess the legal and regulatory prerequisites against international standards
- Evaluate the benefits and risks of foreign investor access and index inclusion
- Produce a sequenced capital market reform plan

**Syllabus**

- Securities law, insolvency, collateral and IOSCO principles
- Capital account opening, foreign investor access and index inclusion
- Sequencing reforms and avoiding common pitfalls
- Workshop: a sequenced capital market development plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with country case studies and diagnostic exercises, ending in a reform planning workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the country's stage of market development.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with financial markets or economic policy is helpful. No technical background in securities or fund regulation is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- CPMI and IOSCO (2012), Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures
- World Bank and IMF (2001), Developing Government Bond Markets: A Handbook
- OECD (2023), G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance
- OECD (2016), OECD Core Principles of Private Pension Regulation
