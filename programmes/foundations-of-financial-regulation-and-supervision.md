## Aim and Purpose

To give new and developing staff a structured induction into the purpose, powers and working methods of a financial regulatory authority, from authorisation through supervision to enforcement. Participants leave able to place their own role in the wider regulatory lifecycle and to approach a first supervisory case with confidence and sound professional standards.

**Coverage:** Why financial services are regulated, the authority's objectives and powers, the regulatory toolkit, risk-based supervision, the lifecycle from authorisation to enforcement, international standards and peer assessment, ethics and independence, and a first supervisory case.

## Intended Audience

- Graduates, new joiners and staff in their first two years at a financial regulatory authority
- Staff moving into supervisory, authorisation, policy or enforcement roles from support or other functions
- Secondees and staff from related public bodies needing a grounding in financial supervision

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the market failures and public interest objectives that justify financial regulation
- Describe the authority's mandate, powers and accountability, and how its functions fit together
- Apply the principles of risk-based supervision to prioritise firms and issues
- Identify the main international standards and how they shape the authority's work
- Recognise the ethical standards, independence and conduct expected of a supervisor
- Work through a first supervisory case from information gathering to proposed response

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Why We Regulate Financial Services

The economic and public interest case for financial regulation, the market failures it addresses and the costs and limits of intervention.

**Objectives**

- Explain the market failures that justify regulating financial services
- Distinguish prudential, conduct, market integrity and financial crime objectives
- Recognise the costs, trade-offs and limits of regulation

**Syllabus**

- Information asymmetry, externalities, moral hazard and systemic risk
- Prudential, conduct and market integrity objectives compared
- Lessons from financial crises and regulatory failures
- The costs of regulation: proportionality and unintended consequences

### Module 2: The Authority: Mandate, Objectives and Powers

How a regulatory authority is constituted, what it is required to achieve, the powers it holds and how it is held to account.

**Objectives**

- Describe the authority's statutory objectives and how they are balanced
- Identify the main powers available to the authority and their limits
- Explain how independence and accountability work together

**Syllabus**

- Legal basis, governance and statutory objectives of the authority
- Rule-making, authorisation, information-gathering and intervention powers
- Independence, transparency and accountability to government and the public
- Working with other domestic authorities and the national FIU

### Module 3: The Regulatory Toolkit

The range of tools the authority uses to achieve its objectives, from rules and guidance through supervision to intervention, and how to choose between them.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish rules, principles, guidance and supervisory expectations
- Match supervisory tools to the risk and the desired outcome
- Explain when and how the authority escalates its response

**Syllabus**

- Rules-based and principles-based regulation
- Guidance, consultation and communicating expectations to the industry
- Supervisory tools: reviews, visits, thematic work and data returns
- Escalation ladder: from feedback letters to formal intervention

### Module 4: Risk-Based Supervision in Practice

How the authority assesses the impact and probability of harm, allocates scarce resources and decides where supervisory attention should go.

**Objectives**

- Explain impact and probability as the basis of risk-based supervision
- Apply a simple risk assessment framework to a firm
- Prioritise supervisory work across a portfolio of firms

**Syllabus**

- Impact, probability and the authority's risk appetite
- Risk assessment frameworks and supervisory ratings
- Proactive, reactive and thematic supervision
- Proportionality: matching intensity to risk

### Module 5: The Regulatory Lifecycle: From Authorisation to Enforcement

How firms and individuals move through the regulatory lifecycle, and how authorisation, supervision, enforcement and resolution teams hand over and work together.

**Objectives**

- Describe each stage of the lifecycle and the function responsible for it
- Explain how information flows between authorisation, supervision and enforcement
- Identify points where risks are most often missed or lost in handover

**Syllabus**

- The gateway: authorisation and fit and proper standards
- Ongoing supervision and the firm's supervisory relationship
- Referral to enforcement and the range of outcomes
- Firm failure, orderly exit and resolution

### Module 6: International Standards and the Global Regulatory Architecture

The international bodies that set standards for financial regulation, how their standards are applied nationally and how the authority is assessed against them.

**Objectives**

- Identify the main international standard-setters and their remits
- Explain how international standards are adopted and assessed
- Recognise the importance of cross-border cooperation and information exchange

**Syllabus**

- The FSB, Basel Committee, IOSCO, IAIS, FATF, CPMI and IOPS
- Core principles and how they translate into national frameworks
- Peer reviews, mutual evaluations and international assessments
- Supervisory colleges, memoranda of understanding and information sharing

### Module 7: Ethics, Independence and the Professional Supervisor

The personal standards expected of a supervisor, the threats to independence and objectivity, and the habits that underpin credible supervisory judgement.

**Objectives**

- Explain the ethical duties and conduct standards that apply to regulatory staff
- Recognise conflicts of interest, capture and other threats to independence
- Apply professional scepticism and sound judgement in everyday work

**Syllabus**

- Confidentiality, conflicts of interest, gifts and hospitality
- Regulatory capture and maintaining independence from industry
- Professional scepticism, challenge and willingness to act
- Accountability for decisions and recording the rationale

### Module 8: Your First Supervisory Case: Integrated Workshop

Brings the programme together in a facilitated case study in which participants assess a firm, form a view of its risks and propose a proportionate supervisory response.

**Objectives**

- Gather and interpret information on a firm's business and risks
- Form an initial risk assessment and identify key concerns
- Propose and justify a proportionate supervisory response

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing: the firm, its business and the information available
- Identifying red flags and framing supervisory questions
- Agreeing a risk view and proposed actions in small groups
- Workshop: presenting the case to a mock supervisory panel

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day induction programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines short briefings with exercises and a running case study, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's structure and mandate.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior regulatory experience is required. A general interest in financial services and the economy is helpful.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2024), Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision
- IAIS (2019, as updated), Insurance Core Principles and Common Framework for the Supervision of Internationally Active Insurance Groups
- FATF (2012, as updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- IMF (2010), The Making of Good Supervision: Learning to Say "No" (Staff Position Note SPN/10/08)
