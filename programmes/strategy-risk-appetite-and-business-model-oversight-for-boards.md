## Aim and Purpose

To give directors the analytical tools supervisors use to judge whether a firm's business model is viable and its strategy sustainable, applied from the boardroom. Participants leave able to challenge management's plans and assumptions, set meaningful risk appetite and evidence effective oversight of strategy to supervisors.

**Coverage:** How supervisors analyse business models, business plan challenge, financial resilience, risk appetite statements and limits, stress testing and reverse stress testing, recovery and wind-down planning, M&A and outsourcing decisions, shareholder influence and board independence, and monitoring delivery.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, non-executive and independent directors and members of board risk and audit committees
- Chief executives, finance and risk directors and senior managers accountable for strategy and planning
- Company secretaries, strategy and planning teams supporting the board

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how supervisors analyse business model viability and strategy sustainability
- Challenge the business plan, its financial projections and key assumptions
- Assess financial resilience using profitability, capital, liquidity and concentration measures
- Set a risk appetite statement with measurable limits that constrains strategy
- Evaluate stress testing, reverse stress testing, recovery and wind-down plans
- Oversee major strategic decisions and the delivery of strategy, independent of undue shareholder influence

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Seeing Your Firm the Way a Supervisor Does

How supervisors analyse corporate strategy and business models, why business model risk sits at the heart of supervisory risk assessment, and what that means for the board.

**Objectives**

- Explain what a business model and a corporate strategy are, and how they differ
- Describe how supervisors conduct business model analysis and score its outcome
- Identify the information supervisors use and how the firm will be judged

**Syllabus**

- Business model components, the value chain and key dependencies
- Viability over one year and sustainability over three to five years
- The supervisory roadmap: preliminary assessment, analysis and scoring
- Where business model risk fits in the supervisory risk framework

### Module 2: Challenging the Business Plan and Its Assumptions

How boards test the business plan as a supervisor would, from the competitive environment to financial projections, and recognise the patterns behind failed strategies.

**Objectives**

- Analyse the firm's macro and competitive environment and its position within it
- Challenge financial projections, growth assumptions and execution risk
- Recognise why strategies fail and identify key vulnerabilities

**Syllabus**

- Environmental and competitive analysis: suppliers, buyers, entrants and substitutes
- Testing projections: growth, margins, costs and sensitivities
- Execution risk, management capacity and change load
- Case study: comparing two contrasting business strategies

### Module 3: Financial Resilience and Key Risk Indicators

The quantitative measures supervisors use to judge financial resilience, and how the board uses them to spot vulnerability before it becomes a problem.

**Objectives**

- Interpret profitability, capital and liquidity indicators
- Assess concentration in income, clients, products, funding and counterparties
- Identify early warning signs of business model stress

**Syllabus**

- Profitability drivers, cost-income and return on capital
- Capital structure, quality and headroom over requirements
- Liquidity, funding and concentration risk
- Early warning indicators for the board dashboard

### Module 4: Risk Appetite Statements and Limits

How to set a risk appetite that genuinely constrains strategy, cascades into limits and triggers, and gives the board a basis for challenge and escalation.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate whether the risk appetite statement is linked to strategy and capacity
- Set quantitative limits and qualitative statements with escalation triggers
- Oversee breaches, cascading and periodic review of appetite

**Syllabus**

- Risk capacity, appetite, tolerance and limits
- Quantitative and qualitative appetite statements
- Cascading appetite into business lines and decisions
- FSB principles for an effective risk appetite framework

### Module 5: Stress Testing and Reverse Stress Testing

How stress testing reveals how the business model breaks, how to challenge the severity and relevance of scenarios, and how reverse stress testing informs strategy.

**Objectives**

- Challenge the design, severity and plausibility of stress scenarios
- Interpret results and their implications for capital, liquidity and strategy
- Use reverse stress testing to identify what would make the business model fail

**Syllabus**

- Scenario design: idiosyncratic, market-wide and combined stresses
- Management actions and their credibility under stress
- Reverse stress testing: working back from failure
- Linking results to risk appetite and the business plan

### Module 6: Recovery and Wind-Down Planning

Why supervisors expect firms to plan for severe stress and orderly exit, and how the board tests that recovery options and wind-down plans would work in practice.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of recovery plans and solvent wind-down planning
- Assess recovery indicators, options and their feasibility
- Evaluate whether the firm could wind down in an orderly way

**Syllabus**

- Recovery indicators, triggers and escalation to the board
- Recovery options: capital, liquidity, disposals and cost actions
- Wind-down costs, timelines and client asset return
- Testing plans through fire drills and playbooks

### Module 7: Major Decisions: M&A, Outsourcing and Shareholder Influence

How the board oversees acquisitions, disposals and material outsourcing, and maintains its independence where shareholders or group entities seek to shape strategy.

**Objectives**

- Challenge the strategic rationale, due diligence and integration risk of M&A
- Assess material outsourcing and intragroup dependency decisions
- Maintain board independence and manage conflicts with shareholder interests

**Syllabus**

- M&A: rationale, valuation, due diligence and integration
- Outsourcing and third-party dependency: oversight and exit
- Group strategy, intragroup arrangements and local board duties
- Shareholder influence, conflicts and independent judgement

### Module 8: Monitoring Delivery and Board Action Plan

Brings the programme together by examining how boards monitor strategic delivery, respond to unexpected events and prepare for supervisory scrutiny of the business model.

**Objectives**

- Specify the management information needed to monitor strategic delivery
- Evaluate management responses when strategy goes off track
- Produce a board action plan for strategy and business model oversight

**Syllabus**

- Tracking delivery: milestones, KPIs and variance against plan
- Responding to unexpected events and changing course
- Preparing for supervisory business model review
- Workshop: assess and score the firm's business model as a supervisor would

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies, analytical exercises and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board or senior management experience in financial services is helpful. No specialist background in finance or risk modelling is required.
