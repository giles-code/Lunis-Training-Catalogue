## Aim and Purpose

To give fund managers and their service providers a practical understanding of the rules and supervisory expectations that govern funds and the people who run them. Participants leave able to strengthen governance, delegation, valuation and liquidity arrangements, and to see their fund range the way a supervisor does.

## What It Covers

Fund manager and fund product rules, delegation and substance, the depositary and trustee interface, valuation, liquidity risk management, costs, value and disclosure, conflicts of interest, private markets and retail access, tokenised funds, and engaging with the supervisor.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance, risk and legal staff at fund managers and investment managers
- Fund administrators, depositaries, trustees, transfer agents and other service providers
- Fund board directors, conducting officers and senior managers overseeing fund ranges

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how fund manager and fund product rules apply across the fund lifecycle
- Assess delegation, substance and the oversight of service providers
- Design sound valuation and liquidity risk management frameworks
- Evaluate costs, value, disclosure and conflicts in investors' interests
- Identify the risks of private markets, retail access and tokenised funds
- Engage effectively with the supervisor and agree a compliance action plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, group exercises and practical checklists, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's fund range and role.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of the investment funds sector is helpful. No prior knowledge of specific fund rules is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Recommendations for Liquidity Risk Management for Collective Investment Schemes (2018)
- IOSCO, Principles for the Valuation of Collective Investment Schemes (2013)
- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
- IOSCO, Examination of Governance for Collective Investment Schemes (2006–2007)
