## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and practitioners a clear map of EU payments regulation as it stands under PSD2 and the Second E-Money Directive, and of the changes brought by the PSD3 and Payment Services Regulation reform package. Participants leave able to assess authorisation, safeguarding, fraud and access arrangements against both regimes and to plan a structured transition.

## What It Covers

The PSD2 and EMD2 framework and its lessons, the PSD3 and PSR reform package, authorisation and the integration of e-money into payment services, safeguarding and wind-down, strong customer authentication, fraud and liability including impersonation fraud and payee verification, open banking and access to data, access to payment systems and bank accounts, instant payments and e-money tokens, and supervision and enforcement.

## Intended Audience

- Authorisation, supervision and policy staff at national competent authorities covering payment and e-money institutions
- Compliance, risk, legal, fraud and product staff at payment institutions, e-money institutions and banks
- Senior managers and directors of payment firms responsible for regulatory change and transition planning

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the current PSD2 and EMD2 framework and the main elements of the PSD3 and PSR reform package
- Apply authorisation, capital and safeguarding requirements, including the integration of e-money into the payments regime
- Assess strong customer authentication, fraud prevention and liability arrangements, including payee verification
- Evaluate open banking interfaces and the rules on access to data, payment systems and bank accounts
- Explain the interplay with instant payments, MiCA e-money tokens and the AML/CFT framework
- Design a supervisory approach or firm transition plan for the reformed regime

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red-flag exercises and change-mapping work, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisors, payment institutions, e-money institutions or banks.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of payment services, e-money or their supervision is helpful. No legal background or prior knowledge of EU law is required.

## Core Reading

- Directive (EU) 2015/2366 on payment services in the internal market (PSD2)
- Directive 2009/110/EC on the taking up, pursuit and prudential supervision of the business of electronic money institutions (Second E-Money Directive)
- Regulation (EU) 2024/886 amending Regulations (EU) No 260/2012 and (EU) 2021/1230 and Directives 98/26/EC and (EU) 2015/2366 as regards instant credit transfers in euro (Instant Payments Regulation)
- CPMI, Fast Payments: Enhancing the Speed and Availability of Retail Payments (2016)
