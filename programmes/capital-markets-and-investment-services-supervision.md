## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical understanding of how investment firms serve clients and how securities markets trade, settle and disclose, together with the tools to assess whether conduct, controls and market integrity arrangements work in practice. Participants leave able to test firms' advice, execution and client asset arrangements, use surveillance and transaction data to identify abuse, and challenge venues and issuers with confidence.

## What It Covers

The investment services perimeter, investment firm authorisation and prudential oversight, suitability and product governance, best execution and order handling, client assets, trading venues and market infrastructure, algorithmic trading, market abuse surveillance and transaction reporting, issuer disclosure, and emerging risks in retail investing and tokenised markets.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and inspectors responsible for brokers, investment advisers, portfolio managers and other investment firms
- Market surveillance, market infrastructure and issuer disclosure staff
- Policy specialists, team leaders and heads of department responsible for securities markets

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the investment services perimeter and how capital markets are structured and regulated
- Assess investment firms' governance, prudential resources and conduct of business arrangements
- Evaluate best execution, order handling and client asset protection using firm evidence
- Challenge trading venues, infrastructures and algorithmic traders on their risk controls
- Apply surveillance and transaction data to detect and respond to market abuse
- Design a risk-based supervisory approach to investment firms and markets, including emerging risks

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, data and file review exercises and supervisory question sets, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's markets and firm population.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervising investment firms or securities markets is helpful. No trading or quantitative background is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
- CPMI-IOSCO, Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures (2012)
- IOSCO, Regulatory Issues Raised by Changes in Market Structure (2013)
- IOSCO, Compliance Function at Market Intermediaries (2006)
