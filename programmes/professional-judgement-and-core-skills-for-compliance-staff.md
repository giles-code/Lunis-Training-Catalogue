## Aim and Purpose

To give compliance, risk and financial crime staff the practical judgement and professional skills that technical knowledge of the rules does not provide. Participants leave able to weigh evidence and reach defensible conclusions, advise and challenge the business, handle difficult conversations, escalate at the right time and write in a way that boards and supervisors trust.

**Coverage:** Professional judgement in the control functions, evidence and professional scepticism, cognitive bias, proportionality and prioritisation, advising the business, investigative questioning and fact-finding, difficult conversations, writing for the board and the regulator, escalation and breach decisions, and ethics and independence.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance, risk and financial crime analysts, officers and advisers in their first years in the role
- Experienced control function staff moving into advisory, investigation or reporting roles who want to sharpen their judgement and writing
- Business-side risk and control staff, such as first-line risk teams, who advise colleagues and escalate concerns

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain why control function work depends on judgement and what makes a judgement defensible
- Assess evidence with professional scepticism and recognise the biases that distort conclusions
- Advise the business clearly and proportionately, and challenge it constructively
- Apply investigative questioning and fact-finding techniques to breaches, complaints and internal reviews
- Write clear, accurate and defensible papers, reports and correspondence for the board and the regulator
- Decide when and how to escalate, and uphold ethical standards and independence under pressure

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Professional Judgement in the Control Functions

Why compliance and risk work depends on judgement rather than rule-checking alone, where uncertainty comes from, and what it means to be personally accountable for a call.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of judgement in risk-based compliance and risk work
- Identify the sources of uncertainty in everyday control function decisions
- Describe what a supervisor or reviewer will expect to see behind a judgement

**Syllabus**

- From checklist compliance to judgement-led advice and oversight
- Ambiguous rules, incomplete facts and commercial pressure
- The cost of over-cautious and under-cautious calls
- Hindsight: how judgements are reviewed after the event

### Module 2: Evidence, Professional Scepticism and Cognitive Bias

How to gather and weigh evidence, test the business's assertions rather than accept them, and recognise the biases that lead experienced professionals to the wrong conclusion.

**Objectives**

- Assess the reliability, relevance and sufficiency of evidence
- Apply professional scepticism to management information and colleagues' assurances
- Use simple techniques to counter individual and group bias

**Syllabus**

- Facts, evidence, opinion and assertion
- Corroboration, triangulation and spotting inconsistencies
- Confirmation bias, anchoring, optimism and groupthink
- Pre-mortems, second opinions and recording the reasoning

### Module 3: Proportionality and Prioritisation

How to match effort and response to the size of the risk, prioritise competing demands across a caseload, and avoid both gold-plating and under-reaction.

**Objectives**

- Apply proportionality to the nature, scale and impact of an issue
- Prioritise work by risk, urgency and impact on customers and markets
- Choose a response that fits the risk, from advice to escalation

**Syllabus**

- Proportionality in principle and in practice
- Impact and probability: prioritising a caseload
- Gold-plating, risk aversion and the cost to the business
- Managing deadlines, workload and knowing when to ask for help

### Module 4: Advising the Business

How to give advice on products, marketing, deals and change that is clear, practical and proportionate, so the business understands the risk and what it can do about it.

**Objectives**

- Establish the facts and the real question before giving advice
- Give advice that sets out risks, options and conditions rather than a bare yes or no
- Record advice so that it can be relied upon and evidenced later

**Syllabus**

- Understanding the business proposal and its commercial purpose
- Structuring advice: issue, rule, risk, options and recommendation
- Oral advice, emails and advice logs
- Exercise: advising on a new product or financial promotion

### Module 5: Investigative Questioning and Fact-Finding

How to plan and conduct fact-finding interviews and meetings for breaches, complaints, whistleblowing and internal reviews, obtaining reliable accounts and recording them accurately.

**Objectives**

- Plan a fact-finding interview with clear objectives and topics
- Use open, probing and closed questions and active listening to obtain reliable accounts
- Record interviews accurately and fairly for later reliance

**Syllabus**

- When an internal review needs interviews, and when to involve legal or HR
- Questioning models: open, probing, closed and summarising
- Handling evasive, defensive or senior interviewees
- Role-play: a fact-finding interview after a suspected breach

### Module 6: Managing Difficult Conversations

How to deliver unwelcome findings and advice, say no to senior colleagues, handle defensive or aggressive reactions and preserve the working relationship.

**Objectives**

- Explain why colleagues react defensively to control function findings
- Apply a structure for delivering difficult messages clearly and fairly
- Hold a position under pressure while keeping the relationship workable

**Syllabus**

- Types of difficult conversation in compliance and risk work
- Preparing and structuring the conversation
- Handling pushback, emotion and appeals to seniority
- Role-play: telling a business head their launch cannot proceed as planned

### Module 7: Writing for the Board and the Regulator

How to write papers, reports, file notes and regulatory correspondence that lead with the message, are precise and accurate, and stand up to scrutiny by directors, auditors and supervisors.

**Objectives**

- Structure documents so the key message and decision come first
- Write in plain, precise and defensible language
- Calibrate tone and accuracy for board papers and correspondence with the authority

**Syllabus**

- Top-down structure: conclusion first, then supporting evidence
- Board and committee papers: what directors need to decide or note
- Regulatory correspondence: accuracy, completeness and candour
- Exercise: rewriting a weak paper and a weak letter to the regulator

### Module 8: Escalation, Breaches and Notification Decisions

How to recognise when an issue must be escalated, write an escalation note that gets a decision, and reason through whether a breach is significant enough to notify the authority.

**Objectives**

- Identify escalation triggers and the right route in the firm
- Assess the significance of a breach and the case for notification
- Write an escalation note that sets out facts, risk and the decision required

**Syllabus**

- Escalation triggers, routes and timing
- Assessing a breach: cause, impact, customers affected and recurrence
- Notification judgements and the cost of late or partial disclosure
- Exercise: escalating an emerging issue to senior management

### Module 9: Ethics, Independence and Integrity

The ethical standards expected of control function staff, how independence is eroded in practice, and how to handle grey-area situations, conflicts and pressure to look the other way.

**Objectives**

- Recognise conflicts of interest and threats to independence in the control functions
- Apply an ethical decision-making framework to grey-area situations
- Know how to speak up and seek support when under pressure

**Syllabus**

- Independence, familiarity and capture by the business
- Conflicts, gifts, hospitality and confidentiality in the second line
- Speaking up and protection for those who raise concerns
- Ethical dilemmas: case discussions from compliance practice

### Module 10: Forming and Defending Judgements: Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together in a case study in which participants investigate an ambiguous issue, form and document a judgement, write it up for a committee and defend it under challenge.

**Objectives**

- Combine evidence, questioning, judgement and writing skills in a realistic scenario
- Document reasoning so that it is defensible under later scrutiny
- Defend a judgement before a mock committee and set personal development priorities

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing, analysis and a fact-finding interview
- Recording the rationale: options considered and reasons for the decision
- Drafting a short committee paper and escalation note
- Workshop: presenting, defending and reflecting on the judgement

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, drafting exercises, role-play and peer feedback, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and control function structure.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of working in or alongside a compliance, risk or financial crime function is helpful. No legal qualification is required.

## Core Reading

- Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011)
- Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony and Cass R. Sunstein, Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment (2021)
- Rebecca Milne and Ray Bull, Investigative Interviewing: Psychology and Practice (1999)
- IOSCO, Compliance Function at Market Intermediaries (2006)
