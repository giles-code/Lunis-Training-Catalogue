## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors the frameworks and practical skills to oversee cross-border groups, branches and third-country firms without gaps or duplication. Participants leave able to assess group structures and intra-group risk, use cooperation arrangements and colleges effectively, and protect their own jurisdiction's interests in normal times and in a crisis.

**Coverage:** Home and host responsibilities, consolidated and group-wide supervision, intra-group exposures and complex structures, MoUs and information sharing, supervisory colleges, branches, subsidiaries and third-country firms, cross-border crisis cooperation, and overcoming obstacles to cooperation.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors responsible for cross-border groups, branches, subsidiaries or third-country firms
- International affairs, legal and cooperation staff managing MoUs and information requests
- Senior supervisors and managers who represent the authority in colleges and crisis management groups

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain home and host responsibilities under international standards such as the Basel Core Principles
- Assess group structures, intra-group exposures and contagion risk on a consolidated basis
- Evaluate MoUs and information-sharing arrangements and use them effectively
- Contribute to joint risk assessments and coordinated decisions in supervisory colleges
- Assess the risks of branches, subsidiaries and third-country firms in the authority's market
- Apply cooperation principles to cross-border crisis management and resolution

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Foundations of Home and Host Supervision

Why cross-border groups need cooperating supervisors, how responsibilities are allocated between home and host, and the risks of supervisory gaps and duplication.

**Objectives**

- Explain the home-host principle in the Basel Core Principles and other sector standards
- Distinguish home and host supervisor roles and responsibilities
- Identify the risks of gaps, duplication and conflicting demands

**Syllabus**

- Basel Core Principles, IAIS and IOSCO standards on cross-border cooperation
- Allocation of home and host responsibilities across sectors
- Asymmetry: large home, small host and systemic branches
- Supervisory gaps, regulatory arbitrage and duplication

### Module 2: Consolidated and Group-Wide Supervision

Why supervisors look at groups rather than single legal entities, how consolidated supervision works, and how to assess group governance and risk management.

**Objectives**

- Explain consolidated versus solo supervision
- Assess group governance, risk management and risk appetite
- Identify perimeter issues in group supervision

**Syllabus**

- Common cross-border group structures across banking, insurance and investment
- Scope of consolidation, holding companies and unregulated entities
- Group-level versus entity-level governance and risk appetite
- Financial conglomerates and cross-sector groups

### Module 3: Intra-Group Exposures, Contagion and Complex Structures

How funding, guarantees and shared services link entities within a group, how stress spreads between them, and the supervisory risks of opaque or complex structures.

**Objectives**

- Assess types of intra-group exposure and dependency
- Identify contagion channels within a cross-border group
- Evaluate the risks of opaque or unnecessarily complex structures

**Syllabus**

- Intra-group funding, guarantees, liquidity and capital flows
- Shared services, outsourcing and operational dependencies
- Ring-fencing, structural subordination and trapped resources
- Case study: assessing a complex cross-border group

### Module 4: MoUs and Information Sharing

How information-sharing gateways and memoranda of understanding work, what a good MoU contains, and how to make and respond to cooperation requests effectively.

**Objectives**

- Explain information-sharing gateways and confidentiality constraints
- Assess whether an MoU is fit for purpose
- Prepare and respond to cross-border information requests

**Syllabus**

- Bilateral and multilateral MoUs, including the IOSCO MMoU
- Confidentiality, data protection and onward-sharing limits
- Making a clear, proportionate cooperation request
- Exercise: reviewing a sample MoU for strengths and gaps

### Module 5: Supervisory Colleges: Joint Risk Assessment and Decisions

How supervisory colleges are governed, how members build a shared risk picture across jurisdictions, and how to reach coordinated decisions and manage disagreement.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose, governance and outputs of supervisory colleges
- Contribute effectively to a joint risk assessment
- Manage disagreement and unequal engagement among college members

**Syllabus**

- College governance: chair, secretariat, membership and workplans
- Building and reconciling a joint risk assessment
- Coordinated decisions, constructive challenge and escalation
- Case study: a college facing a coordination challenge

### Module 6: Branches, Subsidiaries and Third-Country Firms

The supervisory trade-offs between branches and subsidiaries, how cross-border access and passporting regimes allocate responsibility, and how hosts assess third-country firms and equivalence reliance.

**Objectives**

- Compare the supervisory risks of branches and subsidiaries
- Assess third-country firms and reliance on home supervision
- Identify when a host should require subsidiarisation or additional safeguards

**Syllabus**

- Branch versus subsidiary: capital, liquidity, governance and resolvability
- Passporting, cross-border services and regional access regimes
- Equivalence, deference and reliance on home supervisors
- Host safeguards for systemically important branches

### Module 7: Cross-Border Crisis Cooperation and Resolution

How home and host authorities cooperate when a cross-border group comes under stress, from early warning and information sharing to crisis management groups and resolution.

**Objectives**

- Explain the FSB Key Attributes and cross-border resolution cooperation
- Assess recovery and resolution planning for cross-border groups
- Identify the conflicts of interest that arise between authorities in a crisis

**Syllabus**

- Crisis management groups and institution-specific cooperation agreements
- Single and multiple point of entry resolution strategies
- Ring-fencing incentives and host protection in stress
- Coordinated communication with firms, markets and the public

### Module 8: Making Cross-Border Cooperation Work

Brings the programme together by examining the practical obstacles to cooperation, how to escalate when cooperation breaks down, and a cross-border stress simulation.

**Objectives**

- Identify legal, mandate and resource obstacles to cooperation
- Apply escalation options when cooperation breaks down
- Produce a coordinated supervisory response to a cross-border scenario

**Syllabus**

- Legal barriers, differing mandates and resource asymmetry
- Building trust and working relationships with counterpart authorities
- Escalation routes, including standard-setter mechanisms
- Workshop: simulation of a cross-border group under stress

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing on international standards with case studies and practical exercises, ending in a cross-border simulation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of prudential or conduct supervision is helpful. No prior involvement in colleges or international cooperation is required.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee, Principles for Effective Supervisory Colleges (2014)
- IOSCO, Multilateral Memorandum of Understanding (MMoU) and Enhanced MMoU (2016)
- FSB, Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions (2014)
- IAIS, Insurance Core Principles and Common Framework for the Supervision of Internationally Active Insurance Groups (ComFrame)
