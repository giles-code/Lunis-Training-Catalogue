## Aim and Purpose

To equip firms that distribute through platforms, provide licensed services to partners or share data through APIs to grow these models without losing control of regulatory responsibility. Participants leave able to design a partnership model, oversee partners and data flows, manage dependency on large platforms and evidence to supervisors that the firm remains accountable for its customers.

**Coverage:** Platform economics and embedded finance models, choosing a partnership model, the regulatory perimeter and allocation of responsibilities, partner due diligence and oversight, APIs and open finance data sharing, financial crime and client funds across the chain, consumer outcomes in embedded journeys, operational dependency and exit, and governing and evidencing the partnership programme.

## Intended Audience

- Founders, chief executives, product and partnerships leads at payment, e-money, investment and fintech firms
- Compliance, MLRO, risk, outsourcing and third-party risk staff overseeing partners and distributors
- Technology, data and API leads responsible for open finance connections and platform integrations

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how platform economics and embedded distribution are reshaping financial services
- Design a partnership model that allocates regulatory responsibility clearly along the chain
- Oversee partners, agents and programme managers through due diligence, contracts and monitoring
- Manage consent, security, liability and data protection in API-based data sharing
- Deliver and evidence good customer outcomes, financial crime controls and client fund protection in embedded journeys
- Manage dependency on large platforms and evidence accountability to supervisors

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Platform Economics and Embedded Finance Models

How large technology platforms and non-financial brands enter financial services, the data, network and activity loop behind their advantage, and what this means for a licensed firm's strategy.

**Objectives**

- Explain the economics of platforms and the data-network-activities loop
- Distinguish embedded payments, lending, investment and insurance distribution models
- Assess where embedded and platform distribution creates opportunity and risk for the firm

**Syllabus**

- Network effects, ecosystems and cross-subsidisation
- Big tech entry routes: own licence, partnership or acquisition
- Embedded payments, credit, investment and wealth journeys
- Strategic questions: grow, defend, partner or supply

### Module 2: Choosing and Designing a Partnership Model

The main ways licensed firms work with platforms and fintechs, from white-label and agent arrangements to licence-as-a-service and API distribution, and how to select a model that the firm can genuinely control.

**Objectives**

- Compare white-label, agent, distributor, programme manager and licence-as-a-service models
- Evaluate the commercial terms, control rights and risks of each model
- Design a partnership model the firm can oversee with its current resources

**Syllabus**

- White-label, agents, distributors and introducers
- Licence-as-a-service, e-money-as-a-service and middleware providers
- Distribution through big tech platforms, marketplaces and super-apps
- Pricing, revenue share and incentives that drive behaviour

### Module 3: The Regulatory Perimeter and Allocation of Responsibilities

Where the regulatory perimeter falls when regulated and unregulated activities are combined in one journey, and how to allocate and document responsibilities so that the licence holder stays accountable.

**Objectives**

- Identify which activities in an embedded journey are regulated and who performs them
- Apply activity-based and entity-based regulation to mixed-activity partnerships
- Document responsibilities so that accountability cannot fall between partners

**Syllabus**

- Same activity, same risk, same regulation in practice
- Perimeter traps: unlicensed activity, financial promotions and agency
- Responsibility matrices and partnership mapping
- Senior manager accountability for partner-delivered services

### Module 4: Partner Due Diligence, Onboarding and Ongoing Oversight

How to select, onboard and monitor partners and programme managers proportionately, with the contractual rights, management information and escalation needed to act when a partner goes wrong.

**Objectives**

- Design risk-based due diligence and onboarding for partners
- Specify contractual rights to information, audit, step-in and termination
- Monitor partners through management information, testing and periodic review

**Syllabus**

- Partner risk assessment and onboarding criteria
- Contracts: service levels, audit access, step-in and exit
- Monitoring, assurance testing and partner management information
- Case studies: fast growth, weak oversight and partnership failure

### Module 5: APIs, Open Finance and Data Sharing

How open banking and open finance frameworks enable customer-permissioned data sharing, and how a firm designs consent, security, liability and data protection when it provides or consumes data through APIs.

**Objectives**

- Explain the design of open banking and open finance frameworks
- Implement consent, authentication, security and liability arrangements for data sharing
- Assess the strategic value and risks of data access for the firm

**Syllabus**

- Open banking to open finance: scope, data categories and participants
- APIs, standards, consent dashboards and strong authentication
- Data protection, fraud and cyber risk in data sharing
- Data as a strategic asset: reciprocity and platform data advantages

### Module 6: Financial Crime and Client Funds Across the Chain

How AML/CFT, fraud and sanctions controls and the protection of client funds work when onboarding, transaction monitoring and reconciliation are shared between the licence holder and its partners.

**Objectives**

- Allocate customer due diligence, monitoring and reporting duties between partners
- Design fraud and scam controls for embedded and API-driven journeys
- Ensure client funds are safeguarded and reconciled across the partnership

**Syllabus**

- Reliance, outsourcing of customer due diligence and digital identity
- Transaction monitoring, sanctions screening and suspicious activity reporting
- Fraud, mule accounts and authorised push payment scams on platforms
- Safeguarding, client money and reconciliation with partner ledgers

### Module 7: Consumer Outcomes in Embedded Journeys

How platform design, personalisation and frictionless journeys shape customer decisions, and how the firm tests and evidences fair outcomes, suitability and complaints handling when a partner owns the interface.

**Objectives**

- Identify harms from choice architecture, dark patterns and frictionless credit
- Apply disclosure, product governance and suitability in partner-owned journeys
- Design outcome testing, complaints handling and redress across the chain

**Syllabus**

- Choice architecture, defaults and dark patterns
- Embedded credit, buy now, pay later and investment nudges
- Personalised pricing, profiling and fair treatment
- Complaints, redress and outcome monitoring across partners

### Module 8: Operational Dependency, Concentration and Exit

How reliance on platforms for distribution, cloud, data and customer interfaces creates operational dependency and bargaining weakness, and how the firm builds resilience and credible exit options.

**Objectives**

- Assess the firm's dependency on platform partners and technology providers
- Apply operational resilience and third-party risk management to partnerships
- Design credible exit and contingency plans for critical partnerships

**Syllabus**

- Distribution dependency and loss of the customer interface
- Cloud, data and technology dependencies on platform groups
- Mapping partners to important business services and impact tolerances
- Exit planning, substitutability and customer migration

### Module 9: Governing and Evidencing the Partnership Programme

Brings the programme together into a governance model for the firm's partnerships, from board risk appetite and management information to the evidence a supervisor will expect, ending with a partnership review workshop.

**Objectives**

- Set risk appetite and governance for growth through partnerships
- Specify management information and assurance across the partner portfolio
- Evidence to supervisors that the firm remains in control of its customers

**Syllabus**

- Board risk appetite, partner limits and approval of new partnerships
- Portfolio management information and early warning indicators
- What supervisors look for in thematic reviews of partnerships
- Workshop: reviewing an embedded finance partnership through a supervisor's eyes

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with case studies and practical exercises, building to a partnership review workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of payments, e-money, investment distribution or fintech partnerships is helpful. No technical background in APIs is required.
