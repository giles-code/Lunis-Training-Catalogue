## Aim and Purpose

To give compliance and risk professionals in investment services a practical command of the conduct, client protection and market integrity obligations that shape their firm's business. Participants leave able to design and test controls, run a risk-based monitoring programme and see their firm the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** Authorisation scope and permissions, client categorisation, suitability and appropriateness, best execution, conflicts and inducements, product governance and costs and charges disclosure, client assets and money safeguarding, market abuse and surveillance, transaction reporting and record-keeping, and the compliance monitoring programme and supervisory engagement.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance officers, compliance analysts and risk managers at investment firms, wealth managers and brokers
- Heads of business, operations and client asset teams responsible for first-line controls
- Internal auditors and senior managers who oversee investment services compliance

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Map the firm's activities to its permissions and identify perimeter risks
- Apply client categorisation, suitability and appropriateness requirements across the client journey
- Design controls for best execution, conflicts, inducements, product governance and disclosure
- Implement robust client asset and client money safeguarding arrangements
- Operate effective market abuse surveillance, transaction reporting and record-keeping
- Build a risk-based monitoring programme and evidence compliance to supervisors

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Authorisation Scope, Permissions and the Perimeter

How investment services and financial instruments are defined, how the firm's permissions shape what it may do, and how to manage perimeter and scope-creep risks.

**Objectives**

- Map the firm's services and instruments to its permissions
- Identify perimeter risks from new products, channels and jurisdictions
- Maintain an accurate regulatory business plan and notifications

**Syllabus**

- Investment services: execution, dealing, advice, portfolio management and custody
- Financial instruments, derivatives and perimeter judgements
- Scope creep, variations of permission and cross-border services
- Change in control, notifications and the regulatory business plan

### Module 2: Client Categorisation and Onboarding

How firms categorise clients, the protections each category brings, and how onboarding controls ensure categorisation and client information are accurate and kept up to date.

**Objectives**

- Apply retail, professional and eligible counterparty criteria correctly
- Assess opt-up requests and the evidence needed to support them
- Design onboarding controls for client information and terms of business

**Syllabus**

- Client categories and the protections attached to each
- Elective professional clients: quantitative and qualitative tests
- Know-your-client information, client agreements and periodic refresh
- Common categorisation failures found by supervisors

### Module 3: Suitability and Appropriateness

How to gather client information, assess suitability for advice and portfolio management, apply appropriateness tests for non-advised business, and evidence good outcomes.

**Objectives**

- Apply suitability requirements to advice and discretionary management
- Apply appropriateness tests and warnings for non-advised services
- Test advice quality through file reviews and outcome data

**Syllabus**

- Client objectives, risk tolerance, capacity for loss and knowledge
- Suitability reports, ongoing suitability and portfolio drift
- Appropriateness tests, warnings and execution-only business
- Exercise: reviewing a sample of advice files

### Module 4: Best Execution and Order Handling

How firms take all sufficient steps to obtain the best result for clients, handle and allocate orders fairly, and monitor execution quality.

**Objectives**

- Design an execution policy and venue selection process
- Implement fair order handling, aggregation and allocation
- Monitor execution quality and act on the results

**Syllabus**

- Execution factors, execution policy and client disclosure
- Order handling, aggregation and fair allocation
- Execution monitoring, transaction cost analysis and best execution committees
- Payment for order flow and conflicts in order routing

### Module 5: Conflicts of Interest and Inducements

How conflicts arise in investment services, how to identify, prevent and manage them, and how the rules on inducements and research payments apply.

**Objectives**

- Identify conflicts across the firm's business lines and group
- Design a conflicts policy, register and escalation process
- Apply inducement rules to fees, benefits and research

**Syllabus**

- Sources of conflict: proprietary trading, in-house products and remuneration
- Conflicts policies, registers, information barriers and disclosure as a last resort
- Inducements, minor non-monetary benefits and quality enhancement
- Research payments, gifts and hospitality

### Module 6: Product Governance and Costs and Charges Disclosure

Manufacturer and distributor product governance obligations, target market controls, and how to deliver clear, complete costs and charges disclosure before and after the sale.

**Objectives**

- Implement manufacturer and distributor product governance arrangements
- Define and monitor the target market and distribution strategy
- Produce accurate ex-ante and ex-post costs and charges disclosure

**Syllabus**

- Product approval, target market and negative target market
- Distribution strategy, information exchange and product reviews
- Ex-ante and ex-post costs and charges, including third-party product costs
- Illustrations of the cumulative effect of costs on returns

### Module 7: Client Assets and Client Money Safeguarding

How firms protect client assets and money through segregation, reconciliations and governance, and what happens when things go wrong or the firm fails.

**Objectives**

- Design segregation and trust arrangements for client assets and money
- Operate reconciliations and resolve discrepancies promptly
- Govern client asset arrangements and prepare for insolvency

**Syllabus**

- Segregation, trust status, acknowledgement letters and sub-custody
- Internal and external reconciliations, shortfalls and breach reporting
- Title transfer collateral, securities lending and use of client assets
- Client asset oversight, audit reporting and resolution packs

### Module 8: Market Abuse and Surveillance

The forms of market abuse, how inside information is controlled, and how to build surveillance that detects and reports suspicious orders and transactions.

**Objectives**

- Recognise insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and manipulation typologies
- Control inside information through insider lists and personal account dealing
- Design and calibrate trade and communications surveillance

**Syllabus**

- Inside information, insider lists, wall-crossing and personal account dealing
- Manipulation typologies: spoofing, layering, ramping and cross-market abuse
- Surveillance calibration, alert handling and communications monitoring
- Suspicious order and transaction reports: quality and timeliness

### Module 9: Transaction Reporting and Record-Keeping

How to deliver complete and accurate transaction reports, keep the records the authority expects, and use data quality testing to find and fix errors.

**Objectives**

- Explain transaction reporting obligations and common data fields
- Implement reconciliation and data quality controls over reporting
- Maintain records of orders, transactions and communications

**Syllabus**

- Reportable transactions, instrument and client identifiers
- Reporting arrangements, reconciliations and data quality testing
- Error identification, back-reporting and notification
- Record-keeping: orders, communications, call recording and retention

### Module 10: Compliance Monitoring and Supervisory Engagement

Brings the programme together into a risk-based compliance monitoring programme, effective reporting to senior management and the board, and confident engagement with the supervisor.

**Objectives**

- Build a risk-based compliance monitoring plan
- Report findings and remediation to senior management and the board
- Prepare for supervisory visits, thematic reviews and information requests

**Syllabus**

- Compliance risk assessment and monitoring plan design
- Testing methods, findings, remediation tracking and board reporting
- Supervisory visits, thematic reviews and breach notifications
- Workshop: seeing the firm the way a supervisor does

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, file reviews and practical templates, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of compliance, risk or operations in an investment firm is helpful. No prior knowledge of specific investment services rules is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
- FSB, Strengthening Governance Frameworks to Mitigate Misconduct Risk: A Toolkit for Firms and Supervisors (2018)
