## Aim and Purpose

To give the officials who lead and deliver a country's AML/CFT regime a practical understanding of the FATF standards, the assessment methodology and what effectiveness really means. Participants leave able to lead a national risk assessment, coordinate a whole-of-government response, prepare a credible mutual evaluation submission and design an action plan to address deficiencies or exit increased monitoring.

**Coverage:** The FATF standards and assessment methodology, national risk assessment, national strategy and coordination bodies, the legal and institutional framework, beneficial ownership transparency and registers, the FIU and financial intelligence, investigation, prosecution and confiscation, supervision and preventive measures, international cooperation, preparing for a mutual evaluation, and grey-list exit action plans.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance, justice and interior ministries who lead national AML/CFT policy and coordination
- FIU directors and analysts, financial investigators, prosecutors and asset recovery officials
- Members and secretariats of national AML/CFT coordination committees and mutual evaluation teams

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the FATF Recommendations, the immediate outcomes and how effectiveness is assessed
- Lead or contribute to a national money laundering and terrorist financing risk assessment
- Design national coordination arrangements and a risk-based AML/CFT strategy
- Assess the legal framework, beneficial ownership transparency and operational effectiveness
- Prepare evidence, case studies and statistics for a mutual evaluation
- Develop an action plan to address deficiencies and exit increased monitoring

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The FATF Standards and the Global Network

How the FATF and its regional bodies work, what the 40 Recommendations require, and why a country's AML/CFT performance matters for its financial sector, reputation and economy.

**Objectives**

- Describe the FATF, FATF-style regional bodies and the global network
- Summarise the structure and main requirements of the FATF Recommendations
- Explain the economic and reputational consequences of weak AML/CFT systems

**Syllabus**

- The FATF, regional bodies and observer organisations
- The 40 Recommendations: policy, criminal justice, preventive and transparency measures
- Proliferation financing and targeted financial sanctions
- De-risking, correspondent banking and the cost of listing

### Module 2: Technical Compliance and Effectiveness: The Methodology

How assessors rate technical compliance and effectiveness, what the immediate outcomes test, and the procedures and timeline of the current round of mutual evaluations.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish technical compliance ratings from effectiveness ratings
- Explain the immediate outcomes and the core issues assessors examine
- Describe the mutual evaluation process, follow-up and listing triggers

**Syllabus**

- Technical compliance criteria and ratings
- The immediate outcomes, core issues and levels of effectiveness
- Evaluation procedures: questionnaires, on-site visit and plenary
- Follow-up, enhanced follow-up and the listing process

### Module 3: National Risk Assessment

How to plan, conduct and use a national assessment of money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing risks, including sectoral and thematic assessments.

**Objectives**

- Design the scope, methodology and governance of a national risk assessment
- Gather and analyse threat, vulnerability and consequence data
- Translate risk findings into policy priorities and resource allocation

**Syllabus**

- Methodologies, data sources and private sector input
- Threats, vulnerabilities and consequences by sector and product
- Sectoral assessments: legal persons, virtual assets and non-profit organisations
- Communicating results and keeping the assessment current

### Module 4: National Strategy, Coordination and Legal Framework

How to turn risk findings into a national AML/CFT strategy, design an effective coordination body, and ensure the legal and institutional framework meets the standards.

**Objectives**

- Design a national AML/CFT strategy linked to the risk assessment
- Establish coordination mechanisms with clear mandates and accountability
- Assess gaps in criminal, preventive and administrative legislation

**Syllabus**

- Strategy design: priorities, owners, resources and milestones
- National coordination committees: membership, mandate and secretariat
- Criminalisation of money laundering and terrorist financing
- Domestic cooperation, information sharing and data protection

### Module 5: Beneficial Ownership Transparency and Registers

Why opaque legal persons and arrangements are a core vulnerability, the FATF requirements on beneficial ownership, and the policy choices in designing and operating registers.

**Objectives**

- Explain the FATF requirements for legal persons and legal arrangements
- Evaluate register models, verification and access arrangements
- Assess whether competent authorities obtain accurate information in a timely way

**Syllabus**

- Recommendations 24 and 25 and the multi-pronged approach
- Register design: scope, thresholds, verification and sanctions
- Access for authorities, obliged entities and the public
- Trusts, nominees, bearer shares and foreign legal persons

### Module 6: Financial Intelligence and the FIU

The role of the national FIU in receiving, analysing and disseminating financial intelligence, and how its effectiveness is measured and demonstrated to assessors.

**Objectives**

- Describe the FIU's core functions, independence and resources
- Assess the quality and use of suspicious transaction reports and FIU disseminations
- Demonstrate the use of financial intelligence by law enforcement

**Syllabus**

- FIU models, autonomy and access to information
- Reporting quality, feedback and public-private partnerships
- Operational and strategic analysis products
- Statistics and case studies that evidence effectiveness

### Module 7: Investigation, Prosecution and Confiscation

How law enforcement, prosecutors and asset recovery bodies pursue money laundering, terrorist financing and the proceeds of crime, and what assessors expect to see in results.

**Objectives**

- Assess whether investigations and prosecutions match the risk profile
- Evaluate confiscation, asset recovery and asset management arrangements
- Identify practical obstacles in parallel financial investigations

**Syllabus**

- Parallel financial investigations and stand-alone money laundering cases
- Prosecution strategies, sentencing and alternative measures
- Freezing, seizure, confiscation and non-conviction based forfeiture
- Terrorist financing investigations and targeted financial sanctions

### Module 8: Supervision, Preventive Measures and International Cooperation

How financial and non-financial sectors are supervised for AML/CFT, the preventive measures they apply, and how the country cooperates with foreign counterparts.

**Objectives**

- Assess risk-based supervision of financial institutions, DNFBPs and VASPs
- Explain the preventive measures obliged entities must apply
- Evaluate mutual legal assistance, extradition and informal cooperation

**Syllabus**

- Supervisory coverage, risk-based supervision and sanctions
- Customer due diligence, monitoring and reporting obligations
- Lawyers, accountants, real estate, casinos and virtual asset providers
- Mutual legal assistance, extradition and FIU-to-FIU exchange

### Module 9: Preparing for a Mutual Evaluation

Practical preparation for an assessment: organising the national team, assembling evidence, writing the technical compliance and effectiveness submissions, and managing the on-site visit and plenary.

**Objectives**

- Organise a national evaluation team, timetable and governance
- Build a compelling evidence base of statistics and case studies
- Prepare officials for on-site interviews and plenary discussion

**Syllabus**

- Gap analysis and a mock evaluation
- Statistics, case studies and evidence of outcomes
- Drafting the technical compliance and effectiveness submissions
- On-site interviews, draft report review and plenary negotiation

### Module 10: Responding to Findings and Exiting Increased Monitoring

Brings the programme together by examining how to respond to evaluation findings, agree and deliver an action plan under increased monitoring, and sustain effectiveness after exit.

**Objectives**

- Prioritise recommended actions and allocate ownership and resources
- Design and deliver an action plan that meets increased monitoring deadlines
- Sustain reforms and prepare for follow-up assessments

**Syllabus**

- Prioritising recommended actions by impact and feasibility
- Increased monitoring: action plans, reporting and on-site verification
- Political leadership, resourcing and communication with industry
- Workshop: a national action plan to address deficiencies

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over three to four days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with country case studies and practical exercises, including a mock evaluation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the country's evaluation timetable.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT policy, financial intelligence or law enforcement is helpful. No prior involvement in a mutual evaluation is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012, updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- FATF (2013, updated), Methodology for Assessing Technical Compliance with the FATF Recommendations and the Effectiveness of AML/CFT Systems
- FATF (2013), National Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Risk Assessment
- FATF (2023), Guidance on Beneficial Ownership of Legal Persons
