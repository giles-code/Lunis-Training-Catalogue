## Aim and Purpose

To give directors a structured way to think about risks that are hard to quantify and fast-moving, from geopolitical fragmentation and state-sponsored cyber attack to climate, AI and quantum computing. Participants leave able to challenge management's horizon scanning, use scenarios and stress tests to probe resilience, and shape the board's risk appetite and agenda accordingly.

**Coverage:** Horizon scanning and emerging-risk frameworks, geopolitics and economic fragmentation, sanctions and trade restrictions, cyber and state actors, critical third parties and concentration risk, climate and nature, AI and quantum computing, scenario planning and stress testing, and the board's risk appetite and agenda.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, non-executive and independent directors, especially members of risk and strategy committees
- Executive directors, chief risk officers and senior managers responsible for strategy and emerging risk
- Company secretaries, heads of governance and strategy teams supporting the board

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how emerging risks are identified, assessed and escalated to the board
- Assess the firm's exposure to geopolitical fragmentation, sanctions and trade restrictions
- Evaluate cyber, third-party and concentration risks linked to state actors and critical providers
- Challenge management's response to climate, nature, AI and quantum risks
- Use scenario planning and stress testing to test strategy and resilience
- Set the board's risk appetite and agenda for emerging risks, and agree an action plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Horizon Scanning and Emerging-Risk Frameworks

How firms identify and assess risks that are uncertain, interconnected and slow to show in data, and what a good emerging-risk process looks like from the boardroom.

**Objectives**

- Explain the difference between emerging, principal and known risks
- Evaluate the firm's horizon scanning and emerging-risk process
- Identify blind spots, groupthink and signals the board may be missing

**Syllabus**

- Emerging risk taxonomies, risk radars and velocity
- Sources of insight: internal data, external experts and supervisory publications
- Interconnected risks and second-order effects
- Questions to test whether horizon scanning reaches decisions

### Module 2: Geopolitics, Fragmentation, Sanctions and Trade Restrictions

How geopolitical tension, economic fragmentation, sanctions and trade restrictions affect financial firms' markets, clients, operations and strategy, and how boards should respond.

**Objectives**

- Assess the firm's exposure to geopolitical events and fragmentation
- Explain how sanctions and trade restrictions can disrupt business at short notice
- Challenge management's country, client and supply-chain exposure analysis

**Syllabus**

- Geopolitical fragmentation, conflict and shifting alliances
- Rapidly expanding sanctions, counter-sanctions and export controls
- Market, credit, operational and conduct transmission channels
- Exiting markets and clients: strategy, cost and customer impact

### Module 3: Cyber, State Actors and Critical Third Parties

Why state-sponsored cyber activity and dependence on a small number of critical providers create severe but plausible scenarios, and what boards should require on resilience.

**Objectives**

- Recognise the threat from state actors and organised cyber groups
- Assess concentration and dependency on critical third parties
- Evaluate the firm's ability to withstand and recover from severe disruption

**Syllabus**

- State-sponsored attacks, disruption and supply-chain compromise
- Cloud, data and infrastructure concentration risk
- Severe but plausible scenarios and impact tolerances
- Board questions on testing, exit plans and recovery

### Module 4: Climate and Nature as Emerging Risks

How physical, transition and nature-related risks reach the firm's balance sheet, clients and operations, and how boards challenge the time horizons and assumptions used.

**Objectives**

- Explain physical, transition and nature-related risk channels
- Challenge the time horizons and assumptions in climate risk analysis
- Integrate climate and nature risk into strategy and risk appetite

**Syllabus**

- Physical, transition and liability risks
- Nature loss and biodiversity as a financial risk
- Climate scenarios and long-horizon uncertainty
- Disclosure, transition planning and greenwashing risk

### Module 5: AI, Quantum and Technological Disruption

The strategic and risk implications of AI, quantum computing and other disruptive technologies, including their use by competitors and criminals, and the decisions boards need to take now.

**Objectives**

- Assess strategic opportunity and disruption from AI and new entrants
- Explain the quantum threat to cryptography and the migration timeline
- Prioritise technology risks that need board decisions now

**Syllabus**

- AI-enabled competitors, fraud and misinformation
- Quantum computing, harvest-now-decrypt-later and post-quantum migration
- Business model disruption and technological lock-in
- Board questions on readiness, investment and capability

### Module 6: Scenario Planning and Stress Testing

How scenario planning and stress testing turn uncertain risks into decisions, how to judge whether scenarios are severe and imaginative enough, and how results should shape strategy.

**Objectives**

- Explain the difference between forecasting, scenario planning and stress testing
- Challenge scenario severity, combination and management actions
- Use results to inform strategy, capital and resilience decisions

**Syllabus**

- Narrative scenarios versus quantitative stress tests
- Compound and cascading scenarios across risk types
- Reverse stress testing and the limits of models
- Turning scenario results into decisions and triggers

### Module 7: Risk Appetite and the Board Agenda for Emerging Risks

How boards set risk appetite for risks that are hard to measure, decide what reaches the board agenda, and obtain the information and assurance they need.

**Objectives**

- Set qualitative and quantitative risk appetite for emerging risks
- Design a board agenda and information flow that keeps emerging risks visible
- Obtain challenge and assurance from the risk function, internal audit and external experts

**Syllabus**

- Risk appetite statements for hard-to-quantify risks
- Board and committee agendas, deep dives and strategy days
- Management information, early warning indicators and escalation
- The board's own capability, diversity of thought and external advice

### Module 8: Capstone: Board Scenario Exercise

Brings the programme together in a facilitated board simulation in which participants respond to an evolving, multi-risk scenario and agree the board's emerging-risk priorities.

**Objectives**

- Apply the programme's tools to a live, evolving scenario
- Make and communicate board decisions under uncertainty and time pressure
- Produce a board action plan for emerging-risk oversight

**Syllabus**

- Scenario injects: geopolitical shock, cyber attack and third-party failure
- Decision-making, escalation and stakeholder communication under pressure
- Debrief: what the board knew, missed and should have asked
- Workshop: the board's twelve-month emerging-risk plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with scenarios and a board question set, the final module is a facilitated scenario exercise, and modules can be selected or tailored to the board's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board or senior management experience in financial services is helpful. No specialist knowledge of geopolitics, technology or climate science is required.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee, Stress Testing Principles (2018)
- FSB, Enhancing Third-Party Risk Management and Oversight: A Toolkit for Financial Institutions and Financial Authorities (2023)
- G7 Cyber Expert Group, Public Statement on Planning for the Opportunities and Risks of Quantum Computing (2024)
- TNFD, Recommendations of the Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (2023)
