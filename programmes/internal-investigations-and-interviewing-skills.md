## Aim and Purpose

To equip compliance, risk, audit, legal and HR professionals with a structured method for investigating concerns inside the firm, from triage and planning to evidence, interviews and findings. Participants leave able to conduct investigations that are proportionate, fair and defensible, and that the board and the supervisor can rely on.

**Coverage:** Triggers, mandate and independence, whistleblowing and speak-up reports, investigation planning and terms of reference, gathering and preserving evidence, interview structure and fairness, questioning and active listening, document-based questioning and difficult interviews, findings and reporting to the board and the authority, and a role-play workshop.

## Intended Audience

- Heads of compliance, risk, internal audit, financial crime and legal, and the investigators in their teams
- HR and employee relations professionals who conduct disciplinary and misconduct investigations
- Whistleblowing champions, investigation sponsors and senior managers who commission internal reviews

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain when an internal investigation is needed and how to set its mandate, independence and terms of reference
- Triage whistleblowing reports and other concerns while protecting confidentiality and preventing retaliation
- Plan investigations and gather and preserve evidence so that it is reliable and defensible
- Apply a structured interview method, using effective questioning and active listening to test accounts
- Conduct interviews with witnesses and subjects fairly, including difficult interviewees and those with representatives
- Reach evidence-based findings and report them to the board and, where required, to the authority

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Internal Investigations: Triggers, Mandate and Independence

Why and when a firm investigates, the different types of internal investigation, and how to set a clear mandate with the right sponsor, investigator and degree of independence.

**Objectives**

- Identify the events that should trigger an internal investigation
- Distinguish fact-finding, disciplinary and regulatory investigations
- Set terms of reference that secure independence and the board's confidence

**Syllabus**

- Triggers: whistleblowing, complaints, breaches, audit findings and surveillance alerts
- Types of investigation and how their purpose shapes the method
- Sponsors, investigators and when to use external investigators
- Terms of reference: scope, questions, authority and reporting lines

### Module 2: Whistleblowing and Speak-Up Reports

How to receive, triage and protect whistleblowing and speak-up reports so that concerns are investigated properly and those who raise them are not deterred or harmed.

**Objectives**

- Design a triage process for speak-up reports and other concerns
- Protect confidentiality and anonymity and prevent retaliation
- Explain the board's oversight role in whistleblowing arrangements

**Syllabus**

- Channels, intake and acknowledging the reporter
- Triage: seriousness, credibility, urgency and conflicts of interest
- Confidentiality, anonymity and detecting and preventing retaliation
- Psychological safety and why staff stay silent

### Module 3: Planning the Investigation

How to turn a concern into a focused investigation plan with hypotheses, lines of enquiry and an evidence matrix, and decide on interim measures and early notification to the authority.

**Objectives**

- Frame hypotheses and the elements of the suspected breach or misconduct
- Build an investigation plan, evidence matrix and timetable
- Decide on interim measures, legal privilege and notification to the authority

**Syllabus**

- Hypotheses, lines of enquiry and evidence matrices
- Resourcing, timetables and investigation governance
- Interim measures: suspension, access restrictions and customer protection
- Legal privilege, the regulator's interest and when to notify

### Module 4: Gathering and Preserving Evidence

Practical techniques for securing documents, communications, trading and transaction data, and devices quickly and lawfully, with a chain of custody that will withstand challenge.

**Objectives**

- Identify the sources of evidence relevant to the concern
- Preserve documents, communications and data without contamination
- Organise evidence into chronologies and gap analyses

**Syllabus**

- Evidence sources: emails, chats, recorded calls, systems and transaction data
- Legal holds, forensic copies and chain of custody
- Data protection, employee privacy and proportionate searches
- Chronologies, document review and identifying gaps

### Module 5: The Investigative Interview: Structure, Fairness and Mindset

A structured interview model adapted for internal investigations, the difference between witness and subject interviews, and the fairness safeguards that keep findings defensible.

**Objectives**

- Apply a PEACE-style structure to internal investigation interviews
- Distinguish witness and subject interviews and their safeguards
- Adopt a mindset that is curious, sceptical and fair

**Syllabus**

- The PEACE model: plan, engage, account, closure and evaluate
- Witness versus subject interviews: notice, disclosure and representation
- Employee duties to cooperate and rights in disciplinary processes
- Bias, premature conclusions and keeping an open mind

### Module 6: Questioning, Listening and Testing Accounts

The question types and sequencing that obtain full and accurate accounts, how to listen actively, and how to assess credibility and recognise evasion without false conclusions.

**Objectives**

- Use open, probing and funnel questions to obtain a complete account
- Listen actively and use silence, summarising and reflection
- Test accounts for consistency, plausibility and corroboration

**Syllabus**

- Open questions, probing questions and funnel sequencing
- Active listening: silence, summarising and reflecting back
- Evasion patterns: deflection, generalisation and scripted answers
- Limits of non-verbal cues and avoiding false conclusions

### Module 7: Documents, Records and Difficult Interviews

How to put documents and inconsistencies to interviewees fairly, record interviews reliably, and keep control when interviewees are defensive, hostile or accompanied by representatives.

**Objectives**

- Select and sequence documents and put inconsistencies fairly
- Record interviews accurately through notes, recordings and agreed accounts
- Manage difficult interviewees and the role of representatives

**Syllabus**

- Document bundles, sequencing and disclosure in the interview
- Note-taking, recordings, transcripts and agreed notes
- Defensive, hostile and over-talkative interviewees: de-escalation and refocusing
- Lawyers, companions and when to stop, re-plan or escalate

### Module 8: Findings, Reporting and Outcomes

How to weigh the evidence, reach findings on the balance of probabilities, write a report the board can act on, and handle disciplinary, fitness and regulatory consequences.

**Objectives**

- Weigh interview, documentary and data evidence to reach reasoned findings
- Write an investigation report with findings, root causes and recommendations
- Determine reporting to the board, the authority and the national FIU

**Syllabus**

- Standards of proof, weighing evidence and stating uncertainty
- Report structure: scope, method, findings, root causes and recommendations
- Disciplinary outcomes, fitness and propriety and regulatory references
- Reporting to the board, notifying the authority and suspicious activity reports

### Module 9: Role-Play Workshop: From Interview to Board Report

Brings the programme together in a simulated investigation in which participants plan and conduct interviews with a witness and a subject, evaluate the evidence and present findings to the board.

**Objectives**

- Plan and conduct witness and subject interviews in a realistic case
- Evaluate what the interviews established and what remains open
- Present findings and recommendations to a simulated board

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing: whistleblowing report, documents and data
- Role-play: witness interview and subject interview
- Debrief: evidence established, gaps and further enquiries
- Workshop: draft findings and present to the board

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines practical technique with case studies and exercises, building to a full interview role-play, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's investigation and whistleblowing arrangements.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of compliance, audit, legal, HR or risk work in a regulated firm is helpful. No prior investigation or interviewing training is required.
