## Aim and Purpose

To help boards, senior managers and control functions understand the path from a supervisory risk assessment to ratings, findings and required actions, and to manage remediation so that issues are fixed at their root and closed on evidence. Participants leave able to engage constructively with findings, design remediation that addresses underlying causes and respond well to thematic reviews.

## What It Covers

How supervisory conclusions are reached and challenged, risk ratings and engagement, risk mitigation programmes, choosing and planning remediation, evidencing closure, escalation and enforcement risk, and thematic reviews and industry reports.

## Intended Audience

- Board members and senior managers accountable for responding to supervisory findings
- Compliance, risk and internal audit staff who coordinate remediation and track actions
- Project and change managers who deliver remediation programmes

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how supervisors form, test and challenge their conclusions before findings are issued
- Interpret risk ratings and what they mean for the firm's supervisory engagement
- Analyse supervisory findings to identify incidents, control failures and underlying causes
- Design remediation actions that define the outcome and can be evidenced
- Manage remediation to closure and avoid escalation
- Respond effectively to thematic reviews and industry findings

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about one hour each, delivered as a one-day workshop in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, built around a running case study. It can be delivered on its own or with the companion workshops on business models and on governance and supervisory interviews.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervisory engagement or remediation work is helpful. No prior training is required.

## Core Reading

- IMF, Good Supervision: Lessons from the Field (Working Paper WP/23/181, 2023)
- FSB, Strengthening Governance Frameworks to Mitigate Misconduct Risk: A Toolkit for Firms and Supervisors (2018)
- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
