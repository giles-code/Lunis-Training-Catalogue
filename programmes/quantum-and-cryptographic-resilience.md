## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a plain-language understanding of how quantum computing threatens the cryptography that secures payments, data and digital assets, and of what a credible migration to post-quantum cryptography involves. Participants leave able to assess firms' inventories, migration plans and third-party dependencies, identify red flags and contribute to the authority's quantum readiness strategy.

## What It Covers

Quantum computing and the cryptographic threat, harvest now decrypt later, where cryptography sits in financial services, cryptographic inventories and risk assessment, NIST post-quantum standards, migration planning and crypto-agility, third-party dependencies, impacts on payments and crypto-assets, and supervisory expectations.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and inspectors responsible for technology, cyber and operational resilience risk
- Payments, market infrastructure and digital asset specialists in supervisory and central bank functions
- Policy staff and senior managers shaping the authority's cyber and quantum readiness approach

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how quantum computing threatens public-key cryptography and why the risk is already present
- Identify where cryptography is embedded in financial services and which assets are most exposed
- Assess the completeness and quality of a firm's cryptographic inventory and risk assessment
- Evaluate post-quantum migration plans, crypto-agility and the use of NIST standards
- Analyse third-party, payments and crypto-asset dependencies that constrain migration
- Design proportionate supervisory expectations and a quantum readiness plan for the authority

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a plain-language technical briefing with case studies and supervisory question exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of cyber, technology risk or operational resilience supervision is helpful. No background in mathematics or cryptography is required.

## Core Reading

- NIST, FIPS 203, 204 and 205: Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards (2024)
- G7 Cyber Expert Group, Public Statement on Planning for the Opportunities and Risks of Quantum Computing (2024)
- NIST, Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (2024)
