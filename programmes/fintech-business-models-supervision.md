## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical understanding of the main FinTech business models, from digital lending and neobanks to trading apps, insurtech and crypto on-ramps, and of the unit economics and funding dynamics that drive their behaviour. Participants leave able to read a FinTech's model the way a supervisor does, identify red flags early, and apply proportionate licensing, innovation-facilitation and wind-down tools.

## What It Covers

The FinTech landscape and unit economics, digital lending and buy now, pay later, neobanks, wallets and payment apps, crowdfunding and peer-to-peer platforms, robo-advice and trading apps, insurtech, crypto on-ramps and regtech, growth, funding and partnership-chain risk, consumer outcomes, financial crime and operational resilience, licensing, sandboxes and innovation hubs, and failure and wind-down.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, authorisation and innovation-hub staff dealing with FinTech applicants and fast-growing digital firms
- Policy, consumer protection and financial crime specialists assessing new business models
- Compliance, risk and legal teams in FinTech firms who want to see their firm the way a supervisor does

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how the main FinTech business models generate revenue, acquire customers and scale
- Analyse unit economics, cash burn and funding dependency as early indicators of fragility
- Assess consumer outcomes, financial crime and operational resilience risks specific to each model
- Evaluate partnership chains and where regulatory accountability sits within them
- Compare licensing approaches, sandboxes and innovation hubs and their supervisory trade-offs
- Plan a proportionate supervisory response to a FinTech in difficulty, through to orderly wind-down

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with case studies, business model analysis exercises and supervisory question sets, closing with a FinTech failure workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the client's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervision, authorisation or compliance in financial services is helpful. No technical or financial modelling background is required.
