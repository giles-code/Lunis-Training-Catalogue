## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of how crypto-asset firms operate across borders, the risks of regulatory arbitrage, and the cooperation mechanisms supervisors use — including MiCA passporting — to maintain effective oversight.

## Intended Audience

Mid-level supervisory staff building capability in digital assets/crypto-asset regulation.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how crypto-asset firms structure cross-border operations
- Describe the risk of regulatory arbitrage in the crypto sector
- Assess MiCA passporting mechanisms for CASPs
- Evaluate cooperation mechanisms between crypto supervisors
- Formulate a supervisory approach to a cross-border crypto supervision scenario

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Cross-border crypto operations (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain cross-border crypto structures*
- Why crypto-asset firms often operate across multiple jurisdictions
- Common cross-border structuring patterns
- Regulatory arbitrage risk: seeking the most permissive jurisdiction
- Global inconsistency in crypto regulatory maturity
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: cross-border crypto structures participants have encountered

**Session 2: MiCA passporting and cooperation (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess MiCA passporting and evaluate cooperation mechanisms*
- How MiCA passporting operates for CASPs
- Cooperation mechanisms between EU and non-EU crypto supervisors
- International bodies working on crypto standard-setting (FATF, FSB)
- Information-sharing challenges specific to the crypto sector
Activities:
- Group exercise: assess a sample CASP's passporting arrangements

**Session 3: Application to a scenario (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Formulate a supervisory approach*
- Case study: a cross-border crypto firm raising supervisory concerns
Activities:
- Case study: formulate a supervisory approach and cooperation plan

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: cross-border structures, regulatory arbitrage, passporting, cooperation
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Cross-border crypto operations (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: MiCA passporting and cooperation (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Application to a scenario (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Regulatory arbitrage risk is particularly acute in crypto given global inconsistency in regulatory maturity
- MiCA passporting extends familiar EU cross-border supervisory concepts into the crypto-asset sector
- International cooperation on crypto supervision is still maturing and requires active relationship-building
