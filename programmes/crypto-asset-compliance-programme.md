## Aim and Purpose

To equip the control functions of crypto-asset service providers and issuers with an advanced, practical command of the obligations that comprehensive crypto-asset regimes impose, from authorisation to wind-down. Participants leave able to design and evidence effective governance, custody, conduct, market integrity, financial crime and resilience controls, and to engage with supervisors the way a well-run firm should.

**Coverage:** International standards and comprehensive crypto-asset regimes, business models and the regulatory perimeter, authorisation, governance and internal controls, prudential resources and wind-down planning, custody and safeguarding, ICT and operational resilience, conflicts of interest and white paper disclosure, market abuse surveillance, AML/CFT and the travel rule, stablecoin issuance, and supervisory engagement and enforcement.

## Intended Audience

- Heads of compliance, compliance officers and compliance monitoring staff at crypto-asset service providers and issuers
- Risk managers, MLROs, financial crime and internal audit professionals covering crypto-asset activities
- Senior managers and legal staff responsible for authorisation, regulatory engagement and remediation

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Apply FSB, IOSCO and FATF standards and the common architecture of comprehensive crypto-asset regimes to the firm's activities
- Prepare a credible authorisation application and evidence real substance, governance and control
- Design custody, safeguarding, prudential and wind-down arrangements that protect clients and the firm
- Implement conflicts, disclosure and market abuse controls that meet conduct and market integrity expectations
- Operate an effective AML/CFT, sanctions and travel rule framework and resilient ICT arrangements
- Engage confidently with supervisors, manage enforcement risk and respond to crises

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Crypto-Asset Markets and the Global Regulatory Framework

A compliance view of how blockchains, crypto-assets and their intermediaries create risk, and how international standards and comprehensive crypto-asset regimes respond to it.

**Objectives**

- Identify the real control points and failure modes in blockchain-based activities
- Explain the FSB, IOSCO and FATF standards for crypto-asset activities
- Map the common building blocks of comprehensive crypto-asset regimes to the firm

**Syllabus**

- Control points and failure modes: upgrades, forks, oracles, smart contracts
- The ecosystem: issuers, validators, venues, custodians and end users
- FSB recommendations, IOSCO policy recommendations and FATF Recommendation 15
- Comprehensive crypto-asset regimes: objectives, scope and layered rulebooks

### Module 2: Business Models, Perimeter and Cross-Border Operations

Where regulatory obligations attach across the centralised to decentralised spectrum, how they vary by service, and the compliance consequences of operating from one licence across several jurisdictions.

**Objectives**

- Map the firm's activities to regulated crypto-asset services
- Identify business model red flags that attract supervisory concern
- Manage home and host obligations when serving clients across borders

**Syllabus**

- The centralised to decentralised spectrum and perimeter questions
- Obligations by service: trading platforms, brokers, custodians, advisers, issuers
- Red flags: overlapping roles, opaque fees and fragmented group structures
- Cross-border services, home and host supervisors and substance

### Module 3: Preparing a Credible Authorisation Application

How to build an application dossier that meets the authority's completeness and quality thresholds, and to pressure-test the firm's governance and operations as a supervisor would before submission.

**Objectives**

- Build a complete application dossier and programme of operations
- Evidence local substance and manage group and outsourcing dependencies
- Pressure-test the business plan, customer journeys and regulatory history

**Syllabus**

- Core dossier elements: governance, suitability, policies, ICT and safeguarding
- Business plans that do not outpace compliance capability
- Substance, intragroup agreements and avoiding letter-box structures
- Customer journeys, risk warnings and unresolved regulatory history

### Module 4: Governance, Risk Management and Internal Controls

Building governance, risk and compliance functions that are genuinely effective rather than merely documented, and using self-assessment to find weaknesses before a supervisor does.

**Objectives**

- Design a management body, oversight functions and three lines of defence that work in practice
- Operate risk appetite, compliance monitoring and internal audit with real independence
- Detect governance red flags and apply proportionate remediation

**Syllabus**

- Board composition, fitness and propriety and segregation of duties
- Risk appetite, risk registers and independence of control functions
- Compliance monitoring plans and internal audit scope
- Red flags: founder dominance, nominal independence and marginalised risk functions

### Module 5: Prudential Resources, Recovery and Wind-Down Planning

Managing capital, liquidity and financial resilience through crypto market cycles, and preparing credible recovery and orderly wind-down plans that protect clients if the business fails.

**Objectives**

- Assess own funds and liquidity against the firm's risk profile
- Identify prudential red flags and early warning indicators
- Design recovery and orderly wind-down plans that return client assets

**Syllabus**

- Capital requirements, stressed positions and revenue volatility
- Liquidity stress testing and contingency funding
- Red flags: affiliate funding, thin capital and untested projections
- Recovery options, wind-down triggers and client asset return

### Module 6: Custody and Safeguarding of Client Assets

How crypto-asset custody works in practice, from hot and cold storage to sub-custody chains, and how the firm builds and evidences safeguarding that protects client assets in all conditions.

**Objectives**

- Assess how custody architectures affect the firm's safeguarding risk
- Identify key management, reconciliation and sub-custodian risks
- Design and evidence segregation, reconciliation and insolvency protection

**Syllabus**

- Hot and cold storage, hardware security modules, multi-signature and MPC
- Key management, dual control and backup and recovery
- Segregation, reconciliation and the ban on unauthorised use of client assets
- Red flags and custody assurance: walkthroughs, audits and testing

### Module 7: ICT Risk and Operational Resilience

Building and evidencing ICT risk management and operational resilience for the hybrid on-chain and off-chain architecture of crypto-asset firms, including cloud concentration and complex outsourcing chains.

**Objectives**

- Identify ICT and operational risks specific to crypto-asset operations
- Design ICT risk management, incident reporting and resilience testing
- Manage third-party and cloud dependencies with credible exit arrangements

**Syllabus**

- Key infrastructure, API dependencies and smart contract upgrade risk
- ICT incident classification, reporting and internal escalation
- Business continuity, penetration testing and scenario exercises
- Third-party ICT contracts, concentration and exit planning

### Module 8: Conflicts of Interest, Disclosure and White Papers

Identifying and managing the structural conflicts in multi-service crypto-asset firms, and producing white papers, marketing and client disclosures that are fair, clear and not misleading.

**Objectives**

- Identify structural conflicts in combined service models and manage them
- Prepare white papers and disclosures that meet content and liability standards
- Review marketing communications and customer journeys for fair treatment

**Syllabus**

- Conflicts in exchange, custody, market making and proprietary trading
- White papers: content, accuracy, updates and responsibility
- Marketing communications, risk warnings and complaints handling
- Listing and admission policies and token due diligence

### Module 9: Market Integrity and Market Abuse Surveillance

Preventing, detecting and reporting insider dealing and manipulation in crypto-asset markets, with surveillance systems and suspicious order reporting that work as genuine risk intelligence.

**Objectives**

- Identify inside information and insider dealing risks in the firm's activities
- Detect classic and crypto-native manipulation schemes
- Design surveillance, alert calibration and suspicious order reporting

**Syllabus**

- Prohibited behaviours and IOSCO market integrity expectations
- Information barriers, personal account dealing and pre-clearance
- Wash trading, spoofing, pump-and-dump and MEV exploitation
- Surveillance design, investigation workflows and reporting quality

### Module 10: AML/CFT, Sanctions and the Travel Rule

Assessing and controlling money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion risk in crypto-asset activities, using blockchain analytics and implementing the FATF travel rule.

**Objectives**

- Assess the firm's exposure to crypto-specific financial crime typologies
- Build CDD, transaction monitoring, sanctions screening and MLRO arrangements
- Implement the travel rule, including for unhosted wallets and counterparty due diligence

**Syllabus**

- Typologies: mixers, privacy coins, ransomware and sanctions evasion
- Blockchain analytics, wallet screening and alert management
- FATF Recommendation 16 and the travel rule in practice
- MLRO independence, suspicious transaction reporting and the national FIU

### Module 11: Stablecoin Issuance and Reserve Management

The obligations and risks of issuing or distributing stablecoins, focusing on reserve quality, redemption rights, disclosure and the heightened expectations that apply to systemically significant tokens.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish stablecoin designs and their regulatory treatment
- Evaluate reserve quality, liquidity and redemption resilience under stress
- Design reserve, redemption and disclosure controls for an issuer

**Syllabus**

- Single-currency, multi-asset and algorithmic designs and their failure modes
- Reserve composition, custody, attestation and concentration risk
- Redemption rights, run dynamics and stress scenarios
- FSB recommendations on global stablecoin arrangements and significance

### Module 12: Supervisory Engagement, Enforcement and Crisis Readiness

Brings the programme together by preparing the firm for risk-based supervision, inspections and enforcement, and for managing a crisis through to recovery or orderly exit.

**Objectives**

- Prepare for and host supervisory inspections and information requests
- Respond proportionately to supervisory measures and enforcement action
- Lead the firm's crisis response, drawing on lessons from major failures

**Syllabus**

- How risk-based supervision allocates attention and what triggers escalation
- Inspection readiness, management interviews and remediation tracking
- Enforcement tools, rights of defence and lessons from major failures
- Workshop: crisis simulation and the firm's compliance action plan

## Structure and Format

Twelve modules of about three and a half hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions with pre-reading. Modules combine technical briefings with red-flag case studies, self-assessment exercises and a closing crisis simulation, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's services and jurisdictions.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Working knowledge of crypto-assets and of compliance or risk in a regulated firm is helpful; the Crypto and Blockchain Fundamentals for Firms programme provides a suitable foundation.
