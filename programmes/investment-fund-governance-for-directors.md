## Aim and Purpose

To give fund directors a practical grounding in how funds are governed when most activities are delegated, and the tools to oversee the investment manager and other service providers. Participants leave able to set the board's expectations, challenge delegates and management, and evidence sound oversight to supervisors and auditors.

**Coverage:** The fund governance model, delegation and oversight of the investment manager, administrator, depositary and distributors, managerial functions and designated persons, conflicts of interest, valuation and liquidity risk management, fees and value for investors, board information and decision-making, regulatory engagement and effectiveness reviews, and emerging issues including AI, ESG and tokenised funds.

## Intended Audience

- Independent and non-executive directors of investment funds and fund management companies
- Executive directors, designated persons and conducting officers of fund management companies
- Company secretaries, governance leads and service provider relationship managers supporting fund boards

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the fund governance model and the board's responsibilities where activities are delegated
- Evaluate the oversight of the investment manager, administrator, depositary and distributors
- Identify and manage conflicts of interest across the fund and its service providers
- Challenge valuation, liquidity, fees and value for investors using structured board questions
- Assess the quality of board information, decision-making and engagement with the authority
- Anticipate emerging governance issues and agree a board action plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Fund Governance Model

How investment funds and fund management companies are structured and governed, where the board sits among the parties involved, and what fund product and fund manager rules expect of directors.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main fund structures and the parties involved in running a fund
- Define the duties of fund and management company directors
- Distinguish the board's responsibilities from those of its delegates

**Syllabus**

- Fund structures: corporate, contractual and partnership vehicles
- Self-managed funds, management companies and external fund managers
- Directors' duties, independence and acting in investors' best interests
- International standards, including the IOSCO principles for collective investment schemes

### Module 2: Delegation and Oversight of the Investment Manager

How the board delegates portfolio management while retaining responsibility, and the oversight framework needed to hold the investment manager to account without making investment decisions itself.

**Objectives**

- Assess the due diligence and contractual terms underpinning delegation
- Evaluate performance, risk and mandate compliance reporting from the investment manager
- Challenge the manager while respecting the line between oversight and management

**Syllabus**

- Delegation versus abdication: what the board cannot hand over
- Initial and ongoing due diligence, mandates and investment restrictions
- Performance, risk and breach reporting to the board
- Fear of micromanaging: monitoring without directing investment decisions

### Module 3: Overseeing the Administrator, Depositary and Distributors

The board's oversight of the fund's other key service providers, from net asset value calculation and safekeeping to distribution and investor onboarding.

**Objectives**

- Explain the roles of the administrator, depositary, transfer agent and distributors
- Evaluate service levels, controls reports and escalation of errors
- Assess distribution oversight, target markets and financial crime controls

**Syllabus**

- Administration, transfer agency and NAV error handling
- The depositary's independent safekeeping and oversight role
- Distribution networks, target market and investor onboarding
- Service level agreements, controls reports and on-site reviews

### Module 4: Managerial Functions and Designated Persons

How the management company organises its managerial functions, the substance and time commitment the authority expects, and how the board oversees the people who perform them.

**Objectives**

- Explain the managerial functions and how they are allocated
- Assess substance, resourcing and time commitment of designated persons
- Evaluate reporting from designated persons to the board

**Syllabus**

- Core managerial functions: investment, risk, distribution, operations and compliance
- Allocation, capability and time commitment of designated persons
- Substance requirements and avoiding letterbox arrangements
- Designated persons' reporting and the board's monitoring plan

### Module 5: Conflicts of Interest

Where conflicts arise in fund groups and between the fund, its manager and service providers, and how the board identifies, manages and records them.

**Objectives**

- Identify conflicts arising from group structures, fees and related parties
- Evaluate the conflicts policy, register and disclosure to investors
- Challenge decisions where the manager's interests may differ from investors'

**Syllabus**

- Sources of conflict: affiliated service providers, cross-trades and allocation
- Directors' own conflicts, multiple directorships and independence
- Conflicts policies, registers and board minutes
- Case studies of conflicts that harmed investors

### Module 6: Valuation and Liquidity Risk Management

The board's oversight of how fund assets are valued and how liquidity is managed, with particular focus on hard-to-value assets and stressed market conditions.

**Objectives**

- Assess valuation policies, committees and the treatment of hard-to-value assets
- Evaluate liquidity risk management, stress testing and available tools
- Challenge management on matching fund liquidity to redemption terms

**Syllabus**

- Valuation governance, pricing sources and fair value adjustments
- Liquidity mismatch, stress testing and investor concentration
- Liquidity management tools: swing pricing, gates and suspensions
- Board decisions under stress: lessons from fund suspensions

### Module 7: Fees, Costs and Value for Investors

How the board tests whether fees and costs are justified, whether investors receive value, and whether the fund is treating them fairly.

**Objectives**

- Explain the components of fund fees and costs
- Assess value for investors against performance, service and comparable products
- Challenge undue costs and evidence the board's conclusions

**Syllabus**

- Management, performance and transaction fees, and other charges
- Undue costs and supervisory expectations on fund fees
- Value assessment: performance, service, comparable funds and share classes
- Fair treatment, disclosure and closet tracking

### Module 8: Board Information, Decision-Making and Dynamics

How fund boards can get the right information from delegates, overcome information asymmetry and make well-reasoned decisions, given limited meeting time and a crowded agenda.

**Objectives**

- Specify the information the board needs from management and delegates
- Evaluate board packs and decision papers for clarity and balance
- Apply practical techniques to strengthen challenge and counter group think

**Syllabus**

- Agenda overload and prioritising what the board must decide
- Information asymmetry between independent directors and the manager
- Good decision papers: options, assumptions, downside and recorded rationale
- The chair, psychological safety and effective challenge

### Module 9: Regulatory Engagement and Board Effectiveness Reviews

What the authority expects to see from fund boards, how to prepare for supervisory engagement, and how to run effectiveness reviews that lead to change.

**Objectives**

- Explain supervisory expectations of fund and management company boards
- Prepare the board to evidence its oversight to the authority
- Evaluate board effectiveness reviews and act on their findings

**Syllabus**

- Supervisory thematic reviews of fund governance: recurring findings
- Fitness and probity, time commitment and directors' interviews
- Board minutes as evidence of challenge and oversight
- Effectiveness reviews, skills matrices and succession

### Module 10: Emerging Issues: AI, ESG and Tokenised Funds

Brings the programme together into a board action plan, examining the governance questions raised by AI, sustainability claims and tokenised fund structures.

**Objectives**

- Assess governance risks in the use of AI by managers and service providers
- Challenge sustainability claims and the risk of greenwashing
- Produce a board action plan for emerging risks

**Syllabus**

- AI in portfolio management, risk and operations: board oversight questions
- ESG strategies, disclosures and greenwashing risk
- Tokenised fund units and distributed ledger infrastructure
- Workshop: the fund board's twelve-month oversight plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two- to three-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the board's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of investment funds or asset management is helpful. No prior board experience is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Examination of Governance for Collective Investment Schemes (2006–2007)
- IOSCO, Principles for the Valuation of Collective Investment Schemes (2013)
- IOSCO, Recommendations for Liquidity Risk Management for Collective Investment Schemes (2018)
- G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (2023)
