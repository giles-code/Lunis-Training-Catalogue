## Aim and Purpose

To give fund managers and their service providers a practical understanding of the rules and supervisory expectations that govern funds and the people who run them. Participants leave able to strengthen governance, delegation, valuation and liquidity arrangements, and to see their fund range the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** Fund manager and fund product rules, delegation and substance, the depositary and trustee interface, valuation, liquidity risk management, costs, value and disclosure, conflicts of interest, private markets and retail access, tokenised funds, and engaging with the supervisor.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance, risk and legal staff at fund managers and investment managers
- Fund administrators, depositaries, trustees, transfer agents and other service providers
- Fund board directors, conducting officers and senior managers overseeing fund ranges

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how fund manager and fund product rules apply across the fund lifecycle
- Assess delegation, substance and the oversight of service providers
- Design sound valuation and liquidity risk management frameworks
- Evaluate costs, value, disclosure and conflicts in investors' interests
- Identify the risks of private markets, retail access and tokenised funds
- Engage effectively with the supervisor and agree a compliance action plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Fund Regulatory Framework

How fund manager and fund product rules fit together, the main fund types and structures, and the responsibilities of the manager, the fund's governing body and its service providers.

**Objectives**

- Explain how fund manager and fund product rules interact
- Distinguish retail, professional and private fund structures and their regulatory treatment
- Identify the roles and responsibilities of each party in the fund ecosystem

**Syllabus**

- Fund manager authorisation versus fund product approval
- Retail, professional and private funds: structures and investor protections
- Fund boards, managers, administrators, depositaries and distributors
- The IOSCO Objectives and Principles and international fund standards

### Module 2: Delegation, Substance and Oversight

How managers delegate portfolio management, risk management and administration while retaining the substance, expertise and oversight that supervisors expect.

**Objectives**

- Assess whether the manager retains sufficient substance and decision-making
- Design due diligence and ongoing oversight of delegates
- Identify letter-box risks and how supervisors test for them

**Syllabus**

- Delegation models and the limits on what can be delegated
- Substance: people, expertise, resources and decision-making
- Delegate due diligence, service levels and ongoing monitoring
- Intragroup and cross-border delegation

### Module 3: The Depositary and Trustee Interface

The independent role of the depositary or trustee in safekeeping and oversight, and how managers and service providers build an effective working relationship with it.

**Objectives**

- Explain the safekeeping, cash monitoring and oversight duties of the depositary or trustee
- Manage the information flows and escalations between manager and depositary
- Respond effectively to depositary challenges and breach reports

**Syllabus**

- Safekeeping, record-keeping and asset verification
- Cash flow monitoring and oversight of dealing, valuation and investment limits
- Service agreements, escalation and breach reporting
- Independence and conflicts in the depositary relationship

### Module 4: Valuation

How to design and govern a valuation framework that produces fair and reliable values, particularly for hard-to-value and illiquid assets.

**Objectives**

- Design a valuation policy and governance framework
- Assess valuation approaches for illiquid and complex assets
- Identify valuation conflicts and errors and respond to them

**Syllabus**

- Valuation policy, committee and functional independence
- Pricing sources, models and fair value adjustments
- Illiquid assets, private markets and valuation uncertainty
- Pricing errors, investor compensation and NAV suspensions

### Module 5: Liquidity Risk Management

How managers align fund liquidity with redemption terms, use stress testing and liquidity management tools, and respond when liquidity comes under pressure.

**Objectives**

- Align portfolio liquidity with dealing frequency and investor profile
- Design liquidity stress testing and monitoring
- Select and govern liquidity management tools

**Syllabus**

- Liquidity mismatch and lessons from fund suspensions
- Liquidity stress testing for assets and liabilities
- Liquidity management tools: swing pricing, anti-dilution levies, gates and suspensions
- FSB and IOSCO recommendations on fund liquidity

### Module 6: Costs, Value and Disclosure

How firms assess whether funds deliver value for the costs charged, and how to disclose costs, risks and objectives in a way that is fair, clear and not misleading.

**Objectives**

- Assess whether a fund delivers value relative to its costs
- Ensure cost and charges disclosure is complete and accurate
- Evaluate the clarity and accuracy of fund documents and marketing

**Syllabus**

- Fee structures, undue costs and value assessments
- Cost and charges disclosure, including transaction costs
- Prospectus, key information documents and periodic reporting
- Marketing communications and sustainability-related claims

### Module 7: Conflicts of Interest and Fund Governance

How conflicts arise across the fund lifecycle and how managers, boards and service providers identify, manage and evidence their resolution in investors' interests.

**Objectives**

- Identify conflicts across portfolio management, dealing and service provision
- Design conflicts registers, policies and controls
- Assess the role of the fund board and independent oversight in managing conflicts

**Syllabus**

- Common conflicts: allocation, cross-trades, related parties and fees
- Inducements, soft commissions and intragroup arrangements
- Conflicts policies, registers and disclosure
- Board challenge and independent directors

### Module 8: Private Markets and Retail Access

The growth of private equity, private credit and infrastructure funds, the move to give retail investors access, and the governance, valuation and liquidity challenges this brings.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main private market strategies and fund structures
- Assess the risks of offering private market exposure to retail investors
- Design product governance and distribution controls for semi-liquid funds

**Syllabus**

- Private equity, private credit, real estate and infrastructure funds
- Semi-liquid and evergreen structures for wider investor access
- Target market, suitability and distribution controls
- Leverage, valuation and liquidity in private markets

### Module 9: Tokenised Funds and Technology

How tokenisation, distributed ledgers and new technology are changing fund issuance, dealing and servicing, and the regulatory and operational questions this raises.

**Objectives**

- Explain how tokenised fund units are issued, held and transferred
- Assess custody, record-keeping and settlement risks in tokenised models
- Identify how existing fund rules apply to new technology

**Syllabus**

- Tokenised fund units and on-chain registers
- Custody, transfer agency and settlement on distributed ledgers
- Tokenised money market funds as collateral and settlement assets
- Technology, AI and operational resilience in fund servicing

### Module 10: Engaging with the Supervisor

Brings the programme together around supervisory engagement: applications, ongoing reporting, thematic reviews and inspections, closing with a compliance action plan.

**Objectives**

- Prepare effective fund and manager applications and notifications
- Respond well to supervisory reviews, information requests and inspections
- Produce a fund governance and compliance action plan

**Syllabus**

- Applications, notifications and regulatory reporting
- Thematic reviews, inspections and common supervisory findings
- Breach reporting, remediation and investor redress
- Workshop: seeing the fund range the way a supervisor does

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, group exercises and practical checklists, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's fund range and role.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of the investment funds sector is helpful. No prior knowledge of specific fund rules is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Recommendations for Liquidity Risk Management for Collective Investment Schemes (2018)
- IOSCO, Principles for the Valuation of Collective Investment Schemes (2013)
- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
- IOSCO, Examination of Governance for Collective Investment Schemes (2006–2007)
