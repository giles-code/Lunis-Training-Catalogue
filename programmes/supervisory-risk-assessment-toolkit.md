## Aim and Purpose

To equip supervisors to carry out a structured, evidence-based risk assessment of a regulated firm and turn it into a defensible risk rating and supervisory plan. Participants leave able to analyse a firm's business model, finances, governance and controls, test what they find through interviews and panel challenge, and write findings that drive action.

## What It Covers

Risk-based supervisory frameworks, impact and probability, prioritisation and supervisory intensity, business model and strategy analysis, financial analysis, governance and controls assessment, supervisory interviews, thematic reviews, risk governance panels, risk ratings and supervisory plans, and writing findings and following up.

## Intended Audience

- Line supervisors and supervisory analysts responsible for assessing individual firms
- Senior supervisors and team leaders who review assessments, chair or sit on panels and sign off risk ratings
- Risk, methodology and quality assurance staff who design or calibrate the authority's supervisory framework

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how a risk-based supervisory framework rates firms by impact and probability and sets supervisory intensity
- Analyse a firm's business model, strategy and financial position to identify key vulnerabilities
- Assess governance, risk management and internal controls against clear supervisory expectations
- Apply structured interviewing techniques to test management's account and gather reliable evidence
- Challenge and calibrate risk assessments through thematic reviews and risk governance panels
- Translate assessments into risk ratings, supervisory plans and clearly written findings that are followed through

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines short input with case studies, group exercises and practical tools, building to an end-to-end assessment workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's own framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervising regulated firms is helpful. No specialist financial or analytical background is required.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee, Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision (2024)
- IMF, Good Supervision: Lessons from the Field (Working Paper WP/23/181, 2023)
- IMF, The Making of Good Supervision: Learning to Say "No" (Staff Position Note SPN/10/08, 2010)
- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
