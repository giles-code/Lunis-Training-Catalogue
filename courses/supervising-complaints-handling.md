## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' ability to supervise firms' complaints-handling frameworks effectively, including assessing root-cause analysis practices and the operation of redress schemes, so that complaints data is used as a genuine early-warning indicator of conduct risk.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level supervisors assessing consumer protection outcomes.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain regulatory expectations for complaints-handling processes
- Assess the adequacy of a firm's complaints-handling framework
- Evaluate root-cause analysis practices
- Describe how redress schemes operate and their supervisory relevance
- Use complaints data as an indicator of wider conduct risk
- Formulate supervisory conclusions on complaints-handling adequacy

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Complaints-handling fundamentals (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain regulatory expectations for complaints handling*
- What good complaints handling looks like
- Timeliness, fairness, and communication requirements
- The complaints-handling process end to end
- Complaints as a source of supervisory intelligence
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: what makes a complaints process trustworthy?

**Session 2: Assessing frameworks and root-cause analysis (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess complaints frameworks and root-cause analysis practice*
- Reviewing a firm's complaints-handling policy and governance
- Root-cause analysis: from individual complaints to systemic issues
- Using root-cause findings to drive remediation
- Common weaknesses in root-cause practice
Activities:
- Group exercise: review sample complaints data and identify a root cause

**Session 3: Redress schemes and supervisory application (13:30–15:00)**
*Objective: Understand redress schemes and apply complaints data supervisorily*
- How statutory and voluntary redress schemes operate
- Supervisory relevance of redress scheme referrals and outcomes
- Using complaints data as an early-warning indicator
- Escalation and intervention where handling is inadequate
Activities:
- Case study: assess a firm's complaints-handling adequacy and form supervisory conclusions

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (15:00–15:15)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: framework assessment, root cause, redress, judgement
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Complaints-handling fundamentals (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Assessing frameworks and root-cause analysis (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Redress schemes and supervisory application (13:30–15:00)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (15:00–15:15)

**Key Takeaways**
- Complaints data is one of the richest, most under-used sources of supervisory intelligence
- Root-cause analysis turns individual complaints into systemic insight
- A firm's complaints-handling quality is a strong proxy for its wider conduct culture
