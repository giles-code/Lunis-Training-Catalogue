## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a focused, working understanding of MiCA and the EU rules around it, from the crypto-asset market and token regimes through to authorisation, ongoing supervision and enforcement. Participants leave able to read a CASP's business model and application critically, spot red flags early and apply MiCA's supervisory tools proportionately, working with ESMA, the EBA and other national competent authorities.

**Coverage:** The crypto-asset market and MiCA's architecture, asset-referenced and e-money tokens, CASP authorisation, governance, prudential, custody and ICT requirements, market abuse, AML/CFT and the Travel Rule, risk-based supervision, colleges and cross-border cooperation, and enforcement and crisis management.

## Intended Audience

- Authorisation, supervision and inspection staff new to crypto-asset service providers and token issuers
- AML/CFT, market integrity, payments and enforcement specialists who need a working grasp of MiCA
- Managers and policy staff who need an overview before committing teams to deeper training

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how crypto-asset markets work and how MiCA's scope, taxonomy and rulebook fit together
- Distinguish ARTs and EMTs and assess reserve, redemption and significance arrangements
- Apply MiCA's authorisation and ongoing requirements to CASP applications and supervision
- Evaluate market abuse, AML/CFT and Travel Rule controls at CASPs
- Operate risk-based supervision, passporting and cooperation with other authorities, ESMA and the EBA
- Apply MiCA's enforcement and crisis tools proportionately to a realistic case

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Crypto-Asset Market and MiCA's Architecture

How distributed ledgers, crypto-assets and market participants work, where control and risk really sit, and how MiCA's objectives, perimeter and Level 1 to Level 3 rulebook are put together.

**Objectives**

- Explain how distributed ledgers, tokens and crypto market participants operate
- Apply MiCA's taxonomy and perimeter, including the boundary with financial instruments
- Identify the roles of the Commission, ESMA, the EBA and national competent authorities

**Syllabus**

- Ledgers, wallets, smart contracts and the real control points
- Issuers, trading platforms, brokers, custodians and market makers
- ARTs, EMTs and other crypto-assets; the MiFID boundary, NFTs and DeFi
- Level 1 to Level 3: technical standards, guidelines and Q&As

### Module 2: Asset-Referenced and E-Money Tokens

How ARTs and EMTs are structured and regulated under MiCA, the reserve and redemption mechanics that determine stability, and the supervisory arrangements for significant tokens.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish ART and EMT designs and their risk profiles
- Evaluate reserve quality, custody and redemption resilience under stress
- Explain significance classification and the EBA's supervisory role

**Syllabus**

- Issuer authorisation, white papers and lessons from stablecoin failures
- Reserve composition, custody, audit and redemption at par
- EMTs, electronic money and the interplay with PSD2
- Significant tokens: EBA supervision, colleges and recovery and redemption plans

### Module 3: CASP Authorisation and Ongoing Requirements

Running a proportionate authorisation gateway for CASPs and applying MiCA's ongoing requirements on governance, prudential safeguards, custody, conduct and ICT resilience under DORA.

**Objectives**

- Assess a CASP application dossier, business model, substance and group dependencies
- Evaluate governance, prudential, custody and conduct arrangements against MiCA
- Recognise ICT and key management risks and how DORA applies to CASPs

**Syllabus**

- Crypto-asset services, business models and the authorisation dossier
- Management body, conflicts, own funds and complaints handling
- Custody, segregation and safeguarding of client crypto-assets
- Red flags: letter-box entities, founder dominance and affiliated sub-custody

### Module 4: Market Abuse, AML/CFT and the Travel Rule

MiCA's market abuse regime and crypto-native manipulation, illicit finance typologies in crypto markets, and the AML/CFT and Travel Rule obligations that supervisors test at CASPs.

**Objectives**

- Explain MiCA's prohibitions on insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and manipulation
- Assess CASP surveillance, suspicious order reporting and AML/CFT controls
- Evaluate Travel Rule compliance, including transfers involving self-hosted wallets

**Syllabus**

- Wash trading, pump-and-dump schemes, spoofing and MEV
- Laundering typologies: mixers, mules, rapid layering and sanctions evasion
- The Transfer of Funds Regulation and the EU AML package for CASPs
- Blockchain analytics and coordination with the national FIU

### Module 5: Risk-Based Supervision, Colleges and Cross-Border Cooperation

Designing a risk-based supervisory approach to CASPs, monitoring and inspecting firms between engagements, and managing passporting, home–host responsibilities and colleges with other authorities.

**Objectives**

- Prioritise CASPs by impact and probability of harm and set escalation triggers
- Plan and conduct inspections that test practice rather than documentation
- Manage home and host responsibilities and information exchange across borders

**Syllabus**

- Firm risk assessment, supervisory planning and early warning signals
- Inspection methods: data requests, interviews, walkthroughs and customer journeys
- Passport notifications, reverse solicitation and host authority powers
- Colleges, ESMA convergence tools and peer reviews

### Module 6: Enforcement, Crisis Management and Case-Study Workshop

Using MiCA's graduated supervisory and enforcement measures, managing a CASP in distress through to orderly wind-down, and applying the whole programme to a realistic case.

**Objectives**

- Select proportionate supervisory measures, sanctions and public warnings
- Manage a CASP crisis from early distress to orderly wind-down
- Apply the programme to a realistic CASP supervision case

**Syllabus**

- From supervisory measures to fines and withdrawal of authorisation
- Lessons from crypto exchange and crypto lender failures
- Return of client assets and cross-border coordination in a failure
- Workshop: supervising a troubled cross-border CASP

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a three-day course or as six half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with case studies and red-flag exercises, closing with a case-study workshop, and modules can be tailored to the authority's needs; the full fifteen-module programme is available for deeper capability building.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of authorising or supervising financial firms is helpful. No technical background in blockchain is required.

## Core Reading

- Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 on markets in crypto-assets (MiCA)
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets (Transfer of Funds Regulation)
- FSB, High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Crypto-Asset Activities and Markets (2023)
- FATF, Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers (2021)
