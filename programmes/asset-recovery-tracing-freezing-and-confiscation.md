## Aim and Purpose

To give practitioners a complete view of the asset recovery cycle, from identifying and tracing assets to freezing, confiscation, management and final disposal or return. Participants leave able to choose the right legal route, act early to prevent dissipation, manage assets to preserve value and pursue assets held abroad or as crypto-assets.

**Coverage:** The asset recovery cycle and international standards, conviction-based and non-conviction-based confiscation, unexplained wealth and civil routes, identifying and tracing assets, freezing and restraint, asset management and realisation, international requests and cooperation, crypto-assets, and disposal, victim compensation and the return of assets.

## Intended Audience

- Asset recovery officers, financial investigators and asset recovery office staff
- Prosecutors and lawyers handling restraint, confiscation and civil recovery proceedings
- Asset management office staff and customs, tax and FIU officers involved in recovering criminal proceeds

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the asset recovery cycle and the international standards that frame it
- Select the most effective legal route, whether conviction-based or non-conviction-based
- Trace and value assets held directly, through third parties and in corporate vehicles
- Apply freezing and restraint early and proportionately to prevent dissipation
- Manage restrained assets to preserve value and control cost
- Pursue assets abroad and in crypto-assets, and plan for disposal, compensation and return

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Asset Recovery: Cycle, Standards and Strategy

Why recovering criminal assets matters, the stages of the asset recovery cycle, and the international standards and conventions that set expectations for national regimes.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purposes and stages of asset recovery
- Summarise the FATF standards and the relevant international conventions
- Set an asset recovery strategy at the start of a case

**Syllabus**

- The cycle: identification, tracing, freezing, confiscation, management and disposal
- FATF Recommendations 4 and 38 and Immediate Outcome 8
- International conventions against corruption and organised crime
- Asset recovery strategies, priorities and institutional models

### Module 2: Legal Routes: Conviction-Based and Non-Conviction-Based

The main legal routes to recover criminal assets, including object- and value-based confiscation, extended confiscation, non-conviction-based forfeiture and unexplained wealth tools, and how to choose between them.

**Objectives**

- Compare conviction-based, non-conviction-based and civil recovery routes
- Explain object-based, value-based and extended confiscation
- Choose the most effective route for the facts and evidence

**Syllabus**

- Object-based and value-based confiscation and benefit calculation
- Extended confiscation and shifting the burden of proof
- Non-conviction-based forfeiture, civil recovery and unexplained wealth tools
- Safeguards: proportionality, third-party rights and fair trial

### Module 3: Identifying and Tracing Assets

How practitioners locate and trace assets held directly, through family members and associates, in corporate vehicles or abroad, and build an asset profile that supports restraint.

**Objectives**

- Build an asset profile from registers, records and intelligence
- Trace assets through third parties, structures and jurisdictions
- Value assets and identify competing interests

**Syllabus**

- Asset profiling: property, accounts, vehicles, businesses and high-value goods
- Tracing through nominees, associates and corporate vehicles
- Asset recovery office networks and international enquiries
- Valuation, encumbrances and third-party claims

### Module 4: Freezing, Seizure and Restraint

How and when to secure assets before they are dissipated, the evidential thresholds for restraint, and how to draft and manage orders that are proportionate and enforceable.

**Objectives**

- Explain the thresholds and procedure for freezing, seizure and restraint
- Draft proportionate, enforceable restraint applications
- Manage variations, living expenses and third-party challenges

**Syllabus**

- Timing: risk of dissipation and acting early
- Administrative freezes, seizure and court restraint orders
- Drafting applications and supporting statements
- Variations, legal expenses, third parties and discharge

### Module 5: Asset Management and Realisation

How restrained and confiscated assets are managed to preserve value, the role of asset management offices and receivers, and the options for interim sale and final realisation.

**Objectives**

- Plan before seizure for the management of complex assets
- Assess options for preserving value, including interim sale
- Oversee receivers, custodians and asset management offices

**Syllabus**

- Pre-seizure planning: businesses, property, vehicles and perishable assets
- Asset management offices, receivers and custodians
- Interim sale, valuation and cost control
- Realisation, enforcement of orders and dealing with default

### Module 6: International Requests and Cooperation

How to identify, freeze and recover assets held abroad through informal cooperation, mutual legal assistance and recognition of foreign orders, and how to make requests that succeed.

**Objectives**

- Use informal networks before making formal requests
- Draft mutual legal assistance requests for tracing, freezing and confiscation
- Explain the recognition and enforcement of foreign orders

**Syllabus**

- Informal cooperation: asset recovery networks, FIUs and liaison officers
- Mutual legal assistance requests: content, evidence and timing
- Recognition of foreign freezing and confiscation orders
- Common obstacles and how to overcome them

### Module 7: Crypto-Assets and Other Emerging Assets

How to identify, seize, hold and realise crypto-assets and other emerging asset types, working with service providers and maintaining secure custody and continuity.

**Objectives**

- Identify indicators of crypto-asset holdings during an investigation
- Explain secure seizure, custody and realisation of crypto-assets
- Work with service providers to freeze and recover funds

**Syllabus**

- Indicators of crypto-asset holdings: devices, records and statements
- Seizure approaches: private keys, service providers and custody solutions
- Valuation volatility, conversion and realisation
- Other emerging assets: tokenised assets and online accounts

### Module 8: Disposal, Return and Recovery Strategy: Workshop

Brings the programme together by examining victim compensation, return of assets across borders and social reuse, ending with a workshop in which teams build a recovery strategy for a complex case.

**Objectives**

- Explain the options for compensation, return and disposal of recovered assets
- Apply principles of transparent and accountable asset return
- Design an end-to-end recovery strategy for a realistic case

**Syllabus**

- Victim compensation and restitution
- Principles for the transparent and accountable return of assets
- Asset sharing, social reuse and funding models
- Workshop: a recovery strategy from tracing to return

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with worked cases and legal-route exercises, ending in a recovery strategy workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the national legal framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of financial investigation, prosecution or asset recovery work is helpful. No legal qualification is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012), Best Practices on Confiscation (Recommendations 4 and 38) and a Framework for Ongoing Work on Asset Recovery
- UNODC (2012), Manual on International Cooperation for the Purposes of Confiscation of Proceeds of Crime
