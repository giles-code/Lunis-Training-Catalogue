## Aim and Purpose

To give directors a practical understanding of the dynamics that decide whether a board challenges management, weighs real alternatives and acts when things go wrong. Participants leave able to improve their agenda, papers and meetings, test the quality of board decisions and see their board the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** How boards work in practice, the role of the chair, agenda and time management, board papers and information quality, information asymmetry and independent assurance, decision quality and looking back, culture, challenge and psychological safety, group think and bias, technical topics and AI in the boardroom, and board evaluation, succession and skills.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, non-executive and independent directors and board committee members
- Executive directors and senior managers who present to or sit on the board
- Company secretaries and heads of governance responsible for board processes and evaluation

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain why sound governance structures do not guarantee effective board behaviour
- Evaluate the agenda, board papers and information flows against good practice
- Assess the quality of board decisions by their process, not just their outcome
- Challenge management effectively while respecting the line between oversight and management
- Identify group think, bias and unhelpful dynamics, and apply practical counter-measures
- Design a board effectiveness action plan covering evaluation, succession and skills

## Syllabus

### Module 1: How Boards Really Work

Why a board can have the right composition, committees and terms of reference and still fail to oversee the firm, and what the board's dynamics reveal about its effectiveness.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish governance structures from the behaviours that make them work
- Explain the board's role in strategy, risk appetite and holding management to account
- Identify the common impediments to board effectiveness

**Syllabus**

- Form versus substance: what a structural governance review misses
- The board's core tasks: direction, oversight and accountability
- Common impediments: poor information, weak challenge and ineffective chairing
- Seeing your board the way a supervisor does

### Module 2: The Chair: Setting the Tone

The chair's pivotal role in creating the conditions for effective challenge and decision-making, and the behaviours that separate an effective chair from an ineffective one.

**Objectives**

- Explain the chair's responsibilities for board conduct and performance
- Assess chairing behaviours that encourage or suppress contribution
- Identify how the chair's relationship with the chief executive shapes the board

**Syllabus**

- The chair's role: agenda, discussion, conclusion and board performance
- Attributes of an effective chair: authority with humility, patience with incisiveness
- Drawing out quieter voices and bringing circular debate to a close
- Chair and chief executive: partnership without capture

### Module 3: Agenda, Time and Priorities

How expanding board responsibilities collide with fixed meeting time, and how to build an agenda that gives the most important matters the attention they need.

**Objectives**

- Recognise the signs of agenda overload and its effect on scrutiny
- Apply practical rules for prioritising and sequencing board business
- Evaluate the trade-offs in longer, more frequent or larger board meetings

**Syllabus**

- Agenda overload: routine reporting crowding out strategy
- Sequencing, timing and protecting time for unscheduled discussion
- Consent agendas, committees and deciding what not to discuss
- Directors' time commitment and the risk of over-boarding

### Module 4: Board Papers and Information Quality

What good board information looks like, why more information can mean worse oversight, and how to reshape board packs so that directors can see the issues that matter.

**Objectives**

- Define the qualities of good board information
- Evaluate board papers for a clear ask, real options and a balanced view of risk
- Specify improvements to board packs, presentations and dashboards

**Syllabus**

- Good information: accurate, timely, focused, balanced and comparable
- Information overload, attention and reliance on mental shortcuts
- A good paper: the decision required, options, risks and prior challenge
- Presentations, executive summaries and conducting an information audit

### Module 5: Information Asymmetry and Independent Assurance

How directors can oversee a business when management always knows more, by understanding how information is produced and drawing on independent sources of assurance.

**Objectives**

- Explain the causes and consequences of information asymmetry in the boardroom
- Assess the reliance placed on management's own account
- Use the control functions, auditors and outside experts to verify what the board is told

**Syllabus**

- Why the board always knows less, and what independent directors add
- Trusting and verifying: healthy scepticism without hostility
- Sources of assurance: risk, compliance, internal and external audit, outside advisers
- Side conversations, pre-meetings and the risk of ratification

### Module 6: Quality of Decisions and Looking Back

A practical framework for judging how a decision was made rather than how it turned out, and how boards learn by revisiting major decisions and feeding lessons into future practice.

**Objectives**

- Apply a structured test to the process behind significant board decisions
- Evaluate whether minutes record the rationale for a decision, not just the outcome
- Design a routine for revisiting major decisions and learning from them

**Syllabus**

- Why, why now, alternatives, assumptions and downside
- Success measures and how the board will judge the decision later
- Recording rationale and dissent in the minutes
- Post-decision reviews and acting on repeat findings

### Module 7: Culture, Challenge and Psychological Safety

How boardroom culture shapes the quality of discussion, why effective challenge depends on psychological safety, and how to tell constructive challenge from confrontation.

**Objectives**

- Explain how unwritten norms and relationships shape board discussion
- Distinguish effective challenge from confrontation and from avoidance
- Identify the will, skill and knowledge each director needs to challenge well

**Syllabus**

- Boardroom culture: norms, assumptions and the default to collegiality
- Psychological safety and the signs of its absence
- Effective challenge: evidence-based, focused and persistent
- Oversight without micromanagement: where the board's role ends

### Module 8: Group Think, Bias and Board Personalities

The cognitive and social traps that undermine board decisions, the board personalities that distort discussion, and practical counter-measures the chair and directors can use.

**Objectives**

- Recognise the warning signs of group think and common decision biases
- Identify difficult board behaviours and how the chair should respond
- Apply practical techniques to counter bias in board decisions

**Syllabus**

- Group think, framing, availability and motivated blindness
- Decision traps: time pressure, short horizons and isolation
- Board personalities: the dominator, the silent director, the absentee and the box-ticker
- Counter-measures: devil's advocate, anonymous polling and options before advocacy

### Module 9: Technical Topics and AI in the Boardroom

How directors without specialist backgrounds can oversee cyber, technology and other complex risks, and what the use of AI by management and the board means for oversight.

**Objectives**

- Assess whether the board has enough knowledge to test technical statements and assumptions
- Challenge technical presentations that inform without enabling scrutiny
- Evaluate the benefits and risks of AI tools in board preparation and decision-making

**Syllabus**

- Cyber, technology and other complex topics: what directors need to understand
- Getting the most from technical presentations and specialist advisers
- AI-assisted board packs, analysis and preparation: benefits and limits
- Accountability for decisions does not move to the tool

### Module 10: Keeping the Board Effective

Brings the programme together into a board effectiveness action plan covering evaluation, succession, skills, induction and continuing development.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate the design of board effectiveness reviews and the follow-through on findings
- Use a skills matrix to identify gaps and inform succession and development
- Produce a board effectiveness action plan

**Syllabus**

- Board evaluation: internal and external reviews, and tracking actions to completion
- Succession planning for the chair, independent directors and key executives
- Skills matrices, induction and tailored development
- Workshop: the board's twelve-month effectiveness action plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two- to three-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with scenarios, exercises and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the board's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board or senior management experience in financial services is helpful. No prior governance training is required.
