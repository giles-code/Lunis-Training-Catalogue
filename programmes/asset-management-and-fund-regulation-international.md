## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and practitioners a clear map of how funds, their managers and their distribution are regulated around the world, anchored in IOSCO principles and FSB recommendations. Participants leave able to determine which type of regime applies to a given structure, test arrangements for delegation, custody, liquidity, leverage and valuation, and anticipate how authorities are likely to approach them.

## What It Covers

The asset management value chain and regulatory perimeter, the international standard-setting architecture, retail, professional and alternative fund regimes, manager authorisation, delegation and substance, distribution and investor disclosure, depositaries and custody, liquidity management tools, leverage and valuation, cross-border passporting and mutual recognition, sustainability disclosure and greenwashing, and operational resilience and emerging fund models.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, authorisation and policy staff at securities and markets authorities covering funds and asset managers
- Compliance, legal, risk and product staff at fund managers, investment managers, distributors and fund platforms
- Directors, depositary, custody and fund administration staff who need a working map of fund regulation across jurisdictions

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how fund regulation is organised internationally and determine which type of regime applies across the value chain
- Distinguish product-regulated retail funds from manager-regulated professional and alternative fund regimes
- Assess manager authorisation, delegation, substance and depositary arrangements against international standards
- Evaluate liquidity management tools, leverage and valuation and what can go wrong
- Apply the principles of fair distribution, investor disclosure, cross-border recognition and sustainability claims
- Identify how operational resilience, supervisory convergence and new fund models are reshaping the sector

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a technical briefing with comparative case studies and break-out exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisors, managers or distributors.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of asset management, funds or their supervision is helpful. No legal background or knowledge of any particular jurisdiction's rules is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
- IOSCO, Recommendations on Sustainability-Related Practices, Policies, Procedures and Disclosure in Asset Management (2021)
- IOSCO, Thematic Analysis: Emerging Risks in Private Finance (2023)
