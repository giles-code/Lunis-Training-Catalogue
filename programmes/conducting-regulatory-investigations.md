## Aim and Purpose

To give investigators and case teams a practical, step-by-step method for conducting regulatory investigations that are focused, fair and capable of withstanding challenge. Participants leave able to scope and plan a case, gather and review evidence, conduct compelled interviews, prepare findings and warning notices, and feed lessons back into supervision.

**Coverage:** Case selection and referral, scoping and investigation planning, information-gathering powers, evidence handling and document review, interviews under compulsion, findings and warning notices, settlement and decision-making, and lessons for supervision.

## Intended Audience

- Enforcement investigators, case managers and lawyers
- Supervisory staff seconded to or working alongside enforcement teams
- Staff moving into enforcement from supervision, authorisation or other agencies

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Apply case selection criteria and assess referrals consistently
- Scope and plan an investigation with clear hypotheses, milestones and resources
- Use information-gathering powers lawfully, proportionately and effectively
- Handle, review and analyse evidence to an evidential standard
- Plan and conduct compelled interviews that are fair and productive
- Prepare robust findings, manage settlement and feed lessons back into supervision

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Case Selection and Referral

How cases reach enforcement, how referrals are assessed and how the authority decides which matters merit a formal investigation.

**Objectives**

- Assess a referral against the authority's case selection criteria
- Distinguish matters for investigation from supervisory or alternative outcomes
- Record the rationale for opening or declining a case

**Syllabus**

- Sources of cases: supervision, whistleblowers, complaints and market surveillance
- Case selection criteria: seriousness, harm, deterrence and resources
- Alternatives to investigation and early resolution
- Documenting the referral decision

### Module 2: Scoping and Planning the Investigation

How to define the scope of an investigation, frame hypotheses and build a plan that keeps the case focused, proportionate and on time.

**Objectives**

- Define scope, subjects and potential breaches
- Build an investigation plan with hypotheses, milestones and resources
- Manage scope creep, risks and stakeholder expectations

**Syllabus**

- Scoping documents and appointment of investigators
- Hypotheses, elements of the breach and evidence matrices
- Resourcing, timetables and case governance
- Notifying subjects and managing parallel proceedings

### Module 3: Information-Gathering Powers

The powers available to the authority to require information, documents and attendance, and how to use them lawfully and effectively.

**Objectives**

- Identify the information-gathering powers available and their limits
- Draft clear and proportionate information requirements
- Respond to privilege claims, non-compliance and delay

**Syllabus**

- Requirements for information, documents and attendance
- Drafting notices: scope, deadlines and format
- Legal professional privilege, self-incrimination and confidentiality
- On-site inspections, third parties and requests to other authorities

### Module 4: Evidence Handling and Document Review

How to collect, preserve and review large volumes of documents and data so that evidence is reliable, admissible and well organised.

**Objectives**

- Apply sound practice in evidence collection, continuity and storage
- Plan and manage document review, including electronic review tools
- Analyse evidence and build a chronology against the elements of the breach

**Syllabus**

- Chain of custody, forensic copies and data integrity
- Electronic disclosure, search terms and review workflows
- Chronologies, evidence matrices and gap analysis
- Disclosure obligations and unused material

### Module 5: Interviews under Compulsion

How to plan and conduct compelled interviews with firm staff and subjects, balancing effective questioning with the rights of the interviewee.

**Objectives**

- Plan a compelled interview strategy and question outline
- Conduct interviews that are fair, structured and evidentially sound
- Handle legal representatives, privilege objections and evasive answers

**Syllabus**

- Compelled and voluntary interviews: rights, cautions and use of answers
- Interview planning, document bundles and questioning structure
- Managing lawyers, objections and difficult interviewees
- Role-play: interviewing a senior manager under compulsion

### Module 6: Findings, Reports and Warning Notices

How to analyse the evidence against the alleged breaches, draft investigation reports and warning notices, and test the case before it goes to decision.

**Objectives**

- Assess whether the evidence establishes each element of the breach
- Draft clear, balanced investigation findings and warning notices
- Test the case for weaknesses and procedural risk

**Syllabus**

- Analysing evidence against the standard of proof
- Structuring the investigation report and proposed findings
- Warning notices, representations and access to material
- Internal challenge, legal review and case conferences

### Module 7: Settlement and Decision-Making

How cases are resolved by settlement or contested decision, how penalties are determined and how fairness and consistency are maintained.

**Objectives**

- Explain the separation of investigation and decision-making
- Apply penalty frameworks and settlement principles consistently
- Manage negotiations and communicate final outcomes

**Syllabus**

- Independent decision-makers and procedural fairness
- Penalty frameworks: seriousness, aggravation, mitigation and discounts
- Settlement negotiations, agreed facts and admissions
- Final notices, publicity and appeals

### Module 8: Lessons for Supervision and Case Workshop

How enforcement outcomes improve supervision and deterrence, followed by a mock case conference in which participants take a case from referral to recommended outcome.

**Objectives**

- Identify lessons from cases for supervisory practice and policy
- Communicate outcomes to promote credible deterrence
- Apply the full investigation method to a realistic case

**Syllabus**

- Feeding findings back to supervision, authorisation and policy
- Credible deterrence and outcome publicity
- Post-case reviews and continuous improvement
- Workshop: mock case conference from referral to recommended outcome

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. The modules follow the life of a single case, combining short briefings with a running investigation exercise, interview role-play and a mock case conference, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's powers and procedures.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of enforcement, supervision or legal casework is helpful. No prior investigative training is required.

## Core Reading
