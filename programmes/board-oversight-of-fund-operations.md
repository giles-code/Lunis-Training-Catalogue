## Aim and Purpose

To give fund directors a clear, practical view of how a fund's operations actually run, where they break and which service provider is responsible at each step. Participants leave able to read operational reporting critically, challenge service providers and management, and evidence effective oversight to supervisors and auditors.

**Coverage:** The service provider map, fund administration and NAV production, transfer agency and investor services including AML, valuation, financial statements and audit, depositary and custody oversight, portfolio management oversight, distribution and product oversight, operational risk, resilience, technology and AI, and errors, breaches and service provider reviews.

## Intended Audience

- Independent and non-executive directors of investment funds and fund management companies
- Executive directors, designated persons and senior managers responsible for operations and delegate oversight
- Company secretaries, governance leads and service provider relationship managers supporting fund boards

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain who does what across the fund's operating model and where accountability sits
- Identify the key process flows, break points and risk areas in administration, transfer agency and custody
- Challenge valuation, financial reporting and audit outcomes with confidence
- Oversee portfolio management, distribution and product governance without straying into management
- Evaluate operational risk, resilience, technology and AI arrangements across the service provider chain
- Apply a structured approach to errors, breaches and periodic service provider reviews

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Who Does What: The Fund Service Provider Map

An end-to-end map of a fund's operations, the parties that perform each activity and the contractual and regulatory lines of accountability back to the board.

**Objectives**

- Map the fund's key operations to the service providers that perform them
- Distinguish delegation, outsourcing and sub-delegation and their implications for the board
- Identify where operational accountability ultimately rests

**Syllabus**

- The fund operating chain: manager, administrator, transfer agent, depositary and distributors
- Delegation, outsourcing and sub-delegation chains
- Service agreements, service level schedules and key performance indicators
- A board question set for understanding the operating model

### Module 2: Fund Administration and NAV Production

How a net asset value is produced each dealing day, the process flow from trade capture to publication, and the break points and controls the board should understand.

**Objectives**

- Explain the fund accounting and NAV production process step by step
- Identify the key risk areas and controls in the NAV cycle
- Interpret administrator reporting and controls reports

**Syllabus**

- Trade capture, reconciliations, pricing, accruals and fee calculations
- NAV review, sign-off and publication
- Common break points: stale prices, corporate actions and late trades
- Controls reports and what they do not cover

### Module 3: Transfer Agency, Investor Services and AML

The operational mechanics of investor dealing and the register, the anti-money laundering obligations attached to them, and how directors oversee investor-facing operations.

**Objectives**

- Explain subscription, redemption and register maintenance processes
- Assess investor due diligence, screening and suspicious activity controls
- Oversee investor service quality, complaints and dormant accounts

**Syllabus**

- Dealing cycles, cut-off times, late trading and market timing controls
- Investor due diligence, sanctions screening and suspicious activity reporting
- Reliance on distributors and intermediaries for investor identification
- Complaints, unclaimed proceeds and investor communications

### Module 4: Valuation Oversight in Practice

The difference between calculating a NAV and valuing the fund's assets, how the valuation operating model works, and how boards gain assurance on hard-to-value positions.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish NAV calculation from the valuation of underlying assets
- Evaluate the valuation operating model and the choice of calculation agent
- Challenge fair value judgements and price overrides

**Syllabus**

- Valuation policy, pricing hierarchy and independent price verification
- Selecting and overseeing the calculation agent and pricing vendors
- Fair value accounting and level three assets
- Price challenges, overrides and valuation committee reporting

### Module 5: Financial Statements and the External Audit

How to read a fund's financial statements, what the external audit does and does not provide, and the board's role in the audit cycle.

**Objectives**

- Interpret the key components of fund financial statements
- Explain the audit process, audit opinions and management letters
- Oversee the auditor's appointment, independence and findings

**Syllabus**

- Statement of net assets, income statement, portfolio and notes
- The audit cycle: planning, fieldwork, findings and sign-off
- Modified opinions, emphasis of matter and management letter points
- Auditor independence, rotation and non-audit services

### Module 6: Depositary and Custody Oversight

The depositary's safekeeping, cash monitoring and oversight duties, the custody and prime broker models behind them, and how the board builds an effective relationship with the depositary.

**Objectives**

- Explain how custody risk varies by asset type, location and use of assets
- Assess custody, sub-custody and prime broker models and their insolvency risks
- Use depositary reporting to strengthen board oversight

**Syllabus**

- Safekeeping, record-keeping, cash flow monitoring and oversight duties
- Custody chains, prime brokers, rehypothecation and insolvency risk
- Cash, financial instruments and other assets: differing risk profiles
- Depositary reports, escalations and liability for loss

### Module 7: Portfolio Management Oversight

How the board oversees the investment process and compliance with investment restrictions without making investment decisions, using internal limits and escalation triggers.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate the key components of the portfolio management function
- Set internal risk limits and escalation triggers ahead of prospectus limits
- Interpret performance, risk and investment compliance reporting

**Syllabus**

- Investment process, mandate and risk budget
- Pre-trade and post-trade compliance monitoring
- Internal limits that trigger before a prospectus breach
- Performance attribution, style drift and peer comparison

### Module 8: Distribution and Product Oversight

The distribution life cycle from the investor's and the product's perspective, and how the board oversees who is buying the fund, what they are buying and how it is sold.

**Objectives**

- Explain the board's role in distribution strategy and distributor oversight
- Apply product governance questions before launch, during the fund's life and at closure
- Assess distribution data, marketing materials and value for investors

**Syllabus**

- Distribution models, platforms and bipartite versus tripartite agreements
- Target market, stress testing and product reviews
- Distribution data: who is buying, through which channel and why
- Marketing communications and value for investors

### Module 9: Operational Risk, Resilience, Technology and AI

How operational risk is identified and managed across the service provider chain, how operational and digital resilience is tested, and how boards oversee new technology including AI.

**Objectives**

- Describe an effective operational risk framework for the fund and its delegates
- Evaluate cyber, third-party and supply chain resilience
- Appraise the opportunities and threats of technology and AI in fund operations

**Syllabus**

- Risk and control self-assessment, risk registers and operational risk appetite
- Operational and digital resilience: important services, impact tolerances and testing
- Supply chain security, third-party concentration and exit plans
- Transformation programmes and AI as both asset and threat

### Module 10: Errors, Breaches and Service Provider Reviews

Brings the programme together around error and breach management, investor compensation and the periodic review of service providers, closing with a board oversight plan.

**Objectives**

- Apply materiality thresholds and correction policies to NAV errors and breaches
- Oversee root cause analysis, investor compensation and regulatory notification
- Design a risk-based programme of service provider reviews

**Syllabus**

- NAV errors, investment breaches and materiality thresholds
- Root cause analysis, compensation and notification to the authority
- Due diligence visits, scorecards and annual service reviews
- Workshop: the board's operational oversight plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two- to three-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with process maps, case studies and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the fund range and service model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience as a fund director or in fund management or servicing is helpful. No operational or accounting specialism is required.
