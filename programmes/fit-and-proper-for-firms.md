## Aim and Purpose

To give the people who run a firm's appointments process a complete, operational method for assessing fitness and propriety, taught by former regulators who show how the authority tests the firm's work. Participants leave able to run defensible due diligence, submit applications that succeed first time, prepare candidates for interview and manage fitness on an ongoing basis.

**Coverage:** The fit and proper standard and the firm's obligations, role design and succession, integrity and reputation checks, competence, capability and capacity, controllers and change in control, applications to the authority, preparing candidates for regulatory interviews, ongoing fitness and annual certification, and handling adverse information.

## Intended Audience

- Heads of compliance, company secretaries and governance teams who manage approved and certified roles
- HR, talent and nominations committee support staff responsible for senior appointments and references
- Senior managers and chairs who sponsor appointments and sign off fitness assessments

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the components of the fit and proper standard and the firm's primary responsibility for meeting it
- Design role profiles and succession plans that define what fitness means for each key function
- Apply proportionate due diligence on integrity, reputation, competence and capacity, and document the judgement
- Prepare complete and consistent applications to the authority, including for owners and controllers
- Prepare candidates for regulatory interviews so they demonstrate understanding rather than recitation
- Manage ongoing fitness, annual certification and adverse information fairly and in line with notification duties

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Fit and Proper Standard: What the Firm Must Show

The components of the fit and proper test, which roles fall within it, and why the authority expects the firm, not the regulator, to be the first and most rigorous assessor.

**Objectives**

- Explain honesty, integrity, competence, capability and financial soundness as applied by authorities
- Identify the roles that require pre-approval, certification or notification
- Explain the firm's primary responsibility and the consequences of weak assessments

**Syllabus**

- Components of the test and international standards from IOSCO, the Basel Committee and FATF
- Pre-approved, certified and notified roles under accountability regimes
- Proportionality by firm size, role and business model
- Regulatory findings and enforcement cases on poor appointment practice

### Module 2: Role Design, Role Profiles and Succession

How to define each key function so fitness can be assessed against it, how individual roles combine into a collectively suitable board and management team, and how to plan succession.

**Objectives**

- Design role profiles that state responsibilities, competencies and time commitment
- Assess collective suitability across the board and senior team
- Plan succession and interim cover for key functions

**Syllabus**

- Role profiles, statements of responsibilities and responsibilities maps
- Competency frameworks for risk, compliance, MLRO, finance and executive roles
- Skills matrices, independence and collective suitability
- Succession pipelines, interim appointments and handovers

### Module 3: Due Diligence: Honesty, Integrity and Reputation

The checks a firm should complete before appointment, how to obtain and use regulatory references, and how to weigh past conduct, disclosures and non-disclosure.

**Objectives**

- Design a proportionate pre-appointment checking process
- Obtain and interpret regulatory references and previous employer information
- Weigh past conduct by seriousness, recency, pattern and rehabilitation

**Syllabus**

- Criminal, regulatory, civil, insolvency and directorship checks
- Regulatory references, open-source research and media checks
- Weighing past conduct and non-financial misconduct
- Non-disclosure as a fitness issue in its own right

### Module 4: Competence, Capability and Capacity

How to test whether a candidate has the knowledge, experience and time the role requires, using evidence rather than assertion, and how to close gaps without lowering the standard.

**Objectives**

- Assess competence against the role profile using documentary and interview evidence
- Judge capacity, other commitments and conflicts of interest
- Design development plans and conditions where gaps exist

**Syllabus**

- Evidence of experience, qualifications and track record
- Structured competency interviews and scenario questions
- Time commitment, multiple directorships and conflicts of interest
- Closing gaps: induction, development plans and conditional appointments

### Module 5: Owners, Controllers and Change in Control

How the firm supports applications by proposed owners and controllers, maps its ownership chain, and evidences source of wealth and source of funds to the authority's satisfaction.

**Objectives**

- Map ownership chains and identify controllers and beneficial owners
- Prepare controllers for the authority's assessment of reputation and financial soundness
- Evidence source of wealth and source of funds credibly

**Syllabus**

- Control thresholds, acting in concert and indirect holdings
- Mapping ownership and beneficial ownership for the authority
- Source of wealth narratives and supporting documents
- Red flags the authority looks for: nominees, layered structures and secrecy

### Module 6: Applications to the Authority

How to prepare and manage fit and proper applications so they are complete, consistent and timely, and how to handle questions, delays and the risk of withdrawal or refusal.

**Objectives**

- Prepare applications that are complete, consistent and fully disclosed
- Manage timetables, interim arrangements and questions from the authority
- Recognise why applications stall, are withdrawn or are refused

**Syllabus**

- Application content, disclosures and consistency with the business plan
- Timing, interim arrangements and acting before approval
- Answering the authority's questions: evidence, tone and avoiding new issues
- Conditions, minded-to-refuse processes, withdrawals and representations

### Module 7: Preparing Candidates for Regulatory Interviews

How authorities plan and run competence interviews, what they are really testing, and how the firm prepares candidates through briefing and mock interviews without scripting them.

**Objectives**

- Explain how authority interviewers prepare and what they test by role
- Prepare candidates on the firm's risks, controls and their own accountabilities
- Run mock interviews that build genuine understanding rather than recitation

**Syllabus**

- The interview file: how authorities build questions from the application
- Role-specific scenarios for chief executive, chair, compliance, risk and MLRO
- Common failures: generic answers, deferring to others and over-rehearsal
- Role-play: mock interview of a proposed head of compliance

### Module 8: Ongoing Fitness and Annual Certification

How to keep fitness under review after appointment, run annual certification and assessments efficiently, and link fitness to performance, conduct breaches and remuneration.

**Objectives**

- Design an annual fitness and certification process with clear evidence
- Identify triggers for reassessment during the year
- Link conduct breaches, performance and training records to fitness

**Syllabus**

- Annual self-declarations, checks and certification sign-off
- Triggers for reassessment: role changes, incidents and new information
- Conduct rules breaches, training records and remuneration adjustments
- Record-keeping and evidencing assessments to the supervisor

### Module 9: Adverse Information, Removal and Case Workshop

How to respond fairly when adverse information emerges, including investigation, notification to the authority, conditions and removal, followed by a workshop working two cases to decision.

**Objectives**

- Respond to adverse information with a fair and timely process
- Decide on notification, conditions, suspension or removal
- Apply the programme to realistic appointment and reassessment cases

**Syllabus**

- Investigating adverse information fairly and promptly
- Notification duties, regulatory references on departure and settlement agreements
- Conditions, suspension, removal and the risk of prohibition
- Workshop: a director appointment and a mid-year reassessment to decision

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines practical tools with case studies, exercises and mock interviews, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's roles and accountability regime.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of compliance, company secretarial, HR or governance work in a regulated firm is helpful. No prior experience of regulatory applications is required.
