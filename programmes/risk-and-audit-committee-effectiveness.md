## Aim and Purpose

To give committee chairs and members a practical understanding of how effective risk and audit committees work and the tools to oversee risk management, compliance, internal audit and external audit. Participants leave able to sharpen their committee's mandate and agenda, challenge the control functions and auditors, and evidence effective oversight to the board and supervisors.

**Coverage:** Committee mandates and composition, risk appetite frameworks, overseeing the chief risk officer, compliance and internal audit, external audit and financial reporting judgements, assurance mapping, reading risk MI, chairing the committee, reporting to the board and committee evaluation.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs and members of board risk and audit committees
- Non-executive and independent directors preparing to join a risk or audit committee
- Heads of risk, compliance and internal audit, and company secretaries supporting committees

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the distinct mandates of the risk and audit committees and how they interact
- Evaluate the firm's risk appetite framework and its use in decision-making
- Oversee the chief risk officer, compliance and internal audit, including their independence and resourcing
- Challenge external audit and significant financial reporting judgements
- Use assurance mapping and risk MI to identify gaps in oversight
- Assess committee effectiveness and agree an improvement plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Committee Mandates, Composition and Interaction

What risk and audit committees are for, how their terms of reference divide responsibilities, and how they work with each other and the board to avoid gaps and duplication.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose and core responsibilities of each committee
- Evaluate terms of reference, membership and independence
- Identify overlaps and gaps between committees and the board

**Syllabus**

- Supervisory and international expectations, including BCBS corporate governance principles
- Terms of reference: what to include and what to leave to the board
- Composition, independence, skills and cross-membership
- Joint sessions and managing the boundary between risk and audit

### Module 2: Risk Appetite Frameworks

How the risk committee shapes and monitors the firm's risk appetite, and how to tell a framework that drives decisions from one that sits on a shelf.

**Objectives**

- Assess the risk appetite statement, limits and escalation triggers
- Evaluate how risk appetite is cascaded and used in business decisions
- Challenge breaches, limit changes and management's response

**Syllabus**

- Elements of a risk appetite framework: statement, metrics, limits and triggers
- Linking risk appetite to strategy, capital, liquidity and business planning
- Emerging and non-financial risks in the framework
- Handling breaches and requests to change limits

### Module 3: Overseeing the CRO, Compliance and Internal Audit

How committees ensure the control functions have the independence, standing and resources to provide effective challenge and assurance, and how they get the most from their heads.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate the independence, resourcing and reporting lines of the control functions
- Assess risk, compliance and internal audit plans and their coverage
- Identify warning signs of weak or captured control functions

**Syllabus**

- The chief risk officer's role, access and protection from dismissal
- Compliance monitoring plans, findings and regulatory relationships
- Internal audit charter, risk-based plan, quality and follow-up
- Private sessions, appraisal and appointment of function heads

### Module 4: External Audit and Financial Reporting Judgements

The audit committee's oversight of financial reporting and the external auditor, focusing on significant accounting judgements, audit quality and auditor independence.

**Objectives**

- Challenge significant accounting judgements and estimates
- Assess audit quality, auditor independence and non-audit services
- Evaluate the auditor's findings, management letters and management's response

**Syllabus**

- Significant judgements: provisions, valuations, impairment and going concern
- Audit planning, materiality and key audit matters
- Auditor independence, tenure, tendering and non-audit services
- Whistleblowing, fraud risk and internal controls over financial reporting

### Module 5: Assurance Mapping and the Three Lines

How committees build a joined-up view of where assurance comes from across the three lines of defence and external providers, and where it is missing or duplicated.

**Objectives**

- Explain the three lines model and its limitations
- Use an assurance map to identify gaps and overlaps
- Challenge reliance on first-line self-assessment

**Syllabus**

- The three lines model in practice
- Building and using an assurance map
- Combined assurance across risk, compliance, internal and external audit
- Recognising comfort without evidence

### Module 6: Reading Risk MI

The information committees need to oversee risk and control, how to read risk reports and dashboards critically, and how to reshape committee packs so that the key messages stand out.

**Objectives**

- Specify the management information and indicators the committee needs
- Interpret risk reports, trends and forward-looking indicators
- Specify improvements to committee papers and dashboards

**Syllabus**

- The core committee information set: profile, appetite, incidents and actions
- Key risk indicators, trends and emerging risk horizon scanning
- Overdue actions, repeat findings and risk acceptances
- Good committee papers: clear asks, balanced views and exception reporting

### Module 7: Chairing the Committee and Reporting to the Board

How committee chairs set the agenda, run meetings that produce real challenge and give the board a clear, candid account of what the committee has found.

**Objectives**

- Apply good practice in committee agenda setting and chairing
- Encourage challenge from members and candour from management
- Report committee conclusions and escalations to the board effectively

**Syllabus**

- Annual cycle of business and agenda prioritisation
- Chairing for challenge: drawing out members and managing dominant voices
- Relationships with function heads, auditors and the authority
- Committee reports to the board: what, how and when to escalate

### Module 8: Committee Evaluation and Improvement

Brings the programme together into a committee improvement plan, covering self-assessment, external review, skills, succession and learning from failures.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate committee effectiveness using structured self-assessment
- Use a skills matrix to plan membership, succession and development
- Produce a committee improvement plan

**Syllabus**

- Committee self-assessment and external review
- Skills, induction and succession for committee chairs and members
- Lessons from control and audit failures
- Workshop: the committee's twelve-month improvement plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of committee briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies and a committee question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the committee's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board or senior management experience in financial services is helpful. No accounting or audit qualification is required.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee, Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015)
- FSB, Principles for an Effective Risk Appetite Framework (2013)
- Basel Committee, The Internal Audit Function in Banks (2012)
- Institute of Internal Auditors, Global Internal Audit Standards (2024)
