## Aim and Purpose

To give compliance, MLRO and financial crime teams a practical, firm-side command of crypto-asset financial crime: how criminals use the firm's services, how funds are traced on-chain, and what effective controls look like. Participants leave able to design and tune wallet screening and monitoring, handle travel rule and sanctions exposure, investigate alerts, file usable suspicion reports and respond to law enforcement, and evidence all of it to supervisors.

**Coverage:** The firm's crypto-asset financial crime exposure, typologies and red flags, the laundering lifecycle and anonymity-enhancing techniques, customer and wallet due diligence, blockchain analytics in transaction monitoring, the travel rule and counterparty due diligence, sanctions exposure, investigating alerts and suspicious activity reporting, and responding to law enforcement requests.

## Intended Audience

- MLROs, compliance officers and financial crime analysts at crypto-asset service providers
- Financial crime teams at payment, e-money and fintech firms with crypto-asset exposure or crypto-asset business customers
- Product, operations and risk managers responsible for onboarding, wallets, withdrawals and monitoring tools

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain where crypto-asset financial crime risk enters the firm's business model, including indirect exposure through customers and partners
- Identify the main typologies and red flags of crypto-enabled crime in customer behaviour and on-chain activity
- Design customer, wallet and counterparty due diligence proportionate to the firm's risk
- Apply blockchain analytics within transaction monitoring and sanctions controls, and recognise its limits
- Investigate alerts, reach and document a judgement on suspicion, and file reports the national FIU can use
- Respond effectively to law enforcement requests and evidence control effectiveness to supervisors

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Firm's Crypto-Asset Financial Crime Exposure

Where financial crime risk enters crypto-asset, payment and fintech business models, what the FATF standards require of firms, and how to reflect crypto-asset exposure in the business-wide risk assessment.

**Objectives**

- Map financial crime risk across the firm's products, channels and fiat on- and off-ramps
- Summarise the FATF obligations on virtual asset service providers and firms dealing with them
- Reflect direct and indirect crypto-asset exposure in the business-wide risk assessment

**Syllabus**

- Exchanges, brokers, custodians, wallet providers and payment gateways: where risk arises
- Indirect exposure: payment and e-money firms serving crypto-asset businesses
- FATF Recommendations 15 and 16 and the risk-based approach for firms
- Crypto-asset factors in the business-wide risk assessment and risk appetite

### Module 2: Typologies and Red Flags for Compliance Teams

The principal ways criminals use crypto-asset services, from scams and ransomware to mule networks and sanctions evasion, and the customer and transaction red flags front-line and compliance staff should recognise.

**Objectives**

- Identify the main predicate offences and typologies involving crypto-assets
- Recognise red flags in customer profiles, behaviour and transaction patterns
- Translate typologies into monitoring scenarios and staff guidance

**Syllabus**

- Investment and romance scams, money mules and victims as unwitting launderers
- Ransomware, darknet markets, hacks and cyber-enabled crime
- Sanctions evasion, terrorist financing and proliferation financing
- From red-flag indicators to scenarios, guidance and training

### Module 3: The Laundering Lifecycle and Anonymity-Enhancing Techniques

How illicit proceeds are placed, layered and integrated using crypto-assets, how mixers, privacy coins, bridges and chain-hopping obscure the trail, and what the firm can still see and control.

**Objectives**

- Map placement, layering and integration to the firm's own services
- Explain how mixers, privacy coins, bridges and decentralised protocols break transaction links
- Set policy on anonymity-enhanced transfers and exposure to high-risk services

**Syllabus**

- Placement: cash-to-crypto, kiosks, peer-to-peer and over-the-counter trading
- Layering: rapid hops, chain-hopping, bridges, swaps and decentralised protocols
- Mixers, tumblers and privacy coins: what remains visible to the firm
- Integration and off-ramps: where the firm is the exit point

### Module 4: Customer and Wallet Due Diligence

How to onboard and keep under review customers whose activity involves crypto-assets, including source of funds and wealth, ownership of self-hosted wallets and risk-based enhanced due diligence.

**Objectives**

- Design a customer risk assessment that reflects crypto-asset activity
- Verify source of funds and wealth derived from crypto-assets
- Apply proportionate controls to self-hosted wallets and peer-to-peer transfers

**Syllabus**

- Customer risk factors for crypto-asset activity and business customers
- Source of funds and wealth: using on-chain history as evidence
- Self-hosted wallets: proof of ownership, risk scoring and limits
- Enhanced due diligence, periodic review and trigger events

### Module 5: Blockchain Analytics in Transaction Monitoring

How blockchain analytics tools trace funds, cluster and attribute addresses and score risk, and how to configure, tune and govern them within the firm's wider transaction monitoring framework.

**Objectives**

- Explain tracing, clustering and attribution in plain terms
- Set exposure rules, thresholds and scenarios that fit the firm's risk
- Govern analytics tools: selection, validation, coverage gaps and documentation

**Syllabus**

- Following funds on public ledgers: inputs, outputs and hops
- Direct and indirect exposure, risk categories and scoring
- Deposit and withdrawal screening, thresholds, tuning and false positives
- Selecting, validating and overseeing an analytics provider

### Module 6: The Travel Rule and Counterparty Due Diligence

How to implement the travel rule in practice, from collecting and transmitting originator and beneficiary information to due diligence on counterparty service providers and handling missing or inconsistent data.

**Objectives**

- Implement originator and beneficiary data requirements across transfer types
- Conduct risk-based due diligence on counterparty service providers
- Handle missing data, unhosted wallets and counterparties in jurisdictions without the rule

**Syllabus**

- FATF Recommendation 16 for virtual assets: data, timing and thresholds
- Messaging solutions, interoperability and the uneven pace of adoption
- Counterparty due diligence: licensing, controls and nested services
- Missing or mismatched information: return, reject, review or report

### Module 7: Sanctions Exposure in Crypto-Asset Activity

How sanctions risk arises through designated addresses, sanctioned services and jurisdictions, and how the firm screens, blocks, freezes and reports in a twenty-four-hour, borderless market.

**Objectives**

- Identify direct and indirect sanctions exposure in on-chain and customer activity
- Design address, customer and geolocation screening controls
- Respond to a sanctions hit: freezing, holding and reporting to the authority

**Syllabus**

- Designated addresses, sanctioned services and high-risk jurisdictions
- Address screening, IP and device geolocation and evasion indicators
- Exposure through counterparties, nested exchanges and decentralised protocols
- Freezing, holding and reporting crypto-assets and handling tainted deposits

### Module 8: Investigating Alerts and Reporting Suspicion

How to triage and investigate on-chain and customer alerts, reach and record a reasoned judgement on suspicion, and write reports that the national FIU and law enforcement can act on.

**Objectives**

- Run a structured investigation combining on-chain and off-chain information
- Reach and document a defensible decision on suspicion
- Write suspicious activity reports with usable crypto-asset detail

**Syllabus**

- Alert triage, escalation and case management
- Investigation steps: customer profile, tracing, counterparties and open sources
- Decision records, tipping off and restricting or exiting relationships
- Report quality: addresses, transaction hashes, attribution and narrative

### Module 9: Responding to Law Enforcement and Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together by examining how the firm handles production orders, freezing requests and asset recovery, ending with a workshop that takes a case from alert to report and law enforcement response.

**Objectives**

- Handle information requests, production orders and freezing requests correctly
- Support seizure and return of crypto-assets while protecting the firm
- Apply the programme to a realistic case and agree control improvements

**Syllabus**

- Verifying, prioritising and responding to law enforcement requests
- Freezing, preserving and transferring assets: keys, custody and records
- Public-private partnerships and evidencing effectiveness to supervisors
- Workshop: from alert to report, response and control remediation

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a practitioner briefing with worked cases, red-flag and tracing exercises, ending in an alert-to-report workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT compliance or financial crime operations is helpful. No technical background in blockchain is required.
