## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to equip supervisors with the foundational knowledge needed to assess consumer protection outcomes in regulated firms. Participants will learn how product governance, fair value assessment, and disclosure requirements combine to protect consumers, and how to identify where these controls are weak or absent. Through practical exercises, the workshop will build participants' confidence in forming supervisory judgements about consumer outcomes.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level supervisors assessing consumer protection outcomes.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the core principles underpinning consumer protection regulation
- Describe the product governance lifecycle from design to post-sale monitoring
- Assess whether a product's target market and distribution strategy are appropriate
- Apply a fair value assessment framework to a financial product
- Evaluate the adequacy of consumer disclosure documents
- Identify indicators of poor consumer outcomes within firm data
- Link weak consumer protection controls to wider conduct risk
- Formulate supervisory conclusions and recommend next steps

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Introduction to consumer protection supervision (09:30–09:45)**
*Objective: Explain the purpose and structure of the workshop*
- Why consumer protection matters to supervisors
- Overview of the product governance lifecycle
- Link between consumer outcomes and conduct risk
- Course structure and learning journey
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: participants' experience of consumer protection issues
- Quick reflection: what does a poor consumer outcome look like?

**Session 2: Product governance and fair value (09:45–11:00)**
*Objective: Explain product governance and apply a fair value framework*
- The product governance lifecycle: design, target market, distribution, monitoring
- Fair value assessment: price, benefits, costs, and quality
- Common failures in product governance
- Regulatory expectations for ongoing product review
Activities:
- Group exercise: assess a sample product against a fair value framework
- Discussion: where do product governance controls typically break down?

**Session 3: Disclosure and consumer understanding (11:15–12:30)**
*Objective: Evaluate the adequacy of consumer disclosure*
- Principles of clear, fair and not misleading communication
- Assessing disclosure documents for consumer understanding
- Behavioural insights: how consumers actually process information
- Testing and monitoring disclosure effectiveness
Activities:
- Exercise: review a sample disclosure document and identify weaknesses
- Case study: a disclosure failure and its consumer impact

**Session 4: From analysis to supervisory judgement (13:30–14:30)**
*Objective: Translate analysis into supervisory conclusions*
- Identifying indicators of poor consumer outcomes in firm data
- Linking weak controls to wider conduct risk
- Prioritising supervisory follow-up
- Communicating findings to firms
Activities:
- Case study: analyse a firm's consumer outcomes data and produce a supervisory assessment

**Session 5: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: product governance, fair value, disclosure, judgement
- Reinforcing links to conduct risk
- Embedding a structured approach to consumer protection review
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 5 sessions:
- Session 1: Introduction to consumer protection supervision (09:30–09:45)
- Session 2: Product governance and fair value (09:45–11:00)
- Session 3: Disclosure and consumer understanding (11:15–12:30)
- Session 4: From analysis to supervisory judgement (13:30–14:30)
- Session 5: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:30–14:45)

**Key Takeaways**
- Consumer protection is assessed through the whole product lifecycle, not just at point of sale
- Fair value and clear disclosure are the two pillars of good consumer outcomes
- Weak consumer protection controls are often an early warning sign of wider conduct risk
