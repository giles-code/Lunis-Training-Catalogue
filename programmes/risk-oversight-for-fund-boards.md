## Aim and Purpose

To give directors a structured, practical grounding in the risks run by investment funds and their management companies, and in the board's role in setting appetite and overseeing the risk function. Participants leave able to read risk information critically, challenge stress tests and limits, and evidence effective risk oversight to investors, auditors and supervisors.

**Coverage:** Risk concepts and risk appetite, the risk function in a management company, investment risk across market, liquidity, counterparty and leverage, operational and outsourcing risk, risk management information and key risk indicators, stress testing and scenario analysis, and emerging risks from AI, cyber, climate and digital assets.

## Intended Audience

- Independent and non-executive directors of investment funds and fund management companies
- Executive directors, conducting officers and designated persons responsible for risk management
- Chief risk officers, heads of compliance and company secretaries supporting fund boards

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain inherent and residual risk, risk appetite and the board's role in fund risk oversight
- Evaluate the role, resourcing and independence of the management company's risk function
- Assess market, liquidity, counterparty, leverage and operational risk across the fund range
- Challenge risk management information, key risk indicators and limit breaches
- Interrogate stress tests and scenarios and the actions management proposes in response
- Design a board risk dashboard and an oversight plan that keeps pace with emerging risks

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Risk Concepts for Fund Directors

The building blocks of fund risk oversight: how risk is identified, measured and controlled, how inherent and residual risk differ, and how a risk appetite gives the board a basis for challenge.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish inherent risk, controls and residual risk in a fund context
- Explain how risk appetite, tolerances and limits fit together
- Define where risk oversight sits between the fund board and the management company board

**Syllabus**

- The risk management cycle: identify, assess, mitigate, monitor and report
- Inherent and residual risk, and the quality of controls in between
- Risk appetite statements, tolerances and limits for a fund range
- Investor protection as the purpose of fund risk oversight

### Module 2: The Risk Function in a Management Company

What a permanent risk management function should do, how it is organised and governed, and how the board satisfies itself that it is independent, adequately resourced and listened to.

**Objectives**

- Describe the role, scope and outputs of the risk management function
- Assess the independence of the function from portfolio management
- Evaluate the risk management policy, process and resourcing

**Syllabus**

- Risk management policy, process and procedures across the fund range
- Functional and hierarchical independence from portfolio management
- Resourcing, systems, data and delegation of risk activities
- The head of risk's access to the board and private sessions

### Module 3: Investment Risk: Market, Liquidity, Counterparty and Leverage

The main investment risks a fund runs, how each is measured and limited, and the questions directors should ask when a strategy, instrument or market environment changes.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main measures of market, liquidity, counterparty and leverage risk
- Assess whether limits fit each fund's strategy, investors and dealing terms
- Challenge management on concentration, correlation and liquidity mismatch

**Syllabus**

- Market risk measures: volatility, tracking error, value at risk and their limits
- Liquidity risk: asset liquidity, investor profile, dealing terms and management tools
- Counterparty and collateral risk in derivatives, financing and securities lending
- Leverage measures, internal limits that trigger before a breach and escalation

### Module 4: Operational and Outsourcing Risk

How operational risk arises across the fund's delegated operating model, how outsourcing and sub-delegation change the risk profile, and how the board gains assurance that providers are controlled.

**Objectives**

- Identify the main sources of operational risk in a delegated fund model
- Evaluate outsourcing due diligence, monitoring and exit arrangements
- Assess errors, breaches and incidents as evidence of control quality

**Syllabus**

- Risk and control self-assessment, risk registers and loss events
- Outsourcing, sub-delegation chains and concentration on key providers
- Errors, breaches and incidents: root causes and lessons learned
- Business continuity, exit plans and substitutability of providers

### Module 5: Risk MI and Key Risk Indicators for Boards

The information a board needs to oversee fund risk, how to read risk reports critically, and how to distinguish genuine assurance from volume of data.

**Objectives**

- Specify the core risk information set for a fund board
- Interpret key risk indicators, trends and breach reports
- Identify gaps, stale data and comfort without evidence

**Syllabus**

- The core risk information set: profile, appetite, limits, breaches and actions
- Leading and lagging indicators, thresholds and trend analysis
- Exception reporting, overdue actions and risk acceptances
- Common weaknesses in risk reports and what to ask for instead

### Module 6: Stress Testing and Scenario Analysis for Funds

How funds are stress tested for market, liquidity and redemption shocks, what good scenario design looks like, and how the board turns results into decisions rather than reassurance.

**Objectives**

- Explain historical, hypothetical and reverse stress tests for funds
- Challenge the severity, assumptions and plausibility of scenarios
- Agree management actions, triggers and the use of liquidity management tools

**Syllabus**

- Asset-side and liability-side stress tests, including redemption shocks
- Scenario design: severity, correlation breakdown and second-round effects
- Reverse stress testing and the question of what would break the fund
- Lessons from fund suspensions and stressed market episodes

### Module 7: Emerging Risks: AI, Cyber, Climate and Digital Assets

How new technologies and structural trends change the risk profile of funds and managers, and how the board keeps its risk framework current without chasing every headline.

**Objectives**

- Recognise the risks from AI, cyber threats, climate change and digital assets
- Assess how these risks enter the fund through investments, providers and operations
- Build emerging risks into horizon scanning, appetite and board agendas

**Syllabus**

- AI in portfolio management and operations: model, data and dependency risk
- Cyber threats, ransomware and resilience of the operating chain
- Climate and sustainability risks in portfolios and disclosures
- Digital assets, tokenised fund units and custody risk

### Module 8: Workshop: The Board Risk Dashboard

Brings the programme together as participants design a board risk dashboard and escalation framework for a fund range, and test it against a live scenario.

**Objectives**

- Design a board risk dashboard aligned to appetite and limits
- Apply escalation triggers and decision rights to a stressed scenario
- Produce a board action plan for strengthening risk oversight

**Syllabus**

- Selecting indicators, thresholds and reporting frequency
- Escalation routes between management, the risk function and the board
- Scenario exercise: a liquidity and operational event in a fund range
- Workshop: the board's twelve-month risk oversight plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with fund case studies and a board question set, ending in a dashboard design workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the board's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience as a fund director or in fund management, administration or risk is helpful. No quantitative or technical risk background is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Principles for an Effective Risk Appetite Framework (2013)
- IOSCO, Recommendations for Liquidity Risk Management for Collective Investment Schemes (2018)
- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
- Basel Committee, Stress Testing Principles (2018)
