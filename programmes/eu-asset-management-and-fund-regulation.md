## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and practitioners a clear map of how EU law regulates funds, their managers and their distribution, and where the main supervisory risks lie across the fund value chain. Participants leave able to determine which rules apply to a given structure, test arrangements for delegation, liquidity, leverage and depositaries, and anticipate how ESMA and national competent authorities will approach them.

**Coverage:** The EU regulatory architecture and fund value chain, the UCITS Directive, AIFMD and AIFMD II, specialist regimes for ELTIFs, money market funds and venture capital funds, MiFID II and MiFIR for managers and distributors, delegation, substance and depositaries, liquidity management tools, leverage and valuation, cross-border marketing and passporting, SFDR and sustainability, and DORA and supervisory convergence.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, authorisation and policy staff at national competent authorities covering funds and asset managers
- Compliance, legal, risk and product staff at UCITS management companies, AIFMs, investment managers and distributors
- Directors, depositary and fund administration staff who need a working map of the EU fund rulebook

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how EU fund regulation is made and determine which rules apply across the value chain
- Apply the core requirements of UCITS, AIFMD and AIFMD II to funds and their managers
- Assess delegation, substance and depositary arrangements against EU expectations
- Evaluate liquidity management tools, leverage and valuation and what can go wrong
- Apply the rules on cross-border marketing, passporting, MiFID II distribution and SFDR disclosures
- Identify how DORA, ESMA convergence work and reform proposals are reshaping the sector

## Syllabus

### Module 1: EU Regulatory Architecture and the Fund Value Chain

Level-setting on investing, asset management and funds, how EU rules are made and layered with Member State requirements, and a framework for deciding which rules apply at each link of the value chain.

**Objectives**

- Explain directives, regulations, technical standards, guidelines and Q&As and how they interact
- Map the fund value chain from product and distribution to management, operations and administration
- Determine which EU rules apply by asking what, who and how

**Syllabus**

- What investing, asset management and an investment fund are
- Level 1 to Level 3: the Commission, co-legislators, ESMA and national authorities
- UCITS or AIF: the core classification and why managers choose each
- Break-out: mapping supervisory risks to the value chain

### Module 2: The UCITS Directive: Product, Manager and Investor Protection

Why the UCITS framework exists, what it requires of funds and management companies, and whether its product rules have been effective in preventing investor harm.

**Objectives**

- Explain the history and global reach of the UCITS brand
- Apply eligible asset, diversification, borrowing and risk management rules
- Analyse whether UCITS rules prevented harm in a recent fund failure case study

**Syllabus**

- From UCITS I to UCITS V and the role of the management company
- Eligible assets, spread rules, derivatives and efficient portfolio management
- Exchange-traded funds and other specialist UCITS
- Case study: an illiquid UCITS suspension and its lessons

### Module 3: The AIFM Directive and AIFMD II

How AIFMD regulates managers rather than products, the requirements for authorised and registered managers, and the changes introduced by AIFMD II on delegation, liquidity, loan origination and reporting.

**Objectives**

- Explain AIFMD's scope, thresholds and manager-level requirements
- Identify the obligations on authorisation, risk management, remuneration and reporting
- Assess the impact of AIFMD II on managers, funds and supervisors

**Syllabus**

- Why AIFMD was created and what it regulates
- Authorised and sub-threshold managers, third-country managers and private placement
- Reporting to supervisors and transparency to investors
- AIFMD II: loan-originating funds, delegation reporting and changes to UCITS

### Module 4: Specialist Fund Regimes: ELTIFs, MMFs and EuVECA

A short tour of the EU product regulations that sit on top of the UCITS and AIF classification, what each is designed to achieve and the supervisory issues each raises.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose and core rules of the ELTIF, MMF, EuVECA and EuSEF regimes
- Distinguish CNAV, LVNAV and VNAV money market funds and their stability risks
- Identify the retail access and liquidity questions raised by the revised ELTIF regime

**Syllabus**

- ELTIF and ELTIF 2.0: long-term investment and retail access
- Money market funds: types, liquidity buffers and the reform debate
- EuVECA and EuSEF: venture and social entrepreneurship funds
- When a specialist label adds value and when it adds complexity

### Module 5: MiFID II and MiFIR for Managers and Distributors

How MiFID II and MiFIR apply to portfolio managers, investment managers acting as delegates and fund distributors, including product governance, inducements, costs disclosure and transparency.

**Objectives**

- Explain where MiFID II applies across portfolio management and fund distribution
- Apply product governance, suitability, appropriateness and inducement rules
- Assess costs and charges disclosure from manufacturer to end investor

**Syllabus**

- Individual portfolio management and the delegated investment manager
- Product governance: target market, distribution strategy and review
- Inducements, costs and charges, and the PRIIPs key information document
- MiFIR transparency and transaction reporting for managers

### Module 6: Delegation, Substance and Depositaries

How managers delegate portfolio and risk management while retaining responsibility, what supervisors expect of substance, and the depositary's role in safekeeping, oversight and cash flow monitoring.

**Objectives**

- Assess delegation arrangements against EU rules and ESMA substance expectations
- Identify letter-box risk and weak oversight of delegates
- Explain depositary duties, liability and independence for UCITS and AIFs

**Syllabus**

- Delegation conditions, oversight of delegates and AIFMD II reporting
- Substance: senior management, resources and the letter-box test
- Depositary safekeeping, oversight and cash flow monitoring duties
- Depositary liability, independence and conflicts of interest

### Module 7: Liquidity Management Tools, Leverage and Valuation

How EU rules address liquidity mismatch, leverage and valuation in funds, the liquidity management tools now required of open-ended funds, and the red flags that precede fund suspensions and losses.

**Objectives**

- Explain liquidity stress testing and the selection and activation of liquidity management tools
- Assess leverage measures, limits and reporting for UCITS and AIFs
- Identify valuation governance weaknesses, especially for hard-to-value assets

**Syllabus**

- Liquidity mismatch, stress testing and ESMA guidelines
- Liquidity management tools: swing pricing, anti-dilution levies, gates and suspensions
- Leverage: gross and commitment methods, global exposure and supervisory limits
- Valuation policies, independence and red flags

### Module 8: Cross-Border Marketing, Passporting and Disclosure

How funds and managers operate across the single market through product and management passports, the cross-border distribution framework, and the disclosure and costs rules that protect investors.

**Objectives**

- Explain the UCITS and AIFMD marketing and management passports
- Apply rules on pre-marketing, marketing communications and de-notification
- Assess costs and charges against the prohibition on undue costs

**Syllabus**

- Home and host roles in fund and manager passporting
- Cross-border distribution rules, pre-marketing and marketing communications
- Investor classification: retail, professional and semi-professional access
- Undue costs, fee supervision and value for money

### Module 9: Sustainable Finance: SFDR and Greenwashing

How the EU sustainable finance framework applies to funds and managers, from SFDR classification and principal adverse impacts to fund naming rules, and whether disclosures prevent greenwashing.

**Objectives**

- Explain SFDR entity and product disclosures and the Article 6, 8 and 9 categories
- Assess sustainability claims in fund documents, names and marketing
- Evaluate the SFDR review and the case for product categories

**Syllabus**

- SFDR, the Taxonomy Regulation and sustainability preferences under MiFID II
- Principal adverse impacts and pre-contractual and periodic templates
- ESMA guidelines on fund names using ESG or sustainability terms
- Break-out: spotting greenwashing and greenbleaching

### Module 10: DORA, Supervisory Convergence and the Regulatory Horizon

Brings the programme together with the cross-cutting rules that reach asset managers indirectly, ESMA's convergence toolkit, and the reforms likely to shape fund regulation over the coming years.

**Objectives**

- Explain how DORA applies to UCITS management companies and AIFMs
- Describe ESMA's convergence tools and how they shape national supervision
- Identify the regulatory topics that will shape the sector and agree priorities

**Syllabus**

- DORA for asset managers: ICT risk, incidents and third-party providers
- Other rules reaching funds: EMIR, short selling, AML and market abuse
- ESMA Q&As, guidelines, peer reviews and common supervisory actions
- Workshop: the reform horizon and a twelve-month priorities plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies and break-out exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisors, managers or distributors.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of asset management, funds or their supervision is helpful. No legal background or prior knowledge of EU law is required.

## Core Reading

- Directive 2009/65/EC on undertakings for collective investment in transferable securities (UCITS Directive)
- Directive 2011/61/EU on Alternative Investment Fund Managers (AIFMD)
- Directive (EU) 2024/927 amending the AIFMD and UCITS Directive on delegation, liquidity risk management, supervisory reporting, depositary services and loan origination (AIFMD II)
- Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 on sustainability-related disclosures in the financial services sector (SFDR)
