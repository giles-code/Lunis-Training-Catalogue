## Aim and Purpose

To give compliance, MLRO and financial crime teams a practical, firm-side command of crypto-asset financial crime: how criminals use the firm's services, how funds are traced on-chain, and what effective controls look like. Participants leave able to design and tune wallet screening and monitoring, handle travel rule and sanctions exposure, investigate alerts, file usable suspicion reports and respond to law enforcement, and evidence all of it to supervisors.

## What It Covers

The firm's crypto-asset financial crime exposure, typologies and red flags, the laundering lifecycle and anonymity-enhancing techniques, customer and wallet due diligence, blockchain analytics in transaction monitoring, the travel rule and counterparty due diligence, sanctions exposure, investigating alerts and suspicious activity reporting, and responding to law enforcement requests.

## Intended Audience

- MLROs, compliance officers and financial crime analysts at crypto-asset service providers
- Financial crime teams at payment, e-money and fintech firms with crypto-asset exposure or crypto-asset business customers
- Product, operations and risk managers responsible for onboarding, wallets, withdrawals and monitoring tools

## Learning Objectives

- Explain where crypto-asset financial crime risk enters the firm's business model, including indirect exposure through customers and partners
- Identify the main typologies and red flags of crypto-enabled crime in customer behaviour and on-chain activity
- Design customer, wallet and counterparty due diligence proportionate to the firm's risk
- Apply blockchain analytics within transaction monitoring and sanctions controls, and recognise its limits
- Investigate alerts, reach and document a judgement on suspicion, and file reports the national FIU can use
- Respond effectively to law enforcement requests and evidence control effectiveness to supervisors

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a practitioner briefing with worked cases, red-flag and tracing exercises, ending in an alert-to-report workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT compliance or financial crime operations is helpful. No technical background in blockchain is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- FATF, Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers (2021)
- FATF, Virtual Assets Red Flag Indicators of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (2020)
- FATF, Opportunities and Challenges of New Technologies for AML/CFT (2021)
