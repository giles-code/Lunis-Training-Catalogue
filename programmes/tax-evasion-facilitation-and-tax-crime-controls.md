## Aim and Purpose

To give compliance, financial crime and client-facing teams a practical understanding of how firms are drawn into tax crime, as a channel for laundered proceeds or as facilitators of evasion. Participants leave able to assess the firm's exposure, design reasonable prevention procedures, apply tax-aware client and investor due diligence, meet automatic exchange of information obligations and act on suspicion.

## What It Covers

Tax crime as a predicate offence, corporate offences of failing to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion and reasonable prevention procedures, tax evasion risk assessment and associated persons, tax-aware client due diligence and source of wealth, structures and wealth management red flags, fund structures and investor risk, automatic exchange of information under the OECD Common Reporting Standard, and suspicion, reporting and governance.

## Intended Audience

- MLROs, compliance officers and tax compliance teams in investment, wealth management and fund management firms
- Relationship managers, private client advisers, fund administrators and transfer agency teams
- Senior managers accountable for client onboarding, distribution, introducers and tax reporting

## Learning Objectives

- Explain tax crime as a predicate offence and how firms become channels or facilitators
- Assess the firm's tax evasion facilitation risk, including through associated persons
- Design reasonable prevention procedures and evidence them to supervisors and prosecutors
- Apply tax-aware client and investor due diligence, including source of wealth
- Recognise red flags in wealth management, structures and fund investment
- Meet automatic exchange of information obligations and act on suspicion of tax crime

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a practitioner briefing with case studies and structure-mapping exercises, ending in a prevention procedures workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's client base and products.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of compliance, AML/CFT or client onboarding is helpful. No specialist knowledge of tax law is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- OECD, Fighting Tax Crime – The Ten Global Principles, Second Edition (2021)
- OECD, Standard for Automatic Exchange of Financial Account Information in Tax Matters, Second Edition (2017)
- OECD, Ending the Shell Game: Cracking down on the Professionals who enable Tax and White Collar Crimes (2021)
