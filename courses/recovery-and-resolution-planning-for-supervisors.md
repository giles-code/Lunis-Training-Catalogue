## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' skills in assessing the credibility of firms' recovery plans and understanding the supervisor's role in resolution planning and resolvability assessment.

## Intended Audience

Mid-to-senior staff involved in crisis preparedness, recovery, and resolution planning.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the distinction between recovery and resolution planning
- Assess the credibility of a firm's recovery plan
- Describe the supervisor's role in resolution planning
- Evaluate resolvability barriers for a sample firm
- Formulate a supervisory view on recovery plan credibility

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Recovery versus resolution (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain recovery and resolution concepts*
- Recovery planning: firm-led actions to restore viability
- Resolution planning: authority-led actions if recovery fails
- Why the distinction matters for supervisory role clarity
- International standards on recovery and resolution planning
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: recovery and resolution frameworks in participants' own jurisdictions

**Session 2: Assessing recovery plan credibility (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess recovery plan credibility*
- Criteria for assessing recovery plan credibility
- Common weaknesses: unrealistic assumptions, untested options
- Assessing recovery options against a range of stress scenarios
- The role of recovery plan testing and dry-runs
Activities:
- Group exercise: assess a sample recovery plan's credibility

**Session 3: Resolution planning and resolvability (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Describe the resolution planning role and evaluate resolvability*
- The supervisor/resolution authority's role in resolution planning
- Assessing resolvability barriers: structure, data, continuity of services
- Case study: a firm with significant resolvability barriers
Activities:
- Case study: evaluate resolvability barriers and formulate a supervisory view

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: recovery/resolution distinction, credibility, resolvability
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Recovery versus resolution (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Assessing recovery plan credibility (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Resolution planning and resolvability (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Recovery and resolution planning serve different purposes and require different supervisory lenses
- A credible recovery plan must be tested against genuinely severe scenarios, not just moderate stress
- Resolvability barriers identified in advance are far easier to address than those discovered during an actual crisis
