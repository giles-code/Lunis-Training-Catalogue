## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a structured understanding of how investment funds are built, managed, distributed and regulated, and where investor harm and financial stability risk typically arise. Participants leave able to map supervisory risks across the fund value chain, test firms' valuation, liquidity and delegation arrangements, spot red flags early and decide on a proportionate supervisory response.

**Coverage:** Fund structures and the value chain, supervisory risks and international standards, authorisation and depositaries, valuation, liquidity risk management and liquidity management tools, leverage and money market funds, disclosure, costs and distribution, delegation and substance, private market funds, and sustainable and tokenised funds.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, inspectors and authorisation staff responsible for fund managers, funds, depositaries and fund administrators
- Policy, risk and financial stability specialists working on asset management and non-bank financial intermediation
- Team leaders and heads of department setting supervisory priorities for the funds sector

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how investment funds operate across the value chain and where supervisory risks arise
- Apply international standards for collective investment schemes to authorisation and ongoing supervision
- Assess valuation, liquidity and leverage risk, including the use of liquidity management tools
- Challenge managers on disclosure, costs and charges, distribution and delegation arrangements
- Evaluate the risks of private market, sustainable and tokenised funds and the claims made for them
- Design a proportionate supervisory response to fund-level and manager-level red flags

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Investment Funds and the Fund Value Chain

Level-setting on what investment funds are, why investors use them and how the value chain runs from product design and distribution through portfolio management to operations and administration.

**Objectives**

- Explain how an investment fund operates and its benefits and risks for investors
- Describe the roles of managers, depositaries, administrators, distributors and auditors
- Map fund regulation onto the value chain using a simple supervisory schema

**Syllabus**

- Open-ended, closed-ended, listed and money market fund structures
- Retail funds versus alternative funds for professional investors
- The value chain: product, distribution, management, operations and administration
- Market size, growth and concentration in the domestic funds sector

### Module 2: Supervisory Risks and International Fund Standards

The main sources of investor harm and systemic risk in funds and asset management, and how international standards and typical fund regulatory regimes are designed to mitigate them.

**Objectives**

- Identify the key supervisory risks in funds and asset management
- Explain the IOSCO principles and standard-setter work relevant to collective investment schemes
- Distinguish retail fund product rules from regimes for alternative fund managers

**Syllabus**

- Risk mapping: conflicts, investment, liquidity, operational and conduct risk
- IOSCO Objectives and Principles and the collective investment scheme standards
- Product-regulated retail funds versus manager-regulated alternative funds
- Workshop: mapping supervisory risk areas to the regulatory schema

### Module 3: Fund Authorisation, Product Rules and Depositaries

How the authority assesses fund and manager applications, the product rules that protect retail investors, and the safekeeping and oversight role of the depositary.

**Objectives**

- Assess a fund or manager application against authorisation criteria
- Explain eligible asset, diversification and concentration rules for retail funds
- Evaluate whether the depositary provides effective safekeeping and independent oversight

**Syllabus**

- Authorisation of managers and funds: programme of operations, people and resources
- Eligible assets, diversification limits and borrowing restrictions
- Depositary duties: safekeeping, cash monitoring and oversight of the manager
- Red flags in fund applications and depositary arrangements

### Module 4: Fund Valuation and Pricing

How funds value their assets and calculate net asset value, why valuation of illiquid and hard-to-value assets demands close scrutiny, and how supervisors test valuation governance.

**Objectives**

- Explain valuation and pricing standards for collective investment schemes
- Assess valuation governance, independence and conflicts of interest
- Identify red flags in valuation of illiquid and hard-to-value assets

**Syllabus**

- Net asset value calculation, pricing errors and investor compensation
- Valuation policies, committees and the independence of the valuation function
- Hard-to-value assets: models, stale prices and manager discretion
- Case study: valuation red flags in a fund under stress

### Module 5: Liquidity Risk Management and Liquidity Management Tools

How open-ended funds manage the mismatch between dealing frequency and asset liquidity, how stress testing and liquidity management tools work, and what happens when a fund suspends dealing.

**Objectives**

- Explain liquidity mismatch and the FSB and IOSCO recommendations on fund liquidity
- Assess liquidity stress testing, redemption assumptions and liquidity management tools
- Evaluate a manager's handling of a suspension or gating event

**Syllabus**

- Liquidity mismatch, first-mover advantage and dilution of remaining investors
- Liquidity stress testing: redemption modelling and asset liquidity assumptions
- Liquidity management tools: swing pricing, anti-dilution levies, notice periods and gates
- Case study: an open-ended fund suspension and lessons for supervisors

### Module 6: Leverage, Money Market Funds and Systemic Risk

How funds use borrowing and derivatives to create leverage, how leverage is measured and limited, and why money market funds and leveraged funds matter for financial stability.

**Objectives**

- Explain how leverage is created, measured and reported by funds
- Assess the use of leverage limits and supervisory data to identify risk
- Evaluate money market fund structures, run risk and reform options

**Syllabus**

- Gross, commitment and risk-based leverage measures and the IOSCO framework
- Derivatives, securities financing and counterparty exposure in funds
- Money market funds: constant and variable net asset value models and run dynamics
- Using supervisory data to monitor leverage and systemic risk

### Module 7: Disclosure, Costs and Charges, and Distribution

What investors must be told before and after they invest, how fund costs are regulated to prevent undue charges, and how funds are marketed and distributed domestically and across borders.

**Objectives**

- Assess the quality and consistency of key investor disclosures
- Challenge managers on cost transparency, undue costs and value for investors
- Explain cross-border fund distribution, recognition and marketing rules

**Syllabus**

- Prospectus, key information documents and periodic reporting
- Ongoing charges, performance fees, transaction costs and closet indexing
- Assessing value for money and the prevention of undue costs
- Cross-border distribution, recognition regimes and marketing communications

### Module 8: Delegation, Outsourcing and Manager Substance

How fund managers delegate portfolio management, risk management and administration, how supervisors test whether the manager retains real control, and the operational resilience risks delegation creates.

**Objectives**

- Explain the regulatory framework for delegation and outsourcing by fund managers
- Assess whether a manager retains effective oversight and sufficient substance
- Identify concentration, continuity and letter-box entity risks

**Syllabus**

- Delegation models: portfolio management, risk management, administration and distribution
- Substance tests: people, expertise, decision-making and oversight evidence
- Third-party concentration, technology dependency and operational resilience
- Red flags of letter-box entities and white-label platforms

### Module 9: Private Market Funds and Retailisation

How private equity, private credit and real asset funds are structured, why they need a different supervisory lens, and the investor protection implications of opening private markets to retail investors.

**Objectives**

- Explain private equity, private credit and real asset fund structures
- Evaluate liquidity and valuation risk in semi-liquid and long-term funds
- Assess the investor protection implications of retail access to private markets

**Syllabus**

- Closed-ended, evergreen and semi-liquid private market fund structures
- Fee structures, alignment of interest and conflicts in private funds
- Long-term investment fund regimes and retail access to private markets
- Case study: a supervisory risk assessment of a semi-liquid private credit fund

### Module 10: Sustainable Funds, Tokenised Funds and the Supervisory Horizon

Brings the programme together by examining sustainability-related fund disclosures and greenwashing, fund tokenisation, and the regulatory topics shaping fund supervision, ending with a supervisory planning workshop.

**Objectives**

- Assess sustainability claims, fund labels and disclosures for greenwashing risk
- Explain how tokenisation and distributed ledgers change fund operations and risk
- Prioritise emerging fund risks in a supervisory plan

**Syllabus**

- Sustainability disclosures, fund categorisation and naming rules
- Greenwashing and greenbleaching: evidence, red flags and supervisory response
- Tokenised fund units: issuance, transfer agency, custody and settlement
- Workshop: a twelve-month supervisory plan for the funds sector

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, break-out exercises and supervisory question sets, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's funds sector.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervising or authorising asset managers or funds is helpful. No specialist investment or quantitative background is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
- IOSCO, Recommendations for Liquidity Risk Management for Collective Investment Schemes (2018)
- IOSCO, Principles for the Valuation of Collective Investment Schemes (2013)
- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
