## Aim and Purpose

To give managers and service providers in private markets an advanced, practical understanding of where risk and conflicts concentrate as the asset class grows and opens to wider investors. Participants leave able to strengthen valuation, liquidity, leverage and conflicts controls, and see their business the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** Private markets structures and strategies, fund finance and leverage including subscription lines and NAV facilities, valuation, liquidity in semi-liquid and evergreen vehicles, conflicts in continuation vehicles and co-investment, fees, disclosure and investor reporting, retail access and distribution, and risk management and supervisory focus.

## Intended Audience

- Senior managers, investment professionals and partners at private equity, private credit and real assets managers
- Compliance, risk, valuation, finance and investor relations teams at private markets managers
- Fund administrators, depositaries, auditors, lenders and distributors servicing private markets funds

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how private markets structures and strategies create distinct risks for managers and investors
- Evaluate the use of fund finance and leverage at fund and portfolio level
- Design robust valuation governance for illiquid and stressed assets
- Apply liquidity management to semi-liquid and evergreen vehicles
- Manage conflicts, fees and disclosure in line with investor and supervisory expectations
- Strengthen risk management, retail distribution controls and readiness for supervisory scrutiny

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Private Markets Structures and Strategies

The main private markets strategies and fund structures, how their economics work, and why growth and wider investor access are drawing supervisory attention.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish private equity, private credit, real assets and secondaries strategies
- Explain closed-ended, evergreen and semi-liquid fund structures
- Identify the risks that arise from the private markets business model

**Syllabus**

- Buyout, growth, direct lending, asset-based finance, infrastructure and real estate
- Limited partnerships, feeders, parallel funds and evergreen vehicles
- Capital calls, distributions, waterfalls and carried interest
- Links between private markets, banks, insurers and pension funds

### Module 2: Fund Finance and Leverage

How subscription lines, NAV facilities and portfolio-level leverage are used, their effect on returns and risk, and the controls and disclosures they require.

**Objectives**

- Explain how subscription lines and NAV facilities work
- Assess the effect of fund finance on performance metrics and investor risk
- Design leverage limits, monitoring and disclosure

**Syllabus**

- Subscription lines: uses, terms and effect on internal rates of return
- NAV facilities, hybrid facilities and distributions funded by borrowing
- Portfolio company leverage, payment-in-kind interest and covenant erosion
- Leverage measurement, limits and investor disclosure

### Module 3: Valuation of Private Assets

How private equity, private credit and real assets are valued, how valuation governance provides independence and challenge, and how uncertainty is handled under stress.

**Objectives**

- Apply market, income and cost approaches to private assets
- Design valuation governance with functional independence and challenge
- Address valuation uncertainty for stressed and amended positions

**Syllabus**

- Valuation approaches, inputs and the use of comparables
- Valuation policies, committees, independent valuers and audit
- Stressed loans, amendments, extensions and valuation lag
- Case study: challenging a private credit portfolio valuation

### Module 4: Liquidity in Semi-Liquid and Evergreen Vehicles

How semi-liquid and evergreen funds offer periodic liquidity on illiquid assets, the mismatch risks this creates, and the tools and stress testing needed to manage them.

**Objectives**

- Assess liquidity mismatch between assets, redemption terms and investor base
- Design liquidity buffers, redemption limits and liquidity management tools
- Stress test liquidity under adverse market and redemption scenarios

**Syllabus**

- Redemption frequency, notice periods, gates and lock-ups
- Liquidity sleeves, credit facilities and secondary sales
- Liquidity stress testing and early warning indicators
- Fair treatment of redeeming and remaining investors

### Module 5: Conflicts: Continuation Vehicles and Co-Investment

Where conflicts concentrate in private markets, including continuation vehicles, co-investment and cross-fund transactions, and how to identify, manage and evidence fair treatment.

**Objectives**

- Identify conflicts in manager-led secondaries and cross-fund transactions
- Design fair co-investment and deal allocation policies
- Use fairness opinions, advisory committees and disclosure effectively

**Syllabus**

- Continuation vehicles: pricing, process and investor elections
- Co-investment rights, allocation policies and preferential terms
- Cross-fund transactions, related-party services and affiliated lenders
- Advisory committees, consents and conflicts registers

### Module 6: Fees, Expenses, Disclosure and Investor Reporting

How fees and expenses are charged and allocated across funds and portfolio companies, and how to report performance, costs and risks transparently to investors.

**Objectives**

- Explain management fees, carried interest, offsets and portfolio-level fees
- Allocate expenses fairly across funds, co-investors and the manager
- Produce clear and comparable investor reporting on performance and costs

**Syllabus**

- Fee structures, offsets, monitoring fees and fund expenses
- Expense allocation and broken-deal costs
- Performance measures: IRR, multiples and the effect of fund finance
- Quarterly reporting, capital account statements and side letter transparency

### Module 7: Retail Access and Distribution

How private markets are being opened to wealth and retail investors through new vehicles and channels, and the product governance and disclosure controls this demands.

**Objectives**

- Assess the risks of offering private markets to wealth and retail investors
- Design target market, suitability and distribution controls
- Disclose fees, liquidity limits and valuation uncertainty clearly

**Syllabus**

- Wealth platforms, feeder funds, retirement savings and tokenised access
- Product governance, target market and investor eligibility
- Distributor oversight, marketing communications and investor understanding
- Lessons from redemption pressure in semi-liquid funds

### Module 8: Risk Management and Supervisory Focus

Brings the programme together around an integrated risk management framework, the issues supervisors are prioritising in private markets, and a practical readiness plan.

**Objectives**

- Build an integrated risk framework for credit, leverage, liquidity and operational risk
- Anticipate supervisory priorities and international standard-setter work on private finance
- Produce a supervisory readiness action plan

**Syllabus**

- Independent risk function, risk appetite and portfolio monitoring
- Operational and third-party risk in fund administration and servicing
- IOSCO and FSB work on private finance and non-bank leverage
- Workshop: seeing the private markets business the way a supervisor does

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, valuation and liquidity exercises and practical templates, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's strategies and investor base.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience of private markets, fund management or fund servicing is helpful. No prior knowledge of specific fund rules is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Thematic Analysis: Emerging Risks in Private Finance (2023)
- IOSCO, Principles for the Valuation of Collective Investment Schemes (2013)
- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
- FSB, Global Monitoring Report on Non-Bank Financial Intermediation (annual)
