## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build supervisors' ability to assess how firms identify and respond to customer vulnerability. Participants will learn the drivers of vulnerability, how firms should adapt products, communications and service for vulnerable customers, and how to assess whether a firm's vulnerable-customer framework is effective in practice rather than just on paper.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level supervisors assessing consumer protection outcomes.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the drivers and dimensions of customer vulnerability
- Describe regulatory expectations for identifying vulnerable customers
- Assess the adequacy of a firm's vulnerable-customer policy
- Evaluate whether frontline staff are equipped to identify vulnerability
- Analyse case examples of tailored treatment for vulnerable customers
- Identify gaps between a firm's stated policy and its practical delivery
- Formulate supervisory conclusions on vulnerable-customer treatment

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Understanding vulnerability (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain the drivers and dimensions of vulnerability*
- Drivers of vulnerability: health, life events, resilience, capability
- Vulnerability as a spectrum, not a fixed category
- Regulatory expectations for identifying vulnerable customers
- The business case for good vulnerable-customer practice
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: how vulnerability shows up in different sectors
- Exercise: map vulnerability drivers to customer scenarios

**Session 2: Assessing firm frameworks (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess the adequacy of a firm's vulnerable-customer policy and frontline delivery*
- What a good vulnerable-customer policy contains
- Frontline identification: training, tools, escalation routes
- Tailoring products, communications and service
- Common weaknesses in practice versus policy
Activities:
- Group exercise: review a sample vulnerable-customer policy
- Role-play: a frontline interaction with a vulnerable customer

**Session 3: Case examples and supervisory assessment (13:30–15:00)**
*Objective: Apply learning to case examples and form supervisory judgements*
- Case examples of good and poor tailored treatment
- Identifying the gap between stated policy and delivery
- Supervisory tools for assessing vulnerable-customer outcomes
- Escalation and intervention where practice falls short
Activities:
- Case study: assess a firm's vulnerable-customer framework and produce supervisory conclusions

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (15:00–15:15)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: vulnerability drivers, policy assessment, practical delivery
- Reinforcing the spectrum view of vulnerability
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Understanding vulnerability (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Assessing firm frameworks (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Case examples and supervisory assessment (13:30–15:00)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (15:00–15:15)

**Key Takeaways**
- Vulnerability is a spectrum that can affect any customer, not a fixed group
- A good policy is meaningless without frontline capability to act on it
- Supervisors should test delivery in practice, not just review documentation
