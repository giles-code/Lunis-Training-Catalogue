## Aim and Purpose

To give the officials who draft and advise on financial services law a practical understanding of how legislative design choices affect regulatory outcomes, enforceability and international compliance. Participants leave able to structure primary and secondary legislation, draft workable perimeter, licensing and enforcement provisions, and keep the law technology-neutral and consistent with international standards.

## What It Covers

The objectives and architecture of financial services law, principles-based and rules-based drafting, primary and secondary legislation and regulator rule-making powers, defining the regulatory perimeter, licensing and authorisation provisions, supervisory and enforcement powers and sanctions, safeguards and appeals, transitional provisions, consultation and impact assessment, and technology-neutral drafting.

## Intended Audience

- Legislative drafters and parliamentary counsel working on financial services, payments and digital asset legislation
- Legal advisers in finance ministries, central banks and other public bodies who instruct drafters or review bills
- Policy officials who prepare drafting instructions and impact assessments for financial legislation

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how legislative design choices shape regulatory effectiveness and international compliance
- Choose between principles-based and rules-based approaches and between primary and secondary legislation
- Draft clear perimeter definitions, licensing provisions and exemptions
- Design proportionate supervisory and enforcement powers with appropriate safeguards
- Plan transitional provisions, consultation and impact assessment
- Apply technology-neutral drafting techniques that keep legislation current

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with drafting exercises on model provisions, ending in a bill review workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to legislation in preparation.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience of legislative drafting or legal advice in government is helpful. No specialist knowledge of financial services is required.

## Core Reading

- OECD (2012), Recommendation of the Council on Regulatory Policy and Governance
- OECD (2014), The Governance of Regulators, OECD Best Practice Principles for Regulatory Policy
- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- FSB (2014), Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions
