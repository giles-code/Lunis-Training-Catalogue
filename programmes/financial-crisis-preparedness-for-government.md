## Aim and Purpose

To give the officials who will take decisions in a financial crisis a practical understanding of the crisis management toolkit, the finance ministry's role within it and the fiscal stakes involved. Participants leave able to assess national preparedness, take informed decisions on resolution and public support, coordinate with domestic and foreign authorities, and lead a credible crisis response under time pressure.

**Coverage:** How financial crises unfold and their fiscal costs, the finance ministry's role in crisis management, resolution regimes and the FSB Key Attributes, bail-in and loss allocation, deposit insurance and investor protection, public support, guarantees and state aid, liquidity support and funding in resolution, domestic and cross-border crisis management groups, simulations, and crisis communication.

## Intended Audience

- Senior officials in finance ministries and treasuries with responsibility for financial stability and crisis management
- Members and secretariats of national financial stability and crisis management committees
- Legal, fiscal and communications advisers who would support ministers in a financial crisis

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how financial crises develop and the channels through which they hit public finances
- Define the finance ministry's role and decision rights in a financial crisis
- Assess the national resolution regime, safety nets and funding arrangements against international standards
- Evaluate when and how public support should be provided and how losses should be allocated
- Design effective domestic and cross-border crisis coordination arrangements
- Lead crisis decision-making and communication through a realistic simulation

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Anatomy of a Financial Crisis

How banking, market and sovereign crises develop, the warning signs that precede them, and the channels through which they damage public finances and the wider economy.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main types and triggers of financial crises
- Identify the bank-sovereign doom loop and other fiscal transmission channels
- Draw lessons from historical and recent crises

**Syllabus**

- Bank runs, contagion, fire sales and liquidity spirals
- Fiscal costs of crises and contingent liabilities
- The bank-sovereign nexus and market confidence
- Case studies of systemic and idiosyncratic failures

### Module 2: Crisis Management Architecture and the Finance Ministry

How responsibilities are divided between the finance ministry, central bank, resolution authority, supervisor and deposit insurer, and how decisions are taken when public funds may be at risk.

**Objectives**

- Map the roles and legal powers of each authority in a crisis
- Define the finance ministry's decision rights and veto points
- Assess the adequacy of national coordination committees and protocols

**Syllabus**

- Financial safety net participants and their mandates
- Decision rights where public funds or fiscal risk are involved
- Financial stability and crisis management committees
- Memoranda of understanding, protocols and escalation

### Module 3: Resolution Regimes and the Key Attributes

What an effective resolution regime contains, how resolution tools work, and how government can assess whether the national regime meets the FSB Key Attributes.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of resolution and its objectives
- Describe the main resolution tools and powers
- Assess the national regime and resolution planning against the Key Attributes

**Syllabus**

- Resolution objectives and the public interest test
- Sale of business, bridge institutions, asset separation and bail-in
- Resolution planning, resolvability and loss-absorbing capacity
- Resolution of insurers, central counterparties and non-banks

### Module 4: Bail-In, Loss Allocation and Public Support

How losses are allocated to shareholders and creditors through bail-in, when public support may be justified, and how to design it to protect taxpayers and limit moral hazard.

**Objectives**

- Explain the creditor hierarchy and no-creditor-worse-off safeguard
- Evaluate the conditions for public support and the instruments available
- Design safeguards to recover public funds and limit moral hazard

**Syllabus**

- Bail-in mechanics, execution and market impact
- Recapitalisation, guarantees, asset purchases and nationalisation
- State aid principles, burden sharing and conditionality
- Recovering public funds and exit from public ownership

### Module 5: Deposit Insurance and Investor Protection

How deposit insurance and investor compensation schemes protect consumers and confidence, how they are funded, and how they interact with resolution.

**Objectives**

- Explain the design features of an effective deposit insurance system
- Assess coverage, funding and payout speed against IADI Core Principles
- Evaluate the role of investor and policyholder protection schemes

**Syllabus**

- Coverage levels, scope and depositor preference
- Ex ante and ex post funding and government backstops
- Payout readiness and the use of funds in resolution
- Investor compensation and policyholder protection schemes

### Module 6: Liquidity Support and Funding in Resolution

How central bank liquidity, government guarantees and resolution funds provide temporary funding in a crisis, and how to protect the public balance sheet when doing so.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish market-wide liquidity, emergency liquidity assistance and resolution funding
- Assess the role of fiscal backstops and indemnities
- Evaluate the risks to the central bank and public balance sheets

**Syllabus**

- Lender of last resort and emergency liquidity assistance
- Resolution funds and temporary public sector funding mechanisms
- Government guarantees of liabilities and their fiscal cost
- Collateral, indemnities and protecting the public balance sheet

### Module 7: Cross-Border Coordination and Crisis Communication

How authorities cooperate when a failing group spans borders, and how ministers and officials communicate with markets, depositors, parliament and the public during a crisis.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of crisis management groups and cooperation agreements
- Anticipate cross-border conflicts over losses, assets and timing
- Design a crisis communication strategy and key messages

**Syllabus**

- Crisis management groups and institution-specific cooperation agreements
- Home and host tensions, ring-fencing and burden sharing
- Communication with markets, depositors, media and parliament
- Weekend resolution: timing, announcements and market opening

### Module 8: Crisis Simulation and Preparedness Planning

Brings the programme together in a realistic crisis simulation in which participants take decisions under time pressure, followed by a review of national preparedness and an action plan.

**Objectives**

- Apply the crisis toolkit to a realistic failing-institution scenario
- Identify gaps in powers, information, funding and coordination
- Produce a national crisis preparedness action plan

**Syllabus**

- Designing and running crisis simulation exercises
- Simulation: a failing systemic institution over a resolution weekend
- Debrief: decisions, information gaps and coordination lessons
- Workshop: a national crisis preparedness action plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with crisis case studies and decision exercises, culminating in a full crisis simulation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the national crisis management framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience of financial stability, fiscal or financial sector policy is helpful. No prior crisis management experience is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB (2014), Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions
- IADI (2014), IADI Core Principles for Effective Deposit Insurance Systems
- FSB (2016), Guiding Principles on the Temporary Funding Needed to Support the Orderly Resolution of a Global Systemically Important Bank
- FSB (2018), Principles on Bail-in Execution
