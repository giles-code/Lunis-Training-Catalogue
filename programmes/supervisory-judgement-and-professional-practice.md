## Aim and Purpose

To equip supervisors with the professional disciplines that turn information into well-reasoned, proportionate and defensible supervisory judgements. Participants leave able to weigh evidence with scepticism, recognise bias, communicate findings clearly in writing and in person, and escalate and act with integrity and independence.

## What It Covers

The nature of supervisory judgement, evidence and professional scepticism, cognitive bias, proportionality and prioritisation, meetings with boards and senior management, difficult conversations, report and letter writing, findings and risk mitigation programmes, escalation, and ethics and independence.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and senior supervisors responsible for risk assessments, firm engagement and supervisory findings
- Team leaders and managers who review, challenge and sign off supervisory judgements and correspondence
- Staff in enforcement, authorisation and policy functions who rely on, or contribute to, supervisory conclusions

## Learning Objectives

- Explain what supervisory judgement is and why it must be exercised under uncertainty
- Evaluate evidence with professional scepticism and recognise the biases that distort decisions
- Apply proportionality to prioritise risks and select the right supervisory response
- Communicate findings clearly and firmly in meetings, reports and supervisory letters
- Design findings and risk mitigation programmes that are specific, measurable and followed up
- Act with integrity and independence, and escalate and defend judgements under challenge

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, drafting exercises and role-play, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's supervisory approach.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervising regulated firms is helpful. The programme is suitable for both newer and experienced supervisors.

## Core Reading

- IMF, The Making of Good Supervision: Learning to Say "No" (Staff Position Note SPN/10/08, 2010)
- IMF, Good Supervision: Lessons from the Field (Working Paper WP/23/181, 2023)
- Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony and Cass R. Sunstein, Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment (2021)
- Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011)
