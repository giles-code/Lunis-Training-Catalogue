## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and practitioners a clear, practical command of the EU's reformed AML/CFT framework, from the directly applicable single rulebook to AMLA's direct and indirect supervision and the strengthened tools of national supervisors and FIUs. Participants leave able to identify what changes for their institution, close gaps early and see AML/CFT controls the way a supervisor will under the new regime.

## What It Covers

The architecture of the EU AML package and its FATF foundations, the AML Regulation single rulebook, customer due diligence and beneficial ownership, crypto-asset service providers and the Transfer of Funds Regulation, AMLD6 and the roles of national supervisors, FIUs and central registers, AMLA's direct supervision and indirect oversight, supervisory colleges and cooperation, the interplay with restrictive measures, and readiness planning for firms and supervisors.

## Intended Audience

- AML/CFT supervisors, inspectors and policy staff at national competent authorities and self-regulatory bodies
- MLROs, compliance officers, financial crime risk and legal staff at credit institutions, financial institutions and crypto-asset service providers
- Senior managers and management body members accountable for the AML/CFT framework and transition planning

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the architecture of the EU AML package and how its Level 1 to Level 3 rulebook is built out
- Apply the AML Regulation's single rulebook to policies, internal controls and the business-wide risk assessment
- Assess customer due diligence, beneficial ownership and reliance arrangements against the new requirements
- Evaluate the obligations on crypto-asset service providers under the AML Regulation and the Transfer of Funds Regulation
- Explain AMLA's direct supervision, indirect oversight and common methodology, and the continuing roles of national supervisors and FIUs
- Design a transition plan that prepares the firm or authority for the new regime

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red-flag exercises and gap-analysis work, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisors or obliged entities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT compliance or supervision is helpful. No legal background or prior knowledge of EU law is required.

## Core Reading

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 on the prevention of the use of the financial system for the purposes of money laundering or terrorist financing (AML Regulation)
- Directive (EU) 2024/1640 on the mechanisms to be put in place by Member States for the prevention of the use of the financial system for the purposes of money laundering or terrorist financing (AMLD6)
- Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 establishing the Authority for Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AMLA Regulation)
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets (Transfer of Funds Regulation)
