## Aim and Purpose

How the agenda, the quality of papers and the volume of information shape what the board actually scrutinises, and what good and poor board information look like.

## Learning Objectives

- Assess whether the agenda gives proper time to strategic and risk issues
- Distinguish a decision-ready board paper from narrative without a clear ask
- Evaluate whether management information is concise, prioritised and exception-based

## Syllabus

- Agenda curation, routine reporting and items carried over or pushed to the end
- Good papers: decision required, options, recommendation, risks and prior challenge
- Information overload and the shortcuts overwhelmed boards take
- Supervisory questions to test papers and information quality
