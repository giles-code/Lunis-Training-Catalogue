## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors, FIU analysts and investigators a practical understanding of crypto-asset financial crime, from the main typologies and laundering techniques to how transactions are traced and attributed. Participants leave able to recognise red flags, test a service provider's due diligence and monitoring, interpret tracing evidence critically and decide on an effective supervisory or investigative response.

**Coverage:** Crypto-asset fundamentals for financial crime work, the FATF standards for virtual assets, criminal typologies, the laundering lifecycle on-chain, mixers and tumblers, privacy coins and unhosted wallets, blockchain tracing and attribution, customer due diligence and monitoring at service providers, the travel rule, suspicious transaction reporting, freezing and seizure, and international cooperation.

## Intended Audience

- AML/CFT supervisors and inspectors responsible for crypto-asset service providers
- FIU analysts handling crypto-related suspicious transaction reports and intelligence
- Law-enforcement and financial crime investigators working on cases involving crypto-assets

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how crypto-asset networks, wallets and service providers work and where financial crime risk arises
- Identify the main typologies and red flags of crypto-enabled crime
- Analyse how proceeds are placed, layered and integrated using crypto-assets and anonymity-enhancing tools
- Interpret blockchain tracing and attribution evidence and recognise its limits
- Assess service providers' customer due diligence, monitoring, travel rule and sanctions controls
- Apply intelligence, freezing, seizure and cooperation tools to build an effective response

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Crypto-Assets and Financial Crime Risk: Foundations

The technical and regulatory grounding for financial crime work: how public blockchains, wallets and service providers operate, why they attract criminals, and what the FATF standards require.

**Objectives**

- Explain addresses, keys, transactions and public ledgers in plain terms
- Describe the roles of exchanges, custodians, brokers and other service providers
- Summarise the FATF standards and risk-based approach for virtual assets

**Syllabus**

- Blockchains, wallets, keys and transaction records
- Service provider business models and points of fiat conversion
- Pseudonymity, speed and cross-border reach as risk factors
- FATF Recommendations 15 and 16 and the risk-based approach

### Module 2: Typologies: How Criminals Use Crypto-Assets

The principal ways crypto-assets are used in crime, from fraud and ransomware to sanctions evasion and terrorist financing, with the red flags supervisors and investigators should look for.

**Objectives**

- Identify the main predicate offences and typologies involving crypto-assets
- Recognise red flags in customer behaviour and transaction patterns
- Assess emerging typologies and their implications for national risk assessments

**Syllabus**

- Investment and romance scams, fraud and money mule networks
- Ransomware, darknet markets and cyber-enabled crime
- Sanctions evasion, proliferation and terrorist financing
- Red-flag indicators and feeding typologies into risk assessment

### Module 3: The Laundering Lifecycle On-Chain

How criminal proceeds move through placement, layering and integration using crypto-assets, and where along the chain the best opportunities for detection and disruption lie.

**Objectives**

- Map placement, layering and integration to crypto-asset techniques
- Explain chain-hopping, cross-chain bridges and decentralised finance as layering tools
- Identify off-ramps and detection points where intervention is most effective

**Syllabus**

- Placement: cash-to-crypto, kiosks, peer-to-peer and over-the-counter brokers
- Layering: rapid hops, chain-hopping, bridges, swaps and decentralised protocols
- Integration: off-ramps, nested services, merchant payments and high-value assets
- Detection points across the lifecycle

### Module 4: Anonymity-Enhancing Tools: Mixers, Privacy Coins and Unhosted Wallets

How mixers and tumblers, privacy-enhancing coins and self-hosted wallets obscure the audit trail, what can still be seen, and the controls and supervisory responses they call for.

**Objectives**

- Explain how mixing, tumbling and coin-joining break transaction links
- Describe how privacy coins and privacy features limit traceability
- Assess service providers' policies on anonymity-enhanced transfers and unhosted wallets

**Syllabus**

- Custodial and non-custodial mixers, tumblers and coin-joining
- Privacy-enhancing coins and privacy features: what remains visible
- Unhosted wallets and peer-to-peer transfers: risk and controls
- Red flags, enhanced due diligence and restrictions on exposure

### Module 5: Tracing and Attribution: Blockchain Analytics in Practice

How investigators and service providers trace funds across public ledgers, cluster and attribute addresses, and how far analytics outputs can be relied on as intelligence or evidence.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main tracing techniques and clustering heuristics
- Interpret risk scores, exposure reports and attribution data critically
- Assess the evidential value and limits of analytics outputs

**Syllabus**

- Traceability on public ledgers: following inputs and outputs
- Clustering, attribution and labelling of services and illicit actors
- Risk scoring, false positives and gaps in coverage
- From analytics to evidence: documentation, reproducibility and expert testimony

### Module 6: Customer Due Diligence and Controls at Service Providers

What effective AML/CFT controls look like at crypto-asset service providers, and how supervisors test customer due diligence, wallet screening, transaction monitoring and travel rule compliance.

**Objectives**

- Assess customer due diligence and ongoing monitoring for crypto-asset customers
- Evaluate wallet screening, transaction monitoring and sanctions controls
- Test travel rule implementation and counterparty service provider due diligence

**Syllabus**

- Customer risk assessment, onboarding and source of funds and wealth
- Wallet screening, transaction monitoring scenarios and alert handling
- The travel rule: originator and beneficiary data and counterparty checks
- Supervisory testing: sample selection, walk-throughs and common deficiencies

### Module 7: From Intelligence to Action: Reporting, Seizure and Cooperation

How suspicious transaction reports become usable intelligence, how crypto-assets are frozen, seized and safely held, and how authorities cooperate across borders and with the private sector.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate the quality of crypto-related suspicious transaction reports
- Explain the practicalities of freezing, seizing and managing crypto-assets
- Use domestic and international cooperation channels effectively

**Syllabus**

- Suspicious transaction reporting: content, quality and FIU analysis
- Freezing and seizure: private keys, service provider cooperation and custody
- Asset management, valuation and return of seized crypto-assets
- International cooperation, information sharing and public-private partnerships

### Module 8: Investigation Workshop: Following the Funds

Brings the programme together in a case exercise in which teams trace illicit funds, assess a service provider's controls and agree a coordinated supervisory, intelligence and enforcement response.

**Objectives**

- Apply typologies, tracing and red flags to a realistic case
- Assess the control failures that allowed the funds to move
- Agree a coordinated response across supervisor, FIU and law enforcement

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing: a fraud and laundering scheme across several service providers
- Tracing exercise and red-flag analysis
- Control failures and supervisory findings
- Workshop: the joint response and action plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with worked cases, red-flag exercises and supervisory or investigative questions, ending in an investigation workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the participants' roles.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT supervision, financial intelligence or financial crime investigation is helpful. No technical background in blockchain is required.
