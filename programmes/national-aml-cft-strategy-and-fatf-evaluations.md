## Aim and Purpose

To give the officials who lead and deliver a country's AML/CFT regime a practical understanding of the FATF standards, the assessment methodology and what effectiveness really means. Participants leave able to lead a national risk assessment, coordinate a whole-of-government response, prepare a credible mutual evaluation submission and design an action plan to address deficiencies or exit increased monitoring.

## What It Covers

The FATF standards and assessment methodology, national risk assessment, national strategy and coordination bodies, the legal and institutional framework, beneficial ownership transparency and registers, the FIU and financial intelligence, investigation, prosecution and confiscation, supervision and preventive measures, international cooperation, preparing for a mutual evaluation, and grey-list exit action plans.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance, justice and interior ministries who lead national AML/CFT policy and coordination
- FIU directors and analysts, financial investigators, prosecutors and asset recovery officials
- Members and secretariats of national AML/CFT coordination committees and mutual evaluation teams

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the FATF Recommendations, the immediate outcomes and how effectiveness is assessed
- Lead or contribute to a national money laundering and terrorist financing risk assessment
- Design national coordination arrangements and a risk-based AML/CFT strategy
- Assess the legal framework, beneficial ownership transparency and operational effectiveness
- Prepare evidence, case studies and statistics for a mutual evaluation
- Develop an action plan to address deficiencies and exit increased monitoring

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over three to four days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with country case studies and practical exercises, including a mock evaluation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the country's evaluation timetable.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT policy, financial intelligence or law enforcement is helpful. No prior involvement in a mutual evaluation is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012, updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- FATF (2013, updated), Methodology for Assessing Technical Compliance with the FATF Recommendations and the Effectiveness of AML/CFT Systems
- FATF (2013), National Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Risk Assessment
- FATF (2023), Guidance on Beneficial Ownership of Legal Persons
