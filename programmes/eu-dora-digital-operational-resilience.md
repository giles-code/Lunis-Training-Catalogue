## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and practitioners a practical command of DORA and its technical standards, from management body accountability and the ICT risk management framework to incident reporting, resilience testing and third-party risk. Participants leave able to judge whether arrangements work in practice rather than on paper, and to see the firm's digital resilience the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** DORA's scope, proportionality and place in the EU rulebook, management body accountability, the ICT risk management framework, incident classification and reporting, digital operational resilience testing and TLPT, ICT third-party risk and the register of information, the oversight framework for critical ICT third-party providers, information sharing, and supervision and enforcement.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, inspectors and ICT risk specialists at national competent authorities
- Information security, ICT risk, operational resilience, outsourcing and compliance staff at financial entities
- Senior managers and management body members accountable for digital operational resilience

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain DORA's scope, proportionality and relationship with sectoral rules and the NIS2 Directive
- Assess an ICT risk management framework and its governance against DORA and its technical standards
- Apply the incident classification criteria and reporting timelines to major ICT-related incidents
- Evaluate digital operational resilience testing programmes, including threat-led penetration testing
- Assess ICT third-party risk management, contractual provisions and the register of information
- Explain the oversight of critical ICT third-party providers and the supervisory and enforcement toolkit

## Syllabus

### Module 1: DORA Foundations: Scope, Proportionality and Architecture

Why DORA harmonises ICT risk rules across the financial sector, which entities it covers, how proportionality and the simplified framework work, and how the Level 1 text is built out through the ESAs' technical standards.

**Objectives**

- Identify the financial entities and ICT third-party service providers within DORA's scope
- Apply proportionality, including the simplified ICT risk management framework
- Navigate DORA's technical standards, guidelines and interaction with sectoral rules

**Syllabus**

- From fragmented sectoral rules to a single digital resilience regulation
- Financial entities in scope, microenterprises and the simplified framework
- Joint ESA technical standards, delegated acts and supervisory convergence
- DORA as sector-specific law alongside NIS2 and outsourcing guidelines

### Module 2: Management Body Accountability and ICT Governance

The management body's ultimate responsibility for ICT risk under DORA, the role of the ICT risk control function, and how supervisors test whether governance is real rather than nominal.

**Objectives**

- Explain the management body's responsibilities for ICT risk, budgets and policies
- Assess the independence and resourcing of the ICT risk control function
- Challenge board reporting, training and the digital operational resilience strategy

**Syllabus**

- Management body duties: approval, oversight, budget and up-to-date ICT knowledge
- Digital operational resilience strategy and ICT risk tolerance
- Three lines of defence, the ICT risk control function and internal audit
- Red flags: delegated accountability, stale strategy and weak board reporting

### Module 3: The ICT Risk Management Framework

DORA's core requirements for identifying, protecting against, detecting, responding to and recovering from ICT risk, including asset inventories, backup and restoration, and learning from incidents and tests.

**Objectives**

- Map the ICT risk management lifecycle to DORA's requirements
- Assess identification, protection, detection, response and recovery capabilities
- Evaluate ICT business continuity and backup arrangements against recovery objectives

**Syllabus**

- Identification: information and ICT assets, dependencies and critical or important functions
- Protection and prevention: access, encryption, patching and change management
- Detection, response and recovery, ICT business continuity and backup policies
- Learning, evolving and crisis communication after incidents and tests

### Module 4: ICT Incident Classification and Reporting

How financial entities manage ICT-related incidents, apply DORA's classification criteria and thresholds, and report major incidents and significant cyber threats, and how supervisors use reports as early warning.

**Objectives**

- Apply the classification criteria to determine whether an incident is major
- Meet the timelines and content requirements for initial, intermediate and final reports
- Use incident reports and root-cause analysis as supervisory intelligence

**Syllabus**

- Incident management process, logging, escalation and client communication
- Classification criteria: clients, duration, geography, data, criticality and economic impact
- Initial notification, intermediate and final reports and voluntary threat notification
- Simulation: classifying and reporting a ransomware incident

### Module 5: Digital Operational Resilience Testing and TLPT

The risk-based testing programme every in-scope entity must run, and the threat-led penetration testing required of selected entities, including scoping, testers, cross-border recognition and supervisory follow-up.

**Objectives**

- Design a proportionate digital operational resilience testing programme
- Explain which entities must perform TLPT and how it is scoped and run
- Assess test findings, remediation and the attestation process

**Syllabus**

- Testing toolkit: vulnerability assessments, scenario tests and source code reviews
- TLPT identification, scope, threat intelligence and red team phases
- Internal and external testers, pooled testing and mutual recognition
- Red flags: narrowed scope, repeat findings and slipping remediation

### Module 6: ICT Third-Party Risk and the Register of Information

Managing ICT third-party risk across the contract lifecycle, the mandatory contractual provisions, concentration and exit, and the register of information as both a compliance tool and a supervisory dataset.

**Objectives**

- Apply DORA's principles for ICT third-party risk and the third-party risk strategy
- Assess contracts against the key provisions, especially for critical or important functions
- Maintain and use the register of information, including subcontracting chains

**Syllabus**

- Pre-contract due diligence, concentration risk and intragroup providers
- Key contractual provisions, audit rights, termination and exit strategies
- Register of information: structure, data quality and submission to authorities
- Subcontracting of ICT services supporting critical or important functions

### Module 7: Oversight of Critical ICT Third-Party Providers

The EU oversight framework for critical ICT third-party providers: designation, the Lead Overseer, joint examination teams, oversight tools and recommendations, and what it means for financial entities and national authorities.

**Objectives**

- Explain the criteria and process for designating critical ICT third-party providers
- Describe the Lead Overseer's powers, oversight plans and recommendations
- Assess the implications for financial entities' own third-party risk management

**Syllabus**

- Criticality assessment, designation and the role of the ESAs
- Lead Overseer, Joint Oversight Network, Oversight Forum and joint examination teams
- Inspections, recommendations, periodic penalty payments and follow-up by authorities
- Third-country providers, the subsidiary requirement and residual entity responsibility

### Module 8: Supervision, Information Sharing and Enforcement

How competent authorities integrate DORA into risk-based supervision, cyber threat information-sharing arrangements, cooperation with cybersecurity authorities, and the remedial measures and penalties available, ending with a roadmap workshop.

**Objectives**

- Integrate DORA into supervisory planning, inspections and firm risk assessment
- Explain information-sharing arrangements and cooperation with NIS2 authorities and CSIRTs
- Apply remedial measures and administrative penalties proportionately

**Syllabus**

- Supervisory planning, ESA peer reviews and convergence on DORA
- Cyber threat information-sharing arrangements among financial entities
- Cooperation with NIS2 authorities, CSIRTs and other competent authorities
- Workshop: a twelve-month DORA supervisory or remediation roadmap

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, simulations and supervisory question sets, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisors or financial entities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of operational risk, ICT risk or its supervision is helpful. No technical cybersecurity background is required.

## Core Reading

- Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 on digital operational resilience for the financial sector (DORA)
- Directive (EU) 2022/2555 on measures for a high common level of cybersecurity across the Union (NIS2 Directive)
- Basel Committee, Principles for Operational Resilience (2021)
- FSB, Enhancing Third-Party Risk Management and Oversight: A Toolkit for Financial Institutions and Financial Authorities (2023)
