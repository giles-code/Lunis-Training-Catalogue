## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of the practical and legal foundations of supervisory independence, and how authorities defend and account for that independence to government and other stakeholders.

## Intended Audience

Mid-to-senior supervisory staff involved in setting or reviewing their authority's strategy, governance, or resourcing decisions.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the concept and rationale for operational independence
- Describe the legal and institutional foundations of independence
- Assess threats to independence in practice
- Evaluate accountability mechanisms that support independence
- Formulate a response to a scenario challenging an authority's independence

## Syllabus

**Session 1: The rationale for independence (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain why operational independence matters*
- The case for supervisory independence
- Legal and institutional foundations of independence
- Independence versus accountability — not a trade-off
- International standards on regulatory independence (IOSCO, Basel)
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: independence challenges participants have observed or experienced

**Session 2: Threats to independence (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess threats to independence and accountability mechanisms*
- Common threats: political pressure, industry capture, budget leverage
- How accountability mechanisms support rather than undermine independence
- Transparency and public reporting as independence safeguards
- International examples of independence being tested
Activities:
- Group exercise: identify independence threats in a sample scenario

**Session 3: Defending independence in practice (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Formulate a response to an independence challenge*
- Practical strategies for defending independence
- Communicating independence to government and public
- Case study: an authority facing a challenge to its independence
Activities:
- Case study: draft a response strategy to a scenario challenging institutional independence

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: rationale, threats, accountability, defence
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: The rationale for independence (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Threats to independence (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Defending independence in practice (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Independence and accountability are mutually reinforcing, not opposing, principles
- Threats to independence are often subtle — budget leverage and informal pressure as much as overt interference
- Transparency is one of the strongest practical defences of institutional independence
