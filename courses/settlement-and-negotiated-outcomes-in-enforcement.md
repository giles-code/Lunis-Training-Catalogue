## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of the practical and strategic considerations in negotiating settlements with firms under investigation, including when settlement is and is not appropriate.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level enforcement investigators and case officers.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the rationale for settlement in enforcement cases
- Describe common settlement mechanisms, including settlement discounts
- Assess when settlement is and is not appropriate
- Apply negotiation techniques relevant to enforcement settlement
- Evaluate a proposed settlement against the public interest

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Why settle? (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain the rationale for settlement*
- Benefits of settlement: speed, certainty, resource efficiency
- Risks of settlement: perceived leniency, inconsistency
- Common settlement mechanisms and discount structures
- Transparency expectations around settled cases
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: settlement practice in participants' own jurisdictions

**Session 2: When to settle and negotiation technique (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess settlement appropriateness and apply negotiation technique*
- Criteria for assessing whether settlement is appropriate
- Cases where settlement should be resisted
- Negotiation techniques specific to enforcement settlement
- Managing power dynamics between authority and firm
Activities:
- Role-play: a settlement negotiation for a sample enforcement case

**Session 3: Public interest and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Evaluate a settlement against the public interest*
- Assessing whether a settlement serves the public interest
- Balancing settlement benefits against deterrence value
- Case study: a contested settlement decision
Activities:
- Case study: evaluate and either approve or reject a proposed settlement

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: rationale, mechanisms, appropriateness, public interest
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Why settle? (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: When to settle and negotiation technique (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Public interest and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Settlement offers real efficiency benefits but must not be allowed to undermine deterrence
- Not every case is suitable for settlement — knowing when to resist is as important as knowing how to negotiate
- The public interest test should be applied explicitly, not assumed, before any settlement is approved
