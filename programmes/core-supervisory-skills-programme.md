## Aim and Purpose

To equip supervisors to carry a firm from desk-based analysis through an on-site visit to a panel-endorsed risk rating and risk mitigation programme. Participants leave able to judge whether a business model is viable and sustainable, gather reliable evidence on governance through well-planned interviews, and defend or challenge a supervisory judgement before senior colleagues.

## What It Covers

Corporate strategy and business models, the purpose and practicalities of business model analysis, financial and qualitative analysis, key vulnerabilities and scoring, governance risk and board dynamics, planning and conducting supervisory interviews, evaluating interview evidence, risk governance and thematic review panels, panel papers and risk mitigation programmes, and presenting and challenging supervisory findings.

## Intended Audience

- Line supervisors and supervisory analysts who carry out firm risk assessments and on-site visits
- Senior supervisors and team leaders who present to, chair or sit on risk governance and thematic review panels
- Specialists from risk, financial crime and policy teams who contribute to firm assessments and panels

## Learning Objectives

- Explain what a business model is and why business model analysis matters to supervisors
- Apply quantitative and qualitative tools to identify a firm's key vulnerabilities and score business model risk
- Assess governance risk, including how the board works in practice and how effectively it challenges management
- Plan and conduct supervisory interviews that produce reliable, well-recorded evidence
- Prepare an analytical panel paper with ratings and a proportionate risk mitigation programme
- Present, defend and challenge supervisory judgements constructively at a risk governance panel

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about two hours each, delivered as a three-day workshop: business model analysis on Day 1, governance risk assessment and interview skills on Day 2, and risk governance panels on Day 3. Running case-study firms link the days, with a management meeting, mock interviews and a mock panel as role plays; it can be run in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, and days can be delivered separately or tailored to the authority's own risk framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervising regulated firms is helpful. No specialist financial or interviewing background is required.

## Core Reading

- IMF, Good Supervision: Lessons from the Field (Working Paper WP/23/181, 2023)
- IMF, The Making of Good Supervision: Learning to Say "No" (Staff Position Note SPN/10/08, 2010)
- Basel Committee, Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015)
- Rebecca Milne and Ray Bull, Investigative Interviewing: Psychology and Practice (1999)
