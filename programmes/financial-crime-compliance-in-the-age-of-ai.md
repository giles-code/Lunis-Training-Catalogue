## Aim and Purpose

To equip financial crime professionals with a practical command of the risk-based approach and of how AI is changing both the threat and the tools used to counter it. Participants leave able to strengthen risk assessment, due diligence, monitoring, screening and reporting, govern AI-driven controls, and show supervisors that the firm's framework works in practice, not just on paper.

**Coverage:** The risk-based approach and FATF standards, business-wide risk assessment, customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence, transaction monitoring and machine learning, sanctions and adverse media screening, fraud and authorised push payment scams, deepfakes and synthetic identity, generative-AI-enabled crime, model governance for AML tools, suspicious activity reporting, and evidencing effectiveness to supervisors.

## Intended Audience

- MLROs, deputy MLROs and heads of financial crime compliance
- Financial crime, AML, sanctions and fraud analysts, investigators and team leaders
- Compliance, risk, data and technology staff who design, validate or run financial crime systems

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Apply the risk-based approach and FATF standards to the firm's business-wide risk assessment and controls
- Design proportionate customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence, including digital identity verification
- Evaluate machine learning in transaction monitoring, sanctions screening and fraud detection
- Identify AI-enabled threats, including deepfakes, synthetic identities and generative-AI-enabled scams
- Govern AI-driven financial crime tools through model risk management, explainability and fairness testing
- Evidence the effectiveness of the financial crime framework to the board and supervisors

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Risk-Based Approach and Supervisory Expectations

The foundations of an effective financial crime framework: FATF standards, the risk-based approach, the MLRO's role and how supervisors now judge effectiveness rather than technical compliance alone.

**Objectives**

- Explain FATF standards and the risk-based approach as they apply to the firm
- Define the accountabilities of the board, senior management and MLRO
- Distinguish technical compliance from effectiveness in supervisory assessment

**Syllabus**

- FATF Recommendations, immediate outcomes and national risk assessments
- Governance, MLRO independence, resourcing and accountability
- How supervisors assess financial crime frameworks
- Seeing the firm's controls the way a supervisor does

### Module 2: Business-Wide Risk Assessment

Building a business-wide risk assessment that genuinely drives controls and resourcing, drawing on data rather than assertion and covering money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions, proliferation financing and fraud.

**Objectives**

- Design a business-wide risk assessment methodology linked to controls
- Use customer, product, channel and geographic data to evidence inherent risk
- Assess control effectiveness and residual risk credibly

**Syllabus**

- Inherent risk factors: customers, products, channels and geographies
- Data-driven risk scoring and its limitations
- Control effectiveness testing and residual risk
- Linking the assessment to risk appetite, policy and resourcing

### Module 3: Customer Due Diligence and Digital Identity

Proportionate customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence in a digital onboarding world, including beneficial ownership, politically exposed persons and the reliability of remote identity verification.

**Objectives**

- Apply risk-based CDD, EDD and ongoing monitoring
- Evaluate digital identity verification and its vulnerabilities
- Identify beneficial ownership and politically exposed person risks

**Syllabus**

- CDD, simplified and enhanced due diligence and periodic review
- Beneficial ownership, complex structures and PEP screening
- Remote onboarding, biometrics and FATF digital identity guidance
- Perpetual KYC and event-driven review

### Module 4: Transaction Monitoring and Machine Learning

How rules-based and machine learning approaches to transaction monitoring work, what they can realistically achieve, and how to tune, test and combine them to improve detection rather than just reduce alerts.

**Objectives**

- Compare rules-based, machine learning and network analytics approaches
- Evaluate tuning, thresholds and false positive reduction claims
- Design alert handling and investigation workflows that support quality

**Syllabus**

- Scenario design, thresholds and below-the-line testing
- Supervised and unsupervised learning, anomaly detection and graph analytics
- False positives versus missed detection: measuring true performance
- Alert triage, case management and AI-assisted investigation

### Module 5: Sanctions Screening and Adverse Media

Designing and calibrating sanctions, PEP and adverse media screening, including fuzzy matching and natural language processing, and managing evasion risks from ownership structures and trade.

**Objectives**

- Calibrate name and payment screening, including fuzzy matching
- Evaluate natural language processing for adverse media monitoring
- Identify sanctions evasion typologies relevant to the firm

**Syllabus**

- Screening lists, matching logic and threshold calibration
- Ownership and control, and circumvention through intermediaries
- Adverse media analytics and alert quality
- Testing and assuring screening effectiveness

### Module 6: Fraud and Authorised Push Payment Scams

The fraud threats facing firms and their customers, especially authorised push payment scams and money mules, and how behavioural analytics and real-time scoring help to stop losses.

**Objectives**

- Identify fraud typologies, including authorised push payment scams and mule networks
- Evaluate real-time fraud scoring and behavioural analytics
- Align fraud and AML functions to share intelligence and protect customers

**Syllabus**

- Investment, romance, impersonation and purchase scams
- Money mule detection and account takeover
- Behavioural biometrics, device intelligence and payee verification
- Customer protection, reimbursement and fraud and AML convergence

### Module 7: Deepfakes, Synthetic Identities and AI-Enabled Crime

How criminals use generative AI to create synthetic identities, forge documents, impersonate customers and executives, and scale social engineering, and how the firm builds resilience.

**Objectives**

- Recognise generative-AI-enabled typologies and red flags
- Assess the resilience of onboarding and authentication to deepfakes
- Design layered controls and staff awareness against AI-enabled attacks

**Syllabus**

- Synthetic identities and AI-generated documents
- Voice and video deepfakes in onboarding and executive impersonation
- AI-generated phishing, scam content and automated social engineering
- Liveness detection, call-back controls and layered defences

### Module 8: Governing AI in Financial Crime Compliance

Applying model risk management to AI-driven financial crime tools, so that the firm can explain, validate, monitor and defend them, and avoid unfair or discriminatory outcomes.

**Objectives**

- Apply model risk management to AML, screening and fraud models
- Evaluate explainability, human oversight and audit trails
- Assess bias and de-risking effects on customers

**Syllabus**

- Model inventory, validation and performance monitoring
- Explainability for investigators, auditors and supervisors
- Vendor tools, data quality and third-party dependency
- Bias, financial exclusion and unwarranted de-risking

### Module 9: Suspicious Activity Reporting and Intelligence Sharing

Producing high-quality suspicious activity reports, working effectively with the national FIU and law enforcement, and using public-private partnerships and information sharing to improve detection.

**Objectives**

- Apply reporting thresholds and write high-quality, useful reports
- Manage tipping-off, consent and exit decisions
- Use information sharing and partnerships to improve intelligence

**Syllabus**

- Suspicion, reporting thresholds and report quality
- Working with the national FIU and law enforcement
- Tipping-off, defensive reporting and relationship exit
- Public-private partnerships and privacy-preserving data sharing

### Module 10: Evidencing Effectiveness to Supervisors

Brings the programme together by showing how to measure, test and report the effectiveness of the financial crime framework, prepare for supervisory review and plan the next stage of AI adoption.

**Objectives**

- Define metrics and management information that demonstrate effectiveness
- Prepare for supervisory inspections, thematic reviews and independent reviews
- Produce a financial crime and AI action plan for the firm

**Syllabus**

- Effectiveness metrics, quality assurance and board reporting
- Common supervisory findings and remediation programmes
- Inspection readiness: files, walkthroughs and interviews
- Workshop: the firm's financial crime and AI action plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine briefings with typology case studies, alert and report review exercises and group workshops, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and risk profile.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience of AML, sanctions or fraud compliance in a regulated firm is helpful. No data science background is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- FATF, Opportunities and Challenges of New Technologies for AML/CFT (2021)
- FATF, Guidance on Digital Identity (2020)
