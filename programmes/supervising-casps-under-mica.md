## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a rigorous, practical command of MiCA and the wider EU rulebook that surrounds it, from the technology and business models through to authorisation, ongoing supervision, enforcement and crisis management. Participants leave able to look beyond a firm's documentation, identify red flags early and apply MiCA's supervisory powers proportionately, working with ESMA, the EBA and other national competent authorities.

**Coverage:** Distributed ledger technology and the crypto ecosystem, MiCA's scope, taxonomy and rulebook, CASP business models, governance and prudential requirements, authorisation and risk-based supervision, ICT risk under DORA, custody and safeguarding, market abuse, AML/CFT and the Travel Rule, asset-referenced and e-money tokens, passporting and cooperation, and enforcement, crisis management and emerging risks.

## Intended Audience

- Authorisation, supervision and inspection staff responsible for crypto-asset service providers and token issuers
- AML/CFT, market integrity, ICT risk and enforcement specialists covering digital finance
- Heads of department and senior managers setting the authority's crypto-asset supervisory strategy

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how distributed ledgers, crypto-assets and CASP business models work and where risk sits
- Apply MiCA's scope, taxonomy and Level 1 to Level 3 rulebook to authorisation and ongoing supervision
- Assess CASP governance, prudential resources, custody and operational resilience, including under DORA
- Evaluate market abuse, AML/CFT, Travel Rule and sanctions risks and the controls that address them
- Operate passporting, information exchange and colleges with other authorities, ESMA and the EBA
- Apply graduated enforcement and crisis tools, and anticipate emerging risks in digital finance

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Blockchain and Distributed Ledgers for Supervisors

How blockchain and distributed ledgers work in digital finance, where control really sits, and the technological failure modes that drive consumer harm, prudential loss and operational incidents at CASPs.

**Objectives**

- Explain how distributed ledgers validate, record and execute transactions
- Distinguish public, permissioned and hybrid ledgers and the supervisory leverage each allows
- Identify the true control points and failure modes behind crypto incidents

**Syllabus**

- Digital money versus electronic money: who owes what to whom
- Validation, cryptography, tamper-evident records and smart contracts
- Control points: upgrades, pause functions, administrator keys, forks and listings
- Failure modes: code flaws, oracle manipulation, bridges and off-chain dependencies

### Module 2: The Crypto Ecosystem: Assets, Tokenisation and Market Structure

Maps the participants in crypto markets, from protocol developers and token issuers to trading venues, custodians and end users, and how value, risk and accountability flow between them.

**Objectives**

- Map ecosystem participants to accountability and control points
- Analyse the economic incentives that create concentration and integrity risk
- Recognise red flags in tokenisation and market infrastructure providers

**Syllabus**

- Networks, issuers, validators, wallets, venues, brokers and market makers
- Tokenisation: tokenised assets, utility tokens and stablecoins
- Incentives, concentration and conflicts in crypto market structure
- Resilience of networks, bridges, oracles and infrastructure providers

### Module 3: MiCA Fundamentals: Objectives, Scope and Architecture

MiCA's objectives, perimeter and taxonomy, how Level 1 is built out through ESMA and EBA technical standards and guidelines, and where gaps and ambiguity create supervisory risk.

**Objectives**

- Explain MiCA's objectives and its place within the wider EU financial services rulebook
- Apply MiCA's taxonomy and perimeter, including the boundary with financial instruments
- Use the full Level 1, 2 and 3 rulebook in authorisation and thematic work

**Syllabus**

- Consumer protection, market integrity and financial stability objectives
- ARTs, EMTs and other crypto-assets; the boundary with MiFID financial instruments
- Perimeter questions: NFTs issued in series, fractionalisation and DeFi
- Roles of the Commission, ESMA, the EBA and national competent authorities

### Module 4: CASP Business Models and the CeFi–DeFi Spectrum

The business models of trading platforms, brokers, custodians, issuers and other providers, where MiCA obligations attach to each, and how supervisors use business model analysis to test viability and conflicts.

**Objectives**

- Map crypto business models to MiCA's crypto-asset services
- Identify the prudential and conduct risks of each model
- Use business model analysis to stress-test viability and conflicts of interest

**Syllabus**

- The CeFi–DeFi spectrum and where MiCA obligations attach in practice
- Trading platforms, exchange, execution, custody, advice and portfolio management services
- Red flags: venues acting as market maker, opaque fees, own-token reserves
- Group structures designed to fragment regulatory obligations

### Module 5: Governance, Risk Management and Internal Controls in CASPs

How to look beneath governance documentation to judge whether a CASP's management body, control functions and internal controls work in practice, and how to respond when they do not.

**Objectives**

- Assess the management body, suitability and conflicts of interest controls against MiCA
- Evaluate the independence, resources and influence of risk, compliance and internal audit
- Apply proportionate intervention to governance and control weaknesses

**Syllabus**

- MiCA governance requirements: management body, shareholders, conflicts and complaints
- Three lines of defence and risk appetite in a crypto-asset business
- Red flags: founder dominance, nominal independence, marginalised control functions
- Governance of CASPs within wider groups, including non-EU parents

### Module 6: Prudential Risk, Financial Resources and Sustainability

MiCA's prudential requirements for CASPs and token issuers, how capital and liquidity behave across the crypto market cycle, and how supervisors identify financially fragile firms before they fail.

**Objectives**

- Apply MiCA's own funds requirements to different CASP classes and issuer types
- Assess liquidity, revenue volatility and reliance on affiliate funding
- Select supervisory tools from enhanced monitoring to recovery plan requirements

**Syllabus**

- Permanent minimum capital and fixed overhead requirements for CASPs
- Own funds, reserve and liquidity requirements for ART and EMT issuers
- Prudential red flags: thin capital, untested projections, affiliate exposures
- Financial analysis, stress testing, peer benchmarking and early warning indicators

### Module 7: Authorising Crypto-Asset Service Providers

Running a consistent, proportionate MiCA authorisation gateway: assessing the application dossier, substance and group dependencies, and resolving material issues before authorisation rather than deferring them to supervision.

**Objectives**

- Assess the programme of operations, governance, prudential safeguards and ICT arrangements
- Test substance, group dependencies and outsourcing to prevent letter-box entities
- Apply service-specific focus to trading platforms, brokers and custodians

**Syllabus**

- Gateway design: completeness thresholds, risk-based prioritisation and early challenge
- Forward-looking review of business plans, growth assumptions and scalability
- Transitional regime, notification route for licensed firms and prior regulatory history
- Authorising a cross-border hub: passporting implications from day one

### Module 8: Risk-Based Supervision of CASPs in Practice

Designing and operating a risk-based supervisory model for CASPs: prioritising resources, monitoring between engagements, running inspections and using MiCA's supervisory powers to drive and verify remediation.

**Objectives**

- Design a risk framework that prioritises CASPs by impact and probability of harm
- Use regulatory data and market intelligence to set escalation triggers
- Plan, conduct and follow up on-site inspections of CASPs

**Syllabus**

- Firm risk assessment, supervisory planning and resource allocation
- Early warning signals: rapid growth, control function turnover, incidents
- Inspection methodology: document requests, interviews, walkthroughs and customer journeys
- Using MiCA's supervisory powers and keeping a defensible decision trail

### Module 9: Operational Resilience: ICT Risk and DORA

How DORA applies to CASPs alongside MiCA's organisational requirements, the ICT risks specific to crypto operations, and how supervisors assess resilience and use incident reports as early warning.

**Objectives**

- Explain DORA's ICT risk, incident reporting, testing and third-party requirements for CASPs
- Identify crypto-specific ICT risks in key management, cloud and on-chain/off-chain links
- Assess resilience arrangements and respond when standards are not met

**Syllabus**

- DORA applied to CASPs and its interaction with MiCA
- Key management, hot and cold wallet segregation and cloud concentration
- Incident classification and reporting as a supervisory early warning tool
- Resilience testing, register of information and third-party exit provisions

### Module 10: Wallets, Custody and Safeguarding of Crypto-Assets

How crypto-asset custody works in practice, from hot and cold storage to multi-party computation and sub-custody chains, and how supervisors verify that client assets are safeguarded and segregated.

**Objectives**

- Explain custody architectures and how each affects the risk to client assets
- Identify key management, reconciliation, sub-custody and asset misuse risks
- Verify custody arrangements against MiCA safeguarding and liability requirements

**Syllabus**

- Keys, wallets, hardware security modules, multi-signature and MPC
- Custody policies, client agreements, segregation and return of assets
- Red flags: weak reconciliation, single-person key access, affiliated sub-custody
- Custodian liability for loss and client assets on insolvency

### Module 11: Market Integrity: Preventing Crypto Market Abuse

MiCA's market abuse regime and how it adapts established market abuse concepts to crypto, the manipulation schemes specific to crypto markets, and how supervisors assess surveillance and use order reports as intelligence.

**Objectives**

- Explain MiCA's prohibitions on insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and manipulation
- Detect crypto-native manipulation, including wash trading, spoofing and MEV extraction
- Assess CASP surveillance systems and suspicious transaction and order reporting

**Syllabus**

- Inside information in token issuance, listings and CASP operations
- Wash trading, pump-and-dump schemes, spoofing and MEV
- Surveillance calibration, investigation workflows and STORs as intelligence
- Cross-venue analysis, enforcement referral and cross-border cooperation

### Module 12: Financial Crime: AML/CFT and the Travel Rule

Illicit finance typologies in crypto markets, the EU AML framework and Transfer of Funds Regulation as they apply to CASPs, and how supervisors test controls and coordinate with the national FIU.

**Objectives**

- Identify money laundering and terrorist financing typologies in crypto markets
- Evaluate due diligence, transaction monitoring, Travel Rule compliance and the MLRO function
- Coordinate AML/CFT findings with the AML supervisor and the national FIU

**Syllabus**

- Typologies: mixers, high-risk jurisdictions, money mules and rapid layering
- Blockchain analytics: capabilities, limits and use as a supervisory check
- The Travel Rule under the Transfer of Funds Regulation, including self-hosted wallets
- The EU AML package and the interaction between MiCA and AMLR

### Module 13: Stablecoins: Asset-Referenced and E-Money Tokens

How ARTs and EMTs are structured and regulated under MiCA Titles III and IV, the reserve and redemption mechanics that determine stability, and the EBA's role for significant tokens.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish fiat-backed, collateralised and algorithmic designs and their risk profiles
- Evaluate reserve quality, liquidity and redemption resilience under stress
- Explain significance classification and the supervisory handover to the EBA

**Syllabus**

- Stablecoin models and lessons from algorithmic stablecoin collapses
- Reserve composition, custody, audit and redemption at par on demand
- EMTs and electronic money: interplay with PSD2 and the payments framework
- Significant tokens: EBA supervision, colleges and recovery and redemption plans

### Module 14: Passporting, Sanctions and Supervisory Cooperation

How MiCA passporting works for home and host authorities, the sanctions obligations that apply to crypto operations, and the cooperation mechanisms linking national competent authorities, ESMA and the EBA.

**Objectives**

- Manage home and host responsibilities for passporting CASPs
- Assess sanctions screening, including wallet screening and self-hosted wallet controls
- Operate information exchange and coordinated responses to cross-border concerns

**Syllabus**

- Passport notifications, reverse solicitation limits and host authority powers
- Letter-box risk, substance and home–host information gaps
- Significant CASPs, ESMA convergence tools and peer reviews
- Sanctions evasion typologies and EU restrictive measures in crypto markets

### Module 15: Enforcement, Crisis Management and Emerging Risks

Using MiCA's graduated enforcement toolkit, managing a CASP in distress through to orderly wind-down, and anticipating emerging risks in digital finance, ending with a capstone crisis simulation.

**Objectives**

- Apply MiCA's supervisory measures and penalties proportionately and with sound process
- Manage a CASP crisis from early distress to orderly wind-down and cross-border coordination
- Assess emerging risks and their implications for supervisory strategy and capability

**Syllabus**

- From supervisory measures to fines, public warnings and withdrawal of authorisation
- Lessons from major crypto exchange and crypto lender failures
- Emerging risks: DeFi, tokenised infrastructure, AI-driven trading and the digital euro
- Workshop: CASP failure crisis simulation

## Structure and Format

Fifteen modules of about five hours each, organised in five pathways and delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a full programme over several weeks or pathway by pathway. Each module combines pre-reading and a technical briefing with case studies, red-flag exercises and supervisory question sets, and pathways or modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of authorising or supervising financial firms is helpful. No technical background in blockchain is required, as the first pathway builds the foundations.

## Core Reading

- Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 on markets in crypto-assets (MiCA)
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets (Transfer of Funds Regulation)
- Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 on digital operational resilience for the financial sector (DORA)
- FSB, High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Crypto-Asset Activities and Markets (2023)
