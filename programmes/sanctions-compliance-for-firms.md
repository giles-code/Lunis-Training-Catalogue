## Aim and Purpose

To equip practitioners with a working understanding of how financial sanctions and related trade controls operate, and the practical controls needed to comply with them across clients, payments and transactions. Participants leave able to apply ownership and control tests, manage screening and alerts, recognise evasion, handle licences and frozen assets, and evidence a sound framework to supervisors.

**Coverage:** How sanctions regimes work, ownership and control, name, payment and vessel screening, evasion typologies including trade-based and crypto-asset evasion, export controls and dual-use goods, correspondent banking and payments, licensing and frozen assets, investigations and breach reporting, and governance, assurance and testing.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance officers, MLROs, sanctions specialists and financial crime analysts
- Payments, operations, trade finance and client onboarding staff who handle screening alerts or transactions
- Front-office, relationship and product staff in firms with cross-border clients, payments or trade exposure

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how UN, regional and national sanctions regimes are made, applied and enforced
- Apply ownership and control tests and identify indirect exposure to designated persons
- Design and calibrate screening of clients, payments, vessels and counterparties
- Recognise evasion typologies, including trade-based, maritime and crypto-asset evasion
- Manage licences, frozen assets, investigations and the reporting of breaches
- Evaluate the firm's sanctions governance, testing and assurance, and agree improvements

## Syllabus

### Module 1: How Sanctions Regimes Work

The architecture of financial sanctions: who imposes them, the main types of measure, how lists are maintained and updated, and why sanctions obligations differ from anti-money laundering obligations.

**Objectives**

- Explain the roles of UN, regional and national sanctions authorities
- Distinguish asset freezes, financial, sectoral and trade restrictions
- Identify the regimes that apply to the firm through nationality, currency and location

**Syllabus**

- Purpose and policy objectives of sanctions, and how designations are made
- Types of measure: asset freezes, sectoral, investment, services and trade restrictions
- Jurisdictional reach: nationality, territory, currency and extraterritorial effect
- Strict liability, criminal and civil enforcement, and reputational consequences

### Module 2: Ownership, Control and Indirect Exposure

How sanctions extend beyond listed names to entities owned or controlled by designated persons, and how firms identify and evidence indirect exposure through complex structures.

**Objectives**

- Apply aggregate ownership and control tests to corporate structures
- Identify control through nominees, trusts, family members and contractual rights
- Document ownership and control decisions defensibly

**Syllabus**

- Ownership thresholds, aggregation and the meaning of control
- Making funds available indirectly: the prohibition and its practical scope
- Opaque structures, nominees, divestments and restructurings designed to evade
- Exercise: mapping a complex ownership chain

### Module 3: Screening Clients, Payments and Vessels

Designing, calibrating and operating screening across clients, counterparties, payments, securities and vessels, and managing alerts consistently and at scale.

**Objectives**

- Design a screening framework proportionate to the firm's exposure
- Calibrate fuzzy matching, list coverage and thresholds, and justify the settings
- Disposition alerts consistently with a clear audit trail

**Syllabus**

- Customer, counterparty, payment and securities screening: what, when and against which lists
- Fuzzy matching, transliteration, thresholds and false-positive management
- Vessel, port and trade document screening
- Exercise: dispositioning a set of screening alerts

### Module 4: Evasion Typologies: Trade, Maritime and Crypto-Assets

How designated persons and states evade sanctions through front companies, trade, shipping and crypto-assets, and the red flags that should prompt investigation.

**Objectives**

- Recognise common evasion techniques and the red flags they leave
- Identify trade-based and maritime evasion, including transhipment and vessel deception
- Assess crypto-asset evasion risk and the blockchain analytics used to detect it

**Syllabus**

- Front companies, intermediaries, third-country transhipment and wire stripping
- Maritime deception: flag hopping, transponder manipulation and ship-to-ship transfers
- Crypto-asset evasion: mixers, exchanges in high-risk jurisdictions and wallet screening
- Case studies of evasion schemes and the controls that missed them

### Module 5: Export Controls, Dual-Use Goods and Trade Finance

Where trade and export controls overlap with financial sanctions, the role of the financial sector in preventing proliferation and diversion, and the due diligence expected in trade finance.

**Objectives**

- Explain how export controls and dual-use regimes interact with financial sanctions
- Identify proliferation financing and diversion risk in trade transactions
- Apply proportionate due diligence to trade finance and goods-related payments

**Syllabus**

- Export control lists, dual-use goods and end-use controls
- Proliferation financing obligations and the FATF standards
- Trade finance red flags: goods, routes, pricing and parties
- Common high-priority goods and diversion through third countries

### Module 6: Correspondent Banking, Payments and Intermediaries

Sanctions risk in payment chains, correspondent relationships and intermediated business, including message transparency, nested relationships and reliance on others' controls.

**Objectives**

- Assess sanctions risk in correspondent, nested and intermediated relationships
- Apply payment transparency standards and detect stripped or incomplete messages
- Evaluate the limits of reliance on counterparties' screening

**Syllabus**

- Correspondent and nested relationships: due diligence and ongoing review
- Payment message transparency, originator and beneficiary information
- Rejecting versus blocking, and handling payments in transit
- Fast payments, cross-border flows and real-time screening constraints

### Module 7: Licensing, Frozen Assets, Investigations and Breach Reporting

What firms must do when they hold frozen assets or need a licence, how to investigate a potential breach, and how to report to the authority.

**Objectives**

- Manage frozen assets and reporting obligations correctly
- Apply for, rely on and track general and specific licences
- Investigate potential breaches and decide on voluntary disclosure

**Syllabus**

- Freezing, holding and reporting frozen funds and economic resources
- General and specific licences, wind-down periods and licence conditions
- Investigating a potential breach: facts, root cause and remediation
- Reporting breaches and suspicions to the authority and the national FIU

### Module 8: Governance, Risk Assessment, Testing and Assurance

Brings the programme together into a sanctions compliance framework the firm can evidence: risk assessment, policies, accountability, management information, testing and continuous improvement.

**Objectives**

- Build a sanctions risk assessment that drives proportionate controls
- Test screening effectiveness and evidence assurance to the board and supervisors
- Produce an action plan to strengthen the firm's sanctions framework

**Syllabus**

- Sanctions risk assessment, policy and senior management accountability
- Screening tuning, data quality testing and model validation
- Management information, horizon scanning and responding to new designations
- Workshop: sanctions framework gap analysis and action plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a three- to four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies, typologies and practical screening exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business and exposure.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of financial crime compliance, payments or client onboarding is helpful. No prior specialist sanctions knowledge is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- FATF, Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers (2021)
- FSB, Enhancing Cross-border Payments: Stage 3 Roadmap (2020)
- Basel Committee, Compliance and the Compliance Function in Banks (2005)
