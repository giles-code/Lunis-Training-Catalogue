## Aim and Purpose

To give investigators and analysts a clear, practical understanding of the financial services firms they meet in their cases and the information those firms hold. Participants leave able to identify where relevant records are likely to sit, frame precise requests, read the material that comes back and spot what is missing.

**Coverage:** How the financial system fits together, banks and correspondent banking, payment and e-money firms, investment firms, brokers and funds, trust and company service providers, crypto-asset service providers, firms' due diligence, record-keeping and reporting obligations, and making and reading requests for records.

## Intended Audience

- Police and agency investigators new to financial investigation
- FIU, intelligence and asset recovery staff who request or analyse financial records
- Customs, tax and other enforcement officers who need a working knowledge of financial services firms

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how the main types of financial services firm operate and connect
- Identify the records each type of firm is likely to hold
- Describe firms' customer due diligence, record-keeping and reporting obligations
- Frame precise, lawful requests for financial information
- Read and interpret statements, payment messages and onboarding files
- Recognise gaps, inconsistencies and red flags in financial records

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Financial System for Investigators

An introduction to how money moves through the financial system, the main types of firm and how they are regulated, and why understanding them makes investigators more effective.

**Objectives**

- Describe the main sectors and types of financial services firm
- Explain how payment, clearing and settlement systems connect firms
- Outline how firms are licensed and supervised

**Syllabus**

- The main sectors: banking, payments, investment, funds and fiduciary services
- How money moves: payment systems, clearing and settlement
- Licensing, supervision and the role of the supervisor
- Why knowing the firm matters to an investigation

### Module 2: Banks and Correspondent Banking

How retail, private and corporate banks operate, how correspondent relationships move money across borders, and what account, payment and customer records banks hold.

**Objectives**

- Explain retail, private and corporate banking products
- Describe correspondent banking and cross-border payment chains
- Identify the records banks hold and where they sit

**Syllabus**

- Current, savings, loan and trade finance products
- Private banking and wealth management
- Correspondent banking, nested relationships and payment messages
- Bank records: onboarding files, statements, payment data and alerts

### Module 3: Payment and E-Money Firms

How payment institutions, e-money issuers, money remitters and card schemes work, how they differ from banks, and the transaction and device data they hold.

**Objectives**

- Explain the business models of payment, e-money and remittance firms
- Describe the roles of card issuers, acquirers, schemes and payment processors
- Identify the transaction, account and device data these firms hold

**Syllabus**

- Payment institutions, e-money issuers and money remitters
- Cards: issuers, acquirers, schemes and merchants
- Agents, distributors and safeguarded customer funds
- Records: transaction logs, IP and device data and merchant files

### Module 4: Investment Firms, Brokers and Funds

How investment firms, brokers, custodians and investment funds hold and move client assets, and the account, trading and investor records they maintain.

**Objectives**

- Explain the roles of brokers, wealth managers, custodians and fund administrators
- Describe how funds are structured and how investors subscribe and redeem
- Identify trading, custody and investor records relevant to investigations

**Syllabus**

- Brokers, wealth managers and portfolio managers
- Custody, nominee accounts and omnibus structures
- Funds, managers, administrators and transfer agents
- Records: client files, trade data, custody statements and investor registers

### Module 5: Trust and Company Service Providers

What trust and company service providers do, how they form and administer companies, trusts and foundations, and the ownership and control information they hold.

**Objectives**

- Explain the services trust and company service providers offer
- Describe how companies, trusts and foundations are formed and administered
- Identify beneficial ownership and control records held by providers

**Syllabus**

- Company formation, registered office and nominee services
- Trusts and foundations: trustees, settlors, protectors and beneficiaries
- Beneficial ownership information and registers
- Records: due diligence files, minutes, ledgers and bank mandates

### Module 6: Crypto-Asset Service Providers

How crypto-asset exchanges, custodians and brokers operate, how they connect to the traditional financial system, and the customer and transaction records they hold.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main crypto-asset service provider business models
- Describe how fiat on-ramps and off-ramps connect to banks and payment firms
- Identify customer, wallet and travel rule records held by providers

**Syllabus**

- Exchanges, custodial wallet providers, brokers and kiosks
- Fiat on-ramps and off-ramps and banking relationships
- Customer files, wallet addresses and blockchain transaction data
- Travel rule information and requests across providers

### Module 7: What Firms Must Keep: Due Diligence, Records and Reports

The AML/CFT obligations that shape what firms know about their customers, how long records are kept, and how internal alerts and suspicious transaction reports are produced.

**Objectives**

- Explain customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence obligations
- Describe record-keeping requirements and retention periods
- Understand how monitoring alerts and suspicious transaction reports are generated

**Syllabus**

- Customer due diligence, beneficial ownership and enhanced checks
- Record-keeping: what must be kept and for how long
- Transaction monitoring, alerts and internal investigations
- Suspicious transaction reporting and tipping-off constraints

### Module 8: Requesting and Reading Records: Workshop

Brings the programme together in a practical exercise in which participants frame requests to different firms, read the records produced and identify gaps and lines of enquiry.

**Objectives**

- Draft precise, proportionate requests for financial records
- Read statements, payment messages and onboarding files accurately
- Identify gaps, inconsistencies and follow-up enquiries

**Syllabus**

- Choosing the right firm, power and point of contact
- Drafting requests: scope, period, format and confidentiality
- Reading sample records from banks, payment and crypto-asset firms
- Workshop: from records to lines of enquiry

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a plain-language briefing with sample records and request-drafting exercises, ending in a records workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the sectors most relevant to participants' cases.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior knowledge of financial services is required. Some experience of investigation or intelligence work is helpful.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012), Operational Issues: Financial Investigations Guidance
- FATF (2016), Guidance on Correspondent Banking Services
- FATF (2021), Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers
