## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a clear understanding of how AI agents are beginning to act for firms and consumers, and how existing supervisory frameworks apply when software, not people, initiates transactions and decisions. Participants leave able to challenge firms on agent governance, identity, authority and controls, identify red flags and contribute to the authority's policy response.

## What It Covers

AI agents and levels of autonomy, the four-layer architecture of agentic finance, use cases and market structure, know your agent and trust frameworks, governance of autonomous decisions, agent-initiated payments and settlement, agents in markets, resilience and security, financial crime, conduct and liability, and supervisory strategy and policy.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors of banks, payment and e-money firms, investment firms and crypto-asset service providers adopting AI agents
- Policy, innovation, payments and financial crime specialists assessing agent-driven business models
- Senior managers shaping the authority's response to AI and autonomous finance

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how AI agents work, what makes them different from earlier automation and where they are entering finance
- Analyse agentic finance through its governance, decision, asset and settlement layers
- Assess how firms establish agent identity, delegated authority and trust
- Evaluate governance, resilience and market integrity risks where agents act with limited human intervention
- Identify financial crime, consumer protection and liability issues in agent-initiated transactions
- Design a supervisory and policy response to agentic finance, drawing on FSB and other international work

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, grouped into four pathways and delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with scenario exercises, red flags and supervisory questions, and pathways or individual modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some understanding of AI in financial services, for example from AI Governance for Supervisors, is helpful. No technical background is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, The Financial Stability Implications of Artificial Intelligence (2024)
- OECD, Recommendation of the Council on Artificial Intelligence (2019, revised 2024)
- NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) (2023)
- FATF, Guidance on Digital Identity (2020)
