## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' skills in assessing AI systems used in credit decisioning, insurance underwriting, and other financial services for bias and fairness, applying recognised testing methodologies.

## Intended Audience

Mid-level supervisory staff building capability in AI-related supervision and internal AI use.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how bias can enter AI systems used in credit and insurance decisions
- Describe recognised fairness testing methodologies
- Apply a fairness testing approach to a sample AI decisioning system
- Assess the trade-offs between different fairness definitions
- Formulate a supervisory assessment of bias risk in a financial AI system

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Bias in credit and insurance AI (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain how bias enters financial AI decisioning*
- Sources of bias in credit decisioning and insurance underwriting AI
- Historical data bias and its perpetuation through AI models
- Proxy discrimination: how models can encode bias indirectly
- The particular sensitivity of credit and insurance decisions
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: bias concerns in credit/insurance AI participants are aware of

**Session 2: Fairness testing methodologies (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Describe and apply fairness testing methodologies*
- Recognised fairness testing methodologies and metrics
- Testing for disparate impact across protected characteristics
- Practical demonstration of fairness testing on a sample dataset
- Data requirements and limitations for fairness testing
Activities:
- Exercise: apply a fairness testing approach to a sample credit decisioning model

**Session 3: Fairness trade-offs and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Assess fairness trade-offs and formulate an assessment*
- Why different fairness definitions can conflict with one another
- Choosing an appropriate fairness standard for a given use case
- Case study: a credit decisioning model raising fairness concerns
Activities:
- Case study: formulate a supervisory assessment of bias risk for a sample financial AI system

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: bias sources, fairness testing, trade-offs
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Bias in credit and insurance AI (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Fairness testing methodologies (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Fairness trade-offs and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Bias in credit and insurance AI can perpetuate historical discrimination even without explicit discriminatory intent
- Different fairness definitions can genuinely conflict, making the choice of standard a real policy decision
- Fairness testing should be an ongoing discipline throughout a model's lifecycle, not a one-off pre-launch check
