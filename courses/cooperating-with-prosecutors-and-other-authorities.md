## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of how supervisory authorities cooperate with criminal prosecutors and other enforcement bodies, including managing parallel proceedings and information-sharing gateways.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level enforcement investigators and case officers.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the circumstances in which criminal referral is appropriate
- Describe information-sharing gateways with prosecutors and other authorities
- Assess the challenges of managing parallel civil and criminal proceedings
- Evaluate cooperation arrangements with domestic and foreign authorities
- Formulate a cooperation strategy for a sample multi-authority case

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Criminal referral and information gateways (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain referral circumstances and information gateways*
- When criminal referral is appropriate versus civil enforcement
- Statutory information-sharing gateways with prosecutors
- Confidentiality and legal privilege considerations in sharing information
- Building working relationships with prosecutorial authorities
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: referral experiences from participants' own jurisdictions

**Session 2: Managing parallel proceedings (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess the challenges of parallel proceedings*
- Coordinating timing between civil enforcement and criminal proceedings
- Managing the risk of prejudicing a criminal case
- Stay of civil proceedings pending criminal outcome
- Case examples of parallel proceedings management
Activities:
- Group exercise: sequence civil and criminal proceedings for a sample case

**Session 3: Multi-authority cooperation and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Evaluate cooperation arrangements and formulate a strategy*
- Cooperation with domestic authorities (police, tax, FIU)
- Cooperation with foreign prosecutorial authorities
- Case study: a case requiring coordination across multiple authorities
Activities:
- Case study: formulate a multi-authority cooperation strategy for a sample case

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: referral, information gateways, parallel proceedings, cooperation
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Criminal referral and information gateways (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Managing parallel proceedings (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Multi-authority cooperation and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Effective criminal referral depends on information-sharing gateways being understood and used correctly
- Parallel proceedings require careful sequencing to avoid prejudicing either process
- Strong working relationships with prosecutorial and other authorities should be built before they are urgently needed
