## Aim and Purpose

To give the officials who draft and advise on financial services law a practical understanding of how legislative design choices affect regulatory outcomes, enforceability and international compliance. Participants leave able to structure primary and secondary legislation, draft workable perimeter, licensing and enforcement provisions, and keep the law technology-neutral and consistent with international standards.

**Coverage:** The objectives and architecture of financial services law, principles-based and rules-based drafting, primary and secondary legislation and regulator rule-making powers, defining the regulatory perimeter, licensing and authorisation provisions, supervisory and enforcement powers and sanctions, safeguards and appeals, transitional provisions, consultation and impact assessment, and technology-neutral drafting.

## Intended Audience

- Legislative drafters and parliamentary counsel working on financial services, payments and digital asset legislation
- Legal advisers in finance ministries, central banks and other public bodies who instruct drafters or review bills
- Policy officials who prepare drafting instructions and impact assessments for financial legislation

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how legislative design choices shape regulatory effectiveness and international compliance
- Choose between principles-based and rules-based approaches and between primary and secondary legislation
- Draft clear perimeter definitions, licensing provisions and exemptions
- Design proportionate supervisory and enforcement powers with appropriate safeguards
- Plan transitional provisions, consultation and impact assessment
- Apply technology-neutral drafting techniques that keep legislation current

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Architecture of Financial Services Law

How financial services legislation is typically structured, the objectives it must serve, and how international standards and existing national law shape the drafter's task.

**Objectives**

- Describe the typical structure of a financial services statute
- Explain how statutory objectives guide regulators and courts
- Identify the international standards that legislation must reflect

**Syllabus**

- Single statutes, sectoral statutes and consolidation
- Statutory objectives, principles and duties of the regulator
- Translating core principles and FATF standards into national law
- Interaction with company, insolvency, contract and criminal law

### Module 2: Principles, Rules and Outcomes

The choice between principles-based, rules-based and outcomes-based drafting, and how each affects legal certainty, flexibility, enforcement and the burden on firms.

**Objectives**

- Compare principles-based, rules-based and outcomes-based approaches
- Assess the effect of each on legal certainty and enforceability
- Combine principles and detailed rules in a coherent framework

**Syllabus**

- Principles, rules and outcomes: strengths and weaknesses
- Legal certainty, discretion and the risk of litigation
- Guidance, codes and their legal status
- Drafting exercise: converting a rule into a principle and back

### Module 3: Primary Legislation, Secondary Legislation and Rule-Making Powers

What belongs in primary legislation, what can be delegated to ministers or regulators, and how to confer rule-making powers with adequate constraints and accountability.

**Objectives**

- Decide what belongs in primary and what in secondary legislation
- Draft delegated and regulator rule-making powers with appropriate limits
- Build in consultation, parliamentary oversight and review of rules

**Syllabus**

- Framework legislation and the limits of delegation
- Ministerial regulations versus regulator rule-making
- Procedural requirements: consultation, cost-benefit analysis and publication
- Parliamentary scrutiny and sunset or review clauses

### Module 4: Defining the Regulatory Perimeter

How to define regulated activities, instruments and persons so that the perimeter captures the intended risks, resists avoidance and avoids catching activity unintentionally.

**Objectives**

- Draft activity-based and entity-based perimeter definitions
- Design exemptions, exclusions and territorial scope provisions
- Anticipate avoidance and perimeter arbitrage

**Syllabus**

- Activity, instrument and entity definitions
- By-way-of-business tests, exemptions and exclusions
- Territorial scope, reverse solicitation and cross-border services
- Perimeter powers to designate new activities and instruments

### Module 5: Licensing and Authorisation Provisions

How to draft the authorisation regime: the general prohibition, threshold conditions, fit and proper tests, conditions and variations, and the powers to refuse, suspend and withdraw a licence.

**Objectives**

- Draft a general prohibition and its criminal and civil consequences
- Specify threshold conditions and fitness and propriety requirements
- Provide for conditions, variation, suspension and withdrawal of licences

**Syllabus**

- The general prohibition and unenforceability of agreements
- Threshold conditions, controllers and change of control
- Fit and proper requirements and senior manager accountability regimes
- Licence conditions, variation, suspension and cancellation

### Module 6: Supervisory and Enforcement Powers and Sanctions

The investigative, supervisory and enforcement powers regulators need, the sanctions available, and the procedural safeguards, appeals and legal protection that make them fair and durable.

**Objectives**

- Draft information-gathering, investigation and intervention powers
- Design a proportionate and dissuasive sanctions framework
- Provide for due process, appeals and statutory immunity

**Syllabus**

- Information requests, skilled persons, inspections and search powers
- Administrative penalties, prohibition orders, restitution and criminal offences
- Decision-making procedures, publication and appeals tribunals
- Legal protection for the regulator and its staff

### Module 7: Transitional Provisions, Consultation and Impact Assessment

How to move from an old regime to a new one without disruption, and how consultation and impact assessment improve legislation and its legitimacy.

**Objectives**

- Draft transitional, grandfathering and commencement provisions
- Plan consultation with industry, regulators and the public
- Prepare and use impact assessments and post-implementation reviews

**Syllabus**

- Commencement, staged implementation and transitional periods
- Grandfathering, deemed authorisation and wind-down regimes
- Consultation design and handling of responses
- Regulatory impact assessment and post-implementation review

### Module 8: Technology-Neutral and Future-Proof Drafting

Brings the programme together by examining how to draft for innovation such as digital assets and AI without obsolescence, and applying the programme's lessons to a draft bill.

**Objectives**

- Apply technology-neutral drafting to definitions and obligations
- Assess when bespoke provisions for new technologies are justified
- Review a draft bill against the principles of the programme

**Syllabus**

- Technology neutrality: principle, limits and drafting techniques
- Drafting for digital assets, tokenisation and AI
- Sandboxes, pilot regimes and powers to adapt
- Workshop: reviewing and redrafting a financial services bill

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with drafting exercises on model provisions, ending in a bill review workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to legislation in preparation.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience of legislative drafting or legal advice in government is helpful. No specialist knowledge of financial services is required.

## Core Reading

- OECD (2014), The Governance of Regulators, OECD Best Practice Principles for Regulatory Policy
- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- FSB (2014), Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions
