## Aim and Purpose

To give managers in compliance, risk and financial crime functions the people and leadership skills that technical expertise alone does not provide, in a role where they must challenge colleagues who outrank them and out-earn them. Participants leave able to lead and develop a second-line team, prioritise a demanding risk-based workload, influence and challenge the business, and earn the confidence of senior management, the board and the supervisor.

**Coverage:** The move from specialist to second-line manager, influence without authority, challenging the business, prioritisation and resourcing, delegation and performance management, coaching and developing control function staff, reporting upward to executives and the board, credibility with the board and the supervisor, wellbeing and resilience under pressure, and leading through change and incidents.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance, risk and financial crime specialists newly promoted, or preparing for promotion, into team leader and manager roles
- Heads of compliance, chief risk officers, MLROs and their deputies seeking to strengthen how they lead and position the function
- Senior managers who lead other control or assurance teams, or who sponsor the second line within the business

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how the role changes from control function specialist to second-line manager and leader
- Apply influencing and challenge techniques to change business decisions without line authority
- Prioritise and resource the team's workload by risk, and manage performance fairly and early
- Develop team members' judgement and confidence through coaching, feedback and delegation
- Report upward to executives and the board in a way that drives decisions and builds credibility
- Lead the team through pressure, incidents and change while protecting wellbeing, and agree a personal leadership plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: From Specialist to Second-Line Manager

The shift from being the technical expert who gives the answer to leading a team that the business must respect, and what changes in accountability, visibility and relationships on promotion.

**Objectives**

- Explain how the second-line manager role differs from the specialist role
- Assess personal strengths and development needs as a control function manager
- Establish credibility and clear expectations with a new team and with business peers

**Syllabus**

- From doing to enabling: what changes on promotion
- The particular position of the second line: independent, but part of the firm
- Managing former peers and setting team expectations
- Self-assessment: management style and development priorities

### Module 2: Influence Without Authority

How control function managers persuade business heads and executives who do not report to them, by mapping stakeholders, understanding commercial pressures and framing advice so it is acted upon.

**Objectives**

- Map and prioritise stakeholders across the business, executives and the board
- Frame regulatory and risk advice in terms the business values
- Build relationships that preserve influence without compromising independence

**Syllabus**

- Sources of influence for the second line: expertise, trust and the board's mandate
- Understanding commercial drivers, targets and incentives in the business
- Framing advice: risk, outcome and options rather than prohibition
- Avoiding capture: when closeness to the business becomes a risk

### Module 3: Challenging the Business

How to deliver effective challenge to senior colleagues on products, deals, controls and incidents, handle pushback and seniority, and know when to hold a line and when to escalate.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish effective challenge from objection, obstruction and box-ticking
- Apply evidence-based techniques to challenge senior colleagues with confidence
- Decide when to escalate and record challenge so that it can be evidenced

**Syllabus**

- What effective challenge looks like to the board and the supervisor
- Preparing a challenge: evidence, risk, options and the ask
- Handling pushback, commercial pressure and appeals to seniority
- Role-play: challenging a business head on a new product launch

### Module 4: Prioritisation, Resourcing and the Risk-Based Workload

How to set the team's priorities when demand always exceeds capacity, make the case for resources, and say no to low-value work without leaving the firm exposed.

**Objectives**

- Apply risk-based prioritisation to competing advisory, monitoring and regulatory demands
- Build a credible case for headcount, skills and tools
- Agree with senior management what the function will not do and record the risk

**Syllabus**

- Prioritisation tools linked to the compliance and risk assessment
- Regulatory change, incidents and business requests: managing the peaks
- Making the resourcing case: workload data, risk and supervisory expectations
- Saying no and accepting residual risk transparently

### Module 5: Delegation and Managing Performance

How to delegate control work with clear outcomes and quality standards, set meaningful objectives for second-line staff, and address underperformance fairly and early.

**Objectives**

- Delegate work with clear outcomes, authority, checkpoints and sign-off standards
- Set objectives that reward quality of judgement rather than volume of activity
- Manage underperformance and difficult people issues fairly and early

**Syllabus**

- Effective delegation: what, to whom and how much review
- Objectives and measures for control function staff
- Performance conversations, including underperformance
- Quality assurance of the team's work without rewriting it

### Module 6: Coaching and Developing Control Function Staff

How to build judgement, confidence and resilience in compliance, risk and financial crime staff through coaching conversations, feedback and planned development.

**Objectives**

- Apply a structured coaching model in everyday management
- Give feedback that is specific, balanced and actionable
- Plan development and succession for key roles in the function

**Syllabus**

- Coaching models and powerful questions
- Building judgement: talking through the reasoning, not just the answer
- Development paths, qualifications and rotation into the business
- Skills practice: a coaching conversation

### Module 7: Reporting Upward to Executives and the Board

How to write papers and reports for executive committees, risk and audit committees and the board that lead with the message, frame the decision and give an honest view of risk.

**Objectives**

- Structure a paper or report around the decision or assurance required
- Present an honest view of risk and control weaknesses without alarm or comfort
- Handle challenging questions from executives and directors

**Syllabus**

- Writing for decision: the one-page summary and recommendation
- Function reports that inform: themes, trends and risk appetite rather than activity
- Presenting to committees: message first, evidence second
- Skills practice: a short briefing to a mock risk committee

### Module 8: Building Credibility with the Board and the Supervisor

How control function leaders build standing with the chair, committee chairs and non-executive directors, use their independent access well, and represent the function in supervisory engagement.

**Objectives**

- Use independent access to the board and committee chairs effectively
- Position the function as a trusted source of judgement for the board
- Lead the team's engagement with supervisors with candour and consistency

**Syllabus**

- The relationship with the chair, committee chairs and non-executive directors
- Private sessions and raising concerns the executive would prefer not heard
- How supervisors judge the standing and independence of control functions
- Leading the team through a supervisory visit or thematic review

### Module 9: Wellbeing, Resilience and Sustainable Performance

The particular pressures of second-line work, including isolation, conflict with the business, personal accountability and incident peaks, and how managers protect their own and the team's resilience.

**Objectives**

- Explain the specific pressures of control function roles
- Recognise early warning signs of stress and burnout in self and others
- Apply practical techniques to build personal and team resilience

**Syllabus**

- Sources of pressure: conflict, accountability, workload and incidents
- Psychological safety and speaking up within the function
- Workload design and the manager's role in wellbeing
- A personal and team resilience plan

### Module 10: Leading Through Incidents and Change: Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together in a simulated incident and change scenario, followed by a personal leadership plan covering direction for the team, accountability and continuing development.

**Objectives**

- Lead a team through a time-pressured incident and communicate decisions clearly
- Plan and sustain change in the function's structure, tools or approach
- Produce a personal leadership development plan

**Syllabus**

- Decision-making under pressure: bias, incomplete information and ownership
- Leading change: new regulation, restructuring and technology in the second line
- Simulation: a breach escalating to the board and the supervisor
- Workshop: the twelve-month personal leadership plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions spread over several months. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, self-assessment, role-play and skills practice, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's control function structure and leadership framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience in a compliance, risk or financial crime role is helpful. No prior management training is required.
