## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical understanding of how AI and machine learning are built and used in financial services, and the analytical tools to assess whether firms govern them well. Participants leave able to probe firms' AI governance, model risk and customer outcomes, identify red flags and contribute to the authority's own approach to AI supervision.

## What It Covers

AI and machine learning foundations, governance and ethics, explainability and model risk, bias and fairness, AI in trading and operations, AI in advice and consumer services, AI vulnerabilities and failure modes, AI regulation and the financial perimeter, SupTech, and building the authority's approach to AI risk.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and inspectors responsible for overseeing AI use by regulated firms across all sectors
- Risk, policy and technology specialists working on digital finance, model risk or innovation
- Senior managers and heads of department shaping the authority's approach to AI supervision

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how AI and machine learning systems are developed, deployed and fail in financial services
- Assess the adequacy of a firm's AI governance, accountability and oversight arrangements
- Evaluate model risk, explainability, bias and fairness in AI-driven decisions
- Challenge firms on AI risks in trading, operations, advice and consumer-facing services
- Apply AI regulation and international standards to the financial regulatory perimeter
- Design an institution-wide approach to AI supervision, including the authority's own use of SupTech

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, grouped into four pathways and delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red flags and supervisory question exercises, and pathways or individual modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of financial supervision or risk assessment is helpful. No technical background in AI or data science is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, The Financial Stability Implications of Artificial Intelligence (2024)
- IOSCO, The Use of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning by Market Intermediaries and Asset Managers (2021)
- NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) (2023)
- OECD, Recommendation of the Council on Artificial Intelligence (2019, revised 2024)
