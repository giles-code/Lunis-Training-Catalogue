## Aim and Purpose

To equip the people who build, validate and control AI in a regulated firm with a working understanding of how AI systems are developed and fail, and with the governance, model risk and control tools to manage them. Participants leave able to design and operate the firm's AI framework, document and validate models to a standard that withstands scrutiny, and evidence sound control to supervisors and auditors.

## What It Covers

AI and machine learning foundations, the firm's AI governance framework, model risk management and explainability, bias and fairness testing, AI in trading and operations, AI in advice and client-facing tools, vulnerabilities and third-party AI risk, regulatory expectations across jurisdictions, AI for the firm's own compliance and risk functions, and embedding AI governance across the firm.

## Intended Audience

- Compliance, risk and legal professionals responsible for AI policy, oversight and regulatory compliance
- Model risk, model validation, data science and technology staff who build, buy or run AI systems
- Senior managers and business-line owners accountable for AI use cases and their outcomes

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how machine learning and generative AI systems are built, trained and deployed, and the risks each stage introduces
- Design an AI governance framework, inventory and accountability structure fit for the firm's scale and use cases
- Apply model risk management, explainability and fairness testing to the firm's AI models
- Implement controls, kill-switches and escalation for AI used in trading, operations and client-facing services
- Assess AI vulnerabilities, vendor dependency and regulatory obligations across the jurisdictions in which the firm operates
- Evidence effective AI governance to supervisors and auditors and agree an action plan for the firm

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines technical grounding with case studies, group exercises and practical templates, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's use cases.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of compliance, risk, model development or technology in a regulated firm is helpful. No data science background is required.

## Core Reading

- NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) (2023)
- ISO/IEC 42001:2023, Artificial Intelligence – Management System
- IOSCO, The Use of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning by Market Intermediaries and Asset Managers (2021)
- FSB, The Financial Stability Implications of Artificial Intelligence (2024)
