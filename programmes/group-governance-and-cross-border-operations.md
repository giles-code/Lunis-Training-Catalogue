## Aim and Purpose

To give boards and senior managers of firms that operate across borders a practical understanding of how group structures, intragroup arrangements and cross-border access shape governance, risk and regulatory exposure. Participants leave able to balance group direction with the independence of local boards, oversee intragroup services and booking models, manage conflicts between group and entity, and engage confidently with home and host supervisors.

## What It Covers

Group structures and why they matter to supervisors, subsidiaries versus branches, local board independence and group direction, intragroup outsourcing and service agreements, booking models and substance, passporting and third-country access, dealing with multiple supervisors and colleges, conflicts between group and local entity, and group stress and recovery.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, executive, non-executive and independent directors of regulated subsidiaries, and of parent and holding companies
- Country heads, branch managers and senior managers responsible for a local entity within a wider group
- Group and local heads of compliance, risk, legal, governance and regulatory affairs

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how group structures, legal entities and branches determine regulatory responsibility and supervisory scrutiny
- Evaluate the balance between group direction and the independence and accountability of local boards
- Oversee intragroup outsourcing, service agreements and booking models so that local substance and control are demonstrable
- Assess cross-border access options, including passporting, branches and third-country routes, and their governance consequences
- Manage relationships with home and host supervisors and prepare for college and joint engagement
- Identify and resolve conflicts between group and local entity interests, including in stress, and agree an action plan

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions for a group or subsidiary board. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies, scenarios and a director question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's group structure and markets.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board, senior management or control function experience in a firm that is part of a group or operates across borders is helpful. No legal background is required.

## Core Reading

- G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (2023)
- FSB, Enhancing Third-Party Risk Management and Oversight: A Toolkit for Financial Institutions and Financial Authorities (2023)
- Basel Committee, Principles for Effective Supervisory Colleges (2014)
