## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of the cyber threat landscape facing regulated firms, including internal and external threat actors and their likely targets within financial market infrastructure. Structure informed by Toronto Centre's published Cyber Risk programme.

## Intended Audience

Mid-level supervisory staff building capability in cyber and operational resilience supervision.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the categories of cyber threat actor relevant to financial services
- Describe common attack vectors used against regulated firms
- Identify sector-specific vulnerabilities within financial market infrastructure
- Assess how threat intelligence should inform supervisory priorities
- Evaluate a sample threat landscape assessment for a supervised firm

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Threat actors and attack vectors (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain threat actors and attack vectors*
- Categories of threat actor: nation-state, organised crime, insider, opportunistic
- Common attack vectors: phishing, ransomware, supply chain compromise
- Why financial services is a persistently high-value target
- The evolving sophistication of cyber threats
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: cyber incidents participants are aware of in their own sector

**Session 2: Sector-specific vulnerabilities (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Identify sector-specific vulnerabilities*
- Vulnerabilities specific to payment systems, trading infrastructure, custody
- Legacy systems and technical debt as a vulnerability driver
- Third-party and supply chain vulnerabilities
- Mapping vulnerabilities to likely threat actor targeting
Activities:
- Group exercise: map vulnerabilities for a sample financial market infrastructure entity

**Session 3: Threat intelligence and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Assess the role of threat intelligence and evaluate an assessment*
- Using threat intelligence to inform supervisory priorities
- Sources of threat intelligence available to supervisors
- Case study: a sample threat landscape assessment
Activities:
- Case study: evaluate a sample threat landscape assessment for a supervised firm

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: threat actors, attack vectors, vulnerabilities, threat intelligence
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Threat actors and attack vectors (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Sector-specific vulnerabilities (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Threat intelligence and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Understanding threat actor motivation helps supervisors anticipate where attacks are most likely to target
- Legacy systems and third-party dependencies are persistent, often underestimated vulnerabilities
- Threat intelligence should actively shape supervisory priorities, not just inform after-the-fact analysis
