## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a focused, working understanding of MiCA and the EU rules around it, from the crypto-asset market and token regimes through to authorisation, ongoing supervision and enforcement. Participants leave able to read a CASP's business model and application critically, spot red flags early and apply MiCA's supervisory tools proportionately, working with ESMA, the EBA and other national competent authorities.

## What It Covers

The crypto-asset market and MiCA's architecture, asset-referenced and e-money tokens, CASP authorisation, governance, prudential, custody and ICT requirements, market abuse, AML/CFT and the Travel Rule, risk-based supervision, colleges and cross-border cooperation, and enforcement and crisis management.

## Intended Audience

- Authorisation, supervision and inspection staff new to crypto-asset service providers and token issuers
- AML/CFT, market integrity, payments and enforcement specialists who need a working grasp of MiCA
- Managers and policy staff who need an overview before committing teams to deeper training

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how crypto-asset markets work and how MiCA's scope, taxonomy and rulebook fit together
- Distinguish ARTs and EMTs and assess reserve, redemption and significance arrangements
- Apply MiCA's authorisation and ongoing requirements to CASP applications and supervision
- Evaluate market abuse, AML/CFT and Travel Rule controls at CASPs
- Operate risk-based supervision, passporting and cooperation with other authorities, ESMA and the EBA
- Apply MiCA's enforcement and crisis tools proportionately to a realistic case

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a three-day course or as six half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with case studies and red-flag exercises, closing with a case-study workshop, and modules can be tailored to the authority's needs; the full fifteen-module programme is available for deeper capability building.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of authorising or supervising financial firms is helpful. No technical background in blockchain is required.

## Core Reading

- Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 on markets in crypto-assets (MiCA)
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets (Transfer of Funds Regulation)
- FSB, High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Crypto-Asset Activities and Markets (2023)
- FATF, Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers (2021)
