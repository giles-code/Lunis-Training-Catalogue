## Aim and Purpose

To give staff at every level a practical, plain-language understanding of how criminals exploit financial firms and the part each role plays in stopping them. Participants leave able to spot red flags in their own work, follow the firm's controls and report suspicions confidently and lawfully.

## What It Covers

How financial crime works, money laundering, terrorist and proliferation financing, customer due diligence, sanctions, bribery and corruption, fraud and scams, and internal reporting, tipping off and red flags across every role.

## Intended Audience

- All staff in regulated firms, including new joiners as part of induction and existing staff as annual refresher training
- Customer-facing, operations, payments, technology, HR, finance and procurement staff who may encounter financial crime risk
- Line managers who receive concerns from their teams and need to know how to escalate them

## Learning Objectives

- Explain in plain language how money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, bribery and fraud work
- Recognise the red flags most relevant to their own role and team
- Apply the firm's customer due diligence, screening and anti-bribery controls correctly
- Identify fraud and scam tactics targeting customers, staff and the firm
- Report suspicions internally, promptly and without tipping off
- Explain the personal and corporate consequences of financial crime failures and when to speak up

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about ninety minutes each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of short live sessions, and well suited to conversion into e-learning. Modules combine short briefings with real-world examples and scenario questions, work well as annual refresher training, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's policies and business.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior knowledge of financial crime or regulation is required. Familiarity with the firm's own policies is helpful but not essential.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012, as updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- OECD (1997), Convention on Combating Bribery of Foreign Public Officials in International Business Transactions
- FATF, Egmont Group and INTERPOL (2023), Illicit Financial Flows from Cyber-Enabled Fraud
