## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors, FIU analysts and investigators a practical understanding of crypto-asset financial crime, from the main typologies and laundering techniques to how transactions are traced and attributed. Participants leave able to recognise red flags, test a service provider's due diligence and monitoring, interpret tracing evidence critically and decide on an effective supervisory or investigative response.

## What It Covers

Crypto-asset fundamentals for financial crime work, the FATF standards for virtual assets, criminal typologies, the laundering lifecycle on-chain, mixers and tumblers, privacy coins and unhosted wallets, blockchain tracing and attribution, customer due diligence and monitoring at service providers, the travel rule, suspicious transaction reporting, freezing and seizure, and international cooperation.

## Intended Audience

- AML/CFT supervisors and inspectors responsible for crypto-asset service providers
- FIU analysts handling crypto-related suspicious transaction reports and intelligence
- Law-enforcement and financial crime investigators working on cases involving crypto-assets

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how crypto-asset networks, wallets and service providers work and where financial crime risk arises
- Identify the main typologies and red flags of crypto-enabled crime
- Analyse how proceeds are placed, layered and integrated using crypto-assets and anonymity-enhancing tools
- Interpret blockchain tracing and attribution evidence and recognise its limits
- Assess service providers' customer due diligence, monitoring, travel rule and sanctions controls
- Apply intelligence, freezing, seizure and cooperation tools to build an effective response

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with worked cases, red-flag exercises and supervisory or investigative questions, ending in an investigation workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the participants' roles.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT supervision, financial intelligence or financial crime investigation is helpful. No technical background in blockchain is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- FATF, Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers (2021)
- FATF, Guidance on Risk-Based Supervision (2021)
- FATF, Opportunities and Challenges of New Technologies for AML/CFT (2021)
