## Aim and Purpose

To give new directors a practical, jargon-free grounding in their legal and regulatory responsibilities and in how a regulated firm is supervised, run and controlled. Participants leave able to see the firm the way a supervisor does, ask the right questions from their first board meeting and protect themselves through sound oversight.

**Coverage:** Directors' duties, the regulatory system and supervisory expectations of boards, fitness and propriety, senior manager accountability, business model and risk profile, capital and liquidity, conduct and consumer outcomes, financial crime, governance and committees, board information and challenge, and personal liability and protection.

## Intended Audience

- Newly appointed non-executive, independent and executive directors of regulated firms
- Directors moving into financial services from other sectors, or into a new type of regulated firm
- Senior managers preparing for board appointment or approval by the authority

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain directors' legal duties and the regulatory responsibilities that sit on top of them
- Describe how the regulatory system works and what supervisors expect of boards
- Assess the firm's business model, risk profile and financial resilience
- Oversee conduct, consumer outcomes and financial crime using structured board-level questions
- Evaluate governance, committee arrangements and the quality of board information
- Identify personal liability risks and the protections available, and agree a personal induction plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Directors' Duties in a Regulated Firm

The legal duties every director owes, how regulation adds to them, and the distinct roles of the chair, executive and non-executive directors.

**Objectives**

- Explain the core fiduciary and statutory duties of directors
- Distinguish the roles of chair, executive and non-executive directors
- Recognise how regulatory obligations extend directors' responsibilities

**Syllabus**

- Duties of care, skill, diligence, loyalty and independent judgement
- Collective board responsibility and individual accountability
- Conflicts of interest and related-party matters
- Group structures: duties to the firm versus the parent

### Module 2: The Regulatory System and Supervisory Expectations of Boards

How financial regulation is structured, how the authority supervises firms, and what supervisors expect to see from the board in practice.

**Objectives**

- Explain the objectives and architecture of financial regulation
- Describe how risk-based supervision works and how firms are categorised
- Identify what supervisors expect of boards and how they assess them

**Syllabus**

- Regulatory objectives: stability, consumer protection and market integrity
- International standard-setters: FSB, Basel Committee, IOSCO, IAIS and FATF
- Risk-based supervision, supervisory engagement and board meetings with the authority
- Supervisory tools: from information requests to enforcement

### Module 3: Fitness and Propriety

The standards directors must meet on appointment and continuously thereafter, how the authority assesses them and what can lead to withdrawal of approval.

**Objectives**

- Explain the fitness and propriety criteria: competence, integrity and financial soundness
- Describe the approval process and the authority's interview and assessment practice
- Recognise the ongoing obligations to notify and maintain fitness

**Syllabus**

- Competence, capability, honesty, integrity and reputation
- Time commitment, independence and conflicts
- Applications, interviews and how the authority reaches its decision
- Ongoing fitness, notifications and withdrawal of approval

### Module 4: Senior Manager Accountability

How senior manager accountability regimes allocate individual responsibility, what reasonable steps mean in practice, and how this shapes the board's oversight role.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose and core features of senior manager accountability regimes
- Evaluate statements of responsibilities and the firm's responsibilities map
- Apply the reasonable steps standard to the director's own role

**Syllabus**

- Prescribed responsibilities, statements of responsibilities and mapping
- Conduct rules and the duty of responsibility
- Evidencing reasonable steps: delegation, oversight and records
- Handovers and gaps in accountability

### Module 5: The Firm's Business Model and Risk Profile

How to understand quickly what the firm does, how it makes money, where its key risks sit and how a supervisor would view its business model.

**Objectives**

- Analyse how the firm generates revenue and where its key dependencies lie
- Identify the firm's principal risks and how they connect to its strategy
- Assess the business model through a supervisor's eyes

**Syllabus**

- Business model, value chain and key dependencies
- Principal risks and the risk management framework
- Risk appetite: what the board has agreed and why
- How supervisors assess business model viability and sustainability

### Module 6: Prudential Basics: Capital and Liquidity

Plain-language grounding in why regulated firms must hold capital and liquidity, how requirements are set and what the board must monitor and approve.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of capital and liquidity requirements
- Interpret the firm's capital and liquidity position and its buffers
- Recognise the board's role in internal assessments, stress testing and recovery planning

**Syllabus**

- Capital resources, requirements and buffers in plain language
- Liquidity risk, funding and client money
- Internal capital and liquidity assessments and board approval
- Stress testing, recovery and wind-down planning

### Module 7: Conduct and Consumer Outcomes

The board's responsibility for treating customers fairly, how poor outcomes arise and how the board gains assurance that products and services deliver good outcomes.

**Objectives**

- Explain conduct and consumer protection obligations and the shift to outcomes
- Evaluate product governance, pricing, communications and support
- Use conduct MI and complaints data to identify harm

**Syllabus**

- Fair treatment, suitability and consumer protection obligations
- Product governance and value
- Vulnerable customers and communications
- Complaints, redress and conduct MI

### Module 8: Financial Crime

The firm's exposure to money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions breaches, fraud and bribery, and the controls and reporting the board should expect.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main financial crime risks and the firm's legal obligations
- Assess the firm's financial crime risk assessment and control framework
- Oversee the MLRO's reporting and the resources given to financial crime compliance

**Syllabus**

- FATF standards and the risk-based approach
- Customer due diligence, monitoring and suspicious activity reporting
- Sanctions, fraud, bribery and corruption
- The MLRO's annual report and board-level financial crime MI

### Module 9: Governance, Committees and Board Information

How board and committee arrangements work in a regulated firm, how to read board packs critically and how to challenge management effectively.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate the firm's governance structure, committee mandates and reporting lines
- Interpret board information and identify what is missing
- Apply effective challenge techniques in the boardroom

**Syllabus**

- Board, risk, audit, remuneration and nomination committees
- The three lines of defence and access to control function heads
- Reading board packs: quality, timeliness and comfort without evidence
- Constructive challenge, minutes and evidencing oversight

### Module 10: Personal Liability, Protection and Induction Plan

Brings the programme together by examining personal liability, the protections available to directors and a practical plan for the director's first year on the board.

**Objectives**

- Identify the routes to personal regulatory, civil and criminal liability
- Evaluate protections including indemnities, insurance and independent advice
- Produce a personal induction and development plan

**Syllabus**

- Enforcement against individuals: case studies and lessons
- Indemnities, directors' and officers' insurance and independent advice
- Record-keeping, dissent and when to resign
- Workshop: the director's first-year induction plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two- to three-day programme or as a series of induction sessions for individual directors or the whole board. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and the director's experience.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Senior management or board experience is helpful. No prior experience of financial services regulation is required.

## Core Reading

- G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (2023)
- Basel Committee, Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015)
- FSB, Principles for an Effective Risk Appetite Framework (2013)
- FSB, Strengthening Governance Frameworks to Mitigate Misconduct Risk: A Toolkit for Firms and Supervisors (2018)
