## Aim and Purpose

To give technology, security, risk and business leaders a practical, non-mathematical grounding in the quantum threat and what it means for their firm's systems, data and third parties. Participants leave able to scope a cryptographic inventory, prioritise exposure, plan a credible multi-year migration and report quantum readiness to the board and the authority.

## What It Covers

Quantum computing and the cryptographic threat, harvest now decrypt later, where cryptography sits in the firm, building a cryptographic inventory, risk assessment and prioritisation, NIST post-quantum standards and crypto-agility, migration planning and delivery, vendors, payment and market infrastructures and digital assets, and governance, board reporting and supervisory engagement.

## Intended Audience

- Chief information security officers, chief technology officers, architects and security engineering leads
- Operational resilience, technology risk, third-party risk and internal audit staff
- Senior managers and programme sponsors accountable for cyber, data protection and change

## Learning Objectives

- Explain in plain language why quantum computing threatens public-key cryptography and why the firm should act now
- Identify where cryptography sits across the firm's systems, data, products and third parties
- Build a cryptographic inventory and prioritise systems by exposure, data shelf life and criticality
- Plan a credible, crypto-agile migration to the NIST post-quantum standards
- Manage dependencies on vendors, payment schemes, market infrastructures and digital asset networks
- Govern the programme and report quantum readiness to the board and supervisors

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with case studies and practical exercises using inventory and roadmap templates, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's technology estate.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of information security, technology risk or operational resilience is helpful. No knowledge of quantum physics or cryptographic mathematics is required.

## Core Reading

- NIST, FIPS 203, 204 and 205: Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards (2024)
- G7 Cyber Expert Group, Public Statement on Planning for the Opportunities and Risks of Quantum Computing (2024)
- NIST, Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (2024)
- FSB, Enhancing Third-Party Risk Management and Oversight: A Toolkit for Financial Institutions and Financial Authorities (2023)
