## Aim and Purpose

To give regulatory staff a practical understanding of the main financial crime risks, the international framework set by the FATF and the controls firms are expected to operate. Participants leave able to recognise red flags in their own work, ask the right questions of firms and know when and how to escalate concerns, including to the national FIU.

**Coverage:** Money laundering and terrorist financing basics, the FATF framework and mutual evaluations, sanctions, fraud, how firms' controls work, red flags in supervisory work, working with the national FIU and law enforcement, and a red flags case workshop.

## Intended Audience

- New joiners and developing staff across supervisory, authorisation, policy and enforcement functions
- Supervisors and inspectors without a specialist financial crime background
- Staff joining a financial crime or AML/CFT supervision team

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the stages and methods of money laundering and terrorist financing
- Describe the FATF standards and how they shape national frameworks and the authority's role
- Explain how sanctions and fraud risks arise in financial firms
- Describe the controls firms are expected to operate and how supervisors test them
- Identify financial crime red flags in authorisation, supervisory and enforcement work
- Apply appropriate escalation, including cooperation with the national FIU and law enforcement

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Understanding Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

What money laundering and terrorist financing are, how they work in practice and why financial firms are both vulnerable to them and central to preventing them.

**Objectives**

- Explain the stages of money laundering and the sources of criminal proceeds
- Distinguish terrorist and proliferation financing from money laundering
- Recognise why the financial system is attractive to criminals

**Syllabus**

- Placement, layering and integration
- Predicate offences and the scale of illicit finance
- Terrorist financing and proliferation financing compared
- The harm caused and the authority's role in preventing it

### Module 2: The FATF Framework and the Authority's Role

The international AML/CFT standards set by the FATF, how they are implemented nationally and what they require of supervisors.

**Objectives**

- Describe the FATF Recommendations and the risk-based approach
- Explain the authority's responsibilities as an AML/CFT supervisor
- Recognise the importance of mutual evaluations and national risk assessments

**Syllabus**

- The FATF Recommendations, Immediate Outcomes and methodology
- National risk assessments and the risk-based approach
- Supervisory obligations: licensing, monitoring and sanctions
- Mutual evaluations, follow-up and the consequences of grey-listing

### Module 3: Financial Sanctions

How targeted financial sanctions work, the obligations they place on firms and the risks of breach and evasion.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose and types of financial sanctions
- Describe firms' screening, freezing and reporting obligations
- Recognise common sanctions evasion techniques

**Syllabus**

- International, regional and national sanctions regimes in outline
- Asset freezes, prohibitions and ownership and control tests
- Screening, alert handling and reporting obligations
- Evasion: front companies, trade routes and crypto-assets

### Module 4: Fraud and Scams

The main types of fraud affecting financial firms and their customers, how fraud links to money laundering and what firms should do to prevent and detect it.

**Objectives**

- Identify the main internal and external fraud types
- Explain how authorised push payment fraud and investment scams operate
- Assess firms' fraud prevention and detection arrangements

**Syllabus**

- Internal fraud, insider threat and misconduct
- Investment scams, impersonation and authorised push payment fraud
- Money mules and the link between fraud and laundering
- Prevention, detection and customer protection measures

### Module 5: How Firms' Financial Crime Controls Work

The building blocks of a firm's financial crime framework and what good and poor controls look like in practice.

**Objectives**

- Describe the main components of a firm's AML/CFT framework
- Distinguish effective controls from paper compliance
- Frame supervisory questions to test controls

**Syllabus**

- Business-wide risk assessment, policies and governance
- Customer due diligence, beneficial ownership and enhanced due diligence
- Transaction monitoring, suspicious activity reporting and record-keeping
- The MLRO, compliance function, training and independent testing

### Module 6: Red Flags in Supervisory Work

How financial crime risks surface in authorisation applications, supervisory reviews and firm data, and how staff outside specialist teams can spot them.

**Objectives**

- Recognise financial crime red flags in applications and firm information
- Use data and returns to identify unusual patterns
- Decide when concerns should be referred to specialist colleagues

**Syllabus**

- Red flags at the gateway: ownership, source of funds and business plans
- Red flags in supervision: growth, customer base and geography
- Unusual transactions, products and delivery channels
- Escalation routes within the authority

### Module 7: Working with the FIU and Law Enforcement

How the authority cooperates with the national FIU, law enforcement and foreign counterparts, and the legal and practical considerations involved.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of the national FIU and how suspicious activity reports are used
- Describe the gateways for domestic and international information exchange
- Recognise the authority's own reporting and confidentiality obligations

**Syllabus**

- The FIU's functions: receipt, analysis and dissemination
- Domestic cooperation and national coordination arrangements
- International cooperation and memoranda of understanding
- Tipping off, confidentiality and data protection

### Module 8: Financial Crime Red Flags: Case Workshop

Brings the programme together in a case study in which participants review a firm's profile and information, identify financial crime risks and agree the supervisory response.

**Objectives**

- Identify financial crime risks and red flags in a case scenario
- Assess the adequacy of the firm's controls
- Agree and justify an appropriate supervisory response and escalation

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing: firm profile, customer base and transaction data
- Identifying red flags and control weaknesses in small groups
- Deciding on escalation and referral
- Workshop: presenting findings and the proposed supervisory response

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with typologies, case studies and exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's mandate and sectors.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior financial crime experience is required. Some familiarity with financial services or the authority's work is helpful.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012, as updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- FATF (2021), Guidance on Risk-Based Supervision
- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2017, revised 2020), Sound Management of Risks Related to Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism
- FATF (2023), Guidance on Beneficial Ownership of Legal Persons
