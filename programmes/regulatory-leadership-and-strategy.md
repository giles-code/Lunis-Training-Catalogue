## Aim and Purpose

To equip the authority's leaders with the frameworks and judgement to set strategy, govern the institution and account for its performance to government, industry and the public. Participants leave able to translate mandate into priorities, set the regulator's own risk appetite, weigh growth and competitiveness objectives against safety and integrity, and lead organisational change.

**Coverage:** Mandate and strategy, governance of the authority, operational independence and accountability, the regulator's risk appetite and prioritisation, growth and competitiveness objectives, stakeholder and international engagement, measuring supervisory effectiveness, and leading change.

## Intended Audience

- Board members, commissioners and executive leaders of supervisory authorities
- Heads of department and senior managers responsible for strategy, resourcing and supervisory priorities
- Strategy, governance, international affairs and board secretariat staff supporting the authority's leadership

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how a statutory mandate and its primary and secondary objectives translate into strategy
- Evaluate the authority's own governance, independence and accountability arrangements
- Set a risk appetite for the regulator and prioritise supervisory work under resource constraints
- Assess how growth and competitiveness objectives can be pursued without compromising safety and integrity
- Design stakeholder, international engagement and effectiveness measures that build credibility
- Lead organisational change and produce a strategic action plan for the authority

## Syllabus

### Module 1: From Mandate to Strategy

How a supervisory authority interprets its statutory mandate, ranks its primary and secondary objectives, and turns them into a credible strategic plan and work programme.

**Objectives**

- Interpret a statutory mandate and the hierarchy of its objectives
- Explain the role of strategic planning in a supervisory authority
- Align strategy with mandate, international standards and public expectations

**Syllabus**

- Interpreting statutory objectives: primary, secondary and implicit duties
- IOSCO and Basel principles relating to the regulator
- Strategic planning frameworks for public authorities
- From strategy to annual business plan and work programme

### Module 2: Governance of the Authority: Board and Executive

The governance structures and practices that support sound decision-making within the authority's own board and executive, and the governance risks specific to public regulatory bodies.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish the roles of the board and the executive in a supervisory authority
- Assess board effectiveness against recognised good-practice criteria
- Identify governance risks such as capture, groupthink and conflicts of interest

**Syllabus**

- Governance models for regulatory bodies and how they differ from corporate boards
- Board composition, skills, independence and committee structures
- Decision rights, delegation and executive accountability
- Case study: a governance failure inside a regulator

### Module 3: Operational Independence and Accountability

The legal and institutional foundations of supervisory independence, the subtle threats it faces in practice, and how accountability and transparency reinforce rather than weaken it.

**Objectives**

- Explain the rationale and legal foundations of operational independence
- Assess threats to independence, from political pressure to budget leverage
- Formulate a response to a scenario challenging the authority's independence

**Syllabus**

- International standards on regulatory independence and adequate resources
- Threats in practice: political pressure, industry capture, funding leverage
- Accountability to parliament, government and the public as a safeguard
- Case study: defending independence under pressure

### Module 4: The Regulator's Risk Appetite and Prioritisation

How the authority defines the risks it will and will not tolerate, accepts that a non-zero-failure regime is inevitable, and allocates scarce supervisory resources accordingly.

**Objectives**

- Define a risk appetite for the authority and its tolerance for firm failure
- Apply risk-based prioritisation to supervisory coverage and resourcing
- Evaluate trade-offs between breadth and depth of supervision

**Syllabus**

- The non-zero-failure regime and communicating it honestly
- Drafting the authority's risk appetite and tolerance statements
- Budget, headcount, specialist skills and value for money
- Exercise: prioritising a supervisory risk register under constraints

### Module 5: Growth, Competitiveness and Secondary Objectives

How authorities with growth, competitiveness or market development objectives can support a thriving financial sector while preserving safety, consumer protection and market integrity.

**Objectives**

- Explain the rise of growth and competitiveness objectives for regulators
- Assess where growth objectives align with, and conflict with, primary objectives
- Identify regulatory levers that support growth without lowering standards

**Syllabus**

- Secondary objectives on growth, competitiveness and market development
- Proportionality, speed of authorisation and regulatory burden as growth levers
- Innovation, market access and international attractiveness
- Guarding against capture and a race to the bottom

### Module 6: Stakeholder and International Engagement

How the authority maps and manages expectations of government, industry, consumers and international peers, communicates its strategy, and engages with standard-setters and cooperation frameworks.

**Objectives**

- Map stakeholder expectations and balance competing demands
- Communicate strategic priorities clearly to internal and external audiences
- Identify practical steps to strengthen the authority's international engagement

**Syllabus**

- Stakeholder mapping: government, industry, consumers, media and peers
- Strategic communication, public reporting and transparency
- Engagement with IOSCO, BCBS, FSB and other standard-setters
- Resourcing international engagement in a smaller authority

### Module 7: Measuring Supervisory Effectiveness

How to design metrics that measure outcomes rather than activity, benchmark against international assessments, and avoid the perverse incentives that poorly designed targets create.

**Objectives**

- Explain why supervisory effectiveness resists simple measurement
- Distinguish output, process and outcome metrics
- Design a balanced effectiveness scorecard for the authority

**Syllabus**

- Output, process and outcome metrics in supervision
- International assessments as benchmarks, including FSAP-style reviews
- Gaming, perverse incentives and unintended consequences of targets
- Exercise: critiquing and rebuilding a metrics framework

### Module 8: Leading Change in the Authority

Brings the programme together into a leadership plan for change, covering culture, capability, transformation programmes and the authority's response to new risks and expectations.

**Objectives**

- Assess the authority's readiness and capacity for change
- Apply change leadership techniques to a supervisory transformation
- Produce a strategic action plan for the authority

**Syllabus**

- Leading cultural and organisational change in a public authority
- Building supervisory capability, talent and succession
- Managing transformation: technology, structure and new mandates
- Workshop: the authority's three-year strategic action plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day leadership programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing on international good practice with case studies, facilitated discussion and leadership exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Senior management experience in a supervisory authority or public body is helpful. No specialist background in strategy or governance is required.

## Core Reading

- OECD, Best Practice Principles for Regulatory Policy: The Governance of Regulators (2014)
- IMF, The Making of Good Supervision: Learning to Say "No" (Staff Position Note SPN/10/08, 2010)
- IMF, Good Supervision: Lessons from the Field (Working Paper WP/23/181, 2023)
- Basel Committee, Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision (2024)
