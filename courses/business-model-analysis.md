## Aim and Purpose

Equips supervisors to conduct business model analysis (BMA) as a core part of supervisory risk assessment: how firms develop corporate strategy and business models, why and how supervisors assess them, qualitative and quantitative analytical methods, and how to score and conclude a BMA assessment using a case study exercise.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level supervisory staff conducting day-to-day risk assessment and firm engagement.

## Learning Objectives

- Discuss what a business model is and what corporate strategy is
- Explain the purpose of business model analysis for supervisors
- Describe the nature and practicalities of BMA in a regulatory environment
- Identify the steps involved in conducting BMA during supervisory engagement
- Apply appropriate qualitative and quantitative tools to conduct BMA
- Demonstrate how to draw conclusions from BMA

## Syllabus

**Morning | 09:30–12:30** — Level setting and theory What is corporate strategy and what is a business model? — firm characterisation, strategic questions, business model components Why look at business models — the purpose of BMA for supervisors and where strategy/business model risk fits in the supervisory risk framework Facets of business model and strategy risk, the value chain, organisation and management considerations How supervisors undertake BMA — roadmap, proportionality, preliminary assessment, information sources

**Early Afternoon | 13:30–14:30** — Qualitative and quantitative analysis Assessing the business environment: macro environment, competitive environment, supplier/buyer power, threat of new entrants and substitution Quantitative analysis: profitability KRIs, capital structure and quality, concentration risk Qualitative analysis: key dependencies and questions to challenge management assumptions Assessing strategy: why strategies fail, financial projections, execution risk, identifying key vulnerabilities

**Late Afternoon | 14:50–16:00** — Case study, assessment and scoring Case study exercise: SWOT analysis and strategy comparison of two contrasting fund administration businesses, followed by a supervisory assessment exercise Business model assessment and scoring: how to document vulnerabilities and score BMA outcomes (low / medium-low / medium-high / high risk) Review of management responses and discussion of how unexpected events can stress business models
