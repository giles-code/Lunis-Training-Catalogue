## Aim and Purpose

To give policy, legal and supervisory staff a practical, end-to-end grounding in how a supervisory authority develops, consults on and issues rules and guidance. Participants leave able to build an evidence-based case for intervention, test it for proportionality, draft enforceable rules and evaluate their impact after implementation.

## What It Covers

The policy cycle and rule-making powers, problem definition and options analysis, cost-benefit analysis and proportionality, consultation and stakeholder engagement, legal drafting, implementing international standards, policy for innovation including sandboxes, and post-implementation review.

## Intended Audience

- Policy and rule-making staff developing rules, guidance and policy positions
- Legal advisers and drafters working on secondary rules and supervisory guidance
- Supervisors and economists who contribute evidence, impact analysis or consultation input

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the legal hierarchy of rule-making and the stages of the policy cycle
- Define a regulatory problem and evaluate options, including not intervening
- Apply cost-benefit analysis and proportionality tests to proposed rules
- Design and run a consultation and respond transparently to feedback
- Draft clear, consistent and enforceable rules, including those implementing international standards
- Design policy for innovation and evaluate rules through post-implementation review

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a practical briefing with case studies and drafting or analysis exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of policy, legal or supervisory work is helpful. No formal legal or economics training is required.

## Core Reading

- OECD, Recommendation of the Council on Regulatory Policy and Governance (2012)
- OECD, Best Practice Principles for Regulatory Policy: Regulatory Impact Assessment (2020)
- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
