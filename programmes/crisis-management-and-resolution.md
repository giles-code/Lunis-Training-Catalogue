## Aim and Purpose

To build the capability authorities need for the moments that matter most but occur least, from early intervention through recovery and resolution to system-wide stress. Participants leave able to recognise distress early, escalate with confidence, judge the credibility of recovery and resolution plans, and make and communicate decisions under time pressure.

## What It Covers

The authority's role in a crisis, early warning indicators, the intervention ladder, recovery planning, resolution planning and resolvability, resolution tools and orderly failure, systemic risk and contagion, macroprudential tools, crisis communication and coordination, and a firm-distress simulation.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and risk specialists responsible for firms that may come under stress
- Resolution, financial stability and macroprudential staff
- Senior managers and executives who would lead or take decisions in the authority's crisis response

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the roles of supervisors, resolution authorities and other bodies in a financial crisis
- Identify early warning signs and apply a structured intervention ladder
- Assess the credibility of recovery plans and the resolvability of firms
- Evaluate systemic risk, contagion channels and the macroprudential tools to address them
- Communicate and coordinate effectively with the public, industry, government and other authorities
- Make and evaluate supervisory decisions under time pressure in a simulated crisis

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short input with case studies and exercises, ending in a live crisis simulation and debrief, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's sectors and resolution framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervision, financial stability or risk work is helpful. No prior crisis or resolution experience is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions (2014)
- Basel Committee, Guidelines for Identifying and Dealing with Weak Banks (2015)
- IADI, Core Principles for Effective Deposit Insurance Systems
