## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors the practical skills and confidence to use the authority's approved AI tools on real supervisory tasks, within its AI policy and confidentiality rules. Participants leave able to write effective prompts, speed up document review, drafting and analysis, and check AI outputs to the evidential standard supervisory work demands.

## What It Covers

Generative AI essentials, the authority's AI policy and confidentiality, prompting for supervisory tasks, reviewing firm documents and returns, drafting correspondence and reports, data analysis, verification and professional judgement, and building AI into supervisory workflows.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, inspectors, analysts and authorisation staff at all levels of seniority
- Policy, enforcement and financial crime staff who review documents, data and correspondence
- Team leaders responsible for embedding AI tools safely in their teams' work

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how generative AI tools work, what they do well and where they fail
- Apply the authority's AI policy, including data classification and confidentiality rules
- Write structured prompts that produce reliable outputs for supervisory tasks
- Use AI to review firm documents and returns, draft correspondence and analyse data
- Verify AI outputs and retain professional judgement and accountability for every decision
- Design repeatable AI-assisted workflows and share good practice across the team

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module is hands-on, built around live exercises on realistic supervisory tasks using the authority's approved tools, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's AI policy and systems.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Everyday familiarity with office software is helpful. No prior experience of AI tools or data analysis is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, The Use of Supervisory and Regulatory Technology by Authorities and Regulated Institutions (2020)
- BIS Financial Stability Institute, Innovative Technology in Financial Supervision (Suptech): The Experience of Early Users (FSI Insights No 9, 2018)
