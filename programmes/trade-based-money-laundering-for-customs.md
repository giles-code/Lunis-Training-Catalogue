## Aim and Purpose

To give customs officers, FIU analysts and investigators a practical understanding of how international trade is used to move value and disguise criminal proceeds. Participants leave able to spot the red flags in trade and financial data, target high-risk traders and consignments, and build joint cases with FIU, tax and law-enforcement partners.

**Coverage:** How trade-based money laundering works, trade documentation and trade finance, customs data, mis-invoicing and misdescription of goods, phantom and multiple shipments, red-flag indicators and data analysis, free trade zones, and cooperation with FIUs, tax authorities and foreign customs.

## Intended Audience

- Customs officers, trade intelligence analysts and post-clearance audit teams
- FIU analysts and financial investigators handling trade-related intelligence
- Tax investigators and prosecutors working on trade-linked fraud and laundering cases

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how trade transactions are used to move value and launder proceeds
- Interpret customs declarations, trade documents and trade finance records
- Identify mis-invoicing, misdescription and other red flags in trade and financial data
- Assess the vulnerabilities of free trade zones and transhipment
- Analyse trade data to target high-risk traders and consignments
- Build joint investigations with FIUs, tax authorities and foreign partners

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Trade-Based Money Laundering: How It Works

How international trade is used to transfer value and disguise criminal proceeds, why it is hard to detect, and how it links to drugs, fraud, sanctions evasion and informal value transfer.

**Objectives**

- Explain the basic mechanics of value transfer through trade
- Identify the predicate crimes and actors most associated with trade-based laundering
- Recognise why trade-based laundering is hard to detect

**Syllabus**

- Trade as a means of moving value across borders
- Links to drugs, fraud, customs and tax offences and sanctions evasion
- Informal value transfer and trade settlement
- FATF and Egmont Group findings on trends and developments

### Module 2: Trade Documentation, Trade Finance and Customs Data

The documents and data generated by international trade, from invoices and transport documents to letters of credit and customs declarations, and what each can reveal to an investigator.

**Objectives**

- Read the core trade documents and customs declarations
- Explain open-account trade and documentary trade finance
- Identify which agency or firm holds which trade and payment records

**Syllabus**

- Commercial invoices, packing lists and bills of lading
- Customs declarations, tariff classification and valuation
- Open account, letters of credit and documentary collections
- Where the data sits: customs, banks, carriers and FIUs

### Module 3: Mis-Invoicing and Other Techniques

The main techniques identified in international typologies, including over- and under-invoicing, multiple invoicing, misdescription of goods and phantom shipments, and how each shows up in the data.

**Objectives**

- Explain over- and under-invoicing and their financial effect
- Recognise multiple invoicing, misdescription and phantom shipments
- Distinguish trade-based laundering from customs fraud and commercial error

**Syllabus**

- Over- and under-invoicing of goods and services
- Multiple invoicing and over- or under-shipment
- Misdescription, misclassification and phantom shipments
- Service-based and high-value goods schemes

### Module 4: Red Flags, Data Analysis and Free Trade Zones

How customs and FIUs use risk indicators and data analytics to identify suspicious trade, and the particular vulnerabilities of free trade zones and transhipment hubs.

**Objectives**

- Apply trade-based laundering red-flag indicators to cases
- Use price, volume and route analysis to identify anomalies
- Assess the risks posed by free trade zones and transhipment

**Syllabus**

- Risk indicators for traders, goods, routes and payments
- Unit-price analysis, mirror trade data and data matching
- Free trade zones: oversight gaps and risk indicators
- Risk targeting and selection for examination

### Module 5: Joint Investigation with FIUs, Tax and Foreign Partners

How to turn trade anomalies into cases by combining customs, financial and tax information, using domestic gateways and international cooperation between customs administrations, FIUs and tax authorities.

**Objectives**

- Combine customs, financial and tax information lawfully
- Use international customs, FIU and tax cooperation channels
- Plan a joint investigation with clear roles and objectives

**Syllabus**

- Domestic gateways and joint task forces
- Exchange of trade data and customs mutual assistance
- FIU and tax information exchange channels
- Evidencing value transfer for prosecution and recovery

### Module 6: Case Workshop: Investigating a Trade-Based Scheme

Brings the programme together in a case exercise in which teams analyse trade documents and payment data, identify the laundering technique and agree a joint investigation and enforcement plan.

**Objectives**

- Apply red flags and data analysis to a realistic case
- Identify evidential gaps and how to fill them
- Agree a coordinated enforcement and recovery response

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing: a multi-country trade and payment scheme
- Document and data analysis exercise
- Evidence, charges and asset recovery options
- Workshop: the joint investigation plan

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with trade-document exercises and data analysis, ending in a case workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the participants' agencies and trade profile.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of customs, financial intelligence or financial crime investigation is helpful. No specialist knowledge of trade finance is required.
