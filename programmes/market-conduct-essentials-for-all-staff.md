## Aim and Purpose

To give staff at every level a clear, practical understanding of the market conduct rules that apply to them personally, not only to traders and dealers. Participants leave able to recognise inside information, avoid prohibited dealing and disclosure, manage gifts and communications correctly and report concerns promptly.

**Coverage:** Market integrity, inside information and market abuse, information barriers and insider lists, personal account dealing, gifts, hospitality and inducements, approved communication channels and recordkeeping, and responsible use of social media.

## Intended Audience

- All staff in regulated firms, including those outside trading and investment roles who may come across inside information
- Front-office, research, operations, technology, marketing, HR and executive support staff
- Line managers responsible for approving personal dealing, gifts and communications in their teams

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain what inside information is and how staff in any role may come across it
- Recognise insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and market manipulation
- Apply the firm's personal account dealing rules and pre-clearance processes
- Manage gifts, hospitality and inducements in line with the firm's policy
- Use only approved communication channels and meet recordkeeping requirements
- Use social media responsibly and escalate market conduct concerns promptly

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Market Integrity and Why It Matters to Everyone

Why fair and orderly markets matter, how market abuse harms investors and the firm, and why market conduct rules reach staff well beyond the trading floor.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of market conduct rules and the harm market abuse causes
- Recognise how staff in non-trading roles can be exposed to market conduct risk
- Describe the consequences of breaches for individuals and the firm

**Syllabus**

- Fair, orderly and transparent markets and investor confidence
- Who the rules apply to: staff, contractors and connected persons
- Surveillance, investigations and enforcement outcomes
- Personal consequences: criminal, civil and employment sanctions

### Module 2: Inside Information and Market Abuse

What inside information is, how it is controlled through information barriers and insider lists, and the behaviours that amount to insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and market manipulation.

**Objectives**

- Identify inside information and the situations in which staff may receive it
- Recognise insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and market manipulation
- Apply information barrier, wall-crossing and insider list procedures

**Syllabus**

- What makes information inside information: precise, non-public and price-sensitive
- Information barriers, wall-crossing and insider lists
- Insider dealing, unlawful disclosure and market manipulation
- Spotting and reporting suspicious orders and transactions

### Module 3: Personal Account Dealing

The rules on staff dealing for themselves and connected persons, why pre-clearance and holding periods exist, and how to avoid conflicts and front running.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose and scope of the firm's personal account dealing policy
- Apply pre-clearance, holding period and reporting requirements
- Recognise dealing that creates conflicts of interest or market abuse risk

**Syllabus**

- Accounts, instruments and connected persons covered by the rules
- Pre-clearance, restricted lists, holding periods and disclosure
- Front running, derivatives, crypto-assets and other grey areas
- Monitoring, breaches and what happens when things go wrong

### Module 4: Gifts, Hospitality and Inducements

How gifts, hospitality and other benefits can create conflicts, improper inducements or bribery risk, and how to apply the firm's thresholds, approvals and registers.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish acceptable hospitality from improper inducements
- Apply the firm's thresholds, approval and register requirements
- Recognise when a gift or benefit creates a conflict of interest

**Syllabus**

- Gifts, hospitality, entertainment and non-monetary benefits
- Inducements and their effect on independence and customer outcomes
- Thresholds, pre-approval and the gifts and hospitality register
- Red flags: frequency, timing, value and links to business decisions

### Module 5: Communications and Recordkeeping

Why the firm must record and retain business communications, the risks of unapproved channels such as personal messaging apps, and how to communicate professionally and accurately.

**Objectives**

- Explain why business communications are recorded and retained
- Use only approved channels for business communications
- Communicate accurately and professionally, knowing that messages may be reviewed

**Syllabus**

- Recording obligations for calls, messages and electronic communications
- Off-channel communications: personal devices and messaging apps
- Retention, retrieval and supervisory review of records
- Writing as if it will be read aloud: tone, accuracy and rumours

### Module 6: Social Media, Public Statements and Practical Scenarios

How personal and professional use of social media can create market abuse, confidentiality, financial promotion and reputational risk, closing with practical market conduct scenarios.

**Objectives**

- Apply the firm's social media and public statements policy
- Recognise how online posts can spread rumours, disclose information or amount to promotions
- Apply the programme to realistic market conduct scenarios

**Syllabus**

- Personal and professional accounts: what you can and cannot say
- Rumours, market commentary and amplifying unverified information
- Financial promotions, endorsements and online influencers
- Workshop: what would you do? Market conduct dilemmas

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about ninety minutes each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a one- to two-day programme or as a series of short live sessions, and well suited to conversion into e-learning. Modules combine short briefings with real-world examples and scenario questions, work well as annual refresher training, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's policies and business.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior knowledge of markets or regulation is required. The programme is relevant to all staff, not only those in trading or investment roles.
