## Aim and Purpose

To equip firms to produce recovery and wind-down plans that are credible, usable in a crisis and proportionate to the business, rather than documents written once for a supervisor. Participants leave able to design scenarios and triggers, judge which recovery options would really work, plan an orderly solvent wind-down that returns client money and assets, and see their plan the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** Why plans matter, scenario and trigger design, recovery options and their credibility, liquidity and capital in stress, solvent wind-down mechanics, return of client money and assets, operational continuity and critical services, and governance, testing and supervisory review.

## Intended Audience

- Chief financial officers, chief risk officers and finance and treasury teams at investment firms, payment and e-money institutions, crypto-asset service providers and fund managers
- Compliance officers, heads of operations and plan owners responsible for drafting and maintaining recovery and wind-down plans
- Executive and non-executive directors who approve the plan and would lead the firm through a crisis

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain why supervisors require recovery and wind-down plans and what makes them credible
- Design severe but plausible scenarios, early warning indicators and recovery triggers
- Evaluate recovery options for feasibility, timing and value under stress
- Plan an orderly solvent wind-down, including the return of client money and assets
- Identify the people, systems, suppliers and critical services needed to keep operating through a wind-down
- Embed the plan in governance, test it through fire drills and simulations, and evidence it to supervisors

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Why Recovery and Wind-Down Plans Matter

The purpose of recovery and wind-down planning, what supervisors expect of firms across sectors, and the common reasons plans fail when they are needed most.

**Objectives**

- Explain the difference between recovery, solvent wind-down and insolvency
- Describe supervisory expectations across investment, payments, crypto and fund sectors
- Recognise the hallmarks of a plan written for compliance rather than use

**Syllabus**

- Recovery, wind-down and resolution: purpose, scope and proportionality
- Harm to clients, counterparties and markets from disorderly failure
- Lessons from firm failures where plans did not work
- What a credible, usable plan looks like

### Module 2: Scenario Design, Indicators and Triggers

How to build severe but plausible stress scenarios from the firm's own business model, and set early warning indicators and triggers that prompt action in time.

**Objectives**

- Design idiosyncratic, market-wide and combined stress scenarios
- Use reverse stress testing to identify what would make the business unviable
- Set indicators, thresholds and escalation triggers linked to risk appetite

**Syllabus**

- Business model analysis as the starting point for scenarios
- Reverse stress testing and points of non-viability
- Early warning indicators: capital, liquidity, revenue, client and operational
- Calibrating triggers to avoid both false alarms and late action

### Module 3: Recovery Options and Their Credibility

Identifying the actions management could take to restore viability, and testing honestly whether each would work in the time available and in the market conditions of the scenario.

**Objectives**

- Identify a realistic menu of recovery options
- Assess the feasibility, timing, value and dependencies of each option
- Challenge optimistic assumptions about parent support and asset sales

**Syllabus**

- Capital raising, cost reduction, business and asset disposals
- Group and parent support: commitments versus assumptions
- Impediments: legal, operational, market and reputational
- Mapping options against scenarios to test overall recovery capacity

### Module 4: Liquidity and Capital in Stress

How capital and liquidity behave as stress develops, how to project resources through recovery and wind-down, and how the plan links to the firm's internal capital and liquidity adequacy assessment.

**Objectives**

- Project capital and liquidity through a stress scenario and wind-down period
- Identify sources of liquidity strain, including margin calls, client outflows and trapped cash
- Link wind-down resource needs to the firm's internal adequacy assessment

**Syllabus**

- Liquidity forecasting, buffers and contingency funding
- Capital depletion, losses and the timing of breaches
- Estimating wind-down costs and the resources to hold against them
- Integrating recovery and wind-down with internal capital and liquidity assessments

### Module 5: Solvent Wind-Down: Mechanics, Timeline and Costs

The practical steps to close a business in an orderly way: deciding when to switch from recovery to wind-down, sequencing activities, managing counterparties and staff, and communicating with stakeholders.

**Objectives**

- Define the point at which the firm moves from recovery to wind-down
- Build a realistic wind-down timeline and sequence of actions
- Plan communication with clients, counterparties, staff and supervisors

**Syllabus**

- Wind-down triggers and the decision-making process
- Closing positions, transferring clients and terminating contracts
- Staff retention, key person risk and incentives during wind-down
- Stakeholder and supervisor communication plans

### Module 6: Returning Client Money and Assets

How to ensure client money, safeguarded funds and client assets, including crypto-assets, can be identified, reconciled and returned promptly in a wind-down or failure.

**Objectives**

- Assess the quality of segregation, safeguarding and records for client money and assets
- Plan the identification, reconciliation and return of client holdings
- Identify shortfall risks and how to prevent them

**Syllabus**

- Client money, safeguarding and custody arrangements across sectors
- Reconciliation, books and records and resolution packs
- Return mechanics: transfer, distribution and unclaimed balances
- Crypto-asset custody: keys, wallets and returning digital assets

### Module 7: Operational Continuity and Critical Services

Keeping the firm operating long enough to recover or wind down: identifying critical services, people, systems and suppliers, and making contracts and data resilient to a crisis.

**Objectives**

- Identify critical services and the resources needed to deliver them in stress
- Assess contracts with outsourcers and group entities for continuity in wind-down
- Align recovery and wind-down planning with operational resilience and business continuity

**Syllabus**

- Mapping critical services, people, systems and data
- Third-party and intragroup contracts: termination rights and continuity clauses
- Access to systems and records after staff and supplier departures
- Links to business continuity and operational resilience frameworks

### Module 8: Governance, Testing and Supervisory Review

Brings the programme together: who owns and approves the plan, how it is tested through fire drills and crisis simulation, and how supervisors review plans and what they challenge.

**Objectives**

- Define plan ownership, board approval and review cycles
- Design fire drills and crisis simulations that test the plan in practice
- Anticipate supervisory challenge and evidence the plan's credibility

**Syllabus**

- Roles of the board, senior management and control functions
- Fire drills, desk-top exercises and lessons-learned logs
- How supervisors review plans: common findings and red flags
- Workshop: crisis simulation and plan improvement actions

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a briefing with worked examples and templates, the final module runs a crisis simulation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and plan.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of finance, risk or compliance in a regulated firm is helpful. No prior experience of recovery or wind-down planning is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions (2014)
- Basel Committee, Stress Testing Principles (2018)
- Basel Committee, Principles for Sound Liquidity Risk Management and Supervision (2008)
- ISO 22301:2019, Security and Resilience – Business Continuity Management Systems
