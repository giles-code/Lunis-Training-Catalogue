## Aim and Purpose

To give policymakers a clear understanding of the payments ecosystem, the new forms of digital money competing within it and the public policy questions they raise. Participants leave able to frame payments and e-money legislation, weigh the case for a central bank digital currency, set expectations of the central bank and regulators, and engage with international work on faster, cheaper cross-border payments.

**Coverage:** How payment systems work, the roles of central banks, banks and non-bank providers, payments regulation and licensing, fast payments and open banking, competition and access, stablecoins and tokenised deposits, central bank digital currencies, cross-border payments and remittances, operational resilience and financial crime, and a national payments strategy.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance ministries and treasuries responsible for payments, fintech and financial inclusion policy
- Legislative drafters, legal advisers and parliamentary committee staff working on payments or digital money legislation
- Staff of competition authorities, digital ministries and other public bodies with an interest in payments

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how retail, wholesale and cross-border payment systems work
- Describe the regulatory framework for payment and e-money providers and its policy choices
- Assess competition, access and inclusion issues in the payments market
- Evaluate the policy implications of stablecoins and tokenised money
- Weigh the case for and design choices of a central bank digital currency
- Design the elements of a national payments strategy

## Syllabus

### Module 1: How Payment Systems Work

Plain-language grounding in the payments value chain, the forms of money in use today, and the infrastructure that clears and settles retail, wholesale and cross-border payments.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish central bank money, commercial bank money and e-money
- Explain clearing, settlement and the role of payment infrastructures
- Describe card, account-to-account, mobile money and wallet payments

**Syllabus**

- Forms of money and the singleness of money
- Large-value, retail and fast payment systems
- Card schemes, mobile money, wallets and payment initiation
- The PFMI and the oversight of payment infrastructures

### Module 2: Regulating Payment and E-Money Providers

The legal framework for non-bank payment and e-money providers, the core protections it must deliver, and the policy choices in licensing, safeguarding and supervision.

**Objectives**

- Explain the licensing and prudential regime for payment and e-money institutions
- Assess safeguarding and insolvency protections for customer funds
- Allocate oversight responsibilities between the central bank and other authorities

**Syllabus**

- Licensing categories, capital and governance requirements
- Safeguarding, segregation and failures of payment firms
- Conduct, transparency of charges and fraud reimbursement
- Central bank oversight versus conduct supervision

### Module 3: Competition, Access and Innovation

How market structure, network effects and access to infrastructure shape payments competition, and the policy tools government has used to open markets, from open banking to fast payment mandates.

**Objectives**

- Assess concentration and network effects in payments markets
- Evaluate open banking, access regimes and interoperability mandates
- Identify the role of big tech platforms in payments

**Syllabus**

- Interchange, scheme fees and pricing regulation
- Access to settlement accounts and payment infrastructure
- Open banking and open finance frameworks
- Big tech in payments: data, competition and stability issues

### Module 4: Stablecoins and Tokenised Money

The rise of stablecoins and tokenised deposits as payment instruments, their risks to consumers, monetary sovereignty and stability, and the policy options for regulating them.

**Objectives**

- Explain how stablecoins and tokenised deposits differ from existing money
- Assess risks to monetary sovereignty, payments and financial stability
- Evaluate reserve, redemption and issuer requirements

**Syllabus**

- Stablecoin designs and de-pegging case studies
- Global stablecoin arrangements and FSB recommendations
- Currency substitution in smaller and emerging economies
- Tokenised deposits and settlement in central bank money

### Module 5: Central Bank Digital Currencies: The Policy Case

Why central banks are exploring retail and wholesale digital currencies, the objectives they serve, and the risks to banks, privacy and the financial system that government must weigh.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish retail and wholesale CBDC and their objectives
- Assess impacts on bank funding, credit and financial stability
- Evaluate privacy, inclusion and public trust considerations

**Syllabus**

- Motivations: monetary sovereignty, inclusion, efficiency and resilience
- Disintermediation, holding limits and remuneration choices
- Privacy, data and civil liberties
- Lessons from launched, piloted and paused CBDC projects

### Module 6: CBDC Design, Legal Foundations and Governance

The design and legal choices a CBDC requires, the roles of the central bank, government and private intermediaries, and the legislative and accountability questions for government and legislators.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main CBDC architecture and distribution models
- Identify the legal basis, legal tender and central bank mandate questions
- Define the decision rights of government, legislature and central bank

**Syllabus**

- Direct, intermediated and hybrid architectures
- Offline functionality, programmability and interoperability
- Legal basis, legal tender status and central bank law amendments
- Democratic accountability and the decision to launch

### Module 7: Cross-Border Payments, Remittances and Financial Crime

Why cross-border payments remain slow and costly, the G20 programme to improve them, and the financial crime, sanctions and resilience risks that new payment channels create.

**Objectives**

- Explain the frictions in cross-border payments and remittances
- Describe the G20 targets and the national actions they require
- Assess fraud, money laundering and operational resilience risks in payments

**Syllabus**

- Correspondent banking, de-risking and remittance costs
- The G20 roadmap: targets, building blocks and payment system links
- Authorised push payment fraud, scams and reimbursement policy
- Cyber resilience, outages and critical payment infrastructure

### Module 8: Designing a National Payments Strategy

Brings the programme together by examining how a national payments strategy is set, how legislation and institutions are aligned behind it, and how progress is measured.

**Objectives**

- Set national payments objectives for efficiency, inclusion and resilience
- Align legislation, institutions and public-private governance
- Produce a national payments strategy action plan

**Syllabus**

- Payments councils and public-private governance
- Digital public infrastructure, identity and government payments
- Measuring cost, access, usage and resilience
- Workshop: a national payments and digital money strategy

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a plain-language briefing with case studies and policy options analysis, ending in a strategy workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the government's priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with financial services or economic policy is helpful. No technical background in payments or monetary economics is required.

## Core Reading

- BIS and a group of central banks (2020), Central Bank Digital Currencies: Foundational Principles and Core Features

- FSB (2023), High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Global Stablecoin Arrangements
- CPMI and World Bank (2016), Payment Aspects of Financial Inclusion
