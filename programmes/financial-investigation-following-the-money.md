## Aim and Purpose

To give investigators and analysts the methods to make financial investigation a routine part of every case involving profit-generating crime. Participants leave able to obtain and analyse financial records, unpick corporate structures, calculate criminal benefit and present clear, defensible findings to prosecutors and the court.

**Coverage:** The purpose and principles of financial investigation, planning parallel investigations, sources of financial information, production orders and other powers, analysing bank and payment records, cash and informal value transfer, net-worth and source-and-application-of-funds analysis, corporate vehicles and beneficial ownership, digital and crypto-asset evidence, and presenting financial findings.

## Intended Audience

- Police and agency investigators handling acquisitive, organised or economic crime
- Financial investigators, intelligence analysts and FIU staff supporting investigations
- Asset recovery, customs and tax investigators who trace and evidence criminal finances

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the purpose of parallel financial investigation and when to start one
- Identify and obtain financial information lawfully using the appropriate powers
- Analyse bank, payment and cash records to establish money flows and patterns
- Apply net-worth and source-and-application-of-funds methods to evidence criminal benefit
- Trace funds through companies, trusts and nominees to their beneficial owners
- Present financial findings clearly to prosecutors, courts and partner agencies

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Financial Investigation: Purpose and Principles

Why every profit-generating crime should have a financial dimension, the objectives a financial investigation can serve, and what the FATF standards expect of investigative authorities.

**Objectives**

- Explain the evidential, intelligence and asset recovery purposes of financial investigation
- Distinguish parallel financial investigations from stand-alone money laundering cases
- Summarise the FATF expectations for investigative powers and outcomes

**Syllabus**

- Following the money: proving offences, finding assets and mapping networks
- Parallel financial investigation alongside predicate offence cases
- FATF Recommendations 30 and 31 and Immediate Outcome 7
- The financial investigator's role in a multi-disciplinary team

### Module 2: Planning and Managing a Financial Investigation

How to scope a financial investigation, set objectives and hypotheses, prioritise lines of enquiry and manage records, exhibits and disclosure from the outset.

**Objectives**

- Set investigation objectives, hypotheses and a lines-of-enquiry plan
- Prioritise enquiries in proportion to risk and resources
- Maintain decision logs, exhibits and audit trails from the start

**Syllabus**

- Scoping: subjects, periods, predicate offences and assets
- Investigation plans, hypotheses and lines of enquiry
- Coordination with intelligence, asset recovery and prosecutors
- Record-keeping, decision logs and early disclosure planning

### Module 3: Sources of Financial Information

The open, closed and restricted sources investigators can use to build a financial profile, and how to assess their reliability and the conditions attached to their use.

**Objectives**

- Identify open, closed and restricted sources of financial information
- Assess reliability, completeness and handling conditions
- Build an initial financial profile of a subject

**Syllabus**

- Public registers: companies, land, vehicles and court records
- FIU intelligence and tax, customs and law-enforcement holdings
- Open-source research and lifestyle indicators
- Building the subject's financial profile

### Module 4: Investigative Powers: Production Orders and Beyond

The legal tools for obtaining financial records and information, how to draft applications that are precise and justified, and how to work effectively with the institutions that must comply.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main powers to obtain financial information and their thresholds
- Draft focused, justified applications for production and disclosure orders
- Manage institution responses, deadlines and data formats

**Syllabus**

- Production, disclosure, account monitoring and customer information orders
- Search, seizure and powers to obtain electronic records
- Drafting applications: necessity, proportionality and specificity
- Working with institutions: contacts, formats and confidentiality

### Module 5: Analysing Bank and Payment Records

Techniques for reading and analysing account statements, payment data and supporting documents to identify patterns, links and anomalies that test the investigation's hypotheses.

**Objectives**

- Read account opening, statement and payment records accurately
- Apply spreadsheet and analytical techniques to large transaction sets
- Identify patterns indicating laundering, fraud or hidden assets

**Syllabus**

- Account opening files, statements, payment messages and card data
- Cleaning, normalising and reconciling transaction data
- Pattern analysis: round sums, pass-through activity and structuring
- Charting money flows and counterparties

### Module 6: Cash, Value Transfer and Alternative Remittance

How criminal cash is moved, stored and converted, how informal value transfer and alternative remittance operate, and the evidence investigators can obtain about them.

**Objectives**

- Explain how cash proceeds are placed and moved
- Describe informal value transfer and alternative remittance systems
- Identify evidence sources for cash-based and informal transfers

**Syllabus**

- Cash-intensive businesses and cash deposits
- Cross-border cash movements and declarations
- Informal value transfer and alternative remittance
- Evidencing cash: seizures, ledgers and communications

### Module 7: Net-Worth and Source-and-Application-of-Funds Analysis

Indirect methods for proving unexplained income and wealth, how to build a defensible net-worth or source-and-application-of-funds analysis, and how these support charges and confiscation.

**Objectives**

- Explain when indirect methods of proof are appropriate
- Build a net-worth and a source-and-application-of-funds statement
- Test the analysis against likely defence explanations

**Syllabus**

- Direct versus indirect methods of proof
- Net-worth method: assets, liabilities, expenditure and known income
- Source-and-application-of-funds statements
- Anticipating defences: cash savings, gifts, loans and inheritances

### Module 8: Corporate Vehicles and Beneficial Ownership

How companies, trusts, foundations and nominees are used to conceal ownership and move funds, and how investigators trace control through registers, records and cooperation.

**Objectives**

- Recognise how legal persons and arrangements are misused to conceal ownership
- Trace control and beneficial ownership using registers and institution records
- Identify the role of professional enablers

**Syllabus**

- Shell, shelf and front companies, trusts and nominee arrangements
- Beneficial ownership registers, service provider records and their limits
- Mapping ownership and control across jurisdictions
- Professional enablers: lawyers, accountants and company service providers

### Module 9: Digital Payments and Crypto-Asset Evidence

Obtaining and interpreting evidence from payment firms, e-money issuers, online platforms and crypto-asset service providers, and integrating it with traditional financial records.

**Objectives**

- Identify the records held by payment, e-money and crypto-asset service providers
- Integrate digital payment and blockchain data into the financial picture
- Recognise the evidential limits of analytics outputs

**Syllabus**

- Payment firms, e-money accounts and online marketplaces
- Crypto-asset service provider records and blockchain tracing basics
- Device, communications and other digital financial evidence
- Attribution, reliability and continuity of digital evidence

### Module 10: Presenting Financial Findings: Investigation Workshop

Brings the programme together in a case exercise in which teams analyse records, trace funds through corporate vehicles, calculate benefit and present their findings to a mock prosecution review.

**Objectives**

- Prepare clear financial statements, charts and summaries for prosecutors and courts
- Explain analysis and assumptions under challenge
- Apply the programme's methods to a realistic case

**Syllabus**

- Writing financial investigation reports and witness statements
- Charts, schedules and summaries that courts can follow
- Giving evidence and handling cross-examination on financial analysis
- Workshop: tracing, benefit calculation and case presentation

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with worked records and case exercises, ending in an investigation workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the participants' roles and caseload.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of criminal investigation, intelligence analysis or financial crime work is helpful. No accounting qualification is required.
