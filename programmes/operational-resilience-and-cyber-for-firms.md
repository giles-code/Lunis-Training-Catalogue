## Aim and Purpose

To equip the people who run and control a firm's critical operations with the frameworks and tools to keep important business services within tolerance through severe disruption. Participants leave able to map and test their services, manage ICT, cyber and third-party risk, and evidence resilience to the board and supervisors.

**Coverage:** Important business services, mapping, impact tolerances, scenario testing, ICT risk frameworks, incident management and reporting, third-party and critical provider risk, threat-led testing, response and recovery, and evidencing resilience to supervisors.

## Intended Audience

- Operational resilience, business continuity and operational risk professionals
- Chief information officers, information security and ICT risk staff
- Business service owners, outsourcing managers and senior managers accountable for resilience

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the shift from business continuity to outcome-focused operational resilience
- Identify important business services and map the people, processes, technology and third parties that support them
- Design impact tolerances and severe but plausible scenario tests
- Apply ICT, cyber and third-party risk frameworks aligned with international standards
- Implement incident management, reporting and recovery arrangements
- Evidence operational resilience to the board and supervisors and agree an action plan for the firm

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Foundations of Operational Resilience

Why supervisors have moved from business continuity to outcome-focused resilience, and the governance and accountability a firm needs to make the shift.

**Objectives**

- Explain the principles of operational resilience and how they differ from business continuity
- Assess the firm's governance and accountability for resilience
- Identify international standards relevant to the firm's resilience framework

**Syllabus**

- From continuity planning to resilience outcomes
- Board and senior manager accountability
- Basel Committee, FSB and IOSCO principles and ISO 22301
- Lessons from major operational disruptions

### Module 2: Identifying Important Business Services

Selecting the services whose disruption would cause intolerable harm to customers, market integrity or the firm's safety and soundness.

**Objectives**

- Apply criteria to identify the firm's important business services
- Distinguish business services from internal processes and systems
- Document and justify service selection to the board and supervisors

**Syllabus**

- Defining services from the customer's perspective
- Harm to customers, markets and the firm
- Granularity, consistency and common pitfalls
- Board approval and periodic review

### Module 3: Mapping Dependencies

Mapping the people, processes, technology, facilities, data and third parties that deliver each important business service to reveal vulnerabilities and concentration.

**Objectives**

- Design a proportionate mapping approach for important business services
- Identify single points of failure and concentration risks
- Use mapping to prioritise investment and remediation

**Syllabus**

- Mapping resources: people, processes, technology, data and facilities
- Third-party and intra-group dependencies
- Tools, data and keeping maps current
- Finding vulnerabilities through mapping

### Module 4: Impact Tolerances and Scenario Testing

Setting the maximum tolerable disruption for each important business service and testing whether the firm can stay within it under severe but plausible scenarios.

**Objectives**

- Design impact tolerances based on harm rather than recovery targets
- Design severe but plausible scenarios for testing
- Assess test results and plan remediation of vulnerabilities

**Syllabus**

- Impact tolerance metrics: time, volume and customer harm
- Impact tolerance versus risk appetite and recovery time objectives
- Scenario selection, severity and test methods
- Recording results, lessons learned and remediation

### Module 5: ICT Risk Management Frameworks

Building an ICT risk management framework covering governance, asset management, protection, detection and change, aligned with recognised international standards.

**Objectives**

- Design an ICT risk management framework proportionate to the firm
- Apply recognised control frameworks to ICT and information security
- Assess legacy technology, change and data integrity risks

**Syllabus**

- ICT governance, policies and the three lines
- Asset inventories, access management and protection
- NIST and ISO frameworks for cyber and information security
- Change management, legacy systems and technical debt

### Module 6: Cyber Risk and Threat-Led Testing

Understanding the cyber threat landscape facing the firm and using vulnerability assessment, penetration testing and threat-led red teaming to test defences.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main cyber threat actors and attack methods facing financial firms
- Assess the firm's cyber defences against the current threat landscape
- Plan threat-led testing and act on its findings

**Syllabus**

- Threat actors: criminal, state-sponsored and insider
- Ransomware, supply chain attacks and social engineering
- Vulnerability scanning, penetration testing and red teaming
- Threat intelligence and remediating test findings

### Module 7: Incident Management and Reporting

Detecting, classifying, escalating and responding to operational and cyber incidents, and meeting reporting expectations of authorities, customers and counterparties.

**Objectives**

- Design an incident management process with clear classification and escalation
- Apply incident reporting expectations to authorities and stakeholders
- Conduct post-incident reviews that address root causes

**Syllabus**

- Detection, triage, classification and severity
- Crisis management and decision-making under pressure
- Notifying the authority, customers and counterparties
- Post-incident review, root cause and lessons learned

### Module 8: Third-Party and Critical Provider Risk

Managing dependency on outsourced and third-party providers, including cloud and critical service providers, from due diligence to exit.

**Objectives**

- Assess third-party risk across the provider lifecycle
- Design contractual, oversight and exit arrangements for critical providers
- Evaluate concentration risk and dependency on cloud providers

**Syllabus**

- Third-party risk management lifecycle and register
- Due diligence, contracts and audit rights
- Cloud, concentration and critical provider risk
- Exit strategies and substitutability

### Module 9: Response and Recovery

Planning and rehearsing response and recovery so that important business services can be restored within tolerance, including from destructive cyber attacks.

**Objectives**

- Design response and recovery plans linked to impact tolerances
- Apply backup, data recovery and alternative delivery strategies
- Rehearse recovery through exercises involving senior management

**Syllabus**

- Response and recovery planning for important business services
- Immutable backups, data restoration and cyber recovery
- Communications with customers, markets and the authority
- Tabletop exercise: a severe cyber scenario

### Module 10: Evidencing Resilience and Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together into a self-assessment and evidence pack that shows the board and supervisors the firm can remain within tolerance, with a final action-planning workshop.

**Objectives**

- Produce a resilience self-assessment the board can approve and challenge
- Evidence resilience to supervisors through documents, testing and management information
- Produce a prioritised action plan for the firm's resilience

**Syllabus**

- The self-assessment document and board sign-off
- Resilience management information and board reporting
- Seeing the firm's resilience the way a supervisor does
- Workshop: the firm's twelve-month resilience action plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines practical frameworks with case studies, tabletop exercises and templates, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's business model.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of operational risk, business continuity or technology in a regulated firm is helpful. No technical cyber security background is required.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee, Principles for Operational Resilience (2021)
- Basel Committee, Revisions to the Principles for the Sound Management of Operational Risk (2021)
- FSB, Enhancing Third-Party Risk Management and Oversight: A Toolkit for Financial Institutions and Financial Authorities (2023)
- ISO 22301:2019, Security and Resilience – Business Continuity Management Systems
