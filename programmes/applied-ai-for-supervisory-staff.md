## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors the practical skills and confidence to use the authority's approved AI tools on real supervisory tasks, within its AI policy and confidentiality rules. Participants leave able to write effective prompts, speed up document review, drafting and analysis, and check AI outputs to the evidential standard supervisory work demands.

**Coverage:** Generative AI essentials, the authority's AI policy and confidentiality, prompting for supervisory tasks, reviewing firm documents and returns, drafting correspondence and reports, data analysis, verification and professional judgement, and building AI into supervisory workflows.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, inspectors, analysts and authorisation staff at all levels of seniority
- Policy, enforcement and financial crime staff who review documents, data and correspondence
- Team leaders responsible for embedding AI tools safely in their teams' work

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how generative AI tools work, what they do well and where they fail
- Apply the authority's AI policy, including data classification and confidentiality rules
- Write structured prompts that produce reliable outputs for supervisory tasks
- Use AI to review firm documents and returns, draft correspondence and analyse data
- Verify AI outputs and retain professional judgement and accountability for every decision
- Design repeatable AI-assisted workflows and share good practice across the team

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Generative AI Essentials for Supervisory Work

A practical introduction to how generative AI and large language models work, what they can and cannot do, and where they add value in supervisory work.

**Objectives**

- Explain in plain terms how large language models generate text
- Identify strengths and limits, including hallucination and outdated knowledge
- Recognise the supervisory tasks where AI tools add most value

**Syllabus**

- How large language models work: training, prompts and probabilistic output
- Chat assistants, document tools, analytics tools and AI built into office software
- Typical failure modes: hallucination, omission, overconfidence and bias
- Mapping the supervisory day: where AI helps and where it should not be used

### Module 2: Using AI Safely: Policy, Confidentiality and Data

The rules that govern AI use in the authority, covering approved tools, data classification, confidential supervisory information, records and accountability, so staff can use AI with confidence.

**Objectives**

- Apply the authority's AI policy and approved-tool list to everyday tasks
- Classify information correctly before it is shared with an AI tool
- Identify confidentiality, data protection and record-keeping obligations

**Syllabus**

- The authority's AI policy: [approved tools, permitted uses and prohibited uses]
- Confidential supervisory information, personal data and firm data
- Records, audit trails and disclosure of AI assistance
- Scenarios: safe, unsafe and grey-area uses

### Module 3: Prompting for Supervisory Tasks

How to write clear, structured prompts that give AI tools the role, context, task and output format they need, and how to refine prompts until results are dependable.

**Objectives**

- Structure prompts using role, context, task, constraints and format
- Refine outputs through iteration, examples and follow-up questions
- Build a reusable prompt library for common supervisory tasks

**Syllabus**

- Anatomy of a good prompt and common prompting mistakes
- Grounding the tool in source documents rather than its general knowledge
- Step-by-step reasoning, checklists and structured outputs
- Exercise: building team prompt templates

### Module 4: Reviewing Firm Documents and Returns

Using AI to read, summarise and compare firm submissions, policies, board packs and regulatory returns more quickly, while keeping control of what matters to the supervisory judgement.

**Objectives**

- Summarise long firm documents and board packs accurately
- Compare firm policies against requirements and previous submissions
- Extract key data and identify anomalies in firm returns

**Syllabus**

- Summarising board minutes, risk reports and business plans
- Gap analysis of policies against rules, guidance and good practice
- Comparing submissions over time and across peer firms
- Exercise: preparing for a firm meeting with AI-assisted review

### Module 5: Drafting with AI: Letters, Reports and Briefings

How to use AI to draft and improve supervisory letters, inspection reports, internal briefings and meeting notes in the authority's style, while keeping findings accurate and defensible.

**Objectives**

- Draft supervisory correspondence and reports from structured notes
- Edit drafts for clarity, tone and the authority's house style
- Ensure every finding in an AI-assisted draft is supported by evidence

**Syllabus**

- From notes to first draft: findings letters and inspection reports
- Briefing papers and summaries for senior management and committees
- Plain language, tone and consistency with the authority's style
- Exercise: drafting and reviewing a findings letter

### Module 6: Data Analysis with AI Tools

Using AI-enabled analytics to explore, clean, analyse and visualise supervisory data, from firm returns to complaints and market data, without needing to be a data specialist.

**Objectives**

- Use AI tools to clean, structure and explore supervisory datasets
- Generate and check analysis, formulas and charts
- Interpret results and recognise misleading or unreliable analysis

**Syllabus**

- Asking questions of spreadsheets and regulatory return data
- Trend, peer and outlier analysis for risk identification
- AI-generated formulas, queries and code: checking before trusting
- Exercise: identifying outlier firms from return data

### Module 7: Verification, Bias and Professional Judgement

How to check AI outputs to the standard supervisory work requires, recognise bias and error, and keep accountability for conclusions and decisions with the supervisor, not the tool.

**Objectives**

- Apply structured techniques to verify AI outputs against sources
- Recognise bias, omission and automation bias in AI-assisted work
- Maintain accountability and an evidential trail for supervisory decisions

**Syllabus**

- Source checking, citation testing and cross-checking with a second method
- Automation bias and the temptation to over-rely on fluent output
- When AI must not be used: decisions, sanctions and sensitive judgements
- Exercise: finding the errors in an AI-generated analysis

### Module 8: Building AI into Supervisory Workflows

Brings the programme together by redesigning everyday supervisory tasks around AI, measuring the benefit, sharing good practice and agreeing each participant's practical action plan.

**Objectives**

- Redesign recurring supervisory tasks as AI-assisted workflows
- Measure time saved, quality and risk in AI-assisted work
- Produce a personal and team plan for productive, safe AI use

**Syllabus**

- Mapping recurring tasks and choosing the right tool for each step
- Reusable templates, prompt libraries and team guidance
- Feedback to the authority's AI governance and SupTech teams
- Workshop: personal and team AI action plans

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module is hands-on, built around live exercises on realistic supervisory tasks using the authority's approved tools, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's AI policy and systems.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Everyday familiarity with office software is helpful. No prior experience of AI tools or data analysis is required.
