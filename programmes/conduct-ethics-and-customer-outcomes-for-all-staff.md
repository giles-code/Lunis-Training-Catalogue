## Aim and Purpose

To give staff at every level a practical understanding of what good conduct looks like in their own role and why it matters to customers, colleagues and the firm. Participants leave able to recognise ethical dilemmas, put customers' interests first and raise concerns with confidence.

## What It Covers

Culture and ethics, ethical decision-making, conflicts of interest, treating customers fairly and good outcomes, clear communication, customers in vulnerable circumstances, complaints, and speaking up, whistleblowing and personal accountability.

## Intended Audience

- All staff in regulated firms, from new joiners to experienced colleagues, as induction or annual refresher training
- Customer-facing, product, marketing, operations, technology and support staff whose work affects customer outcomes
- Team leaders and managers who set the tone for conduct and respond when concerns are raised

## Learning Objectives

- Explain what good conduct and a healthy culture look like in a regulated firm
- Apply ethical judgement to everyday dilemmas and grey areas
- Identify and manage conflicts of interest in their own work
- Deliver fair treatment and good outcomes for customers, including those in vulnerable circumstances
- Recognise, handle and learn from complaints
- Speak up, escalate concerns and take personal accountability for their own conduct

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about ninety minutes each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of short live sessions, and well suited to conversion into e-learning. Modules combine short briefings with real-world examples and scenario questions, work well as annual refresher training, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's policies and business.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior knowledge of regulation is required. Some experience of working with customers is helpful but not essential.

## Core Reading

- FSB (2018), Strengthening Governance Frameworks to Mitigate Misconduct Risk: A Toolkit for Firms and Supervisors
- G20/OECD (2022), High-Level Principles on Financial Consumer Protection
- FSB (2014), Guidance on Supervisory Interaction with Financial Institutions on Risk Culture: A Framework for Assessing Risk Culture
- ISO (2021), ISO 37002:2021 Whistleblowing Management Systems – Guidelines
