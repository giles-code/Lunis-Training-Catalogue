## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a firm grounding in risk-based AML/CFT, sanctions and market abuse supervision, together with the technical understanding to assess firms' use of AI to detect financial crime. Participants leave able to challenge AI-driven monitoring and screening, recognise emerging AI-enabled typologies and form evidence-based supervisory judgements.

**Coverage:** The AML/CFT framework and risk-based approach, ML/TF typologies, supervising AML/CFT controls, AI in transaction monitoring, model risk in financial crime systems, sanctions and adverse media screening, fraud and behavioural analytics, generative AI typologies, market abuse surveillance, and bias, accountability and SupTech.

## Intended Audience

- AML/CFT, sanctions and financial crime supervisors and inspectors
- Market surveillance, conduct and data analytics staff working on financial crime risk
- Senior managers shaping the authority's financial crime strategy and use of SupTech

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the international AML/CFT framework and apply a risk-based supervisory approach
- Identify ML/TF, fraud and market abuse typologies and the red flags they leave
- Assess how firms use AI and machine learning in monitoring, screening and fraud detection
- Evaluate model risk, explainability, bias and human oversight in financial crime AI
- Challenge firms' resilience to new typologies enabled by generative AI
- Design the authority's supervisory response, including its own use of SupTech

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The AML/CFT Framework and Risk-Based Supervision

The international foundations of AML/CFT supervision, the FATF Recommendations and mutual evaluations, and how authorities apply a risk-based approach to their populations of firms.

**Objectives**

- Explain the FATF standards and the supervisor's role within the national AML/CFT system
- Apply a risk-based approach to supervisory planning and resourcing
- Describe how mutual evaluations assess supervisory effectiveness

**Syllabus**

- FATF Recommendations, immediate outcomes and mutual evaluations
- National risk assessment, sector risk assessment and firm risk scoring
- Roles of the supervisor, the national FIU and law enforcement
- Regional AML frameworks and the move to supranational supervision

### Module 2: ML/TF Typologies and Red Flags

The main money laundering and terrorist financing typologies across banking, payments, investment and digital asset channels, and how to recognise red flags in real cases.

**Objectives**

- Identify key ML/TF typologies across sectors and channels
- Recognise red flags in customer, transaction and structural data
- Determine appropriate escalation when red flags are found

**Syllabus**

- Placement, layering and integration in modern financial channels
- Trade-based laundering, professional enablers and complex structures
- Terrorist and proliferation financing typologies
- Case study: tracing red flags through a laundering scheme

### Module 3: Supervising AML/CFT Controls in Practice

How to assess a firm's AML/CFT governance, business-wide risk assessment, due diligence and monitoring, and translate findings into consistent supervisory action.

**Objectives**

- Assess a firm's AML/CFT governance and business-wide risk assessment
- Evaluate customer due diligence, monitoring and suspicious activity reporting
- Apply consistent supervisory judgement and follow-up

**Syllabus**

- Board oversight, the AML compliance officer and management information
- Customer due diligence, enhanced due diligence and beneficial ownership
- Desk-based reviews, onsite inspections and sample testing
- From findings to remediation plans and enforcement referral

### Module 4: AI and Machine Learning in Transaction Monitoring

How firms move from rules-based to AI-driven monitoring using anomaly detection and network analytics, and the detection trade-offs supervisors should expect firms to manage.

**Objectives**

- Explain how AI and machine learning techniques are applied to transaction monitoring
- Assess claims about false-positive reduction and detection performance
- Challenge whether systems are tuned for detection rather than workload

**Syllabus**

- Rules-based versus AI-driven monitoring: capabilities and limits
- Anomaly detection, behavioural profiling and graph analytics
- False positives, false negatives and reading performance metrics critically
- Case study: an AI monitoring system under supervisory review

### Module 5: Supervising AI-Driven Financial Crime Systems

How to assess model risk management, validation, explainability and governance for AI systems deployed for AML/CFT purposes, including the human oversight that must accompany them.

**Objectives**

- Explain the model risk lifecycle for financial crime AI
- Assess validation, drift monitoring and explainability standards
- Evaluate ownership, sign-off and human oversight controls

**Syllabus**

- Model risk management expectations applied to financial crime AI
- Independent validation, back-testing and drift monitoring
- Explainability: balancing sophistication with the need to justify alerts
- Supervisory questions for a firm's AI model governance

### Module 6: Sanctions Screening and Adverse Media Monitoring

How firms meet sanctions obligations and use fuzzy matching and natural language processing for sanctions, PEP and adverse media screening, and how supervisors test calibration and control effectiveness.

**Objectives**

- Explain sanctions regimes and firms' screening obligations
- Assess the calibration and alert quality of AI-enabled screening tools
- Evaluate a firm's response to a potential sanctions breach

**Syllabus**

- UN, regional and national sanctions regimes and extraterritorial reach
- Fuzzy matching and NLP in name screening and PEP identification
- Calibration failures, list management and alert escalation
- Case study: a potential sanctions breach and the reporting response

### Module 7: Fraud Detection and Behavioural Analytics

How AI-enabled fraud detection works across payments and banking channels, from real-time scoring to behavioural biometrics, and how supervisors assess firms' fraud control frameworks.

**Objectives**

- Explain real-time fraud scoring and the data signals it relies on
- Identify payment fraud typologies, including authorised push payment fraud
- Assess the adequacy of a firm's fraud control framework

**Syllabus**

- Transaction, device and behavioural signals in fraud scoring
- Behavioural biometrics: strengths, limits and privacy considerations
- Authorised push payment fraud, account takeover and card fraud
- Criteria for assessing fraud controls and customer reimbursement

### Module 8: Generative AI and Emerging Financial Crime Typologies

How criminals use generative AI for synthetic identities, deepfake onboarding and large-scale social engineering, and the resilience firms and supervisors need to build.

**Objectives**

- Recognise financial crime typologies enabled by generative AI
- Assess firms' identity verification and liveness controls
- Challenge firms' resilience against AI-enabled social engineering

**Syllabus**

- Synthetic identity fraud and deepfake-enabled onboarding
- Voice cloning, deepfake video and AI-generated phishing
- Liveness detection, multi-factor verification and their limits
- Case study: a firm targeted by a deepfake-enabled fraud

### Module 9: Market Abuse Detection and Surveillance

How market abuse is detected through trade and communications surveillance, including AI-assisted tools, and how supervisors assess the adequacy of firms' own surveillance capability.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main categories and evolving typologies of market abuse
- Assess firms' trade surveillance systems and alert tuning
- Evaluate cross-market and cross-asset surveillance challenges

**Syllabus**

- Insider dealing, market manipulation and unlawful disclosure
- Spoofing, layering and cross-market manipulation
- Surveillance alerts, tuning and suspicious transaction reporting
- Case study: investigating a suspected market abuse alert

### Module 10: Bias, Accountability and the Authority's Own SupTech

Brings the programme together by examining fairness and accountability in financial crime AI and how the authority can use data and AI in its own supervision.

**Objectives**

- Assess bias and discriminatory outcomes in financial crime AI, including de-risking
- Evaluate audit trails and human-in-the-loop safeguards
- Design priorities for the authority's financial crime SupTech

**Syllabus**

- Bias sources, testing methods and remediation options
- Audit trails, accountability and customer impact of AI-driven decisions
- SupTech for AML/CFT: risk scoring, network analysis and data sharing
- Workshop: the authority's financial crime and AI action plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, grouped into three pathways and delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red flags and supervisory question exercises, and pathways or individual modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT, sanctions or conduct supervision is helpful. No technical background in AI or data science is required.

- FATF, Opportunities and Challenges of New Technologies for AML/CFT (2021)
