## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a structured, outcomes-focused approach to consumer and investor protection that applies across banking, insurance, investment and payments. Participants leave able to assess whether firms deliver fair outcomes in practice, recognise signs of consumer harm and exclusion, and turn analysis into proportionate supervisory action.

**Coverage:** Consumer protection principles, product governance, fair value, disclosure and consumer understanding, vulnerable customers, complaints handling and redress, issuer disclosure, financial inclusion and de-risking, outcomes monitoring, and emerging digital harms.

## Intended Audience

- Conduct and consumer protection supervisors and inspectors across all sectors
- Policy staff working on consumer protection, disclosure or financial inclusion
- Senior managers setting the authority's consumer protection priorities

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the principles and international standards underpinning consumer and investor protection
- Assess product governance, fair value and disclosure from the consumer's perspective
- Evaluate how firms identify and treat vulnerable customers and handle complaints
- Apply the principles of full, accurate and timely issuer disclosure to supervisory review
- Balance financial inclusion against financial crime and prudential objectives
- Use consumer outcomes data to prioritise and justify supervisory intervention

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Foundations of Consumer Protection Supervision

Why consumer and investor protection matters to supervisors, the international principles that underpin it, and how outcomes-based supervision differs from rules-based compliance checking.

**Objectives**

- Explain the rationale for consumer and investor protection
- Describe the relevant international principles and standards
- Distinguish outcomes-based from rules-based consumer supervision

**Syllabus**

- Information asymmetry, behavioural biases and consumer harm
- IOSCO investor protection objective and G20/OECD consumer protection principles
- Links between consumer outcomes, conduct risk and firm culture
- The customer journey as a supervisory lens

### Module 2: Product Governance and Target Markets

How firms should design, distribute and review products for a defined target market, and how supervisors test whether product governance works in practice.

**Objectives**

- Explain the product governance lifecycle from design to review
- Assess target market definition and distribution strategy
- Identify common product governance failures

**Syllabus**

- Product approval, target market and negative target market
- Distribution chains and manufacturer versus distributor responsibilities
- Ongoing product review and management information
- Red flags in product governance files

### Module 3: Fair Value Assessment

How to assess whether the price consumers pay is reasonable relative to the benefits, costs and quality they receive, and how to challenge firms' value assessments.

**Objectives**

- Apply a fair value framework to a financial product
- Challenge the evidence behind a firm's value assessment
- Identify pricing practices that cause foreseeable harm

**Syllabus**

- Price, benefits, costs and quality as value components
- Price walking, cross-subsidy and hidden charges
- Value across distribution chains and add-on products
- Exercise: testing a firm's fair value assessment

### Module 4: Disclosure, Communications and Consumer Understanding

How to evaluate whether disclosures and communications are clear, fair and not misleading, drawing on behavioural insights into how consumers actually process information.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate disclosure documents for consumer understanding
- Apply behavioural insights to communications and choice architecture
- Assess how firms test and monitor communication effectiveness

**Syllabus**

- Clear, fair and not misleading communications
- Behavioural insights: framing, defaults and information overload
- Digital journeys, dark patterns and financial promotions
- Consumer testing and monitoring of disclosure effectiveness

### Module 5: Identifying and Protecting Vulnerable Customers

How to assess whether firms identify customers in vulnerable circumstances and tailor products, communications and service, and how to spot the gap between policy and delivery.

**Objectives**

- Explain the drivers of vulnerability and vulnerability as a spectrum
- Assess a firm's vulnerable customer policy and frontline practice
- Identify gaps between stated policy and actual outcomes

**Syllabus**

- Drivers of vulnerability: health, life events, resilience and capability
- Frontline identification, training, tools and escalation routes
- Tailoring products, communications and service
- Case study: good and poor vulnerable customer treatment

### Module 6: Supervising Complaints Handling and Redress

How to supervise firms' complaints handling and root-cause analysis, how redress schemes operate, and how complaints data can serve as an early warning of wider harm.

**Objectives**

- Explain expectations for timely, fair complaints handling
- Assess root-cause analysis and its use in remediation
- Use complaints and redress data as supervisory intelligence

**Syllabus**

- The complaints process end to end and governance oversight
- Root-cause analysis: from individual complaints to systemic issues
- Ombudsman, statutory and voluntary redress schemes
- Complaints data as an early warning indicator

### Module 7: Issuer Disclosure as Investor Protection

How issuer disclosure functions as a core investor protection mechanism under the IOSCO Principles for Issuers, and how supervisors assess whether disclosure is full, accurate and timely.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of disclosure in protecting investors and markets
- Assess disclosure quality, timeliness and completeness
- Identify red flags linking disclosure weaknesses to wider risks

**Syllabus**

- IOSCO Principles for Issuers: full, accurate and timely disclosure
- Financial versus narrative and non-financial disclosure
- The role of auditors and other gatekeepers
- Case study: a disclosure red flag scenario

### Module 8: Financial Inclusion and Access to Services

The policy and supervisory considerations around financial inclusion, including barriers to access, the de-risking problem, and how proportionate regulation can support inclusive product design.

**Objectives**

- Explain how financial inclusion is defined, measured and constrained
- Assess the tension between financial crime controls and access
- Identify regulatory levers that support inclusive products

**Syllabus**

- Barriers: cost, documentation, trust, geography and digital literacy
- De-risking and FATF guidance on proportionate controls
- Simplified accounts, tiered due diligence and inclusive design
- Access to cash, branch closures and digital exclusion

### Module 9: Consumer Outcomes Monitoring and Supervisory Judgement

How to use firm data, complaints, market intelligence and thematic work to identify poor consumer outcomes, prioritise follow-up and communicate findings to firms.

**Objectives**

- Identify indicators of poor consumer outcomes in firm and market data
- Prioritise supervisory follow-up proportionately
- Communicate findings and required remediation to firms

**Syllabus**

- Outcome indicators: claims, cancellations, persistency and switching
- Thematic reviews, mystery shopping and consumer research
- Linking weak controls to wider conduct risk
- Remediation, redress exercises and enforcement referral

### Module 10: Emerging Consumer Harms and the Authority's Response

Brings the programme together by examining new sources of consumer harm, from digital distribution to AI-driven pricing, and building the authority's consumer protection plan.

**Objectives**

- Anticipate consumer harms from digital and AI-driven business models
- Evaluate the authority's consumer protection tools and gaps
- Produce a prioritised consumer protection action plan

**Syllabus**

- Digital distribution, buy-now-pay-later and finfluencers
- AI-driven pricing, personalisation and fairness
- Scams, fraud and the shifting boundary of firm responsibility
- Workshop: the authority's consumer protection priorities

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a practical briefing with case studies, red flags and supervisory question exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of conduct or consumer-facing supervision is helpful. No specialist background is required.

## Core Reading

- G20/OECD High-Level Principles on Financial Consumer Protection (2022)
- World Bank, Good Practices for Financial Consumer Protection (2017)
- CPMI and World Bank, Payment Aspects of Financial Inclusion (2016)
