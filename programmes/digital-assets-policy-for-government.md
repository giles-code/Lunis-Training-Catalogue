## Aim and Purpose

To give policymakers a clear, hype-free understanding of the technology, markets and risks behind crypto-assets, stablecoins and tokenisation, and the regulatory approaches countries have taken. Participants leave able to set a national policy stance, frame legislation and perimeter decisions, ask the right questions of regulators and industry, and meet international commitments from the FSB, IOSCO and FATF.

**Coverage:** Blockchain and distributed ledger technology, the crypto-asset market and its participants, risks to consumers, markets and financial stability, regulatory approaches and international standards, stablecoins and monetary sovereignty, tokenisation of real-world assets, innovation hubs and sandboxes, financial crime and the travel rule, and legislative design.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance ministries and treasuries developing digital asset, fintech and innovation policy
- Legislative drafters, legal advisers and parliamentary committee staff working on digital asset legislation
- Staff of investment promotion agencies, innovation offices and other public bodies engaging with the digital asset sector

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain blockchain, crypto-assets, stablecoins and tokenisation in plain language
- Assess the consumer, market integrity, financial crime and stability risks of digital assets
- Compare regulatory approaches and the international standards that apply to them
- Evaluate the policy implications of stablecoins and tokenised finance for the national system
- Weigh the benefits and risks of innovation hubs, sandboxes and investment promotion
- Design the core elements of a digital asset legislative and policy framework

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Blockchain and Digital Assets Explained

Plain-language grounding in how distributed ledgers, cryptography, wallets and smart contracts work, and the main types of digital asset they support.

**Objectives**

- Explain how distributed ledgers and consensus mechanisms work
- Distinguish payment tokens, utility tokens, security tokens, stablecoins and NFTs
- Separate genuine use cases from marketing claims

**Syllabus**

- Distributed ledgers, consensus, keys and wallets
- Public and permissioned blockchains and smart contracts
- A taxonomy of digital assets for policymakers
- The vocabulary officials need: DeFi, staking, bridges, oracles and layer 2

### Module 2: The Crypto-Asset Market and Its Risks

How the crypto-asset market is structured, who the main participants are, and the consumer, market integrity and financial stability risks revealed by recent market failures.

**Objectives**

- Describe exchanges, brokers, custodians, issuers and DeFi protocols
- Explain the risks of conflicts of interest, commingling and market manipulation
- Assess the links between crypto-asset markets and the wider financial system

**Syllabus**

- Market structure: centralised platforms, vertical integration and DeFi
- Consumer harm: fraud, mis-selling, custody losses and platform failures
- Case studies of exchange and lender collapses
- Financial stability: interconnectedness and spillover channels

### Module 3: Regulatory Approaches and International Standards

The policy choices countries have made, from prohibition to bespoke regimes, and the international recommendations from the FSB, IOSCO, BCBS and FATF that shape national frameworks.

**Objectives**

- Compare prohibition, existing-law, bespoke and hybrid regulatory approaches
- Explain the international standards and the peer review of their implementation
- Identify the gaps and arbitrage risks created by divergent national regimes

**Syllabus**

- Same activity, same risk, same regulation: principle and practice
- FSB global framework and IOSCO policy recommendations for crypto-assets
- Prudential treatment of bank crypto exposures
- Regional crypto-asset regimes compared: licensing, conduct and market abuse

### Module 4: Stablecoins, Monetary Sovereignty and Payments

Why stablecoins matter to government, the risks they pose to monetary sovereignty, payments and financial stability, and the policy options for issuance, reserves and redemption.

**Objectives**

- Explain how fiat-backed, asset-backed and algorithmic stablecoins work
- Assess the implications for monetary policy, capital flows and payment systems
- Evaluate policy options for reserve, redemption and issuer requirements

**Syllabus**

- Stablecoin designs and lessons from de-pegging events
- Currency substitution and monetary sovereignty in smaller economies
- Reserve quality, redemption rights and issuer authorisation
- Stablecoins, tokenised deposits and central bank digital currencies

### Module 5: Tokenisation and the Future of Financial Markets

How tokenisation of securities, funds and real-world assets could change market infrastructure, and what legal and regulatory changes government may need to make to enable it safely.

**Objectives**

- Explain tokenisation and its potential benefits for issuance, settlement and access
- Identify the legal certainty issues for ownership, settlement finality and insolvency
- Assess the policy case for pilot regimes and legislative change

**Syllabus**

- Tokenised bonds, funds and real-world assets
- Distributed ledger market infrastructure and settlement assets
- Legal foundations: property law, finality and custody
- Pilot regimes, sandboxes and public sector issuance

### Module 6: Innovation Hubs, Sandboxes and Investment Promotion

How governments attract digital asset business without compromising standards, the design of innovation hubs and sandboxes, and the reputational risks of competing on light-touch regulation.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish innovation hubs, regulatory sandboxes and accelerator programmes
- Evaluate the economic case and the reputational risks of attracting digital asset firms
- Define the respective roles of ministries, promotion agencies and regulators

**Syllabus**

- Innovation hub and sandbox design: eligibility, safeguards and exit
- Attracting firms: licensing speed, standards and the race to the bottom
- Case studies of financial centre strategies and their consequences
- Protecting regulatory independence in investment promotion

### Module 7: Financial Crime, Sanctions and Tax

The money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions and tax evasion risks of digital assets, and the government's responsibilities under FATF standards and international tax transparency frameworks.

**Objectives**

- Explain how digital assets are used for money laundering, fraud and sanctions evasion
- Describe FATF requirements for virtual assets and service providers, including the travel rule
- Identify tax reporting and information exchange obligations

**Syllabus**

- Typologies: mixers, privacy coins, scams and ransomware
- FATF Recommendation 15, VASP registration and the travel rule
- Blockchain analytics and law enforcement capability
- The OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and tax policy choices

### Module 8: Designing Digital Asset Legislation and Policy

Brings the programme together by examining how to define the perimeter, structure a licensing regime, allocate powers between authorities and plan implementation for a national digital asset framework.

**Objectives**

- Define the regulatory perimeter and key legal definitions
- Design licensing, conduct, custody and market abuse provisions and allocate supervisory powers
- Produce a national digital asset policy and legislative action plan

**Syllabus**

- Perimeter, definitions and technology neutrality
- Licensing, prudential, custody, conduct and market abuse provisions
- Transitional arrangements, enforcement powers and supervisory capacity
- Workshop: a national digital asset policy and legislative roadmap

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a plain-language briefing with case studies and policy options analysis, ending in a legislative design workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the government's priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with financial services policy is helpful. No technical background in blockchain or computing is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2021), Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and Virtual Asset Service Providers
- OECD (2022), Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and Amendments to the Common Reporting Standard
