## Aim and Purpose

To give authorisation and supervisory staff a practical, end-to-end method for fit and proper assessments, from controllers and qualifying holdings through source of wealth to competence, capacity and approval interviews. Participants leave able to gather and test evidence, reach defensible decisions, impose conditions or refuse where necessary, and monitor fitness on an ongoing basis.

**Coverage:** The fit and proper test and its components, controllers and qualifying holdings, source of wealth and source of funds, honesty and integrity, competence and capacity, approval interviews, conditions, refusals and procedural fairness, ongoing fitness and reassessment, and a case workshop.

## Intended Audience

- Authorisation and licensing staff responsible for approving owners, directors and key function holders
- Supervisors and inspectors who assess changes in control or monitor ongoing fitness
- Enforcement and legal staff involved in fitness-related decisions and challenges

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the purpose and components of the fit and proper test and the standards behind it
- Assess controllers, qualifying holdings and ownership chains, including beneficial ownership
- Evaluate source of wealth and source of funds evidence critically
- Assess the competence, capacity, honesty and integrity of directors and key function holders
- Plan and conduct effective approval interviews
- Reach, record and communicate defensible decisions, including conditions and refusals

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Fit and Proper Test: Purpose and Standards

Why the authority assesses the fitness and propriety of owners and individuals, the components of the test and the international standards that underpin it.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of fit and proper assessments in protecting firms and markets
- Describe the components of the test: integrity, competence, capacity and financial soundness
- Apply the burden and standard of proof to fitness decisions

**Syllabus**

- The gateway's role in preventing harm before it occurs
- Honesty, integrity, reputation, competence, capability and financial soundness
- International standards from the Basel Committee, IOSCO, IAIS and FATF
- Burden of proof, proportionality and consistency of decisions

### Module 2: Controllers, Qualifying Holdings and Ownership Chains

How to identify who owns and controls an applicant firm, assess proposed acquirers and see through complex or opaque ownership structures.

**Objectives**

- Identify controllers, qualifying holdings and ultimate beneficial owners
- Assess proposed acquirers and changes in control
- Recognise structures designed to obscure ownership or influence

**Syllabus**

- Control thresholds, acting in concert and indirect holdings
- Mapping ownership chains and identifying beneficial owners
- Assessing acquirers: reputation, financial soundness and strategy
- Red flags: nominees, trusts, layered structures and secrecy jurisdictions

### Module 3: Source of Wealth and Source of Funds

How to test where an owner's wealth and the funds for an acquisition or capital injection come from, and when the evidence is not good enough.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish source of wealth from source of funds
- Evaluate the credibility and sufficiency of supporting evidence
- Identify financial crime risks in funding arrangements

**Syllabus**

- Building a wealth narrative and testing its plausibility
- Documentary evidence: what to request and how to verify it
- Borrowed funds, complex financing and politically exposed persons
- Red flags and when to refer concerns to financial crime specialists

### Module 4: Honesty, Integrity and Reputation

How to assess the integrity of individuals and owners, the checks the authority should carry out and how to weigh past conduct and disclosures.

**Objectives**

- Design proportionate background and due diligence checks
- Assess the relevance and seriousness of past conduct
- Respond to non-disclosure, misleading information and adverse findings

**Syllabus**

- Criminal, regulatory, civil and insolvency checks
- Open-source research and information from other authorities
- Weighing seriousness, recency, pattern and rehabilitation
- Non-disclosure as a fitness issue in its own right

### Module 5: Competence, Capability and Capacity

How to assess whether directors and key function holders have the knowledge, experience and time to perform their roles, individually and as a board.

**Objectives**

- Assess competence against the specific role and the firm's risk profile
- Evaluate time commitment, multiple directorships and conflicts of interest
- Consider the collective suitability of the board and senior team

**Syllabus**

- Role profiles, experience and qualifications
- Capacity, other commitments and conflicts of interest
- Collective suitability, board composition and independence
- Key function holders: risk, compliance, MLRO and finance

### Module 6: Conducting Approval Interviews

How to plan and conduct interviews with proposed directors and key function holders that test competence and understanding rather than recitation.

**Objectives**

- Plan an approval interview with clear objectives and lines of questioning
- Use open and probing questions to test knowledge and judgement
- Record the interview and its conclusions as evidence

**Syllabus**

- Preparing the interview plan from the application file
- Scenario-based and probing questions for different roles
- Assessing answers: depth, ownership and red flags
- Role-play: interviewing a proposed chief executive and compliance officer

### Module 7: Decisions: Approvals, Conditions and Refusals

How to reach, document and communicate fit and proper decisions, including conditional approvals, withdrawals and refusals, with procedural fairness.

**Objectives**

- Weigh evidence to reach a reasoned and defensible decision
- Design conditions and undertakings that are clear and enforceable
- Apply procedural fairness when minded to refuse

**Syllabus**

- Structuring the decision paper and recommendation
- Conditional approvals, undertakings and restrictions
- Minded-to-refuse processes, representations and appeals
- Encouraging withdrawal and recording the rationale

### Module 8: Ongoing Fitness and Case Workshop

How fitness is monitored after approval and when reassessment is required, followed by a workshop in which participants work a complete case to a decision.

**Objectives**

- Identify triggers for reassessing fitness during supervision
- Coordinate with supervision and enforcement on fitness concerns
- Apply the full assessment method to a realistic case

**Syllabus**

- Ongoing obligations, notifications and annual certifications
- Triggers for reassessment and removal of approval
- Linking gateway findings to supervisory priorities
- Workshop: assessing a change in control and a director application to decision

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with realistic application files, interview role-play and decision exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's processes.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of authorisation, licensing or supervisory work is helpful. Participants new to the gateway may benefit from attending a foundation programme first.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2024), Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision
- IAIS (2019, as updated), Insurance Core Principles and Common Framework for the Supervision of Internationally Active Insurance Groups
- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- FATF (2023), Guidance on Beneficial Ownership of Legal Persons
