## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of how AI and natural language processing are applied to sanctions screening, PEP identification, and adverse media monitoring, and how to assess whether these tools are appropriately calibrated and effectively governed.

## Intended Audience

Mid-level supervisory and financial crime specialist staff assessing firms' financial crime controls.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how fuzzy matching and NLP are applied to sanctions screening
- Describe the challenges of PEP identification at scale
- Assess the calibration of an adverse media monitoring system
- Evaluate alert quality and escalation processes
- Identify supervisory red flags in a firm's screening framework

## Syllabus

**Session 1: AI in sanctions and PEP screening (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain fuzzy matching, NLP screening, and PEP challenges*
- Fuzzy matching techniques in sanctions list screening
- NLP-based approaches to name and entity matching
- Challenges of PEP identification: scale, data quality, false matches
- Regulatory expectations for sanctions screening effectiveness
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: sanctions screening challenges from participants' own experience

**Session 2: Adverse media monitoring and calibration (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess calibration and evaluate alert quality*
- How adverse media monitoring tools work
- Calibrating sensitivity to avoid both under- and over-alerting
- Assessing alert quality and escalation workflows
- Common calibration failures
Activities:
- Group exercise: assess the calibration of a sample adverse media monitoring configuration

**Session 3: Supervisory red flags and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Identify supervisory red flags*
- Common red flags in a firm's screening framework
- Case study: a screening framework with calibration weaknesses
Activities:
- Case study: identify red flags and propose supervisory follow-up

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: fuzzy matching, PEP challenges, calibration, red flags
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: AI in sanctions and PEP screening (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Adverse media monitoring and calibration (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Supervisory red flags and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Sanctions screening effectiveness depends heavily on matching calibration, which is easy to get wrong in both directions
- PEP identification at scale is a genuinely hard data problem, not just a policy setting
- Alert quality, not just alert volume, is the right measure of a screening system's effectiveness
