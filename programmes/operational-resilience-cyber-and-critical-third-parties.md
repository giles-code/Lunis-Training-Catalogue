## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a structured, evidence-based approach to operational resilience and cyber risk, so that they test firms' resilience claims rather than accept them. Participants leave able to assess governance, impact tolerances, testing, incident response and third-party risk, identify red flags and contribute to the authority's own resilience strategy.

**Coverage:** Operational resilience foundations, the cyber threat landscape, supervisory tolerance and strategy, ICT governance and controls, important business services and impact tolerances, scenario and threat-led testing, incident management and reporting, third-party and cloud risk, critical third-party oversight and systemic concentration, and sector-wide resilience.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and inspectors assessing operational resilience, ICT and cyber risk across banking, insurance, investment, payments and market infrastructure
- Technology risk, cyber and outsourcing specialists supporting supervisory teams
- Policy staff and senior managers shaping the authority's resilience, cyber and critical third-party approach

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the principles of operational resilience and how they differ from business continuity and traditional operational risk
- Assess a firm's ICT and cyber governance, controls and use of threat intelligence
- Evaluate important business services, impact tolerances and the credibility of resilience testing
- Challenge firms' incident response, recovery and reporting, and apply proportionate supervisory intervention
- Assess third-party, cloud and concentration risk, including the oversight of critical third parties
- Design a risk-based supervisory strategy for operational resilience and cyber risk across the regulated population

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Foundations of Operational Resilience

What operational resilience means, why it has become a core supervisory objective, and how it builds on business continuity, operational risk management and international standards.

**Objectives**

- Explain the core concepts of operational resilience and their supervisory rationale
- Distinguish operational resilience from business continuity and operational risk management
- Identify the international principles shaping operational resilience regimes

**Syllabus**

- From preventing failure to assuming disruption: the resilience mindset
- Operational resilience regimes and their common building blocks
- Basel Committee, FSB, IOSCO and CPMI principles on resilience and cyber
- Proportionality across sectors and firm sizes

### Module 2: The Cyber Threat Landscape for Financial Services

The threat actors, attack vectors and sector-specific vulnerabilities facing regulated firms and market infrastructure, and how threat intelligence should shape supervisory priorities.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main categories of cyber threat actor and their motivations
- Identify sector-specific vulnerabilities in payments, trading, custody and core systems
- Assess how threat intelligence should inform supervisory priorities

**Syllabus**

- Threat actors: nation-state, organised crime, insiders and opportunists
- Attack vectors: phishing, ransomware, supply chain compromise and data extortion
- Legacy systems and technical debt as vulnerability drivers
- Sources and supervisory use of threat intelligence

### Module 3: Supervisory Tolerance and Strategy for Cyber Risk

How the authority sets an explicit tolerance for cyber and operational disruption, prioritises scarce specialist resource and applies proportionality across the regulated population.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of a supervisory risk tolerance for cyber and operational disruption
- Apply risk-tiering and proportionality to the allocation of cyber supervisory resource
- Evaluate the balance between thematic reviews and firm-specific deep dives

**Syllabus**

- Why cyber risk needs an explicit tolerance statement
- Risk-tiering firms by systemic importance and digital exposure
- Thematic reviews, deep dives and data-led monitoring
- Building and retaining specialist cyber expertise in the authority

### Module 4: Assessing ICT and Cyber Governance and Controls

How to assess board oversight, ICT risk management frameworks, security policies and data integrity controls, and how to test whether stated policy is followed in practice.

**Objectives**

- Assess board and senior management oversight of ICT and cyber risk
- Evaluate a firm's ICT risk management framework and three lines of defence
- Identify gaps between stated security policy and operational practice

**Syllabus**

- Board engagement, reporting and accountability for technology risk
- ICT risk management frameworks, asset inventories and change control
- Access management, encryption, patching and data integrity controls
- Red flags and supervisory questions on cyber governance

### Module 5: Important Business Services and Impact Tolerances

How firms identify important business services, map their dependencies and set impact tolerances, and how supervisors judge whether those choices are credible and owned by the board.

**Objectives**

- Explain how important business services are identified and mapped
- Assess whether impact tolerances are appropriately set and justified
- Challenge board engagement in setting and approving tolerances

**Syllabus**

- Identifying important business services from the customer and market perspective
- Mapping people, processes, technology, facilities and third parties
- Impact tolerance versus risk appetite: the difference that matters
- Common weaknesses: services defined too narrowly, tolerances set too loosely

### Module 6: Resilience Testing and Threat-Led Penetration Testing

The range of resilience and cyber testing firms carry out, from vulnerability scanning to severe-but-plausible scenarios and threat-led penetration testing, and how supervisors use test and audit results as evidence.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main types of scenario, cyber and threat-led testing
- Assess the quality, independence and coverage of a firm's testing programme
- Evaluate whether test and audit findings are remediated in a timely way

**Syllabus**

- Designing severe-but-plausible scenarios against impact tolerances
- Threat-led penetration testing: scoping, threat intelligence and red teaming
- Internal audit and testing data as supervisory evidence
- Red flags: repeat findings, narrowed scope and downgraded severity

### Module 7: Incident Management, Reporting and Supervisory Intervention

How firms detect, contain and recover from major operational and cyber incidents, what incident reporting should tell the authority, and how supervisors intervene without obstructing recovery.

**Objectives**

- Assess the adequacy of a firm's incident response and recovery procedures
- Evaluate incident reports and notifications for completeness and timeliness
- Apply proportionate supervisory intervention during a live incident

**Syllabus**

- Incident response phases: detection, containment, eradication and recovery
- Incident classification, reporting thresholds and root-cause analysis
- Recovery time objectives, backups and data restoration
- Simulation: supervisory response to a ransomware incident

### Module 8: Third-Party, Cloud and Outsourcing Risk

Why reliance on third parties and cloud providers has grown, the risks it creates for individual firms, and how supervisors assess due diligence, contracts, oversight and exit planning.

**Objectives**

- Explain the risks of outsourcing and cloud adoption, including the shared responsibility model
- Assess a firm's third-party risk management framework and register of arrangements
- Evaluate the credibility of exit strategies for critical outsourced services

**Syllabus**

- Outsourcing, cloud and intragroup arrangements: what is critical
- Due diligence, contractual rights, audit access and sub-outsourcing
- Ongoing oversight and performance monitoring of providers
- Exit planning and substitutability in practice

### Module 9: Critical Third Parties and Systemic Concentration

How sector-wide reliance on a small number of technology providers creates systemic risk, and how critical third-party oversight regimes and cross-authority cooperation are developing in response.

**Objectives**

- Analyse concentration risk arising from shared reliance on dominant providers
- Explain the design of critical third-party oversight regimes
- Evaluate how firm-level supervision and direct provider oversight fit together

**Syllabus**

- Mapping sector-wide dependencies and single points of failure
- Designation, oversight powers and resilience testing of critical third parties
- International work on third-party risk: FSB, BCBS and IOSCO
- Cooperation with other authorities and cross-border information sharing

### Module 10: Sector-Wide Resilience and the Authority's Strategy

Brings the programme together into a sector-level view, from market-wide exercises and information sharing to crisis coordination and the authority's own resilience supervision roadmap.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of sector-wide exercises and information-sharing arrangements
- Assess how operational incidents can escalate into financial stability events
- Design priorities for the authority's resilience and cyber supervision programme

**Syllabus**

- Sector-wide exercises and cyber information-sharing arrangements
- Contagion from operational failure to liquidity and confidence
- Emerging risks: AI-enabled attacks and quantum threats to cryptography
- Workshop: the authority's operational resilience supervision roadmap

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red flags and supervisory question exercises, including a live incident simulation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of financial supervision or operational risk is helpful. No technical background in IT or cybersecurity is required.

- FSB, Effective Practices for Cyber Incident Response and Recovery (2020)
