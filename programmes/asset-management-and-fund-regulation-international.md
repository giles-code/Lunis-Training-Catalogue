## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and practitioners a clear map of how funds, their managers and their distribution are regulated around the world, anchored in IOSCO principles and FSB recommendations. Participants leave able to determine which type of regime applies to a given structure, test arrangements for delegation, custody, liquidity, leverage and valuation, and anticipate how authorities are likely to approach them.

**Coverage:** The asset management value chain and regulatory perimeter, the international standard-setting architecture, retail, professional and alternative fund regimes, manager authorisation, delegation and substance, distribution and investor disclosure, depositaries and custody, liquidity management tools, leverage and valuation, cross-border passporting and mutual recognition, sustainability disclosure and greenwashing, and operational resilience and emerging fund models.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, authorisation and policy staff at securities and markets authorities covering funds and asset managers
- Compliance, legal, risk and product staff at fund managers, investment managers, distributors and fund platforms
- Directors, depositary, custody and fund administration staff who need a working map of fund regulation across jurisdictions

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how fund regulation is organised internationally and determine which type of regime applies across the value chain
- Distinguish product-regulated retail funds from manager-regulated professional and alternative fund regimes
- Assess manager authorisation, delegation, substance and depositary arrangements against international standards
- Evaluate liquidity management tools, leverage and valuation and what can go wrong
- Apply the principles of fair distribution, investor disclosure, cross-border recognition and sustainability claims
- Identify how operational resilience, supervisory convergence and new fund models are reshaping the sector

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Asset Management Value Chain and Regulatory Perimeter

Level-setting on investing, asset management and collective investment, who does what across the value chain, and a framework for deciding where the regulatory perimeter falls for a given activity or structure.

**Objectives**

- Explain what asset management and collective investment schemes are and why investors use them
- Map the value chain from product design and distribution to management, custody and administration
- Determine which activities fall inside the perimeter by asking what, who and how

**Syllabus**

- Collective investment versus segregated mandates and wealth management
- Fund vehicles: corporate, contractual, trust and partnership structures
- Activities, entities and products as three lenses on regulation
- Break-out: placing a fund structure inside the regulatory perimeter

### Module 2: How Fund Regulation Is Organised Internationally

How international standard-setters shape national fund rules, the division of labour between IOSCO and the FSB, and how standards are turned into legislation, rules and guidance at national and regional level.

**Objectives**

- Explain the roles of IOSCO, the FSB and the G20 in setting asset management standards
- Describe the IOSCO Objectives and Principles relevant to collective investment schemes and managers
- Analyse how authorities translate standards into primary law, rules, guidance and supervisory practice

**Syllabus**

- The international standard-setting architecture and peer review
- IOSCO principles for collective investment schemes and market intermediaries
- FSB work on asset management and non-bank financial intermediation
- Layers of national and regional rule-making and their interaction

### Module 3: Retail, Professional and Alternative Fund Regimes

The two dominant models of fund regulation, product rules for retail funds and manager rules for alternative funds, and the specialist regimes for money market, long-term and venture funds that sit between them.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish product-regulated retail funds from manager-regulated alternative fund regimes
- Explain eligible asset, diversification, borrowing and concentration rules for retail funds
- Assess when a specialist fund label adds investor protection and when it adds complexity

**Syllabus**

- Retail fund product rules and the logic of a regulated fund brand
- Alternative fund manager regimes: thresholds, registration and private placement
- Investor classification: retail, professional and qualifying investor access
- Specialist regimes for money market, long-term and venture capital funds

### Module 4: Authorising Managers: Governance, Delegation and Substance

What authorities require of fund managers at authorisation and on an ongoing basis, how managers delegate functions while retaining responsibility, and how substance is evidenced and tested.

**Objectives**

- Explain fitness, capital, governance, conflicts and remuneration requirements for fund managers
- Assess delegation arrangements and the manager's retained oversight of delegates
- Identify letter-box risk and how firms evidence substance to supervisors

**Syllabus**

- Authorisation criteria: programme of operations, people, capital and resources
- Fund governance: boards, independent oversight and conflicts of interest
- Delegation of portfolio and risk management and third-country delegates
- Substance: senior management, decision-making and the letter-box test

### Module 5: Fund Distribution, Suitability and Investor Disclosure

How funds reach investors through advisers, platforms and direct channels, the conduct rules that govern distribution, and the disclosure and costs principles designed to protect investors.

**Objectives**

- Explain product governance, target market and distribution oversight obligations
- Apply suitability, appropriateness and conflict of interest principles to fund distribution
- Assess key investor disclosures and costs and charges from manager to end investor

**Syllabus**

- Distribution models: advised, execution-only, platforms and digital channels
- Product governance, target market and inducements
- Prospectus, key information documents, periodic reports and marketing communications
- Costs and charges, performance fees and value for investors

### Module 6: Depositaries, Custody and Safeguarding Investor Assets

Why the separation of fund assets from the manager is fundamental to investor protection, the safekeeping and oversight roles of depositaries and custodians, and what happens when safeguarding fails.

**Objectives**

- Explain IOSCO standards on the safekeeping and segregation of fund assets
- Assess depositary and custodian duties, independence and liability
- Evaluate safeguarding arrangements for non-traditional and digital assets

**Syllabus**

- Segregation, safekeeping and asset verification across custody chains
- Depositary oversight of the manager and cash flow monitoring
- Independence, conflicts of interest and liability for loss of assets
- Case study: a safeguarding failure and its lessons for investors

### Module 7: Liquidity, Leverage and Valuation: The Prudential Toolkit

How international standards address liquidity mismatch, leverage and valuation in funds, the liquidity management tools open-ended funds are expected to have, and the red flags that precede suspensions and losses.

**Objectives**

- Explain the FSB and IOSCO recommendations on liquidity mismatch and liquidity management tools
- Assess leverage measures, limits and reporting for different fund types
- Identify valuation governance weaknesses, especially for hard-to-value assets

**Syllabus**

- Liquidity mismatch, stress testing and fund liquidity classification
- Anti-dilution tools, gates, notice periods and suspensions
- Leverage: the IOSCO framework, gross and commitment measures
- Valuation policies, independence and red flags

### Module 8: Cross-Border Distribution, Passporting and Mutual Recognition

How funds and managers operate across borders through regional passports, bilateral mutual recognition and private placement, and how home and host authorities share responsibility for investor protection.

**Objectives**

- Compare regional fund passports, mutual recognition and private placement routes
- Explain home and host responsibilities and supervisory cooperation arrangements
- Assess the entry conditions and marketing rules that apply to foreign funds

**Syllabus**

- Regional fund passports: design features and conditions for access
- Bilateral mutual recognition and equivalence-based arrangements
- Private placement, reverse solicitation and pre-marketing
- Information sharing, the IOSCO MMoU and host supervision

### Module 9: Sustainability Disclosure and Greenwashing Risk

How sustainability-related disclosure applies to funds and managers, from entity-level reporting to product labels and naming conventions, and whether disclosure prevents greenwashing.

**Objectives**

- Explain the IOSCO recommendations on sustainability-related practices in asset management
- Assess sustainability claims in fund documents, names, labels and marketing
- Evaluate disclosure-based and label-based approaches to sustainable fund regulation

**Syllabus**

- Entity-level disclosure and the ISSB standards
- Product categories, sustainability labels and fund naming rules
- Stewardship, engagement and evidencing sustainability outcomes
- Break-out: spotting greenwashing and greenbleaching

### Module 10: Operational Resilience, Convergence and Emerging Fund Models

Brings the programme together with the cross-cutting rules that reach asset managers, the tools that drive supervisory convergence, and the private market, exchange-traded and tokenised fund models reshaping the sector.

**Objectives**

- Explain operational resilience, technology and third-party risk expectations for asset managers
- Describe how peer reviews, guidance and supervisory colleges drive convergence
- Identify emerging fund models and regulatory topics and agree priorities

**Syllabus**

- Operational resilience, cyber risk and critical third-party providers
- Private markets, semi-liquid funds and retail access
- Exchange-traded funds and tokenised fund units
- Workshop: the reform horizon and a twelve-month priorities plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a technical briefing with comparative case studies and break-out exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisors, managers or distributors.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of asset management, funds or their supervision is helpful. No legal background or knowledge of any particular jurisdiction's rules is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
- IOSCO, Recommendations on Sustainability-Related Practices, Policies, Procedures and Disclosure in Asset Management (2021)
- IOSCO, Thematic Analysis: Emerging Risks in Private Finance (2023)
