## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and central bankers a clear, technology-neutral understanding of how stablecoins, tokenised deposits and central bank digital currencies work, and of the risks they pose to consumers, payment systems and monetary and financial stability. Participants leave able to assess issuers' reserves, redemption and resilience, challenge business models and contribute to the authority's policy on digital money.

**Coverage:** The forms of digital money and the singleness of money, stablecoin design and business models, authorisation and prudential requirements, reserves, redemption and run risk, tokenised deposits, retail and wholesale CBDCs, payments and financial stability, financial crime and consumer protection, cross-border interoperability, and supervising digital money issuers.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors responsible for e-money, payment, crypto-asset and banking firms engaging with digital money
- Central bank staff in payments, market infrastructure, financial stability and monetary policy functions
- Policy, legal and innovation teams shaping the authority's approach to stablecoins, tokenised deposits and CBDCs

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how stablecoins, tokenised deposits and CBDCs differ in design, legal claim and risk
- Assess stablecoin reserve composition, custody, redemption arrangements and run risk
- Evaluate authorisation, prudential and conduct requirements for issuers of digital money
- Analyse the implications of digital money for payment systems, bank funding, monetary sovereignty and financial stability
- Identify financial crime and consumer protection risks and the supervisory responses to them
- Design a supervisory and policy approach to digital money issuers and arrangements

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Landscape of Digital Money

A map of public and private money in digital form, the role of trust and settlement in money, and why the singleness of money matters to supervisors and central banks.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish central bank money, commercial bank money, e-money and stablecoins
- Explain the singleness of money and why it underpins trust in payments
- Identify the drivers of private and public innovation in digital money

**Syllabus**

- The two-tier monetary system and settlement in central bank money
- E-money, stablecoins, tokenised deposits and CBDCs compared
- Legal claims: on whom, for what and when
- BIS, CPMI and FSB perspectives on the future monetary system

### Module 2: Stablecoin Design and Business Models

How stablecoins are structured, issued, distributed and redeemed, the economics of issuers, and why different stabilisation mechanisms carry very different risks.

**Objectives**

- Explain fiat-backed, asset-referenced, crypto-collateralised and algorithmic designs
- Analyse the revenue model and incentives of stablecoin issuers
- Map the roles of issuers, reserve managers, custodians, distributors and wallet providers

**Syllabus**

- Single-currency and asset-referenced stablecoins
- Crypto-collateralised and algorithmic models and their failures
- Issuance, primary redemption and secondary market trading
- Use cases: crypto trading, payments, remittances and settlement

### Module 3: Regulating Stablecoins: Authorisation and Prudential Requirements

How regional crypto-asset regimes and international recommendations approach stablecoin issuers, from authorisation and governance to capital, liquidity, recovery and orderly redemption plans.

**Objectives**

- Explain the international standards for stablecoin arrangements
- Compare regulatory approaches to single-currency and asset-referenced stablecoins
- Assess an issuer's authorisation application against core requirements

**Syllabus**

- FSB high-level recommendations and the Basel Committee prudential treatment
- Authorisation, governance, fit and proper and conflicts of interest
- Own funds, liquidity, recovery and redemption plans
- Enhanced requirements for significant or systemic stablecoins

### Module 4: Reserve Assets, Redemption and Run Risk

How reserve composition, custody and redemption design determine whether a stablecoin holds its peg under stress, and what de-pegging episodes teach supervisors about testing resilience.

**Objectives**

- Assess reserve composition, segregation, custody and attestation
- Evaluate redemption rights and their practical operation under stress
- Analyse run dynamics and lessons from de-pegging episodes

**Syllabus**

- Eligible reserve assets, concentration and liquidity under stress
- Segregation, bankruptcy remoteness and custody of reserves
- Attestations versus audits: what reserve reports actually prove
- Run risk compared with bank runs and money market fund runs

### Module 5: Tokenised Deposits and Bank-Issued Digital Money

How banks are representing deposits on distributed ledgers, how tokenised deposits differ from stablecoins in legal and prudential terms, and the supervisory questions they raise.

**Objectives**

- Explain how tokenised deposits work and how they differ from stablecoins
- Assess prudential, deposit protection and operational implications for banks
- Evaluate interoperability between tokenised deposits of different banks

**Syllabus**

- Tokenised deposits as bank liabilities: legal and accounting treatment
- Capital, liquidity and deposit insurance implications
- Programmability, smart contracts and operational risk
- Shared ledgers and settlement between banks

### Module 6: Central Bank Digital Currencies: Retail and Wholesale

The motivations for retail and wholesale CBDCs, the main design choices central banks face, and the implications for privacy, intermediaries and the supervisory perimeter.

**Objectives**

- Explain the policy objectives behind retail and wholesale CBDCs
- Analyse key design choices: architecture, holding limits, privacy and offline use
- Assess the roles and supervision of intermediaries distributing a CBDC

**Syllabus**

- Retail CBDC: objectives, architecture and remuneration
- Wholesale CBDC and settlement of tokenised assets
- Privacy, data protection and holding limits
- Intermediary roles: onboarding, wallets and customer service

### Module 7: Payments, Monetary Sovereignty and Financial Stability

How digital money could reshape payment systems, bank funding and monetary transmission, including currency substitution risks for smaller and emerging economies.

**Objectives**

- Analyse the impact of digital money on bank deposits, funding and credit intermediation
- Assess the application of the PFMI to systemic stablecoin arrangements
- Evaluate currency substitution and monetary sovereignty risks

**Syllabus**

- Deposit outflows, disintermediation and fire-sale channels
- Systemic stablecoin arrangements and the PFMI
- Monetary policy transmission and currency substitution
- Interconnections between stablecoins, crypto markets and traditional finance

### Module 8: Financial Crime and Consumer Protection

The money laundering, sanctions evasion and fraud risks associated with digital money, and the consumer protection issues arising from disclosure, redemption and wallet custody.

**Objectives**

- Identify typologies of illicit use of stablecoins and other digital money
- Apply FATF standards, including the travel rule, to digital money arrangements
- Evaluate consumer disclosure, redemption and complaints arrangements

**Syllabus**

- Illicit finance typologies, unhosted wallets and peer-to-peer transfers
- FATF standards, the travel rule and freezing capabilities
- Blockchain analytics as a supervisory and compliance tool
- Consumer disclosures, marketing and redemption at par

### Module 9: Cross-Border Payments and Interoperability

How digital money is being used to improve cross-border payments, the risks of regulatory arbitrage and fragmentation, and the role of international cooperation and multi-currency platforms.

**Objectives**

- Explain the G20 objectives for faster, cheaper and more transparent cross-border payments
- Assess interoperability and fragmentation risks across ledgers and jurisdictions
- Evaluate cooperation arrangements for globally active stablecoin issuers

**Syllabus**

- The G20 cross-border payments roadmap and digital money
- Multi-currency and multi-CBDC platform experiments
- Regulatory arbitrage and offshore issuance of stablecoins
- Supervisory colleges and cross-border information sharing

### Module 10: Supervising Digital Money Issuers in Practice

Brings the programme together into a practical supervisory approach for digital money issuers, from ongoing monitoring and stress testing to recovery, wind-down and the authority's policy stance.

**Objectives**

- Apply a supervisory assessment framework to a digital money issuer
- Formulate a supervisory response to reserve or redemption pressure
- Design priorities for the authority's digital money policy and supervision

**Syllabus**

- Ongoing monitoring: reserve data, redemption flows and on-chain indicators
- Liquidity stress testing and recovery and wind-down planning
- Coordination between supervisors, central banks and payment overseers
- Workshop: supervisory response to a de-pegging scenario

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red flags and supervisory question exercises, closing with a de-pegging scenario workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisory or central bank audiences.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

A basic understanding of crypto-assets and blockchain is helpful, for example from a fundamentals course. Familiarity with payments or banking supervision is useful but not essential.

## Core Reading

- CPMI-IOSCO, Application of the Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures to Stablecoin Arrangements (2022)
- BIS and central banks, Central Bank Digital Currencies: Foundational Principles and Core Features (2020)
