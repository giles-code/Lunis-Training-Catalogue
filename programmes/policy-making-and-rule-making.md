## Aim and Purpose

To give policy, legal and supervisory staff a practical, end-to-end grounding in how a supervisory authority develops, consults on and issues rules and guidance. Participants leave able to build an evidence-based case for intervention, test it for proportionality, draft enforceable rules and evaluate their impact after implementation.

**Coverage:** The policy cycle and rule-making powers, problem definition and options analysis, cost-benefit analysis and proportionality, consultation and stakeholder engagement, legal drafting, implementing international standards, policy for innovation including sandboxes, and post-implementation review.

## Intended Audience

- Policy and rule-making staff developing rules, guidance and policy positions
- Legal advisers and drafters working on secondary rules and supervisory guidance
- Supervisors and economists who contribute evidence, impact analysis or consultation input

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the legal hierarchy of rule-making and the stages of the policy cycle
- Define a regulatory problem and evaluate options, including not intervening
- Apply cost-benefit analysis and proportionality tests to proposed rules
- Design and run a consultation and respond transparently to feedback
- Draft clear, consistent and enforceable rules, including those implementing international standards
- Design policy for innovation and evaluate rules through post-implementation review

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Policy Cycle and Rule-Making Powers

Where a supervisory authority's rule-making power comes from, what primary legislation, secondary rules and guidance can each achieve, and how the policy cycle fits together.

**Objectives**

- Explain the legal hierarchy of legislation, rules and guidance
- Identify the sources and limits of the authority's rule-making powers
- Describe the stages of the policy cycle from problem to review

**Syllabus**

- Primary legislation, secondary rules and guidance: what each can do
- Delegated powers, their limits and legal challenge
- Binding rules versus non-binding guidance and supervisory statements
- The policy cycle: define, analyse, consult, decide, implement, review

### Module 2: Problem Definition and Options Analysis

How to establish whether there is a genuine market failure or harm that justifies intervention, gather the evidence, and compare regulatory and non-regulatory options.

**Objectives**

- Define a regulatory problem in terms of harm, cause and scale
- Gather and assess evidence to support the case for intervention
- Compare options, including non-regulatory and do-nothing alternatives

**Syllabus**

- Market failures, consumer harm and systemic risk as rationales
- Evidence sources: supervisory data, complaints, research and surveys
- Option sets: rules, guidance, disclosure, supervision or no action
- Exercise: writing a problem statement and options paper

### Module 3: Cost-Benefit Analysis and Proportionality

Practical techniques for assessing the costs and benefits of proposed rules, testing proportionality across firms of different sizes, and anticipating unintended consequences before rules are finalised.

**Objectives**

- Apply cost-benefit techniques to a proposed rule
- Assess proportionality and distributional effects across firm sizes
- Identify unintended consequences and market reactions in advance

**Syllabus**

- Impact assessment frameworks and data sources
- Quantifying compliance costs and valuing hard-to-measure benefits
- Proportionality: is the intervention justified by the risk?
- Case study: a rule with significant unintended consequences

### Module 4: Consultation and Stakeholder Engagement

How to design and run a consultation that improves rule quality and legitimacy, hears from less-resourced stakeholders, and responds to feedback transparently.

**Objectives**

- Design a consultation document, questions and timeline
- Plan stakeholder engagement beyond the largest industry voices
- Analyse responses and draft a transparent feedback statement

**Syllabus**

- Statutory and voluntary consultation requirements
- Consultation questions: open, closed and avoiding leading questions
- Engagement channels: written responses, roundtables, targeted outreach
- Analysing responses and handling contentious or high-volume feedback

### Module 5: Drafting Clear and Enforceable Rules

Core legal drafting skills for rules and guidance that are precise, internally consistent and enforceable, written for firms, courts and the public alike.

**Objectives**

- Apply principles of precision, clarity and consistency to regulatory drafting
- Structure rules logically with definitions, obligations and exceptions
- Draft obligations that are testable and resistant to legal challenge

**Syllabus**

- Principles-based versus rules-based drafting
- Defined terms, cross-references and version control
- Common pitfalls: ambiguity, loopholes and inconsistent definitions
- Exercise: redrafting a provision for enforceability

### Module 6: From International Standard to Domestic Rule

How international standards from bodies such as the FSB, BCBS, IOSCO, IAIS and FATF become domestic rules, and how to use national discretion deliberately rather than by default.

**Objectives**

- Explain how international standards are implemented in domestic frameworks
- Assess the risks of over- and under-implementation
- Identify and use the discretion available within a standard

**Syllabus**

- Standard-setters, peer reviews and assessment programmes
- Minimum versus maximum harmonisation and regional frameworks
- Gold-plating, competitiveness and assessment failure risks
- Case study: implementing a sample international standard

### Module 7: Policy for Innovation: Sandboxes and Innovation Hubs

How authorities design policy for new business models and technologies, from innovation hubs and regulatory sandboxes to technology-neutral rules and perimeter guidance.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose and design of innovation hubs and regulatory sandboxes
- Assess the risks of innovation facilitation, including perimeter and consumer risks
- Apply technology-neutral principles when drafting rules for new activities

**Syllabus**

- Innovation hubs, sandboxes and testing environments compared
- Eligibility, safeguards, exit and lessons-learned reporting
- Technology neutrality and same activity, same risk, same regulation
- Perimeter guidance for new products and business models

### Module 8: Post-Implementation Review and Policy Workshop

Brings the programme together by examining how the authority evaluates whether rules have achieved their aims, and applying the full policy cycle to a live scenario.

**Objectives**

- Design a post-implementation review with measurable success criteria
- Evaluate evidence on whether a rule has met its objectives
- Produce a policy proposal applying the full policy cycle

**Syllabus**

- Setting success measures at the design stage
- Post-implementation review methods and data
- Amending, simplifying or withdrawing rules that do not work
- Workshop: from problem statement to draft rule and review plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a practical briefing with case studies and drafting or analysis exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of policy, legal or supervisory work is helpful. No formal legal or economics training is required.
