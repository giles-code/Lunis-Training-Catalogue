## Aim and Purpose

To give participants a reliable, comparable picture of fund regulation in key jurisdictions: the fund vehicles and regulator, how funds and managers are authorised, how funds are distributed and accessed cross-border, and recent developments. Participants leave able to compare jurisdictions, plan market entry and engage confidently with local regulators and advisers.

**Coverage:** Ireland and Luxembourg, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, France and Germany, the Cayman Islands and other offshore centres, Hong Kong, Mainland China, Singapore, Australia, Japan, and Taiwan and South Korea.

## Intended Audience

- Staff of fund managers, distributors and fund service providers planning or managing business in these jurisdictions
- Compliance, legal, product and business development professionals responsible for fund ranges and market access
- Staff of supervisory authorities and policy teams benchmarking their fund regime against other jurisdictions

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Describe the fund vehicles, regulators and legal frameworks in each jurisdiction
- Explain how funds and their managers are authorised, registered or licensed
- Compare the routes for distributing domestic and foreign funds, including cross-border access schemes
- Identify jurisdiction-specific rules that differ from international norms
- Assess recent and forthcoming regulatory developments in each market
- Apply the briefings to fund domicile, distribution and market entry decisions

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Ireland and Luxembourg: The Leading EU Fund Domiciles

How the two largest EU cross-border fund centres regulate funds and managers, the vehicles they offer and the local rules that sit on top of the EU framework.

**Objectives**

- Describe the fund vehicles and regulatory structures in Ireland and Luxembourg
- Explain local authorisation, governance and substance expectations for funds and management companies
- Compare the two domiciles for UCITS and alternative fund launches

**Syllabus**

- Regulators and vehicles: ICAV, investment company, unit trust, FCP, SICAV and limited partnerships
- Fund authorisation, fast-track and manager-supervised alternative funds, and management company rules
- Cross-border distribution from both domiciles using EU passports
- Recent developments: implementation of AIFMD amendments, loan-originating funds and long-term fund vehicles

### Module 2: United Kingdom

How the UK regulates funds and managers outside the EU, its domestic fund range, and how overseas funds access UK investors and UK funds access EU investors.

**Objectives**

- Describe the UK regulatory structure, legislation and fund vehicles
- Explain the FCA's authorisation and product rules for UK funds and managers
- Assess cross-border access between the UK and the EU and other markets

**Syllabus**

- The FCA, FSMA and the UK fund range: UK UCITS, non-UCITS retail and qualified investor schemes
- Vehicles: unit trusts, OEICs, authorised contractual schemes and long-term asset funds
- The Overseas Funds Regime, national private placement and access to the EU
- Recent developments: Consumer Duty, sustainability labels and reform of fund and manager rules

### Module 3: Switzerland

How Switzerland regulates domestic funds, fund management companies and portfolio managers, and the conditions for offering foreign funds to Swiss retail and qualified investors.

**Objectives**

- Describe FINMA's role and the Swiss collective investment and financial services legislation
- Explain authorisation requirements for funds, fund management companies and portfolio managers
- Apply the rules for offering foreign funds to different classes of Swiss investor

**Syllabus**

- FINMA, the Collective Investment Schemes Act and the financial services and institutions acts
- Vehicles: contractual funds, SICAVs, SICAFs and limited partnerships
- Foreign funds: approval for retail, Swiss representative and paying agent requirements
- Recent developments: the limited qualified investor fund and swing pricing practice

### Module 4: France and Germany

How the two largest continental EU domestic fund markets regulate funds and managers, the national vehicles they use and the rules for marketing foreign funds to local investors.

**Objectives**

- Describe the regulators, legislation and fund vehicles in France and Germany
- Explain national product, liquidity management and distribution rules
- Identify the requirements for marketing foreign funds in each market

**Syllabus**

- France: the AMF, the Monetary and Financial Code and French retail, professional and private equity vehicles
- Germany: BaFin, the Capital Investment Code, contractual funds and institutional special funds
- Distribution through banks, insurers and advisers, and marketing of foreign funds
- Recent developments: liquidity management tools and national fund modernisation reforms

### Module 5: Cayman Islands and Offshore Centres

How the leading offshore fund domicile regulates hedge, private equity and other alternative funds, and how offshore funds are distributed into onshore markets.

**Objectives**

- Describe the Cayman Islands fund vehicles and the role of the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority
- Explain the registration regimes for open-ended and closed-ended funds
- Assess governance, AML and substance expectations for offshore funds and their onshore distribution

**Syllabus**

- Vehicles: exempted companies, segregated portfolio companies, unit trusts and exempted limited partnerships
- Registration of mutual funds and private funds, audit, valuation and custody requirements
- Distribution into onshore markets through private placement regimes
- Recent developments: AML, beneficial ownership and economic substance, and other offshore centres

### Module 6: Hong Kong

How Hong Kong regulates funds and asset managers, the domestic vehicles it offers and its role as a gateway between Mainland China and international markets.

**Objectives**

- Describe the SFC's role, the Securities and Futures Ordinance and the fund codes
- Explain authorisation of retail funds and licensing of asset managers
- Assess the routes for distributing offshore funds and for Mainland cross-border access

**Syllabus**

- The SFC, licensing of asset managers and the fund manager code of conduct
- Vehicles: unit trusts, open-ended fund companies and limited partnership funds
- SFC authorisation of offshore funds and mutual recognition of funds
- Recent developments: Mainland connect schemes, tokenised funds and virtual asset products

### Module 7: Mainland China

How Mainland China regulates public and private funds and their managers, the progressive opening of its asset management sector to foreign firms, and the channels for cross-border investment.

**Objectives**

- Describe the roles of the CSRC, AMAC and other financial regulators
- Explain licensing of public fund managers and registration of private fund managers
- Identify the inbound, outbound and mutual recognition channels for cross-border investment

**Syllabus**

- The CSRC, the Securities Investment Fund Law and AMAC self-regulation
- Public fund management companies, private fund managers and the bank wealth management sector
- Foreign ownership, qualified investor schemes, connect schemes and mutual recognition
- Recent developments: asset management rules, private fund regulation and fee reform

### Module 8: Singapore

How Singapore regulates funds and fund managers, the domestic vehicles it offers and the routes for offering domestic and foreign funds to Singapore investors.

**Objectives**

- Describe MAS's role, the Securities and Futures Act and the Code on Collective Investment Schemes
- Explain licensing of fund management companies and authorisation of retail funds
- Compare the authorised, recognised and restricted scheme routes for fund offers

**Syllabus**

- MAS, the Securities and Futures Act and fund management licensing
- Vehicles: unit trusts, companies, limited partnerships and the variable capital company
- Authorised, recognised and restricted schemes and the ASEAN CIS Framework
- Recent developments: asset tokenisation initiatives and fund centre incentives

### Module 9: Australia

How Australia regulates managed funds and their responsible entities, the corporate collective investment vehicle, and the distribution rules shaped by superannuation and advice reforms.

**Objectives**

- Describe ASIC's role, the Corporations Act and Australian fund vehicles
- Explain licensing of managers and registration of managed investment schemes
- Apply design, distribution and disclosure obligations to fund offers

**Syllabus**

- ASIC, financial services licensing and the role of superannuation investors
- Vehicles: registered managed investment schemes and corporate collective investment vehicles
- Disclosure, design and distribution obligations and foreign manager access
- Recent developments: the Asia Region Funds Passport, conflicted remuneration and private markets scrutiny

### Module 10: Japan

How Japan regulates investment trusts and their managers, the role of its self-regulatory organisations and the policy push to grow household investment and attract new asset managers.

**Objectives**

- Describe the roles of the Japanese Financial Services Agency, SESC and self-regulatory organisations
- Explain registration of investment management businesses and of domestic and foreign funds
- Assess the distribution environment for domestic and foreign investment trusts

**Syllabus**

- The Financial Instruments and Exchange Act and the Act on Investment Trusts and Investment Corporations
- Vehicles: contractual investment trusts and investment corporations
- Notification and distribution of foreign funds and the Asia Region Funds Passport
- Recent developments: expanded tax-advantaged savings accounts and customer-oriented business conduct

### Module 11: Taiwan and South Korea

How two major North Asian distribution markets regulate domestic funds, their managers and the offering of offshore funds, including local agent and presence requirements.

**Objectives**

- Describe the regulators, legislation and fund vehicles in Taiwan and South Korea
- Explain the requirements for registering offshore funds and appointing local agents
- Compare public and private fund regimes and recent reforms in both markets

**Syllabus**

- Taiwan: the Financial Supervisory Commission, securities investment trust enterprises and domestic funds
- Taiwan: offshore fund approval, master agents and local presence incentives
- South Korea: the FSC and FSS, the Capital Markets Act, investment trusts and foreign fund registration
- Recent developments: Korean private fund reform and regional asset management hub ambitions

## Structure and Format

Eleven country briefings designed as recorded webinars of about 30 minutes to an hour each, supported by a live Q&A session with the trainer. Each briefing follows a common structure for easy comparison, and briefings can be taken individually, as a full series or as add-ons to the global and regional briefings.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with investment funds and international fund regulation is helpful, for example from the global and regional briefings. No legal background is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Examination of Governance for Collective Investment Schemes (2006–2007)
