## Aim and Purpose

To give participants a concise, structured understanding of the international standards and regional frameworks that govern how funds are set up, managed, distributed and invested across borders. Participants leave able to identify which rules apply to a fund's activities, anticipate regulatory change and see their fund business the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** International standard-setters and their standards for funds, cross-border regulatory approaches and regional fund passports, the global regulatory agenda, the EU legislative framework, UCITS, AIFMD, MiFID II and MiFIR for fund distribution, the EU regulatory agenda, the structure of fund regulation in Asia Pacific, and fund distribution and investing rules in Asia Pacific.

## Intended Audience

- Staff of fund managers, distributors, depositaries, administrators and other fund service providers working on cross-border business
- Compliance, legal, risk and product professionals who need a reliable map of international and regional fund rules
- Staff of supervisory authorities and policy teams responsible for funds, asset management or international affairs

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how international standards shape national and regional fund rules
- Distinguish national treatment, recognition, equivalence and passporting, and their effect on cross-border fund business
- Identify the main EU rules for funds, managers and distributors and how they fit together
- Apply the UCITS, AIFMD and MiFID II frameworks to typical fund structures and distribution models
- Describe how fund regulation, distribution and investing are regulated across Asia Pacific
- Assess the global and regional regulatory agenda and its implications for funds

## Syllabus

### Module 1: International Regulatory Standards for Funds

Why international standards for funds exist, who sets them and how they influence national and regional rules, including through extraterritorial effects and rules that become de facto global standards.

**Objectives**

- Explain the roles of the G20, FSB and IOSCO in fund regulation
- Describe how international standards are translated into national and regional rules
- Identify where foreign rules can reach a fund's activities abroad

**Syllabus**

- The standard-setting architecture: G20, FSB, IOSCO and their members
- IOSCO principles for collective investment schemes and fund managers
- FSB work on non-bank financial intermediation and fund vulnerabilities
- Extraterritoriality and de facto global standards

### Module 2: Cross-Border Fund Regulation: Passports and Recognition

How regulation applies when funds are distributed, managed or invested across borders, and the main approaches authorities use to grant or restrict access, from national treatment to regional fund passports.

**Objectives**

- Explain how cross-border distribution, portfolio management and investment are regulated
- Compare national treatment, unilateral and mutual recognition, equivalence and passporting
- Assess the practical effect of regional fund passports and recognition schemes

**Syllabus**

- Distribution: passports, private placement and local registration
- Delegation of portfolio management and sub-advisory arrangements
- Equivalence, substitute compliance and mutual recognition in practice
- Regional fund passports in Europe and Asia Pacific

### Module 3: The Global Regulatory Agenda for Funds

The issues currently at the top of the international agenda for funds and asset managers, and what they are likely to mean for fund rules, supervisory priorities and business models.

**Objectives**

- Identify the priority themes of the FSB and IOSCO for funds
- Explain current international work on liquidity, leverage and money market funds
- Anticipate how emerging themes may affect fund products and distribution

**Syllabus**

- Liquidity mismatch in open-ended funds and anti-dilution tools
- Leverage in non-bank financial intermediation and money market fund resilience
- Exchange-traded funds, private markets and private credit
- Crypto-assets, tokenisation, sustainability and retail investor behaviour

### Module 4: Introduction to EU Fund Regulation

How EU financial regulation is made and applied, the institutions and agencies involved, and which EU rules apply at each stage of the fund value chain.

**Objectives**

- Explain the difference between EU directives, regulations, technical standards and guidelines
- Describe the roles of the EU institutions, ESMA and national competent authorities
- Map the main EU rules onto fund management, investment and distribution

**Syllabus**

- EU and member state rules: how they interact
- The legislative process and the levels of EU rulemaking
- The fund value chain and where each rule applies
- The EU fund rulebook: UCITS, AIFMD, MiFID II, money market fund and product disclosure rules

### Module 5: The UCITS Framework

The origins, structure and global reach of the UCITS framework, its rules for funds, management companies and depositaries, and how the marketing and management company passports operate.

**Objectives**

- Explain the history of UCITS and its success as a global retail fund brand
- Describe the product, management, depositary and disclosure rules for UCITS
- Apply the marketing and management company passports to cross-border structures

**Syllabus**

- From the first UCITS directive to the latest amendments
- Eligible assets, diversification, leverage and risk management rules
- Management companies, depositaries and investor disclosure
- Passports, exchange-traded funds and money market funds

### Module 6: The AIFMD Framework

Why the AIFM framework was created, which managers and funds it covers, its core requirements and how alternative funds are marketed within and into the EU.

**Objectives**

- Explain the scope of AIFMD and the types of fund and manager it covers
- Describe the authorisation, organisational, delegation, leverage and liquidity rules
- Compare marketing under the passport and under national private placement regimes

**Syllabus**

- Scope, authorisation, capital and organisational requirements
- Delegation, risk management, leverage, liquidity and reporting
- Marketing passports, national private placement and non-EU managers
- Specialist EU fund labels and recent amendments, including loan origination

### Module 7: MiFID II and MiFIR for Fund Distribution

How MiFID II and MiFIR affect funds and their managers, particularly in portfolio management, trading, product governance and distribution to retail and professional investors.

**Objectives**

- Explain where MiFID II and MiFIR touch the fund value chain
- Apply product governance, suitability, appropriateness and inducements rules to fund distribution
- Identify trading, best execution, research and reporting obligations relevant to managers

**Syllabus**

- Investment services, fund managers and the interaction with fund rules
- Best execution, research payments and trade and transaction reporting
- Product governance and target market
- Inducements, costs and charges disclosure, suitability and appropriateness

### Module 8: The EU Regulatory Agenda for Funds

How the EU sets its financial services agenda and the current initiatives most relevant to funds, from capital markets integration and retail investment to sustainability, digital finance and supervisory convergence.

**Objectives**

- Explain how the EU regulatory agenda is set and who influences it
- Describe the current EU initiatives most relevant to funds and managers
- Assess the implications of these initiatives for fund products, costs and distribution

**Syllabus**

- Drivers of the agenda: international commitments, member states and the EU institutions
- Capital markets integration, retail investment reform and value for money
- Sustainable finance disclosures, digital operational resilience and tokenisation
- Supervisory convergence and the relationship with non-EU fund centres

### Module 9: Fund Regulation in Asia Pacific

The main features of Asia Pacific fund markets, how fund regulation is organised across the region's jurisdictions, and the themes driving regulatory change.

**Objectives**

- Describe the main fund centres, products and investors in Asia Pacific
- Compare the forms of regulation used across the region, from legislation to codes and self-regulatory bodies
- Identify the key regional themes, including market integration and the opening of Mainland China

**Syllabus**

- Market overview: domestic and offshore funds, investors and fund centres
- Regulators, legal forms of regulation and self-regulatory organisations
- New domestic fund vehicles and the competition between fund centres
- Regional themes: integration, Mainland China and intermediary remuneration

### Module 10: Fund Distribution and Investing Rules in Asia Pacific

How the distribution of domestic, offshore and cross-border funds is regulated across Asia Pacific, the regional recognition schemes, and the rules on investing fund assets in and out of the region.

**Objectives**

- Explain how domestic and offshore funds are authorised and distributed in the region
- Describe the regional fund passport, mutual recognition and market access schemes
- Apply local investing, capital control and delegation rules to cross-border fund structures

**Syllabus**

- Authorisation and local disclosure for offshore funds sold to retail investors
- Mutual recognition of funds, the Asia Region Funds Passport and the ASEAN CIS Framework
- Market access schemes, capital controls and offshore delegation
- Workshop: mapping regulatory access for a cross-border fund range

## Structure and Format

Ten modules designed as recorded webinars of about 30 minutes to an hour each, supported by a live Q&A session with the trainer. Modules can be taken as a full series or selected by region, and country briefings are available as add-ons; the content can also be delivered live in person or virtually.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with investment funds or asset management is helpful. No legal background or prior knowledge of EU or Asia Pacific regulation is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
- Directive 2009/65/EC on undertakings for collective investment in transferable securities (UCITS Directive)
- Directive 2011/61/EU on Alternative Investment Fund Managers (AIFMD)
