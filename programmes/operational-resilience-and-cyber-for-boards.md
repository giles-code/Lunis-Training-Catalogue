## Aim and Purpose

To give directors a practical, jargon-free understanding of operational resilience and cyber risk, and of the board's accountability for them. Participants leave able to set the board's expectations, challenge management and the control functions, take decisions in a crisis and evidence effective oversight to supervisors.

**Coverage:** Board accountability for technology and cyber risk, important business services and impact tolerances, the cyber threat landscape, third-party and concentration risk, incident response and crisis decision-making, testing and scenario exercises, reporting and metrics, and the board's own technical literacy.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, non-executive and independent directors and board committee members, especially on risk, audit and technology committees
- Executive directors and senior managers accountable for operations, technology, security and resilience
- Company secretaries and heads of governance supporting board oversight

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the board's accountability for operational resilience, technology and cyber risk
- Evaluate the identification of important business services and the setting of impact tolerances
- Assess cyber, third-party and concentration risk using structured board-level questions
- Challenge incident response, crisis management and testing arrangements
- Specify the reporting, metrics and assurance the board needs
- Identify gaps in the board's own technical literacy and agree an action plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Board Accountability for Technology and Cyber Risk

Why supervisors place ultimate responsibility for technology and cyber risk with the board, what that means in practice, and how oversight is divided across the board, its committees and management.

**Objectives**

- Explain the board's accountability for technology, cyber and resilience risk
- Distinguish the board's responsibilities from those of management
- Allocate oversight across committees and the three lines of defence

**Syllabus**

- Supervisory expectations of boards on technology and cyber risk
- Senior executive accountability for resilience and security
- Committee mandates: board, risk, audit and technology
- Common governance failures behind major outages and breaches

### Module 2: Operational Resilience: Concepts and Frameworks

The shift from preventing disruption to assuming it will happen, and the building blocks of an operational resilience framework the board should expect to see.

**Objectives**

- Explain operational resilience and how it differs from business continuity
- Evaluate the firm's resilience framework against international standards
- Identify how resilience links to strategy, risk appetite and change

**Syllabus**

- From business continuity to operational resilience
- International principles, including BCBS and ISO 22301
- Resilience by design: change, legacy technology and investment decisions
- Resilience risk appetite and the board's role in setting it

### Module 3: Important Business Services and Impact Tolerances

How firms identify the services whose disruption would cause intolerable harm, map the resources behind them and set impact tolerances that the board must approve.

**Objectives**

- Assess how important business services have been identified
- Challenge impact tolerances and the harm they are designed to prevent
- Evaluate mapping of people, processes, technology, facilities and third parties

**Syllabus**

- Identifying important business services from the customer's perspective
- Setting impact tolerances: maximum tolerable disruption and its rationale
- Mapping dependencies and single points of failure
- Self-assessment and the board's approval

### Module 4: The Cyber Threat Landscape

Plain-language grounding in who attacks financial firms, how attacks unfold and why cyber risk is a business risk rather than solely a technical one.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main cyber threat actors and their motives
- Recognise common attack paths, including ransomware and social engineering
- Assess the firm's exposure and the maturity of its cyber controls

**Syllabus**

- Threat actors: criminals, state-sponsored groups, hacktivists and insiders
- Attack paths: phishing, ransomware, supply chain and data theft
- Frameworks for cyber maturity, including the NIST Cybersecurity Framework
- The vocabulary directors need: vulnerabilities, patching, privileged access, backups

### Module 5: Third-Party and Concentration Risk

How reliance on outsourcing, cloud and technology providers moves risk outside the firm but not responsibility, and how concentration on a few providers creates systemic exposure.

**Objectives**

- Assess the oversight of critical outsourcing and technology providers
- Evaluate concentration risk and dependency on a small number of providers
- Challenge exit plans, contractual rights and fourth-party risk

**Syllabus**

- Outsourcing and third-party risk: accountability stays with the firm
- Due diligence, contracts, audit rights and ongoing monitoring
- Cloud, concentration and fourth-party dependencies
- Exit planning and stressed exit scenarios

### Module 6: Incident Response and Crisis Decision-Making

How the firm detects, escalates and responds to severe incidents, and how the board takes decisions under pressure, with incomplete information and intense external scrutiny.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate incident response, escalation and crisis management arrangements
- Explain the board's role during a crisis and how it differs from management's
- Assess decision-making under pressure, including ransom and disclosure decisions

**Syllabus**

- Incident detection, classification and escalation to the board
- Crisis governance: who decides what, and when the board convenes
- Hard decisions: ransom payments, service shutdowns and customer redress
- Communicating with customers, the authority, markets and media

### Module 7: Testing and Scenario Exercises

How firms test whether they can remain within impact tolerances, from severe but plausible scenarios to threat-led penetration testing, and what the board should learn from the results.

**Objectives**

- Assess the design and severity of resilience scenario testing
- Explain threat-led penetration testing and red teaming
- Evaluate how test findings are remediated and reported

**Syllabus**

- Severe but plausible scenarios and testing against impact tolerances
- Penetration testing, red teaming and threat intelligence
- Board participation in crisis simulations and tabletop exercises
- Tracking remediation and lessons learned

### Module 8: Reporting, Metrics and Assurance

The information boards need to oversee resilience and cyber risk, how to read dashboards critically and how to obtain credible independent assurance rather than relying on assertion.

**Objectives**

- Specify the management information and indicators the board needs
- Interpret resilience and cyber reports and identify what is missing
- Obtain independent assurance from internal audit and external reviewers

**Syllabus**

- The core board information set: services, tolerances, incidents and remediation
- Key risk indicators: patching, privileged access, backups and third parties
- Recognising comfort without evidence and green dashboards
- Internal audit, external testing and certifications as assurance

### Module 9: The Board's Own Technical Literacy

How directors without technology backgrounds can gain enough knowledge to test management's statements, and how the board uses experts without surrendering its judgement.

**Objectives**

- Assess the board's collective technology and cyber knowledge
- Challenge technical presentations that inform without enabling scrutiny
- Identify when and how to use external advisers

**Syllabus**

- How much technical knowledge is enough for a director
- Skills matrices, induction and continuing training on technology risk
- Getting the most from the chief information security officer
- External advisers and technology specialists on the board

### Module 10: Workshop: A Severe Cyber Incident

Brings the programme together in a board simulation of an unfolding cyber incident affecting an important business service, followed by a board action plan.

**Objectives**

- Apply the programme's frameworks to a live crisis scenario
- Evaluate the board's decisions, information needs and communications under pressure
- Produce a board action plan for resilience and cyber oversight

**Syllabus**

- Simulation: a ransomware attack on a critical service provider
- Decision points: escalation, tolerance breach, ransom and disclosure
- Debrief: what the board needed to know and when
- Workshop: the board's twelve-month resilience oversight plan

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two- to three-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with scenarios and a board question set, culminating in a crisis simulation, and modules can be selected or tailored to the board's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board or senior management experience in financial services is helpful. No technical background in technology or cyber security is required.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee, Principles for Operational Resilience (2021)
- FSB, Effective Practices for Cyber Incident Response and Recovery (2020)
- FSB, Enhancing Third-Party Risk Management and Oversight: A Toolkit for Financial Institutions and Financial Authorities (2023)
- NIST, Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (2024)
