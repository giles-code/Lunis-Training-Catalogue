## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' skills in communicating effectively in cross-border supervisory contexts, including supervisory colleges and international cooperation frameworks, recognising cultural differences in communication style and expectations.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level supervisory staff developing core professional judgement skills.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how cultural differences affect cross-border supervisory communication
- Identify communication style differences relevant to supervisory colleges
- Apply techniques for clear communication across language and cultural barriers
- Assess the risk of miscommunication in international cooperation
- Practise cross-cultural communication in a simulated scenario

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Culture and communication in supervision (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain the effect of cultural differences on communication*
- How cultural norms shape communication style: directness, hierarchy, formality
- Why cross-border supervisory communication carries particular risk
- Common misunderstandings in international supervisory settings
- Building cultural awareness without stereotyping
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: cross-cultural communication experiences from participants' own international work

**Session 2: Communication styles in practice (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Identify style differences and apply clear communication techniques*
- Communication style differences relevant to supervisory colleges
- Techniques for clarity across language barriers
- Using structured communication to reduce ambiguity
- Written versus spoken communication considerations
Activities:
- Group exercise: identify communication risks in a sample multinational college discussion

**Session 3: Managing miscommunication risk and practice (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Assess miscommunication risk and practise communication*
- Assessing where miscommunication risk is highest in international cooperation
- Checking understanding without causing offence
- Case study: a cross-border miscommunication and its consequences
Activities:
- Role-play: a cross-cultural supervisory college discussion

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: cultural effects, style differences, managing risk
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Culture and communication in supervision (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Communication styles in practice (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Managing miscommunication risk and practice (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Cultural differences in communication style are a real source of risk in cross-border supervisory work, not a minor courtesy issue
- Checking understanding explicitly is more effective than assuming shared meaning across cultures
- Awareness of cultural difference should inform communication technique without lapsing into stereotyping
