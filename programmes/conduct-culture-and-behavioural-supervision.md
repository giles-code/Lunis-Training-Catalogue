## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical grounding in how consumer behaviour, firm culture and incentives drive conduct outcomes, and how to supervise them. Participants leave able to assess culture and the governance of conduct, challenge product governance and sales practices, use behavioural and data tools, and choose effective supervisory interventions.

## What It Covers

Behavioural economics in consumer markets, assessing culture and the governance of conduct, incentives and remuneration, product governance and fair value, sales practices and distribution, data and behavioural supervision tools, misconduct and individual accountability, and supervisory interventions.

## Intended Audience

- Conduct and consumer protection supervisors, including those supervising retail-facing firms
- Prudential and governance supervisors seeking to assess culture and its link to risk
- Policy, data and enforcement staff working on conduct risk and customer outcomes

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how behavioural biases in consumers and firms shape conduct outcomes
- Assess a firm's culture and the governance and accountability arrangements for conduct
- Evaluate incentives, product governance and sales practices for conduct risk
- Apply data and behavioural tools to identify poor outcomes and emerging harm
- Identify indicators of misconduct and test individual accountability
- Select and design proportionate supervisory interventions that change behaviour

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, file and journey reviews and a supervisory question set, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's conduct priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of conduct or consumer protection supervision is helpful. No background in economics or behavioural science is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Strengthening Governance Frameworks to Mitigate Misconduct Risk: A Toolkit for Firms and Supervisors (2018)
- FSB, Guidance on Supervisory Interaction with Financial Institutions on Risk Culture (2014)
- Group of Thirty, Banking Conduct and Culture: A Call for Sustained and Comprehensive Reform (2015)
- G20/OECD High-Level Principles on Financial Consumer Protection (2022)
