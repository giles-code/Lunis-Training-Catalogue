## Aim and Purpose

To give new and developing staff a structured induction into the purpose, powers and working methods of a financial regulatory authority, from authorisation through supervision to enforcement. Participants leave able to place their own role in the wider regulatory lifecycle and to approach a first supervisory case with confidence and sound professional standards.

## What It Covers

Why financial services are regulated, the authority's objectives and powers, the regulatory toolkit, risk-based supervision, the lifecycle from authorisation to enforcement, international standards and peer assessment, ethics and independence, and a first supervisory case.

## Intended Audience

- Graduates, new joiners and staff in their first two years at a financial regulatory authority
- Staff moving into supervisory, authorisation, policy or enforcement roles from support or other functions
- Secondees and staff from related public bodies needing a grounding in financial supervision

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the market failures and public interest objectives that justify financial regulation
- Describe the authority's mandate, powers and accountability, and how its functions fit together
- Apply the principles of risk-based supervision to prioritise firms and issues
- Identify the main international standards and how they shape the authority's work
- Recognise the ethical standards, independence and conduct expected of a supervisor
- Work through a first supervisory case from information gathering to proposed response

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day induction programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines short briefings with exercises and a running case study, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's structure and mandate.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No prior regulatory experience is required. A general interest in financial services and the economy is helpful.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2024), Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision
- IAIS (2019, as updated), Insurance Core Principles and Common Framework for the Supervision of Internationally Active Insurance Groups
- FATF (2012, as updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- IMF (2010), The Making of Good Supervision: Learning to Say "No" (Staff Position Note SPN/10/08)
