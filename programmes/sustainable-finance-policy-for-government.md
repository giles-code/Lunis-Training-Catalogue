## Aim and Purpose

To give policymakers a practical understanding of the sustainable finance toolkit, the international standards that underpin it and the trade-offs in applying it nationally. Participants leave able to design a sustainable finance roadmap, decide on taxonomy and disclosure policy, support a credible green bond market, set expectations of regulators on climate risk and guard against greenwashing.

**Coverage:** The policy case for sustainable finance, climate risk and the financial system, sustainable finance taxonomies, ISSB disclosure standards and their adoption, green, social and sustainability-linked bonds and sovereign issuance, transition finance and transition plans, greenwashing and investor protection, blended finance and mobilising private capital, and national sustainable finance roadmaps.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance, environment and climate ministries developing sustainable finance and green growth policy
- Debt management office staff and public agencies involved in green bond issuance and climate finance
- Legislative drafters, legal advisers and parliamentary committee staff working on sustainability disclosure or climate legislation

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how climate and nature risks affect the financial system and public finances
- Evaluate the design choices for a national or regional sustainable finance taxonomy
- Assess options for adopting ISSB standards and wider sustainability disclosure
- Describe how labelled bond markets and sovereign green issuance can be developed
- Weigh policy tools for mobilising private capital and supporting credible transition
- Design a national sustainable finance roadmap with clear roles for government and regulators

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Policy Case for Sustainable Finance

Why governments pursue sustainable finance, the scale of the investment gap, and how international commitments and the G20 sustainable finance agenda shape national policy.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of finance in meeting climate and development commitments
- Describe the G20 sustainable finance roadmap and the international architecture
- Distinguish the roles of government, central banks, regulators and markets

**Syllabus**

- Climate and development investment needs and the financing gap
- International commitments and nationally determined contributions
- G20, FSB, NGFS, ISSB and IOSCO roles in sustainable finance
- The sustainable finance policy toolkit at a glance

### Module 2: Climate Risk and the Financial System

How physical and transition risks reach banks, insurers, investors and the public balance sheet, and what government should expect of central banks and regulators.

**Objectives**

- Explain physical, transition and liability risk transmission channels
- Assess the use of climate scenario analysis and stress testing
- Identify fiscal risks from climate events and insurance protection gaps

**Syllabus**

- Transmission channels to credit, market, insurance and sovereign risk
- NGFS scenarios and supervisory climate stress tests
- Insurance protection gaps and disaster risk finance
- Questions government should ask regulators about climate risk

### Module 3: Sustainable Finance Taxonomies

What taxonomies are for, the main design choices from green-only to traffic-light and transition taxonomies, and how to achieve interoperability with major international frameworks.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose and uses of a sustainable finance taxonomy
- Compare design choices on scope, thresholds and transition activities
- Assess interoperability and the cost of divergence

**Syllabus**

- Taxonomy objectives: classification, disclosure and capital allocation
- Technical screening criteria and do-no-significant-harm tests
- Transition, amber and social taxonomies
- Interoperability and international taxonomy comparisons

### Module 4: Sustainability Disclosure and the ISSB Standards

The global baseline for sustainability-related disclosure, the choices government and regulators face in adopting it, and the assurance and capacity issues that follow.

**Objectives**

- Explain the requirements of IFRS S1 and IFRS S2
- Evaluate adoption options, phasing and proportionality for smaller entities
- Assess assurance, data and capacity requirements

**Syllabus**

- From TCFD to the ISSB global baseline
- Full adoption, partial adoption and phased approaches
- Scope 3 emissions, scenario analysis and proportionality
- Assurance standards, data infrastructure and market capacity

### Module 5: Green Bonds and Sovereign Sustainable Issuance

How green, social, sustainability and sustainability-linked bond markets work, how governments issue sovereign labelled bonds, and the policies that support credible domestic markets.

**Objectives**

- Describe the main labelled bond types and market standards
- Explain the design and reporting of sovereign green bond frameworks
- Evaluate policy support for domestic labelled bond markets

**Syllabus**

- Use-of-proceeds and sustainability-linked structures
- Sovereign frameworks: eligible expenditure, allocation and impact reporting
- External review, verification and labelling standards
- Incentives, listings and building the domestic investor base

### Module 6: Transition Finance and Transition Plans

How to finance the transition of high-emitting sectors credibly, the role of transition plans for firms and financial institutions, and the policy choices on whether to require them.

**Objectives**

- Explain transition finance and the risks of carbon lock-in
- Assess the elements of a credible transition plan
- Evaluate the case for mandatory or voluntary transition plans

**Syllabus**

- Transition pathways for energy, industry and agriculture
- Transition plan frameworks for corporates and financial institutions
- Just transition and social considerations
- Mandatory, comply-or-explain and voluntary approaches

### Module 7: Greenwashing, Investor Protection and Market Integrity

How greenwashing arises in products, labels and corporate claims, and the legislative, regulatory and enforcement tools available to protect investors and market credibility.

**Objectives**

- Identify common forms of greenwashing in financial products and claims
- Evaluate fund labelling, naming and marketing rules
- Assess the role of ESG ratings and data providers

**Syllabus**

- Greenwashing typologies and enforcement case studies
- Sustainable fund labels, naming and marketing standards
- ESG ratings and data providers: IOSCO recommendations
- Coordinating consumer, securities and competition authorities

### Module 8: Mobilising Private Capital and National Roadmaps

Brings the programme together by examining blended finance, public development banks and other tools to crowd in private capital, and how to build a national sustainable finance roadmap.

**Objectives**

- Evaluate blended finance, guarantees and green investment banks
- Prioritise policy measures for the national context and sequence them
- Produce a national sustainable finance roadmap

**Syllabus**

- Blended finance, guarantees and first-loss structures
- Public development banks, green investment banks and country platforms
- Governance of a roadmap: ministries, regulators and industry
- Workshop: a national sustainable finance roadmap

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over two to three days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with country case studies and policy exercises, ending in a roadmap workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the government's priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with financial services, climate or economic policy is helpful. No technical background in sustainability reporting or climate science is required.

## Core Reading

- ISSB (2023), IFRS S1 General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information and IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures
- G20 Sustainable Finance Working Group (2021), G20 Sustainable Finance Roadmap
- FSB (2021), FSB Roadmap for Addressing Climate-Related Financial Risks
- NGFS (2019), A Call for Action: Climate Change as a Source of Financial Risk
- IOSCO (2021), Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) Ratings and Data Products Providers: Final Report
