## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of how supervisory authorities engage in cross-border cooperation and international standard-setting, and how to participate effectively in supervisory colleges and MoU frameworks.

## Intended Audience

Mid-to-senior supervisory staff involved in setting or reviewing their authority's strategy, governance, or resourcing decisions.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the main channels of international supervisory cooperation
- Describe the role of standard-setting bodies such as IOSCO, BCBS and the FSB
- Assess the purpose and structure of memoranda of understanding
- Evaluate the benefits and limitations of international cooperation frameworks
- Identify practical steps to strengthen an authority's international engagement

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Channels of international cooperation (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain cooperation channels and standard-setting bodies*
- Bilateral MoUs, supervisory colleges, and multilateral fora
- The role of IOSCO, BCBS and the FSB in setting international standards
- How international standards translate into domestic practice
- Benefits of international engagement for a smaller authority
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: participants' own authority's international engagements

**Session 2: MoUs and cooperation frameworks (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess MoU purpose and structure, and evaluate frameworks*
- What a good MoU contains
- Multilateral versus bilateral MoU frameworks
- Limitations of cooperation frameworks in practice
- Case examples of effective and ineffective cooperation
Activities:
- Group exercise: review a sample MoU and identify strengths and gaps

**Session 3: Strengthening international engagement (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Identify practical steps to strengthen engagement*
- Building relationships with international standard-setters
- Resourcing international engagement within a small authority
- Case study: an authority strengthening its international profile
Activities:
- Case study: propose a plan to strengthen an authority's international cooperation

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: channels, standard-setters, MoUs, strengthening engagement
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Channels of international cooperation (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: MoUs and cooperation frameworks (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Strengthening international engagement (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- International cooperation channels range from informal bilateral contact to formal multilateral standard-setting
- A good MoU is only as useful as the working relationship behind it
- Even smaller authorities benefit significantly from active international engagement
