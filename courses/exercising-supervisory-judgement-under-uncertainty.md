## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' skills in exercising supervisory judgement when information is incomplete or ambiguous, recognising common cognitive biases and applying frameworks that improve consistency and defensibility of decisions.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level supervisory staff developing core professional judgement skills.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why supervisory judgement is often exercised under genuine uncertainty
- Identify common cognitive biases that affect supervisory decisions
- Apply structured judgement frameworks to reduce inconsistency
- Evaluate the defensibility of a supervisory judgement
- Formulate a well-reasoned judgement for an ambiguous scenario

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Judgement and uncertainty (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain the nature of supervisory judgement under uncertainty*
- Why supervision inherently involves judgement calls, not just rule application
- Sources of uncertainty: incomplete information, evolving risk, ambiguous rules
- The cost of both over- and under-cautious judgement
- Judgement versus discretion — what accountability requires
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: judgement calls participants have found genuinely difficult

**Session 2: Cognitive biases in supervisory decisions (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Identify cognitive biases*
- Common biases: confirmation bias, anchoring, groupthink, hindsight bias
- How biases specifically distort supervisory risk assessment
- Techniques for recognising bias in your own reasoning
- Structural safeguards against biased decision-making
Activities:
- Exercise: identify biases at play in a set of sample supervisory decisions

**Session 3: Structured judgement frameworks (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Apply frameworks and evaluate defensibility*
- Structured frameworks for exercising judgement consistently
- Documenting the reasoning behind a judgement for defensibility
- Case study: an ambiguous scenario requiring a reasoned judgement
Activities:
- Case study: formulate and document a judgement for an ambiguous scenario

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: uncertainty, bias, structured frameworks
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Judgement and uncertainty (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Cognitive biases in supervisory decisions (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Structured judgement frameworks (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Supervisory judgement under uncertainty is unavoidable — the goal is consistency and defensibility, not certainty
- Cognitive biases affect experienced supervisors just as much as novices, and awareness is the first defence
- A well-documented reasoning process is what makes a judgement defensible under later scrutiny
