## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' awareness of how criminals are using generative AI to create new financial crime typologies — including synthetic identity fraud, deepfake-enabled onboarding fraud, and AI-generated social engineering — and how supervisors and firms can build resilience against these emerging threats.

## Intended Audience

Mid-level supervisory and financial crime specialist staff assessing firms' financial crime controls.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how generative AI is being used to create new financial crime typologies
- Describe synthetic identity fraud and deepfake-enabled onboarding fraud
- Assess the risk of AI-generated phishing and social engineering
- Evaluate a firm's resilience against generative-AI-enabled fraud
- Identify emerging supervisory priorities in this fast-moving risk area

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Generative AI and new fraud typologies (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain new typologies enabled by generative AI*
- How generative AI lowers the barrier to sophisticated fraud
- Synthetic identity fraud: constructing convincing false identities
- Deepfake-enabled onboarding and verification fraud
- The pace of typology evolution versus the pace of regulation
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: generative AI fraud cases participants are aware of

**Session 2: Social engineering and phishing risk (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess AI-generated social engineering risk*
- AI-generated phishing: scale, personalisation, and sophistication
- Voice cloning and deepfake video in social engineering
- Why traditional staff training may be insufficient against AI-enabled attacks
- Detecting AI-generated content and its limits
Activities:
- Group exercise: assess a firm's exposure to AI-enabled social engineering

**Session 3: Building resilience and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Evaluate resilience and identify supervisory priorities*
- Firm-level resilience measures against generative-AI-enabled fraud
- Liveness detection and multi-factor verification as counter-measures
- Case study: a firm targeted by a deepfake-enabled fraud attempt
Activities:
- Case study: evaluate a firm's resilience and recommend improvements

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: new typologies, social engineering risk, resilience
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Generative AI and new fraud typologies (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Social engineering and phishing risk (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Building resilience and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Generative AI has meaningfully lowered the skill barrier for sophisticated financial crime
- Deepfake and synthetic identity risks challenge onboarding and verification controls that were designed for an earlier threat model
- This is a fast-moving risk area requiring continuous horizon-scanning rather than a one-off assessment
