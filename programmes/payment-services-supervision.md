## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical understanding of how payment and e-money institutions make money, move funds and fail, and the risk-based tools needed to supervise a large and diverse population of firms. Participants leave able to test safeguarding and operational resilience with evidence, challenge firms on fraud, agents and third-party access, and act early when a firm heads towards failure.

**Coverage:** The payments landscape and regulatory framework, authorisation of payment and e-money institutions, risk-based supervision, safeguarding, operational and security risk, fraud and authorised push payment scams, open banking, agents, distributors and financial crime, instant payments and stablecoins, and wind-down and failure.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors, inspectors and authorisation staff responsible for payment institutions, e-money institutions and their agents
- Payment system oversight, consumer protection and financial crime specialists
- Policy staff, team leaders and heads of department responsible for payments and fintech

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how payment and e-money services work and how they are regulated
- Assess applications and ongoing supervision of payment institutions using a risk-based approach
- Evaluate safeguarding of client funds using reconciliation and audit evidence
- Challenge firms on operational, security, fraud and financial crime risk
- Apply supervisory expectations to open banking, agents, instant payments and stablecoins
- Design an early intervention and wind-down response for a failing payment firm

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Payments Landscape and Regulatory Framework

How retail payments flow from payer to payee, who the participants are, and how payment services, e-money issuance and payment systems are regulated and overseen.

**Objectives**

- Explain the payment chain and the roles of banks, payment firms and schemes
- Distinguish payment services, e-money issuance and payment system operation
- Relate CPMI, FATF and PFMI standards to the national framework

**Syllabus**

- Card, account-to-account, wallet and remittance payment models
- Regulated payment services and e-money: what falls inside the perimeter
- Payment institution, e-money institution and small registered firm categories
- International standards: CPMI, PFMI and FATF payment transparency rules

### Module 2: Authorising Payment and E-Money Institutions

How the authority assesses applications from payment and e-money firms, matching requirements to the real business model and spotting weaknesses before they become supervisory problems.

**Objectives**

- Assess a licence application against category-specific requirements
- Evaluate business plans, capital, governance and safeguarding proposals
- Identify common application weaknesses and gateway red flags

**Syllabus**

- Business model, programme of operations and financial projections
- Initial capital, own funds methods and fit and proper assessment
- Safeguarding, security, AML and outsourcing plans at the gateway
- Group exercise: assessing a sample payment institution application

### Module 3: Risk-Based Supervision of Payment Institutions

How to apply proportionate, risk-based supervision to a large and diverse payments population, using regulatory returns, data and risk indicators to focus supervisory effort.

**Objectives**

- Explain why payment institutions pose different risks from banks
- Apply risk indicators and data to segment and prioritise firms
- Plan proportionate supervisory engagement for different firm types

**Syllabus**

- Key risk categories: safeguarding, operational, financial crime and conduct
- Regulatory returns, complaints and transaction data as risk indicators
- Proportionality across small, high-growth and systemically important firms
- Business model analysis and the economics of payment firms

### Module 4: Safeguarding Client Funds

How safeguarding is meant to protect users' funds if a firm fails, the common ways it breaks down, and how supervisors verify it through reconciliations and audit evidence rather than policy statements.

**Objectives**

- Explain segregation, insurance and guarantee methods of safeguarding
- Assess reconciliation practice, safeguarding accounts and audit evidence
- Identify commingling, shortfalls and other safeguarding red flags

**Syllabus**

- When safeguarding starts and ends, and which funds are relevant
- Safeguarding accounts, acknowledgement letters and investment of funds
- Daily reconciliation, shortfalls and safeguarding audits
- Case study: safeguarding failures revealed on insolvency

### Module 5: Operational, Security and ICT Risk

How payment firms manage the operational and security risks at the heart of their business, from strong customer authentication to incident reporting, cloud dependency and third-party failure.

**Objectives**

- Assess operational and security risk management frameworks
- Evaluate strong customer authentication and incident reporting
- Challenge firms on ICT, cloud and third-party resilience

**Syllabus**

- Security policies, risk assessments and major incident reporting
- Strong customer authentication and its exemptions
- Cloud, outsourcing and dependence on banking and scheme partners
- Operational resilience: important services, tolerances and testing

### Module 6: Payment Fraud and Authorised Push Payment Scams

How payment fraud, including authorised push payment scams, is committed and prevented, how losses are allocated between firms and victims, and how supervisors use fraud data.

**Objectives**

- Explain the main payment fraud typologies, including authorised push payment scams
- Assess fraud prevention controls, payee verification and transaction monitoring
- Evaluate reimbursement, liability allocation and fraud reporting data

**Syllabus**

- Unauthorised fraud versus authorised push payment scams
- Payee verification, risk-based friction and transaction monitoring
- Liability allocation and reimbursement models for scam victims
- Fraud data, cross-sector information sharing and supervisory follow-up

### Module 7: Open Banking and Third-Party Provider Access

How account information and payment initiation services work, the access rights and interfaces that support them, and the supervisory issues raised by open banking and the move towards open finance.

**Objectives**

- Explain account information and payment initiation services
- Assess interface quality, access obstacles and consent management
- Evaluate liability, data protection and conduct risks in open banking

**Syllabus**

- Access rights, dedicated interfaces and fallback mechanisms
- Consent, authentication and data sharing
- Liability between account providers and third-party providers
- From open banking to open finance: emerging supervisory questions

### Module 8: Agents, Distributors and Financial Crime

How payment firms extend their reach through agents, e-money distributors and cross-border provision, and why these networks concentrate money laundering, fraud and oversight risk.

**Objectives**

- Explain agent and distributor models and the principal's responsibilities
- Assess oversight of agent networks and cross-border activity
- Identify money laundering and sanctions red flags in payment flows

**Syllabus**

- Registration, due diligence and monitoring of agents and distributors
- Cross-border provision and host authority cooperation
- Money laundering typologies in remittances, wallets and prepaid products
- Correspondent and bank partner de-risking and its consequences

### Module 9: Instant Payments, Payment Systems and Stablecoins

How instant payment systems settle in near real time, the oversight of payment system operators and access to systems, and the supervisory questions raised by stablecoins used for payments.

**Objectives**

- Explain instant payment operation, settlement risk and liquidity demands
- Assess payment system oversight and non-bank access to systems
- Evaluate the risks of stablecoins and other new entrants in payments

**Syllabus**

- Instant payments: settlement, liquidity and fraud implications
- Payment system operator oversight and the PFMI
- Direct and indirect access to payment systems for non-banks
- Stablecoins in payments: reserves, redemption and run risk

### Module 10: Early Intervention, Wind-Down and Failure

Brings the programme together by examining how payment firms fail, what credible wind-down planning looks like, and how the authority intervenes early to protect users and their funds.

**Objectives**

- Identify early warning signs of financial or operational distress
- Assess the credibility of a firm's wind-down plan
- Design a supervisory response to a failing payment firm

**Syllabus**

- Early warning indicators: losses, funding, partner withdrawal and complaints
- Wind-down plans: triggers, resources, communications and return of funds
- Insolvency, distribution of safeguarded funds and user communication
- Workshop: responding to a payment firm in distress

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, group exercises and supervisory question sets, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's payments sector.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of supervision or authorisation is helpful. No specialist payments or technology background is required.

## Core Reading

- FSB, Enhancing Cross-border Payments: Stage 3 Roadmap (2020)
- CPMI-IOSCO, Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures (2012)
- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
