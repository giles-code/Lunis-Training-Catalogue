## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a structured, practical approach to investigative interviewing in on-site inspections, reviews and governance assessments. Participants leave able to plan an interview, question with purpose, recognise evasion, record reliable evidence and draw defensible conclusions, drawing on a course already delivered to a supervisory authority.

**Coverage:** The purpose and mindset of supervisory interviews, a PEACE-style interview structure, planning and preparation, open, probing and funnel questioning, active listening and reading evasion, document-based questioning and recording evidence, interviewing boards and control functions, difficult interviewees, and moving from interview to finding.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and inspectors who conduct on-site visits, thematic reviews and governance assessments
- Staff assessing fitness and propriety of directors and senior managers, including authorisation teams
- Team leaders who plan inspections and review interview evidence before findings are issued

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the purpose of supervisory interviews and the mindset they require
- Plan an interview with clear objectives, lines of enquiry and supporting documents
- Apply open, probing and funnel questioning within a structured interview model
- Evaluate the credibility, consistency and reliability of responses, including evasion
- Manage difficult, evasive or defensive interviewees while keeping control of the interview
- Record interview evidence accurately and link it to supervisory findings and action

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Supervisory Interview: Purpose and Mindset

Why supervisors interview, how supervisory interviews differ from enforcement interviews and recruitment interviews, and how mindset shapes what the interviewer hears.

**Objectives**

- Explain the role of interviews in risk assessment, inspections and governance reviews
- Distinguish supervisory from enforcement interviews and their evidential standards
- Recognise how assumptions and mindset affect an interview

**Syllabus**

- Interviews in the supervisory cycle: inspections, reviews and fitness and propriety
- Supervisory versus enforcement interviews: purpose, powers and safeguards
- Introducing a PEACE-style structure for supervisory interviews
- The right supervisory perspective: curious, sceptical and fair

### Module 2: Planning and Preparing the Interview

How to set objectives, research the interviewee and the firm, select documents, build lines of enquiry and agree roles within the interview team.

**Objectives**

- Define interview objectives linked to the supervisory risk assessment
- Prepare an interview plan with lines of enquiry and supporting evidence
- Organise the interview team, logistics and introductions

**Syllabus**

- Setting objectives and identifying the right interviewees
- Desk research: board papers, management information and prior findings
- Building the interview plan and question map
- Roles: lead questioner, note-taker and observer

### Module 3: Questioning Techniques: Open, Probing and Funnel

The question types that elicit full and accurate accounts, how to sequence them using the funnel technique, and the questioning habits that undermine an interview.

**Objectives**

- Apply open, probing and closed questions for the right purpose
- Use the funnel technique to move from broad accounts to specific facts
- Avoid leading, multiple and assumptive questions

**Syllabus**

- Open questions to obtain a free account
- Probing and clarifying questions to test detail and gaps
- Funnel sequencing: from general to specific to confirmation
- Exercise: rewriting weak questions into effective ones

### Module 4: Active Listening, Credibility and Reading Evasion

How to listen for what is said and not said, assess the credibility and consistency of answers, and recognise and respond to evasion, deflection and rehearsed responses.

**Objectives**

- Apply active listening and summarising techniques during an interview
- Evaluate the consistency and plausibility of responses against other evidence
- Recognise verbal signs of evasion and follow them up

**Syllabus**

- Active listening: silence, summarising and reflecting back
- Testing accounts for consistency, plausibility and corroboration
- Evasion patterns: deflection, generalisation, scripted and partial answers
- Limits of non-verbal cues and avoiding false conclusions

### Module 5: Document-Based Questioning and Recording Evidence

How to use board papers, policies, minutes and data to question interviewees effectively, and how to record interview evidence so it is accurate, complete and usable later.

**Objectives**

- Use documents to test accounts and confront inconsistencies
- Record interview evidence accurately and contemporaneously
- Assess when an interview record must meet a higher evidential standard

**Syllabus**

- Selecting and sequencing documents for disclosure in the interview
- Putting inconsistencies to the interviewee fairly
- Note-taking, recordings, transcripts and agreed notes
- Storing, sharing and relying on interview records

### Module 6: Interviewing Boards, Senior Managers and Control Functions

How to tailor interviews to chairs, non-executives, chief executives and heads of risk, compliance and internal audit, and what each interview should reveal about governance and control.

**Objectives**

- Tailor questioning to the role and accountabilities of the interviewee
- Assess board challenge, executive accountability and control function independence
- Identify governance red flags from interview evidence

**Syllabus**

- Interviewing chairs and non-executive directors on challenge and oversight
- Interviewing executives on strategy, risk and accountability
- Interviewing heads of risk, compliance and internal audit on independence and resources
- Governance red flags: dominant individuals, weak challenge and information gaps

### Module 7: Managing Difficult Interviews

Techniques for handling defensive, hostile, evasive or over-talkative interviewees, the involvement of legal advisers, and keeping control of the interview without compromising fairness.

**Objectives**

- Apply techniques to manage defensive, hostile and evasive interviewees
- Maintain control and fairness when advisers or colleagues intervene
- Decide when to pause, re-plan or escalate an interview

**Syllabus**

- Types of difficult interviewee and why they behave as they do
- De-escalation, re-framing and returning to the question
- Legal advisers, compliance chaperones and interruptions
- When to stop, re-plan or escalate

### Module 8: From Interview to Finding: Role-Play Workshop

Brings the programme together in a full simulated interview based on a case study, followed by evaluation of the evidence and drafting of supervisory conclusions and next steps.

**Objectives**

- Conduct a complete interview using the techniques covered in the programme
- Evaluate interview evidence alongside documents and data
- Draw supervisory conclusions and link them to proportionate action

**Syllabus**

- Evaluating the interview: what was established and what remains open
- Weighing interview evidence with documentary and data evidence
- From evidence to finding, follow-up request or escalation
- Workshop: role-play interview, debrief and draft findings

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with questioning exercises, case studies and a closing role-play workshop, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's inspection approach.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of on-site inspections or firm engagement is helpful. No prior interview training is required.
