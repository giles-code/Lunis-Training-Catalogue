## Aim and Purpose

To equip firms to understand how AI agents are moving from giving information to taking action, and the new risks this creates for authority, identity, payments, liability and financial crime. Participants leave able to design controls and governance for agentic AI and to see their firm's agent strategy the way a supervisor does.

**Coverage:** AI agents and agentic finance, delegated authority and mandates, identity and know your agent, payments by agents, controls and kill-switches, liability and customer outcomes, financial crime, third-party models, and governance and board oversight.

## Intended Audience

- Product, digital, technology and innovation leads designing or deploying AI agents
- Compliance, risk, financial crime and legal professionals overseeing agentic AI
- Senior managers and directors accountable for AI strategy and its outcomes

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how AI agents act and transact and where they are used in financial services
- Design delegated authority, mandates and identity controls for agents
- Assess payment, liability and customer outcome risks from agent actions
- Implement controls, monitoring and kill-switches for agentic systems
- Identify financial crime and third-party model risks and adapt controls
- Establish governance and board oversight and agree a readiness plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: From Generative AI to Agentic Finance

What AI agents are, how they differ from chatbots and traditional automation, and where they are beginning to act and transact in financial services.

**Objectives**

- Explain how AI agents plan, use tools and act on behalf of users
- Distinguish assistive, delegated and autonomous agent models
- Identify current and near-term agentic use cases in the firm's sector

**Syllabus**

- Agents, tools, memory and multi-agent systems
- From advice to action: agents that execute, trade and pay
- The layers of agentic finance: governance, decision, assets and settlement
- Use cases across banking, payments, investment and insurance

### Module 2: Delegated Authority and Mandates

How a firm defines what an agent is permitted to do, on whose behalf and within what limits, whether the agent is the firm's own or a customer's.

**Objectives**

- Define the scope, limits and duration of authority given to an agent
- Design mandates and permissions for firm-operated and customer agents
- Identify where delegated authority creates legal and conduct uncertainty

**Syllabus**

- Principals, agents and mandates: who is acting for whom
- Permissions, spending limits, scopes and expiry
- Customer consent, revocation and changes of instruction
- Agents acting across firms and platforms

### Module 3: Identity and Know Your Agent

How firms identify, authenticate and trust AI agents, link them to an accountable person or entity, and adapt customer due diligence to agent-initiated activity.

**Objectives**

- Explain the know your agent concept and emerging trust frameworks
- Design controls to authenticate agents and bind them to a principal
- Adapt onboarding and due diligence for agent-initiated relationships

**Syllabus**

- Agent identity, credentials and verifiable attestations
- Binding agents to customers, firms and developers
- Distinguishing legitimate agents from bots and impersonation
- Due diligence implications of agent-initiated onboarding and activity

### Module 4: Payments and Transactions by Agents

How agents initiate payments and transactions across traditional and new payment rails, and the authorisation, authentication and dispute issues this raises.

**Objectives**

- Explain how agents initiate payments across card, account and tokenised rails
- Assess strong authentication and authorisation for agent-initiated payments
- Design dispute, refund and error-handling processes for agent transactions

**Syllabus**

- Agent-initiated payments, tokens and programmable money
- Authentication, consent and transaction limits
- Unauthorised, mistaken and disputed agent transactions
- Settlement, reconciliation and stablecoin rails

### Module 5: Controls, Monitoring and Kill-Switches

The control environment for agents that act: pre-deployment testing, guardrails, real-time monitoring, human intervention and the ability to stop an agent quickly.

**Objectives**

- Design layered controls and guardrails for agentic systems
- Specify monitoring, logging and alerting for agent behaviour
- Implement and test kill-switches and human override

**Syllabus**

- Testing, red-teaming and staged deployment
- Guardrails, hard limits and tool restrictions
- Real-time monitoring, audit trails and anomaly detection
- Kill-switches, rollback and incident response

### Module 6: Liability, Conduct and Customer Outcomes

Who is responsible when an agent gets it wrong, how conduct and consumer protection rules apply to agent actions, and how firms secure good customer outcomes.

**Objectives**

- Analyse liability allocation between the firm, customer and technology provider
- Apply suitability, fair treatment and disclosure obligations to agent actions
- Identify risks of manipulation, herding and harm to vulnerable customers

**Syllabus**

- Liability for errors, unauthorised acts and agent failures
- Suitability, best interests and the advice boundary
- Dark patterns, agent-to-agent manipulation and collusion
- Vulnerable customers and outcome monitoring

### Module 7: Financial Crime and Third-Party Model Risk

How agents can be exploited for fraud, money laundering and sanctions evasion, and how firms manage dependence on third-party models and agent platforms.

**Objectives**

- Recognise financial crime typologies involving AI agents
- Adapt transaction monitoring and fraud controls to agent activity
- Assess due diligence, concentration and exit risk for third-party models

**Syllabus**

- Agent-enabled fraud, account takeover and synthetic identities
- Laundering and sanctions evasion through automated transactions
- Third-party models, agent platforms and concentration risk
- Contracts, audit rights and exit planning

### Module 8: Governance, Board Oversight and Readiness

Brings the programme together into a governance framework and readiness plan, covering accountability, board oversight, supervisory engagement and international standards.

**Objectives**

- Define accountability for agentic AI under senior manager accountability regimes
- Specify the information and assurance the board needs on agents
- Produce an agentic AI readiness plan for the firm

**Syllabus**

- Accountability, risk appetite and approval of agent use cases
- Board reporting, assurance and supervisory engagement
- International work by the FSB, IOSCO, BIS and OECD
- Workshop: the firm's agentic AI readiness plan

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, control design exercises and group discussion, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's agentic use cases.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with AI, payments or digital products in a regulated firm is helpful. No technical background in AI is required.

## Core Reading

- OECD, Recommendation of the Council on Artificial Intelligence (2019, revised 2024)
- NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) (2023)
- FSB, The Financial Stability Implications of Artificial Intelligence (2024)
- FATF, Guidance on Digital Identity (2020)
