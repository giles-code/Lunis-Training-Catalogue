## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a rigorous, practical command of MiCA and the wider EU rulebook that surrounds it, from the technology and business models through to authorisation, ongoing supervision, enforcement and crisis management. Participants leave able to look beyond a firm's documentation, identify red flags early and apply MiCA's supervisory powers proportionately, working with ESMA, the EBA and other national competent authorities.

## What It Covers

Distributed ledger technology and the crypto ecosystem, MiCA's scope, taxonomy and rulebook, CASP business models, governance and prudential requirements, authorisation and risk-based supervision, ICT risk under DORA, custody and safeguarding, market abuse, AML/CFT and the Travel Rule, asset-referenced and e-money tokens, passporting and cooperation, and enforcement, crisis management and emerging risks.

## Intended Audience

- Authorisation, supervision and inspection staff responsible for crypto-asset service providers and token issuers
- AML/CFT, market integrity, ICT risk and enforcement specialists covering digital finance
- Heads of department and senior managers setting the authority's crypto-asset supervisory strategy

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how distributed ledgers, crypto-assets and CASP business models work and where risk sits
- Apply MiCA's scope, taxonomy and Level 1 to Level 3 rulebook to authorisation and ongoing supervision
- Assess CASP governance, prudential resources, custody and operational resilience, including under DORA
- Evaluate market abuse, AML/CFT, Travel Rule and sanctions risks and the controls that address them
- Operate passporting, information exchange and colleges with other authorities, ESMA and the EBA
- Apply graduated enforcement and crisis tools, and anticipate emerging risks in digital finance

## Structure and Format

Fifteen modules of about five hours each, organised in five pathways and delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a full programme over several weeks or pathway by pathway. Each module combines pre-reading and a technical briefing with case studies, red-flag exercises and supervisory question sets, and pathways or modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of authorising or supervising financial firms is helpful. No technical background in blockchain is required, as the first pathway builds the foundations.

## Core Reading

- Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 on markets in crypto-assets (MiCA)
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets (Transfer of Funds Regulation)
- Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 on digital operational resilience for the financial sector (DORA)
- FSB, High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Crypto-Asset Activities and Markets (2023)
