## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' skills in identifying and assessing bias and discriminatory outcomes in AI-driven financial crime systems, and the accountability, audit, and human-in-the-loop safeguards supervisors should expect firms to maintain.

## Intended Audience

Mid-level supervisory and financial crime specialist staff assessing firms' financial crime controls.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how bias can enter AI-driven financial crime systems
- Describe the risk of discriminatory outcomes in automated decisioning
- Apply techniques for testing AI systems for bias
- Evaluate the accountability and audit trail supporting an AI system's decisions
- Formulate a supervisory assessment of bias risk in a sample AI system

## Syllabus

**Session 1: How bias enters AI systems (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain sources of bias in financial crime AI*
- Sources of bias: training data, proxy variables, historical patterns
- How bias in financial crime AI can produce discriminatory outcomes
- The particular sensitivity of financial crime decisions for affected customers
- Regulatory and ethical expectations around fairness
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: bias risks participants have observed in automated systems

**Session 2: Testing for bias (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Apply bias-testing techniques*
- Techniques for testing AI systems for bias against protected characteristics
- Interpreting bias-testing results
- Remediation options when bias is identified
- Limitations of bias-testing methodologies
Activities:
- Group exercise: interpret sample bias-testing results for a financial crime AI system

**Session 3: Accountability and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Evaluate accountability and formulate an assessment*
- Audit trail requirements for AI-driven decisions
- Human-in-the-loop safeguards and their design
- Case study: a bias concern identified in a firm's AI system
Activities:
- Case study: formulate a supervisory assessment of bias risk and recommend safeguards

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: bias sources, testing, accountability, safeguards
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: How bias enters AI systems (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Testing for bias (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Accountability and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Bias in financial crime AI systems can produce real discriminatory harm, not just a technical inaccuracy
- Bias testing should be an ongoing discipline, not a one-off check at deployment
- Human-in-the-loop safeguards and clear audit trails are essential accountability mechanisms for automated decisions
