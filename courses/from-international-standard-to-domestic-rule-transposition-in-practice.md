## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to build participants' understanding of how international and EU-level standards are translated into domestic rules, including the risks of over- or under-implementation, so that transposition decisions are made deliberately rather than by default.

## Intended Audience

Junior to mid-level policy officers drafting or contributing to rules, guidance, and consultation papers.

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the transposition process from international standard to domestic rule
- Distinguish minimum harmonisation from maximum harmonisation approaches
- Assess the risks of over-implementation and under-implementation
- Evaluate domestic discretion available within an international standard
- Formulate a transposition approach for a sample international standard

## Syllabus

**Session 1: The transposition process (09:30–10:45)**
*Objective: Explain the transposition process and harmonisation approaches*
- How international standards (IOSCO, FATF, Basel) become domestic rules
- EU directives versus regulations and their transposition implications
- Minimum versus maximum harmonisation
- Timing and sequencing of transposition
Activities:
- Facilitated discussion: transposition experiences from participants' own jurisdictions

**Session 2: Risks of over- and under-implementation (11:00–12:30)**
*Objective: Assess risks of over- and under-implementation*
- Consequences of over-implementation: competitiveness, unintended burden
- Consequences of under-implementation: assessment failures, reputational risk
- Case examples of both failure modes
- Balancing domestic context against international consistency
Activities:
- Group exercise: assess a sample transposition decision for over/under-implementation risk

**Session 3: Domestic discretion and application (13:30–14:45)**
*Objective: Evaluate discretion and formulate a transposition approach*
- Identifying the discretion available within an international standard
- Using discretion deliberately rather than by default
- Case study: transposing a sample international standard
Activities:
- Case study: formulate a transposition approach for a sample standard, identifying where discretion is used and why

**Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)**
*Objective: Consolidate learning*
- Recap: transposition process, over/under-implementation, discretion
Activities:
- Reflection and discussion
- Q&A

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: The transposition process (09:30–10:45)
- Session 2: Risks of over- and under-implementation (11:00–12:30)
- Session 3: Domestic discretion and application (13:30–14:45)
- Session 4: Wrap-up and key takeaways (14:45–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Transposition is a deliberate policy choice, not a mechanical copy-paste exercise
- Both over- and under-implementation carry real risks that need active management
- Understanding available discretion lets an authority tailor standards sensibly to domestic context
