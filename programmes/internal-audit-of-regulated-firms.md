## Aim and Purpose

To give internal auditors a practical grounding in auditing the risks that matter most to supervisors, from regulatory compliance and conduct to financial crime, AI and operational resilience. Participants leave able to plan risk-based coverage, test controls and outcomes rigorously, and report in a way that drives remediation and stands up to supervisory scrutiny.

**Coverage:** The role and independence of internal audit, risk-based audit planning, auditing regulatory compliance, conduct and culture, financial crime controls, models and AI, operational resilience and third parties, reporting to the audit committee, follow-up and working with supervisors.

## Intended Audience

- Heads of internal audit, audit managers and auditors in banks, insurers, investment firms, fund managers and payment firms
- Co-sourced and outsourced audit providers delivering assurance to regulated firms
- Audit committee members and compliance and risk staff who rely on internal audit work

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the role, independence and standards of internal audit in a regulated firm
- Design a risk-based audit plan aligned with the firm's regulatory exposure
- Apply audit techniques to compliance, conduct, culture and financial crime controls
- Assess models, AI, operational resilience and third-party arrangements
- Report findings to the audit committee and track remediation to closure
- Engage constructively with supervisors and see the firm the way a supervisor does

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The Role and Independence of Internal Audit

What internal audit is for in a regulated firm, how it sits within the three lines of defence, and the structural safeguards that keep it independent and credible with supervisors.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of internal audit and its place in the three lines of defence
- Assess the independence, reporting lines and resourcing of the function
- Identify what supervisors expect of internal audit and why it matters to them

**Syllabus**

- Internal audit's mandate, charter and unrestricted access
- Independence, objectivity and the head of audit's reporting lines
- Professional standards, quality assurance and external assessment
- How supervisors use internal audit work and judge its quality

### Module 2: Risk-Based Audit Planning

How to build an audit universe and a risk-based plan that reflects the firm's business model, regulatory exposure and emerging risks, and how to defend it to the audit committee.

**Objectives**

- Build an audit universe linked to the firm's risk profile and regulatory obligations
- Prioritise audit coverage using risk assessment and supervisory priorities
- Design a flexible plan that responds to change and emerging risks

**Syllabus**

- Audit universe, risk assessment and coverage cycles
- Using risk registers, incidents, supervisory findings and horizon scanning
- Balancing assurance, advisory and regulator-requested reviews
- Agreeing and refreshing the plan with the audit committee

### Module 3: Auditing Regulatory Compliance

How to audit the firm's compliance framework and its adherence to regulatory requirements, including the compliance function itself, regulatory reporting and the management of regulatory change.

**Objectives**

- Plan audits of the compliance framework and compliance monitoring
- Test the accuracy and controls behind regulatory returns
- Evaluate how the firm identifies and implements regulatory change

**Syllabus**

- Auditing the compliance function: mandate, resources and monitoring programme
- Regulatory reporting: data lineage, reconciliations and sign-off
- Regulatory change management and obligations registers
- Breach identification, recording and notification to the authority

### Module 4: Auditing Conduct, Culture and Customer Outcomes

How internal audit can give assurance on conduct, culture and the fair treatment of customers, moving beyond process testing to evidence of actual outcomes.

**Objectives**

- Design audit approaches to culture and behaviour
- Test whether controls deliver fair customer outcomes in practice
- Report soft-control findings credibly to management and the board

**Syllabus**

- Auditing culture: indicators, behavioural evidence and soft controls
- Product governance, sales, complaints and vulnerable customers
- Remuneration, incentives and conduct risk
- Outcome testing and file review techniques

### Module 5: Auditing Financial Crime Controls

How to audit the firm's anti-money laundering, counter-terrorist financing, sanctions, fraud and anti-bribery controls, aligned with the risk-based approach set out in FATF standards.

**Objectives**

- Assess the firm's financial crime risk assessment and control framework
- Test customer due diligence, monitoring and reporting effectiveness
- Evaluate sanctions screening and fraud controls, including system tuning

**Syllabus**

- Business-wide risk assessment and the risk-based approach
- Customer due diligence, ongoing monitoring and suspicious activity reporting
- Sanctions screening: lists, thresholds, alerts and testing
- Fraud, bribery and the governance of financial crime systems

### Module 6: Auditing Models, Data and AI

How internal audit gives assurance over models, data and AI systems, including model risk management, data governance and the particular challenges of machine learning and generative AI.

**Objectives**

- Assess the firm's model risk management and AI governance framework
- Audit data quality, model validation and ongoing monitoring
- Use technology and analytics within the audit process itself

**Syllabus**

- Model inventory, tiering, validation and change control
- Data governance, lineage and quality controls
- AI-specific risks: explainability, bias, drift and third-party models
- Data analytics and AI tools in audit work

### Module 7: Auditing Operational Resilience and Third Parties

How to audit the firm's ability to withstand and recover from disruption, including important business services, cyber security, outsourcing and reliance on critical third parties.

**Objectives**

- Assess the mapping of important business services and impact tolerances
- Audit cyber, incident management and scenario testing arrangements
- Evaluate outsourcing and third-party risk management across the lifecycle

**Syllabus**

- Important business services, mapping and impact tolerances
- Cyber security, incident response and business continuity, including ISO 22301
- Outsourcing and third-party due diligence, contracts, oversight and exit
- Concentration risk and reliance on intragroup and cloud providers

### Module 8: Reporting, Follow-Up and Working with Supervisors

Brings the programme together: reporting findings to the audit committee, tracking remediation to closure, and building a constructive relationship with supervisors, ending with an audit improvement plan.

**Objectives**

- Write audit reports and committee papers that drive action
- Run an effective issue-tracking and validation process
- Engage confidently with supervisors and agree an improvement plan

**Syllabus**

- Report writing, ratings and the head of audit's annual opinion
- Issue tracking, validation of remediation and escalation of overdue actions
- Supervisory meetings, information requests and regulator-commissioned reviews
- Workshop: an improvement plan for the internal audit function

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short briefings with case studies, audit planning exercises and report-writing practice, and can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and audit plan.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of internal audit, risk or compliance in a regulated firm is helpful. No specialist technical background is required.
