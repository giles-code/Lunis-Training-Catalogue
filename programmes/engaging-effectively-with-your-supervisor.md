## Aim and Purpose

To give senior leaders and control functions an inside view of risk-based supervision, taught by former supervisors using the same frameworks, evidence and judgements authorities apply to firms. Participants leave able to assess their own firm as a supervisor would, prepare for meetings and inspections with confidence, and respond to findings in a way that builds a constructive relationship.

**Coverage:** Risk-based supervision and impact and probability, supervisory intensity and the supervisory cycle, business model analysis, financial analysis, governance assessment, risk management and controls, data, returns and thematic reviews, supervisory meetings, inspections and interviews, risk ratings, panels and findings, and building a constructive relationship.

## Intended Audience

- Chief executives, senior managers and executive directors who meet the supervisor
- Heads of compliance, risk, finance and internal audit and their teams who prepare the firm for supervision
- Company secretaries, regulatory liaison and relationship managers who coordinate supervisory engagement

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how risk-based supervision works and how authorities decide how closely to supervise the firm
- Assess the firm's business model and financial position using the analysis supervisors apply
- Evaluate the firm's governance, risk management and controls against supervisory expectations and red flags
- Prepare directors and managers for supervisory meetings, inspections and interviews
- Interpret risk ratings and findings and respond with credible, evidence-based remediation
- Design a strategy for a constructive, open and no-surprises relationship with the authority

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Inside the Authority: Risk-Based Supervision

How authorities organise supervision around risk, combining the impact of a firm's failure with the probability of it happening, and why this framework shapes every interaction the firm has with its supervisor.

**Objectives**

- Explain the objectives and mandate that drive supervisory decisions
- Explain how impact and probability combine into a view of the firm
- Identify where the firm sits in its authority's framework and why

**Syllabus**

- Statutory objectives and outcomes-based supervision
- Impact: size, customer reach, systemic importance and substitutability
- Probability: inherent risk, controls, governance and financial resilience
- International principles on risk-based supervision across sectors

### Module 2: Supervisory Intensity and the Supervisory Cycle

How authorities set their own risk appetite, vary intensity across the regulated population and plan their work, and what each engagement model means in practice for the firm.

**Objectives**

- Explain how authorities allocate scarce supervisory resource
- Distinguish the engagement models applied to high-impact and smaller firms
- Anticipate the supervisory cycle and the touchpoints it creates

**Syllabus**

- The authority's risk appetite and tolerance for firm failure
- Intensity tiers, firm categorisation and minimum engagement models
- Supervisory plans, desk-based review and proactive versus reactive work
- How firms move between categories and what triggers closer attention

### Module 3: Your Business Model Through a Supervisor's Eyes

How supervisors analyse strategy and business models, test viability and sustainability, and spot a stretched model, so the firm can present and challenge its own plans in the same terms.

**Objectives**

- Characterise the firm's business model as a supervisor would
- Assess viability and sustainability and the key dependencies
- Recognise the signs of a stretched or failing business model

**Syllabus**

- Products, customers, channels, value chain and revenue drivers
- Viability and sustainability: the supervisor's two questions
- Dependencies: owners, outsourcers, distributors, banking partners and major clients
- Signs of stress: rapid growth, outlier returns and concentration

### Module 4: Your Financials Through a Supervisor's Eyes

How supervisors read financial statements, regulatory returns and projections, and how the firm can anticipate the questions its numbers will raise.

**Objectives**

- Read the firm's financial statements and returns with a supervisory lens
- Assess capital, liquidity and the credibility of projections
- Identify the financial red flags that prompt supervisory challenge

**Syllabus**

- Balance sheet, income statement, cash flow and related party disclosures
- Capital, liquidity buffers, safeguarding and wind-down resources
- Projections, assumptions and why strategies fail
- Red flags: aggressive accounting, late filings and unexplained movements

### Module 5: Governance as the Supervisor Assesses It

What supervisors look for when they assess governance, the evidence they use and the red flags that lower a rating, so the board and executives can test themselves first.

**Objectives**

- Explain the components of governance risk supervisors assess
- Identify the evidence supervisors use, from board packs to minutes
- Assess the firm's governance against common supervisory red flags

**Syllabus**

- Governance risk: board, management, accountability, culture and group influence
- Evidence: board packs, minutes, evaluations and committee papers
- Challenge versus confrontation, information asymmetry and dominant individuals
- Rating governance and its link to other risk categories

### Module 6: Risk Management and Controls: Working, Not Just Existing

How supervisors judge whether risk management and the control functions actually work, how they test controls, and how control quality changes the net risk rating.

**Objectives**

- Explain how supervisors move from inherent risk to net risk
- Assess the stature, independence and resourcing of the control functions
- Evidence that controls operate effectively, not merely on paper

**Syllabus**

- Risk appetite, risk identification and management information
- Compliance, risk and internal audit: independence, stature and coverage
- Walk-throughs, sample testing and outcomes evidence
- Net risk: weighing controls against inherent risk

### Module 7: Data, Returns and Thematic Reviews

How authorities use regulatory returns and data analytics to compare firms and find outliers, and how thematic reviews work, so the firm understands what its data says before the supervisor does.

**Objectives**

- Explain how supervisors use returns and data to identify outliers
- Assure the accuracy and consistency of the firm's regulatory reporting
- Prepare for and respond to thematic reviews and information requests

**Syllabus**

- Regulatory returns as supervisory data: ownership, accuracy and sign-off
- Peer comparison, outlier analysis and supervisory dashboards
- Thematic reviews: triggers, sampling, questionnaires and feedback
- Information requests: scope, deadlines and consistency of answers

### Module 8: Supervisory Meetings, Inspections and Interviews

How supervisors plan meetings, on-site inspections and interviews with directors and managers, what they test, and how the firm prepares people and documents without scripting them.

**Objectives**

- Explain how supervisors plan meetings, inspections and interviews
- Prepare directors and managers to give accurate, owned answers
- Manage document requests, logistics and follow-up during an inspection

**Syllabus**

- Visit planning, document requests and interview schedules
- What supervisors test in interviews with chairs, executives and control functions
- Preparation: briefing packs, consistency and owning the answer
- Mock supervisory meeting with the chief executive and chair

### Module 9: Risk Ratings, Panels, Findings and Remediation

How supervisory assessments are challenged and calibrated in internal panels, how ratings become findings letters and risk mitigation programmes, and how the firm responds credibly.

**Objectives**

- Explain how panels calibrate ratings and what drives the outcome
- Interpret findings letters and the supervisory tools that may follow
- Design a remediation response that fixes root causes and closes with evidence

**Syllabus**

- Risk governance panels: pack, challenge and calibration of ratings
- Findings letters, risk mitigation programmes and deadlines
- Escalation tools: attestations, skilled person reviews, directions and licence conditions
- Responding to findings: accuracy, root causes and evidence of closure

### Module 10: A Constructive Relationship and Capstone Workshop

Brings the programme together around building trust with the authority, followed by a workshop in which participants assess a case-study firm as a supervisor would and plan its engagement.

**Objectives**

- Design an engagement strategy built on openness and no surprises
- Apply the supervisor's method to assess a firm end to end
- Produce an action plan to strengthen the firm's supervisory relationship

**Syllabus**

- Openness, notifications and the cost of surprises
- Relationship mapping, regulatory liaison and board reporting on supervision
- Common misunderstandings between firms and supervisors
- Workshop: assessing a case-study firm and planning its supervisory engagement

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module pairs the supervisor's method with practical preparation for the firm, using case studies, exercises and mock supervisory meetings, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and supervisory category.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of senior management, compliance, risk or finance work in a regulated firm is helpful. No prior experience of working for an authority is required.
