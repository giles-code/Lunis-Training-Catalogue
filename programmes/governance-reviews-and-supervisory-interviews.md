## Aim and Purpose

To show licensees how a supervisory governance assessment works, from desk review of board packs to interviews with directors and key staff, so that they can strengthen and evidence their governance. Participants leave knowing what supervisors look for in the board, senior management, risk architecture and internal audit, and how to prepare for interviews without scripting or over-coaching.

**Coverage:** How supervisors assess governance, the governance pillars, board packs and board dynamics, decision-making and challenge, the role of interviews in supervision, and preparing for and taking part in supervisory interviews.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, executive and non-executive directors likely to be interviewed by the supervisor
- Senior managers and control function holders in risk, compliance and internal audit
- Company secretaries and governance staff who prepare board packs and coordinate supervisory visits

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how supervisors assess governance and what evidence they use
- Assess the firm's governance against the four pillars of board, senior management, risk architecture and internal audit
- Evaluate board packs and minutes as a supervisor would read them
- Demonstrate effective board challenge and decision-making with evidence
- Prepare directors and staff for supervisory interviews openly and appropriately
- Respond to interview follow-up and governance findings constructively

## Syllabus

### Module 1: How Supervisors Assess Governance

Why governance is a core supervisory risk, how a governance assessment is scoped and carried out, and how its conclusions affect the firm's ratings and supervisory engagement.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose of a supervisory governance assessment
- Describe the stages from desk review to conclusions
- Anticipate how governance findings are rated and followed up

**Syllabus**

- Governance risk and why supervisors assess it
- Proportionality and materiality in governance reviews
- Desk analysis, interviews and conclusions
- What strong, adequate and weak governance look like

### Module 2: The Four Pillars of Governance

What supervisors expect from the board, senior management, the risk architecture and internal audit, and how these pillars work together, including over outsourced activities.

**Objectives**

- Assess the firm against each governance pillar
- Identify gaps between documented and actual governance
- Evidence oversight of outsourced and technology services

**Syllabus**

- The board: composition, skills, committees and oversight
- Senior management and effective delegation
- Risk architecture: strategy, risk appetite, risk function and three lines
- Internal audit, outsourcing and what the board still owns

### Module 3: Board Packs, Board Dynamics and Decision-Making

What the board pack and minutes reveal to a supervisor, and how agendas, chairing, culture and challenge shape the quality of board decisions.

**Objectives**

- Apply five tests to the firm's own board pack
- Recognise the behavioural red flags supervisors look for
- Evidence the quality of a major board decision

**Syllabus**

- Reading a board pack: what it shows and what it leaves out
- Agenda, chairing, information quality and psychological safety
- Groupthink, dominant personalities and information asymmetry
- Case study: board pack review

### Module 4: The Role of Interviews in Supervision

Why supervisors interview directors and staff, who they interview, how they plan and triangulate evidence, and how they judge credibility and consistency.

**Objectives**

- Explain how interviews test documentary evidence
- Describe how supervisors plan and sequence interviews
- Recognise how answers are assessed for credibility

**Syllabus**

- Documents tell supervisors what; interviews test how and why
- Who is interviewed and what each perspective reveals
- Question types, probing and follow-up
- Credibility, consistency and corroboration

### Module 5: Preparing for and Taking Part in Supervisory Interviews

How to prepare directors and staff for interviews openly and appropriately, answer clearly and accurately, and avoid the pitfalls that undermine credibility.

**Objectives**

- Prepare interviewees without scripting or over-coaching
- Answer questions directly with evidence and examples
- Handle questions outside the interviewee's knowledge honestly

**Syllabus**

- Preparing well: knowing the firm, the papers and your role
- Answering the question asked, with specific evidence
- Avoiding vague, hedged or rehearsed answers
- Interview practice: both sides of the table

### Module 6: After the Visit: Findings and Follow-Up

How governance findings are communicated, how to respond to them, and how to turn the review into a lasting improvement in governance.

**Objectives**

- Respond to governance findings constructively
- Agree realistic remediation and evidence its completion
- Produce a governance improvement plan

**Syllabus**

- Feedback letters and requests for further information
- Responding to findings: accuracy, ownership and context
- Remediation actions and evidencing closure
- Workshop: a governance improvement plan for your firm

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about one hour each, delivered as a one-day workshop in person, virtually or in a hybrid format. It combines short briefings with a board pack review case study and interview practice. It can be delivered on its own or with the companion workshops on business models and on supervisory findings and remediation.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of board or senior management work in a regulated firm is helpful. No prior experience of supervisory interviews is required.
