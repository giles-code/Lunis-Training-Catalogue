## Aim and Purpose

To give compliance and financial crime teams a firm-side understanding of how terrorist and proliferation networks raise, move and use funds, and why these risks need controls beyond those built for money laundering. Participants leave able to assess the firm's exposure, tune screening and monitoring, apply targeted financial sanctions without delay, recognise dual-use and procurement red flags, and report urgently and well.

**Coverage:** How terrorist and proliferation financing differ from money laundering, the FATF standards and firms' obligations, risk assessment, terrorist financing typologies, non-profit and humanitarian customers, proliferation networks and dual-use goods red flags, screening and targeted financial sanctions, monitoring, investigation and urgent reporting, and governance and assurance.

## Intended Audience

- MLROs, compliance officers and financial crime analysts in investment, fund, payment, crypto-asset and fintech firms
- Sanctions and screening teams responsible for customer, investor and payment screening
- Senior managers and risk owners for products, corridors or customer segments with terrorist or proliferation financing exposure

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain how terrorist and proliferation financing differ from money laundering and what the FATF standards require of firms
- Assess the firm's terrorist and proliferation financing risk across customers, products, channels and geographies
- Identify typologies and red flags, including non-profit abuse, procurement networks and dual-use goods
- Apply targeted financial sanctions screening and freezing without delay
- Design monitoring, investigation and urgent reporting arrangements for these risks
- Evidence effective governance and controls to the board and supervisors

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Terrorist and Proliferation Financing: Why They Differ

Why terrorist and proliferation financing need distinct controls from anti-money laundering, and what the FATF standards and United Nations sanctions regimes require of regulated firms.

**Objectives**

- Explain the purpose-based nature of terrorist and proliferation financing
- Summarise the FATF Recommendations and firm obligations on both risks
- Recognise why controls built for money laundering can miss them

**Syllabus**

- Small sums, legitimate sources and the purpose of the funds
- FATF Recommendations 1, 5 to 8 and the obligations they create for firms
- United Nations Security Council targeted sanctions regimes
- Where anti-money laundering controls fall short

### Module 2: Assessing the Firm's Terrorist and Proliferation Financing Risk

How to assess the firm's exposure to both risks using national risk assessments, typologies and the firm's own data, and how to set risk appetite and controls in proportion.

**Objectives**

- Use national and sectoral risk assessments as inputs to the firm's own
- Assess inherent risk across customers, products, channels and geographies
- Document a proliferation financing risk assessment that meets the FATF standard

**Syllabus**

- National risk assessments, typology reports and public-private alerts
- Risk factors for investment, fund, payment, crypto-asset and fintech business
- Proliferation financing risk assessment and mitigation
- Risk appetite, control mapping and residual risk

### Module 3: Terrorist Financing Typologies and Red Flags

How terrorist organisations, small cells and lone actors raise, move and store funds, and the indicators that may appear in customer behaviour, payments and investment activity.

**Objectives**

- Identify the principal sources and channels of terrorist funding
- Recognise terrorist financing indicators in the firm's own data
- Distinguish genuine indicators from profiling and unwarranted de-risking

**Syllabus**

- Self-funding, personal credit, crime and abuse of donations
- Crowdfunding, online payments, prepaid products and crypto-assets
- Indicators in payments, investment accounts and onboarding
- Avoiding profiling: indicators, context and proportionate response

### Module 4: Non-Profit Organisations and Humanitarian Payments

How to serve non-profit and humanitarian customers proportionately, identify the minority at risk of abuse, and manage payments to high-risk regions without unwarranted de-risking.

**Objectives**

- Apply a risk-based approach to non-profit customers in line with FATF Recommendation 8
- Assess non-profit governance, programmes and partners proportionately
- Use humanitarian exemptions and licences while managing residual risk

**Syllabus**

- Forms of abuse: diversion, affiliation, false representation and sham organisations
- Due diligence on non-profit governance, funding and delivery partners
- Humanitarian exemptions, licences and payments to conflict regions
- De-risking, financial exclusion and the firm's reputational judgement

### Module 5: Proliferation Financing Networks and Dual-Use Goods Red Flags

How proliferation networks procure weapons-related and dual-use goods, conceal their activity through front companies, shipping and intermediaries, and the red flags that may surface in a firm's customers and payments.

**Objectives**

- Explain how procurement networks source goods and move funds
- Recognise dual-use goods, trade and corporate red flags
- Apply enhanced due diligence to customers with trade or technology exposure

**Syllabus**

- Procurement networks, front companies and third-country diversion
- Dual-use goods, end-user and routing red flags in payments and invoices
- Exposure through investors, portfolio companies, payment flows and crypto-assets
- Enhanced due diligence on technology, logistics and trading customers

### Module 6: Screening and Targeted Financial Sanctions

How targeted financial sanctions on terrorism and proliferation work for firms, from list management and screening to freezing without delay, ownership and control, and reporting frozen assets.

**Objectives**

- Explain designation, freezing obligations, exemptions and delisting
- Design screening of customers, investors, beneficial owners and payments
- Freeze without delay and report frozen assets correctly

**Syllabus**

- United Nations and national designations relating to terrorism and proliferation
- Screening scope, timing, list updates and alert disposition
- Ownership and control, indirect provision of funds and associated persons
- Freezing without delay, reporting, licences and handling false matches

### Module 7: Monitoring, Investigation and Urgent Reporting

How to build terrorist and proliferation financing indicators into transaction monitoring, investigate alerts quickly and file urgent, usable reports with the national FIU.

**Objectives**

- Design monitoring scenarios that reflect terrorist and proliferation typologies
- Investigate alerts and reach a timely decision on suspicion
- File urgent reports and respond to authority requests without tipping off

**Syllabus**

- Scenario design, thresholds and network analysis
- Investigating alerts: profiling, links and open-source information
- Urgent reporting, report quality and working with the national FIU
- Law enforcement requests, account monitoring and continuing relationships

### Module 8: Governance, Training and Assurance

The governance that makes terrorist and proliferation financing controls work: policies, accountability, staff awareness, management information and independent testing that can be evidenced to the board and supervisors.

**Objectives**

- Allocate accountability for these risks across the three lines of defence
- Specify management information and testing that show controls work
- Evidence the framework to the board and supervisors

**Syllabus**

- Policy, procedures and senior management accountability
- Targeted training for front-line, operations and screening staff
- Screening and monitoring testing, quality assurance and internal audit
- Board reporting and what supervisors expect to see

### Module 9: Case Workshop: Detecting and Responding

Brings the programme together in a case exercise in which teams identify terrorist and proliferation financing indicators, decide on screening, freezing and reporting, and agree control improvements.

**Objectives**

- Apply typologies and red flags to a realistic multi-product case
- Decide on freezing, reporting and relationship management
- Agree an action plan to close gaps in the firm's framework

**Syllabus**

- Case briefing: a payment and investment network with trade links
- Red-flag analysis and screening decisions
- Freezing, reporting and the firm's response
- Workshop: gap analysis and twelve-month action plan

## Structure and Format

Nine modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a practitioner briefing with case studies and red-flag exercises, ending in a case workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the firm's sector and risk profile.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT compliance or sanctions screening is helpful. No prior specialist knowledge of terrorist or proliferation financing is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- FATF, Terrorist Financing Risk Assessment Guidance (2019)
- FATF, Guidance on Proliferation Financing Risk Assessment and Mitigation (2021)
- FATF, Best Practices on Combating the Abuse of Non-Profit Organisations (2023)
