## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical understanding of environmental, social and governance risks, the disclosure and reporting frameworks that apply to them, and the tools to assess whether firms' claims match reality. Participants leave able to review sustainability risk assessments, challenge governance, product design and disclosures, identify greenwashing and embed sustainability in routine supervisory work.

## What It Covers

Sustainable finance and ESG risk foundations, climate and nature-related financial risk, the regulatory and disclosure landscape, sustainability reporting standards and ESG data, sustainability risk assessment, governance and risk management, product governance and labelling, greenwashing and greenbleaching, climate scenario analysis, and integrating sustainability into supervision.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and inspectors in banking, insurance, investment funds, investment services and pensions
- Sustainable finance, policy and risk specialists supporting supervisory teams
- Authorisation and product teams reviewing sustainability-related products and disclosures

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the key environmental, social and governance risks and how they affect financial products and services
- Describe the main sustainable finance disclosure, taxonomy and reporting frameworks and how they interact
- Assess a firm's sustainability risk assessment, governance and risk management
- Evaluate product governance, labelling and disclosures for substance and consistency
- Identify greenwashing and greenbleaching and select a proportionate supervisory response
- Design an approach for integrating sustainable finance into day-to-day supervision

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day workshops. Each module combines a technical briefing with class discussion, case studies and supervisory question exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the authority's sectors and needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of financial supervision or risk assessment is helpful. No prior knowledge of sustainable finance or ESG is required.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee, Principles for the Effective Management and Supervision of Climate-related Financial Risks (2022)
- NGFS, Guide for Supervisors: Integrating Climate-related and Environmental Risks into Prudential Supervision (2020)
- ISSB, IFRS S1 General Requirements and IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures (2023)
- IOSCO, Recommendations on Sustainability-Related Practices, Policies, Procedures and Disclosure in Asset Management (2021)
