## Aim and Purpose

To give prosecutors and judges a practical understanding of how financial crime cases are built, proved and tried, and of the evidential issues that most often cause them to fail. Participants leave able to frame charges, assess complex financial evidence and expert testimony, manage disclosure and case management fairly, and secure effective sentencing and confiscation outcomes.

**Coverage:** Money laundering offences and the international standards, charging decisions and case strategy, proving criminal property and knowledge, complex financial and digital evidence, expert witnesses, disclosure and case management, sentencing, confiscation and ancillary orders, and mutual legal assistance and cross-border evidence.

## Intended Audience

- Prosecutors handling money laundering, fraud, corruption and other economic crime
- Judges and magistrates hearing financial crime, restraint and confiscation cases
- Senior investigators and legal advisers who prepare financial crime cases for prosecution

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the elements of money laundering offences and the standards that shape them
- Assess the evidence needed to prove criminal property and the required mental element
- Evaluate complex financial and digital evidence and the reliability of expert testimony
- Apply disclosure and case management principles to large financial cases
- Secure proportionate sentences and effective confiscation orders
- Use mutual legal assistance to obtain evidence and recover assets abroad

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Money Laundering Offences and International Standards

The elements of money laundering offences, the range of predicate offences, and the international standards that shape national offences and expectations of effective prosecution.

**Objectives**

- Explain the physical and mental elements of money laundering offences
- Summarise the expectations of FATF Recommendation 3 and Immediate Outcome 7
- Distinguish the main types of money laundering prosecution

**Syllabus**

- Concealment, acquisition, possession and use of criminal property
- All-crimes and listed-offence approaches to predicate offences
- Self-laundering, third-party laundering and stand-alone cases
- FATF Recommendation 3, Immediate Outcome 7 and common weaknesses

### Module 2: Charging Decisions and Case Strategy

How prosecutors decide whether and what to charge in financial crime cases, work with investigators from an early stage and build a case theory the evidence can sustain.

**Objectives**

- Apply evidential and public interest tests to financial crime cases
- Select charges that reflect the criminality and can be proved
- Shape investigations through early prosecutorial advice

**Syllabus**

- Early prosecutorial involvement and investigative advice
- Choosing charges: predicate, laundering, conspiracy and corporate liability
- Case theory, indictment design and keeping cases manageable
- Alternatives: negotiated outcomes, deferred prosecution and non-trial resolution

### Module 3: Proving Criminal Property and Knowledge

How to prove that property derives from crime and that the defendant knew or suspected it, including through circumstantial evidence and inference where the predicate offence cannot be identified.

**Objectives**

- Explain how criminal origin can be proved without a conviction for the predicate offence
- Assess circumstantial evidence of knowledge, suspicion and wilful blindness
- Anticipate common defences and how to address them

**Syllabus**

- Proving criminal origin by inference from the circumstances
- Knowledge, suspicion, recklessness and wilful blindness
- Indicators from conduct, lifestyle and transaction patterns
- Common defences: legitimate business, ignorance and reliance on others

### Module 4: Complex Financial and Digital Evidence

How to understand, test and present large volumes of financial and digital evidence, from bank records and corporate documents to communications data and blockchain analysis.

**Objectives**

- Assess the admissibility, reliability and continuity of financial evidence
- Scrutinise schedules, charts and summaries prepared by investigators
- Evaluate digital and blockchain evidence critically

**Syllabus**

- Bank, corporate and accounting records as evidence
- Schedules, charts and summaries: accuracy and fairness
- Electronic and communications evidence and continuity
- Blockchain tracing outputs and their evidential limits

### Module 5: Expert Witnesses in Financial Cases

When expert evidence is needed in financial cases, how to instruct and test experts, the duties experts owe the court, and how judges manage conflicting expert opinion.

**Objectives**

- Identify issues that require expert rather than factual evidence
- Instruct experts with clear questions and appropriate material
- Test expert methodology, independence and the limits of opinion

**Syllabus**

- Forensic accountants, market specialists and blockchain analysts
- Letters of instruction, independence and the expert's duty to the court
- Testing methodology, assumptions and conclusions
- Managing competing experts and joint statements

### Module 6: Disclosure and Case Management in Large Cases

How to meet disclosure obligations fairly in document-heavy financial cases, use technology to manage material, and keep complex trials on track through active case management.

**Objectives**

- Apply disclosure duties to large volumes of material
- Use technology-assisted review defensibly
- Manage complex cases actively from first hearing to trial

**Syllabus**

- Disclosure principles: relevance and material that undermines or assists
- Search terms, technology-assisted review and audit trails
- Third-party, foreign and sensitive material
- Case management hearings, timetables and narrowing the issues

### Module 7: Sentencing, Confiscation and Ancillary Orders

How courts approach sentencing in money laundering and financial crime cases, the link with confiscation proceedings, and the ancillary orders that disrupt future offending.

**Objectives**

- Apply sentencing factors to money laundering and economic crime
- Explain benefit, the available amount and enforcement of confiscation
- Use ancillary orders to prevent further offending

**Syllabus**

- Sentencing factors: culpability, harm, role and sums laundered
- Confiscation: benefit, extended confiscation and the available amount
- Enforcement of confiscation orders and default terms
- Ancillary orders: disqualification, prevention orders and compensation

### Module 8: Cross-Border Cases and Mock Hearing Workshop

Brings the programme together by examining mutual legal assistance and foreign evidence, ending with a mock hearing in which participants argue and decide the key issues in a laundering case.

**Objectives**

- Draft effective mutual legal assistance requests for evidence and assets
- Manage the admissibility and use of evidence obtained abroad
- Apply the programme to argue and decide a realistic case

**Syllabus**

- Mutual legal assistance: dual criminality, content and timing
- Joint investigation teams and direct cooperation between prosecutors
- Admissibility and use of foreign evidence
- Workshop: mock hearing on knowledge, evidence and confiscation

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a legal and technical briefing with case studies and evidential exercises, ending in a mock hearing workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the legal system and the participants' roles.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience of criminal prosecution, judicial work or financial crime investigation is helpful. No accounting or technical background is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF (2012, as updated), International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations
- UNODC (2012), Manual on Mutual Legal Assistance and Extradition
- World Bank and UNODC StAR Initiative (2021), Asset Recovery Handbook: A Guide for Practitioners, Second Edition
- FATF (2018), Professional Money Laundering
