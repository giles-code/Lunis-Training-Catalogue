## Aim and Purpose

To give directors a practical understanding of their responsibilities for preventing money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions breaches, fraud, bribery and the facilitation of tax evasion. Participants leave able to set the board's expectations, read financial crime MI critically, oversee remediation and see their firm the way a supervisor does.

## What It Covers

The board's AML/CFT, sanctions, fraud, bribery and tax evasion responsibilities, financial crime risk appetite, the role and reporting of the MLRO, reading financial crime MI, culture and conduct, supervisory findings and remediation, and directors' personal liability.

## Intended Audience

- Chairs, non-executive and independent directors and board committee members, especially on risk and audit committees
- Executive directors and senior managers accountable for financial crime, compliance and operations
- Company secretaries and heads of governance supporting board oversight

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the board's responsibilities across AML/CFT, sanctions, fraud, bribery and tax evasion
- Set a financial crime risk appetite with measurable limits and escalation triggers
- Evaluate the role, independence and reporting of the MLRO
- Interpret financial crime MI and identify what is missing
- Oversee responses to supervisory findings and remediation programmes
- Assess directors' personal exposure and evidence effective oversight

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about two hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a two-day programme or as a series of board briefing sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with case studies and a board question set, and modules can be selected or tailored to the board's needs.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Board or senior management experience in financial services is helpful. No specialist background in financial crime compliance is required.

## Core Reading

- FATF, International Standards on Combating Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism and Proliferation: The FATF Recommendations (as updated)
- FATF, Guidance on Risk-Based Supervision (2021)
- Basel Committee, Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015)
