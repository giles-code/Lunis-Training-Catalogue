## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical method for assessing board dynamics and decision-making, not just board composition and terms of reference. Participants leave able to read board papers and minutes critically, test the quality of decisions and challenge, plan and conduct board-level interviews, and draw evidence-based conclusions.

**Coverage:** How the board works in practice, agendas, papers and information quality, information asymmetry and side conversations, oversight versus micromanagement, judging decision quality, culture, challenge and psychological safety, groupthink, board personalities, evaluation, succession, skills and AI in the boardroom, and case-study interviews.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and governance specialists who assess boards and senior management of regulated firms
- Staff conducting fit and proper, governance or culture reviews and interviewing chairs and directors
- Senior supervisors and team leaders who review governance assessments and decide the supervisory response

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain why sound governance structures can coexist with ineffective board dynamics
- Assess agendas, board papers and management information for quality and real decision support
- Evaluate the quality of board decisions by testing the process rather than the outcome
- Identify signs of weak challenge, low psychological safety and groupthink
- Plan and conduct evidence-led interviews with chairs, directors and executives
- Reach and evidence a supervisory conclusion on board effectiveness and the appropriate response

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Beyond Structures: Why Board Dynamics Matter

Why a governance assessment can confirm the right structures and still miss how the board actually works, and the supervisor's framework for assessing dynamics systematically.

**Objectives**

- Distinguish governance form from governance substance
- Apply the supervisor's eye: look for, concerns, evidence, questions and conclusion
- Identify the evidence sources available for assessing board dynamics

**Syllabus**

- Composition, committees and policies versus behaviour and challenge
- How weak board dynamics have contributed to firm failures
- Evidence sources: packs, minutes, evaluations, observation and interviews
- The five questions of the supervisor's eye

### Module 2: Agendas, Board Papers and Information Quality

How the agenda, the quality of papers and the volume of information shape what the board actually scrutinises, and what good and poor board information look like.

**Objectives**

- Assess whether the agenda gives proper time to strategic and risk issues
- Distinguish a decision-ready board paper from narrative without a clear ask
- Evaluate whether management information is concise, prioritised and exception-based

**Syllabus**

- Agenda curation, routine reporting and items carried over or pushed to the end
- Good papers: decision required, options, recommendation, risks and prior challenge
- Information overload and the shortcuts overwhelmed boards take
- Supervisory questions to test papers and information quality

### Module 3: Information Asymmetry and the Line Between Oversight and Management

How boards manage the gap between what they know and what management knows, how decisions get made before the meeting, and where oversight ends and micromanagement begins.

**Objectives**

- Assess how the board validates information and uses independent assurance
- Recognise when the board meeting has become a ratification exercise
- Judge whether the board is under-engaged, appropriately engaged or micromanaging

**Syllabus**

- Independent assurance: internal and external audit, risk function and outside experts
- Pre-meetings and side conversations: legitimate preparation or foregone conclusion
- Reading minutes for recorded debate and challenge
- Oversight versus direction of day-to-day decisions and staff

### Module 4: Judging the Quality of Board Decisions

How to test a significant board decision on the strength of its process rather than its outcome, and how to tell whether a board learns from its own history.

**Objectives**

- Apply a structured test of why, why now, alternatives, assumptions and downside
- Assess whether success measures and the recorded rationale are adequate
- Evaluate whether the board revisits decisions and learns from experience

**Syllabus**

- Process versus outcome: good decisions that go wrong and vice versa
- Alternatives, key assumptions and the worst case
- Success measures and minutes that record why, not just what
- Look-back reviews, repeat findings and organisational learning

### Module 5: Culture, Challenge and Psychological Safety

How board culture shows itself in meetings, minutes and interviews, the role of the chair in enabling challenge, and how to tell effective challenge from confrontation or avoidance.

**Objectives**

- Explain why challenge depends on psychological safety
- Read the signs of board culture: airtime, silence, interruptions and openness
- Assess the effectiveness of the chair in enabling challenge

**Syllabus**

- Psychological safety and how its absence shows up
- Observable signs of culture in meetings and minutes
- Effective and ineffective chairs
- Challenge, confrontation and avoiding conflict to keep the peace

### Module 6: Groupthink and Board Personalities

Why some boards drift into consensus at the expense of realistic appraisal, the warning signs, and how recognising common board personalities helps plan interviews without prejudging findings.

**Objectives**

- Identify the conditions and warning signs of groupthink
- Assess the countermeasures a board uses to protect independent judgement
- Use board personality types as an interview planning aid, not a finding

**Syllabus**

- Risk factors: long tenure, social closeness and a dominant chair or chief executive
- Warning signs: quick unanimity, self-censorship and private doubts
- Countermeasures: devil's advocate, anonymous polling, 'what would change our mind?'
- The dominator, silent director, absentee, box-ticker and permanent devil's advocate

### Module 7: Keeping the Board Effective: Evaluation, Succession, Skills and AI

The mechanisms a board uses to stay effective over time, what good practice looks like in each, and the emerging question of how AI is used in matters that come to the board.

**Objectives**

- Assess the quality and follow-through of board evaluations
- Evaluate succession planning and the use of a skills matrix
- Identify how reliance on AI in board decision-making is governed and assured

**Syllabus**

- Internal and external board evaluation and tracking actions to completion
- Succession for the chair, independent directors and key executives
- Skills matrices, capability gaps and appointments on relationships
- AI in the boardroom: validation, accountability and model limits

### Module 8: Case-Study Interview Workshop

A practical workshop in which participants plan and conduct interviews with the board of a case-study firm, test real board decisions and reach an evidenced supervisory conclusion.

**Objectives**

- Plan an interview strategy and question set for the chair, directors and executives
- Test whether the board, rather than a shareholder or management, owned key decisions
- Formulate a supervisory conclusion on board effectiveness and the response

**Syllabus**

- Testing an optimistic business plan and an intra-group outsourcing decision
- A board's response to a breach of its own risk appetite limit
- Role-play interviews with feedback on questioning and evidence
- Workshop: findings, rating and proposed supervisory response

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short input with analysis of board packs and minutes, group exercises and role-play interviews, ending in a case-study interview workshop, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's governance framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of governance assessment or supervisory interviews is helpful. No board experience is required.
