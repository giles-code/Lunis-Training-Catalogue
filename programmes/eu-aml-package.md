## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors and practitioners a clear, practical command of the EU's reformed AML/CFT framework, from the directly applicable single rulebook to AMLA's direct and indirect supervision and the strengthened tools of national supervisors and FIUs. Participants leave able to identify what changes for their institution, close gaps early and see AML/CFT controls the way a supervisor will under the new regime.

**Coverage:** The architecture of the EU AML package and its FATF foundations, the AML Regulation single rulebook, customer due diligence and beneficial ownership, crypto-asset service providers and the Transfer of Funds Regulation, AMLD6 and the roles of national supervisors, FIUs and central registers, AMLA's direct supervision and indirect oversight, supervisory colleges and cooperation, the interplay with restrictive measures, and readiness planning for firms and supervisors.

## Intended Audience

- AML/CFT supervisors, inspectors and policy staff at national competent authorities and self-regulatory bodies
- MLROs, compliance officers, financial crime risk and legal staff at credit institutions, financial institutions and crypto-asset service providers
- Senior managers and management body members accountable for the AML/CFT framework and transition planning

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the architecture of the EU AML package and how its Level 1 to Level 3 rulebook is built out
- Apply the AML Regulation's single rulebook to policies, internal controls and the business-wide risk assessment
- Assess customer due diligence, beneficial ownership and reliance arrangements against the new requirements
- Evaluate the obligations on crypto-asset service providers under the AML Regulation and the Transfer of Funds Regulation
- Explain AMLA's direct supervision, indirect oversight and common methodology, and the continuing roles of national supervisors and FIUs
- Design a transition plan that prepares the firm or authority for the new regime

## Syllabus

### Module 1: The EU AML Package: Architecture and Objectives

Why the EU replaced successive directives with a single rulebook and a new authority, how the package builds on the FATF standards, and how its Level 1 texts are built out through technical standards and guidelines.

**Objectives**

- Explain the weaknesses of the directive-based framework that the package addresses
- Map the AML Regulation, AMLD6, the AMLA Regulation and the Transfer of Funds Regulation
- Navigate the Level 2 and Level 3 measures and the phased transition

**Syllabus**

- Lessons from fragmented transposition and cross-border supervisory failures
- The four legal acts and how they divide responsibilities
- Regulatory and implementing technical standards, guidelines and AMLA's mandates
- Application dates, transitional provisions and the influence of FATF evaluations

### Module 2: The Single Rulebook: Scope, Policies and Internal Controls

The scope of the AML Regulation, including newly covered sectors, and its harmonised requirements for business-wide risk assessment, internal policies, compliance roles and group-wide arrangements.

**Objectives**

- Determine which entities and activities fall within the AML Regulation
- Apply the requirements for business-wide risk assessment, policies and internal controls
- Assess the roles of the management body, compliance manager and compliance officer

**Syllabus**

- Obliged entities, including crypto-asset service providers and newly covered sectors
- Business-wide risk assessment, documented methodology and link to risk appetite
- Management body accountability, compliance manager and compliance officer roles
- Group-wide policies, outsourcing limits and third-country branches and subsidiaries

### Module 3: Customer Due Diligence and Beneficial Ownership

The harmonised customer due diligence regime under the AML Regulation, including simplified and enhanced measures, PEPs and high-risk third countries, and the strengthened rules for identifying beneficial owners of complex structures.

**Objectives**

- Apply harmonised CDD, ongoing monitoring and periodic review requirements
- Identify beneficial owners through ownership and control, including multi-layered structures
- Evaluate enhanced due diligence for PEPs, high-risk third countries and higher-risk relationships

**Syllabus**

- CDD triggers, identification, verification and remote onboarding
- Beneficial ownership thresholds, control by other means and nominee arrangements
- Enhanced due diligence, PEPs and the EU lists of high-risk third countries
- Reliance, outsourcing, discrepancy reporting and access to central registers

### Module 4: Crypto-Asset Service Providers and the Transfer of Funds Rules

How the package brings crypto-asset service providers fully into the AML/CFT framework, and how the Transfer of Funds Regulation applies the travel rule to crypto-asset transfers, including those involving self-hosted wallets.

**Objectives**

- Explain the AML/CFT obligations on crypto-asset service providers and the link to MiCA authorisation
- Apply the travel rule to crypto-asset transfers, including missing or incomplete information
- Assess controls for self-hosted wallets, third-country counterparties and high-risk exposures

**Syllabus**

- Crypto-asset service providers as obliged entities under the AML Regulation and MiCA
- Originator and beneficiary information for transfers of funds and crypto-assets
- Self-hosted wallet transfers and verification of ownership or control
- Correspondent relationships with third-country providers and blockchain analytics

### Module 5: AMLD6: National Supervisors, FIUs and Central Registers

What remains with Member States under AMLD6: the powers and risk-based approach of national supervisors, strengthened FIU mandates and cooperation, and the interconnected registers of beneficial ownership and bank accounts.

**Objectives**

- Explain the minimum powers, independence and risk-based approach required of national supervisors
- Describe the strengthened role of FIUs and their cooperation through AMLA
- Use beneficial ownership and bank account registers as supervisory and compliance tools

**Syllabus**

- Risk-based supervision, sector risk assessments and administrative sanctions powers
- FIU powers, suspension of transactions and joint analyses
- Central beneficial ownership registers, verification and legitimate interest access
- Bank account registers, the single access point and real estate information

### Module 6: AMLA: Direct Supervision, Indirect Oversight and Methodology

AMLA's mandate and governance, the selection and direct supervision of high-risk cross-border obliged entities through joint supervisory teams, and its indirect oversight of national supervisors and the non-financial sector.

**Objectives**

- Explain AMLA's governance, tasks and powers
- Apply the criteria and process for selection for direct supervision
- Assess how AMLA's common methodology and convergence tools will change national supervision

**Syllabus**

- AMLA's General Board, Executive Board and supervisory and FIU compositions
- Selection for direct supervision: cross-border activity and residual risk
- Joint supervisory teams, direct supervisory powers and administrative sanctions
- Common risk assessment methodology, peer reviews and oversight of national supervisors

### Module 7: Supervisory Colleges, Cooperation and Sanctions Interplay

How AML/CFT supervisors, FIUs, prudential authorities and AMLA cooperate across borders through colleges and information exchange, and how AML/CFT controls interact with EU restrictive measures and targeted financial sanctions.

**Objectives**

- Operate AML/CFT supervisory colleges and home–host cooperation effectively
- Coordinate AML/CFT findings with prudential supervisors, FIUs and resolution authorities
- Assess how firms integrate targeted financial sanctions into AML/CFT controls

**Syllabus**

- AML/CFT colleges, information exchange and cooperation with prudential supervisors
- AML/CFT considerations in authorisation, qualifying holdings and withdrawal
- Targeted financial sanctions and screening obligations under the AML Regulation
- Sanctions evasion typologies and criminal enforcement of restrictive measures

### Module 8: Preparing Firms and Supervisors for the New Regime

Turning the package into a practical transition plan: gap analysis against the single rulebook, data and system changes, readiness for AMLA's methodology and possible selection, and the shift in supervisory expectations, ending with a planning workshop.

**Objectives**

- Conduct a structured gap analysis against the AML Regulation and emerging technical standards
- Prioritise remediation of data, systems, policies and governance
- Design a transition roadmap for the firm or authority

**Syllabus**

- Gap analysis: policies, CDD data, beneficial ownership and reporting
- Preparing for AMLA data requests, methodology and possible direct supervision
- Supervisory transition: methodologies, staffing and coordination with AMLA
- Workshop: a twelve-month AML package transition roadmap

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies, red-flag exercises and gap-analysis work, and modules can be selected or tailored for supervisors or obliged entities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of AML/CFT compliance or supervision is helpful. No legal background or prior knowledge of EU law is required.

## Core Reading

- Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 on the prevention of the use of the financial system for the purposes of money laundering or terrorist financing (AML Regulation)
- Directive (EU) 2024/1640 on the mechanisms to be put in place by Member States for the prevention of the use of the financial system for the purposes of money laundering or terrorist financing (AMLD6)
- Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 establishing the Authority for Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AMLA Regulation)
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets (Transfer of Funds Regulation)
