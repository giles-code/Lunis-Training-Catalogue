## Aim and Purpose

To give officials in finance ministries, treasuries and other public bodies a practical, non-technical grounding in the financial system, the architecture of financial regulation and the respective roles of government and independent regulators. Participants leave able to frame financial services policy, ask the right questions of regulators and industry, and represent the national interest in international standard-setting and peer review.

## What It Covers

How the financial system works, the economic case for regulation, regulatory architecture and models, independence and accountability of regulators, international standards and peer reviews, banking, insurance, securities and payments, financial stability, financial crime, the policy levers available to government, and designing a national financial services policy.

## Intended Audience

- Officials in finance ministries and treasuries responsible for financial services, banking and capital markets policy
- Senior officials and advisers to ministers who oversee or appoint financial regulators
- Staff of public agencies, cabinet offices and parliamentary committees who work on financial sector issues

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how the financial system channels savings into investment and where it can fail
- Compare the main models of regulatory architecture and their strengths and weaknesses
- Distinguish the roles of government and independent regulators and design effective accountability
- Explain the international standards and peer review processes that apply to the national system
- Identify the policy levers available to government and the trade-offs between them
- Design the elements of a coherent national financial services policy

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as an intensive programme over three to four days or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a plain-language briefing with case studies and policy discussion, and modules can be selected or tailored to the ministry's priorities.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of public policy or economic affairs is helpful. No technical background in finance or regulation is required.

## Core Reading

- BCBS (2024), Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision
- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- IAIS (2019), Insurance Core Principles and Common Framework for the Supervision of Internationally Active Insurance Groups
- IMF (2021), Financial Sector Assessment Program Review: Towards a More Stable and Sustainable Financial System
