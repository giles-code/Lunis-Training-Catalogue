## Aim and Purpose

The aim of this workshop is to give participants hands-on practice in supervisory decision-making, escalation, and coordination under time pressure through a simulated firm-distress scenario.

## Intended Audience

Mid-to-senior staff involved in crisis preparedness, recovery, and resolution planning.

## Learning Objectives

- Apply supervisory decision-making frameworks under time pressure
- Practise escalation protocols in a simulated crisis
- Coordinate a supervisory response across multiple functions
- Evaluate the effectiveness of decisions made during the simulation
- Identify lessons for real-world crisis preparedness

## Syllabus

**Session 1: Simulation briefing and setup (09:30–10:00)**
*Objective: Explain the simulation format and scenario*
- Overview of the simulation exercise and ground rules
- Introduction to the firm-distress scenario
- Roles and responsibilities within the simulation
Activities:
- Briefing and scenario introduction
- Team formation

**Session 2: Simulation Phase 1: initial response (10:00–11:30)**
*Objective: Apply decision-making frameworks under time pressure*
- Initial information reveal and rapid assessment
- Deciding on immediate supervisory actions
- Escalation decisions under incomplete information
Activities:
- Live simulation exercise: respond to the unfolding scenario in real time

**Session 3: Simulation Phase 2: coordination and escalation (12:30–14:00)**
*Objective: Coordinate a response across functions*
- Scenario escalates: coordinating across supervision, legal, communications
- Managing external stakeholders within the simulation
- Making decisions as the situation evolves
Activities:
- Live simulation exercise continues, with cross-functional coordination required

**Session 4: Debrief and lessons learned (14:15–15:00)**
*Objective: Evaluate decisions and identify lessons*
- Facilitator-led debrief of decisions made during the simulation
- Comparing team approaches and outcomes
- Extracting lessons for real-world crisis preparedness
Activities:
- Group debrief and discussion
- Individual reflection on lessons learned

## Structure and Format

Proposed as a half-day workshop (approx. 3.5 hours substantive content), comprising 4 sessions:
- Session 1: Simulation briefing and setup (09:30–10:00)
- Session 2: Simulation Phase 1: initial response (10:00–11:30)
- Session 3: Simulation Phase 2: coordination and escalation (12:30–14:00)
- Session 4: Debrief and lessons learned (14:15–15:00)

**Key Takeaways**
- Crisis decision-making under time pressure feels very different from calm, deliberate policy discussion
- Coordination failures, not just poor individual decisions, are often what turns a problem into a crisis
- Simulation exercises surface preparedness gaps that no amount of paper planning reveals
