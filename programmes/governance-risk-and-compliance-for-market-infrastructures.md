## Aim and Purpose

To give those who run and oversee market infrastructures a complete, practical view of what the PFMI and related standards require of them and how authorities test compliance. Participants leave able to strengthen governance and the risk framework, challenge models and stress tests, rehearse default management and recovery, and evidence resilience to their supervisors and overseers.

**Coverage:** The role and systemic importance of market infrastructures, the PFMI and the authorities' expectations, governance and ownership, the comprehensive risk framework, credit and liquidity risk, collateral, margin and procyclicality, default management, settlement finality and asset safekeeping, operational resilience and cyber, access, tiering, links and trading venue controls, and recovery, wind-down and supervisory engagement.

## Intended Audience

- Board members, risk and audit committee members and senior executives of trading venues, CCPs, CSDs, payment systems and trade repositories
- Chief risk officers, heads of compliance, internal audit and risk, treasury, default management and operations staff
- Regulatory affairs, legal and strategy teams preparing for supervisory assessments or new services

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

- Explain the infrastructure's systemic role and how the PFMI frame what is expected of it
- Evaluate governance, ownership and the comprehensive risk framework against the PFMI
- Challenge credit, liquidity, margin and collateral models, stress tests and backtesting
- Prepare and rehearse default management, recovery and orderly wind-down arrangements
- Strengthen settlement, safekeeping, operational and cyber resilience and access arrangements
- Evidence compliance and resilience to supervisors and overseers and agree an action plan

## Syllabus

### Module 1: Market Infrastructures, Systemic Importance and the PFMI

What trading venues, CCPs, CSDs, payment systems and trade repositories do, why they both reduce and concentrate systemic risk, and how the PFMI and authorities' responsibilities shape what is expected of them.

**Objectives**

- Explain the functions and interdependencies of the main types of infrastructure
- Identify why the firm's infrastructure is systemically important and what follows
- Apply the structure of the PFMI, key considerations and disclosure framework

**Syllabus**

- The trade lifecycle from execution to clearing, settlement and reporting
- Systemic importance, interdependencies and lessons from past disruptions
- PFMI principles, key considerations and the assessment methodology
- Supervision and oversight: what securities authorities and central banks expect

### Module 2: Governance, Ownership and the Board

How ownership, board composition and committee structures shape decisions on risk, access and fees, and how the board balances commercial objectives with its public interest and risk management duties.

**Objectives**

- Assess board composition, independence and risk committee effectiveness
- Manage conflicts between profitability, risk standards and open access
- Design governance that gives participants and stakeholders a credible voice

**Syllabus**

- Ownership models: user-owned, listed groups and vertical silos
- Board, risk committee and participant representation
- Conflicts of interest, fees and commercial incentives
- Transparency: disclosure framework and quantitative public disclosures

### Module 3: The Comprehensive Risk Management Framework

How an infrastructure builds an integrated framework covering credit, liquidity, operational, business, custody and investment risk, and the role of the second and third lines in testing that it works.

**Objectives**

- Design an integrated risk framework, appetite and policies across all risk types
- Organise the risk, compliance and internal audit functions for an infrastructure
- Manage general business, custody and investment risk and the capital to cover them

**Syllabus**

- Risk appetite, policies and model governance
- Independent risk, compliance and internal audit functions
- General business risk, custody and investment risk
- Model validation and independent review

### Module 4: Credit and Liquidity Risk Management

How infrastructures measure and cover credit and liquidity exposures to participants, the cover standards and stress testing behind them, and how to defend model assumptions under supervisory challenge.

**Objectives**

- Measure current and potential future exposures to participants
- Design stress testing, cover standards and qualifying liquid resources
- Defend scenarios, model assumptions and backtesting results to the authority

**Syllabus**

- Current and potential future exposure; cover 1 and cover 2 standards
- Liquidity needs, qualifying resources and settlement bank dependencies
- Stress scenario design, reverse stress testing and backtesting
- Weaknesses supervisors look for: optimistic scenarios, wrong-way risk and stale models

### Module 5: Collateral, Margin and Procyclicality

How initial and variation margin and collateral policies protect the infrastructure, the trade-off between prudence and procyclicality, and how to calibrate and communicate margin to participants.

**Objectives**

- Calibrate initial margin models, look-back periods and confidence levels
- Set eligible collateral, haircuts and concentration limits
- Manage procyclicality and the transparency of margin calls in stress

**Syllabus**

- Initial and variation margin: purpose, methods and calibration
- Collateral eligibility, haircuts, concentration limits and reuse
- Anti-procyclicality tools and margin transparency for participants
- Lessons from margin spikes in commodity and bond market stress

### Module 6: Default Management and Fire Drills

How the infrastructure prepares for and handles a participant default, from declaration, close-out and auction to the default waterfall, and how fire drills test that procedures work under pressure.

**Objectives**

- Design default rules, procedures and the sequence of the default waterfall
- Size the default fund and the infrastructure's own resources at risk
- Plan and run default management fire drills and auctions

**Syllabus**

- Declaring a default, porting client positions and close-out
- Default waterfall: margin, default fund, own resources and assessments
- Auction design, participant obligations and hedging
- Fire drill design, findings and remediation

### Module 7: Settlement, Finality and Asset Safekeeping

How settlement finality, money settlement, delivery versus payment and CSD safekeeping remove principal and custody risk, including the questions raised by DLT-based and tokenised settlement.

**Objectives**

- Establish the legal basis and moment of settlement finality
- Manage DvP and PvP models, money settlement assets and settlement bank risk
- Protect participants' assets through account structures and reconciliation

**Syllabus**

- Legal certainty and the moment of finality
- DvP, PvP and central bank versus commercial bank money
- Settlement fails, buy-ins and integrity of the issue
- DLT settlement, tokenised assets and stablecoin arrangements under the PFMI

### Module 8: Operational Resilience, Cyber and Critical Service Providers

How infrastructures manage operational, cyber and third-party risk, meet the expectation of rapid recovery and evidence resilience against severe but plausible scenarios.

**Objectives**

- Design operational risk and business continuity arrangements to recovery objectives
- Strengthen cyber resilience, including recovery from data corruption
- Manage dependencies on critical service providers and cloud

**Syllabus**

- Two-hour recovery objective and severe but plausible scenarios
- CPMI-IOSCO cyber resilience guidance, threat-led testing and data integrity
- Critical service providers, cloud concentration and exit options
- Incident management, root cause analysis and notification

### Module 9: Access, Tiering, Links and Trading Venue Controls

How to set fair, risk-based access criteria, manage the risks of tiered participation, client clearing and links, and operate the trading controls and surveillance expected of a venue.

**Objectives**

- Set risk-based, fair and open participation requirements and exit procedures
- Manage risks from indirect participants, client clearing and links
- Operate trading venue controls that support orderly markets

**Syllabus**

- Access criteria, monitoring and suspension of participants
- Tiered participation, client segregation and portability
- CCP links, CSD links and interoperability exposures
- Trading venue rules, circuit breakers and market surveillance

### Module 10: Recovery, Wind-Down and Supervisory Engagement

Brings the programme together: recovery planning and loss allocation, orderly wind-down and resolution, preparing for supervisory assessment and cooperative oversight, and a workshop on an infrastructure under stress.

**Objectives**

- Design recovery plans, triggers and tools for uncovered losses and liquidity shortfalls
- Prepare orderly wind-down plans and support resolution planning
- Evidence PFMI compliance to supervisors and overseers in assessments and crises

**Syllabus**

- Recovery tools: cash calls, variation margin gains haircutting and tear-ups
- Orderly wind-down, liquid net assets and the Key Attributes
- Self-assessments, cooperative oversight and crisis management groups
- Workshop: a participant default with cross-border spillovers

## Structure and Format

Ten modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a five-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a briefing with case studies, model challenge and fire drill exercises, and modules can be selected or tailored to the type of infrastructure and to board or practitioner audiences.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Experience of trading, clearing, settlement or payment systems is helpful. No prior study of the PFMI is required.

## Core Reading

- CPMI-IOSCO, Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures (2012)
- FSB, Key Attributes of Effective Resolution Regimes for Financial Institutions (2014)
- IOSCO, Regulatory Issues Raised by Changes in Market Structure (2013)
- FSB, Effective Practices for Cyber Incident Response and Recovery (2020)
