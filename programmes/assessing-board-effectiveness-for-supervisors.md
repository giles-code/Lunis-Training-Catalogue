## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical method for assessing board dynamics and decision-making, not just board composition and terms of reference. Participants leave able to read board papers and minutes critically, test the quality of decisions and challenge, plan and conduct board-level interviews, and draw evidence-based conclusions.

## What It Covers

How the board works in practice, agendas, papers and information quality, information asymmetry and side conversations, oversight versus micromanagement, judging decision quality, culture, challenge and psychological safety, groupthink, board personalities, evaluation, succession, skills and AI in the boardroom, and case-study interviews.

## Intended Audience

- Supervisors and governance specialists who assess boards and senior management of regulated firms
- Staff conducting fit and proper, governance or culture reviews and interviewing chairs and directors
- Senior supervisors and team leaders who review governance assessments and decide the supervisory response

## Learning Objectives

- Explain why sound governance structures can coexist with ineffective board dynamics
- Assess agendas, board papers and management information for quality and real decision support
- Evaluate the quality of board decisions by testing the process rather than the outcome
- Identify signs of weak challenge, low psychological safety and groupthink
- Plan and conduct evidence-led interviews with chairs, directors and executives
- Reach and evidence a supervisory conclusion on board effectiveness and the appropriate response

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Modules combine short input with analysis of board packs and minutes, group exercises and role-play interviews, ending in a case-study interview workshop, and can be selected or tailored to the authority's governance framework.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of governance assessment or supervisory interviews is helpful. No board experience is required.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee, Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015)
- G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (2023)
- FSB, Guidance on Supervisory Interaction with Financial Institutions on Risk Culture (2014)
- Amy C. Edmondson, The Fearless Organization: Creating Psychological Safety in the Workplace (2018)
