## Aim and Purpose

To give tax investigators, FIU analysts and financial investigators a shared understanding of how tax crime and money laundering intersect, and how each agency's tools can strengthen the others' cases. Participants leave able to recognise the laundering of tax crime proceeds, use international information exchange, unpick offshore structures and run effective joint investigations.

## What It Covers

Tax crimes as predicate offences for money laundering, the OECD principles for fighting tax crime, offshore structures and beneficial ownership, exchange of information on request and automatically, inter-agency cooperation and joint investigations, professional enablers, and recovering the proceeds of tax crime.

## Intended Audience

- Tax crime investigators, tax auditors and revenue intelligence officers
- FIU analysts and financial investigators handling tax-related intelligence
- Prosecutors and asset recovery officers working on tax and laundering cases

## Learning Objectives

- Explain the links between tax crime and money laundering and the relevant international standards
- Identify indicators of tax evasion and laundering in financial and tax data
- Analyse offshore structures, nominee arrangements and beneficial ownership
- Use exchange of information on request, automatically and spontaneously
- Plan joint investigations across tax, FIU and law-enforcement agencies
- Disrupt professional enablers and recover the proceeds of tax crime

## Structure and Format

Six modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a multi-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a technical briefing with case studies and structure-mapping exercises, ending in a joint investigation workshop, and modules can be selected or tailored to the participating agencies.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some experience of tax investigation, financial intelligence or financial crime work is helpful. No specialist knowledge of international tax law is required.

## Core Reading

- OECD (2021), Fighting Tax Crime – The Ten Global Principles, Second Edition
- OECD (2019), Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Awareness Handbook for Tax Examiners and Tax Auditors
- OECD (2021), Ending the Shell Game: Cracking down on the Professionals who enable Tax and White Collar Crimes
- OECD (2017), Standard for Automatic Exchange of Financial Account Information in Tax Matters, Second Edition
- FATF (2024), Guidance on Beneficial Ownership and Transparency of Legal Arrangements
