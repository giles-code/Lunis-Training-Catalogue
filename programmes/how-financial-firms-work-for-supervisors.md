## Aim and Purpose

To give supervisors a practical understanding of the business models of banks, investment firms, funds, payment firms and crypto-asset firms, and of the financial information that reveals their health. Participants leave able to read a firm's accounts with a supervisory eye, understand capital and liquidity basics and frame the right questions for management.

## What It Covers

How different financial firms make money, reading the balance sheet, income statement and cash flow, capital and liquidity basics, group structures and intra-group dependencies, sector-specific risks across banking, investment, funds, payments and crypto-assets, and a business model case workshop.

## Intended Audience

- New joiners and developing staff in supervisory, authorisation and policy functions
- Supervisors and inspectors moving into a new sector or wishing to strengthen their financial analysis
- Staff from legal, enforcement or support functions who need to understand firms' business and finances

## Learning Objectives

- Explain how banks, investment firms, funds, payment firms and crypto-asset firms generate revenue and incur risk
- Interpret the main financial statements and identify warning signs of financial weakness
- Describe the basic principles of capital and liquidity and why they matter to the authority
- Analyse group structures, intra-group dependencies and where risks can be hidden
- Identify the key risks for each sector and the supervisory questions they raise
- Apply business model analysis to form an initial view of a firm's viability and sustainability

## Structure and Format

Eight modules of about three hours each, delivered in person, virtually or in a hybrid format, either as a four-day programme or as a series of half-day sessions. Each module combines a short briefing with worked examples and exercises using realistic financial statements, and modules can be selected or tailored to the sectors the authority supervises.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

No accounting qualification is required. Basic numeracy and some familiarity with financial services are helpful.

## Core Reading

- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2024), Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision
- Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2008), Principles for Sound Liquidity Risk Management and Supervision
- IOSCO (2017), Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation
- FSB (2023), High-level Recommendations for the Regulation, Supervision and Oversight of Crypto-asset Activities and Markets
