## Aim and Purpose

To give participants a reliable, comparable picture of fund regulation in key jurisdictions: the fund vehicles and regulator, how funds and managers are authorised, how funds are distributed and accessed cross-border, and recent developments. Participants leave able to compare jurisdictions, plan market entry and engage confidently with local regulators and advisers.

## What It Covers

Ireland and Luxembourg, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, France and Germany, the Cayman Islands and other offshore centres, Hong Kong, Mainland China, Singapore, Australia, Japan, and Taiwan and South Korea.

## Intended Audience

- Staff of fund managers, distributors and fund service providers planning or managing business in these jurisdictions
- Compliance, legal, product and business development professionals responsible for fund ranges and market access
- Staff of supervisory authorities and policy teams benchmarking their fund regime against other jurisdictions

## Learning Objectives

- Describe the fund vehicles, regulators and legal frameworks in each jurisdiction
- Explain how funds and their managers are authorised, registered or licensed
- Compare the routes for distributing domestic and foreign funds, including cross-border access schemes
- Identify jurisdiction-specific rules that differ from international norms
- Assess recent and forthcoming regulatory developments in each market
- Apply the briefings to fund domicile, distribution and market entry decisions

## Structure and Format

Eleven country briefings designed as recorded webinars of about 30 minutes to an hour each, supported by a live Q&A session with the trainer. Each briefing follows a common structure for easy comparison, and briefings can be taken individually, as a full series or as add-ons to the global and regional briefings.

## Useful Prior Knowledge and Experience

Some familiarity with investment funds and international fund regulation is helpful, for example from the global and regional briefings. No legal background is required.

## Core Reading

- IOSCO, Objectives and Principles of Securities Regulation (2017)
- IOSCO, Examination of Governance for Collective Investment Schemes (2006–2007)
- FSB, Revised Policy Recommendations to Address Structural Vulnerabilities from Liquidity Mismatch in Open-Ended Funds (2023)
